## Abstract-derived summary

Seventy children with falciparum cerebral malaria in southern Benin were followed to outcome (50 survived, 20 died). Cerebrospinal fluid multiplex PCR for 14 pathogens excluded co-infection. Seventeen plasma and seven urinary markers of inflammation and oxidative stress were measured at admission and again at days 3 and 30. Univariately, TNF, IL-1beta, IL-10, IL-8, CXCL9, granzyme B and angiopoietin-2 were higher, and urinary PGE2 metabolite lower, in children who died. In multivariable analysis, high admission IL-8 was the main independent death risk (adjusted OR 14.2, P=0.002). Follow-up samples identified resolution markers (CXCL5, CCL17, CCL22, urinary 15-F2t-isoprostane) alongside neutrophil, endothelial and oxidative signals of worsening.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** primary human cohort; associations with mortality.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Admission IL-8 was the main mortality risk factor; follow-up profiles reflect neutrophil, endothelial, inflammatory and oxidative processes with possible therapeutic implications.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** INF cytokine pathway rated directly demonstrated in both, at association level. Canine: CB037 (IL-6, MCP-1, TNF higher in non-survivors; IL-8 fell with severity), CB017, CB098/CB101, CB082, CB109, CB107. The IFN/regulatory axis (IL-10, CXCL9, granzyme B) is speculative (CB109, CB082, CB075, CB008). Differences: IL-8 direction conflicts; cerebral sequestration is P. falciparum-specific; the canine cohorts are small and often unadjusted for co-infection; no primary human babesiosis equivalent was found.
- **Key limitations:** 20 deaths; single country; no uncomplicated-malaria controls; observational.
