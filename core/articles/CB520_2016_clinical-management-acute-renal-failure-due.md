## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a single Golden retriever that presented in recumbent condition. Diagnostic work-up, the methods of which the abstract does not specify, led the authors to attribute acute renal failure to infection with both Babesia canis and Babesia gibsoni. The animal was managed with vigorous fluid therapy together with antiparasitic drugs directed at both Babesia species, and the report describes this management as successful. The abstract gives no laboratory values, renal markers, age, sex, duration of illness or follow-up period, so the extent of renal injury cannot be quantified from the summary alone. The report is a single-animal clinical description centred on the management of a renal complication of babesiosis in a dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute renal failure in a dog with B. canis and B. gibsoni infection was successfully managed with aggressive fluid therapy and chemotherapeutics against both parasites.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report documents acute renal failure as a complication in a dog infected with two Babesia species, which fits the renal-injury category. It is a single animal, so it shows that the combination can occur but gives no information on frequency, mechanism or prognostic markers. The link between the infection and the renal failure rests on the authors' clinical judgement.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single animal; abstract only, with no laboratory data, renal markers or follow-up; species-identification and diagnostic methods not stated; co-infection with other agents not discussed in the abstract; management focus rather than pathophysiology; no control or comparison.
