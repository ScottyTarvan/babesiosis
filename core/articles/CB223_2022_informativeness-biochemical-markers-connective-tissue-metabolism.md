## Abstract-derived summary

Twelve dogs with babesiosis (German Shepherd, Labrador, Rottweiler; 1-5 years) were compared with ten healthy dogs of similar age. Haematology, serum chemistry and urinalysis were done, plus colorimetric assays of serum glycoproteins, sialic acids and chondroitin sulfates. Infected dogs showed a cytolytic and cholestatic pattern, with higher bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase and beta-lipoproteins; the authors ascribe the bilirubin rise mainly to unconjugated pigment from intravascular haemolysis. Glycoproteins rose 1.63-fold, sialic acids 1.36-fold and chondroitin sulfates 1.8-fold. The authors read these as signs of a systemic inflammatory and protective response, with chondroitin sulfate linked to vascular protection against haemolysis products, and propose the markers for assessing acute-phase inflammation.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Raised glycoproteins, sialic acids and chondroitin sulfates indicate a systemic inflammatory-protective reaction and could be used to assess the acute phase of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A descriptive biomarker study. Raised protein-bound carbohydrate markers fit a nonspecific acute phase response, consistent with the acute phase protein literature. The "compensatory endothelial protection" reading of chondroitin sulfate is speculative: no endothelial, glycocalyx or coagulation variable was measured, and the cited support is extrapolated from rodent liver and in vitro work. Unconjugated bilirubin was not measured (only total bilirubin), so the haemolytic origin is inferred, not shown. No severity, outcome or disease-control comparison, so diagnostic "informativeness" is not actually tested (no ROC or sensitivity data).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=12 vs 10; single time point and early-stage dogs only (authors state results apply only to uncomplicated cases); species not identified and co-infections not excluded; smear-only diagnosis; no non-babesial inflammatory disease controls; multiple comparisons without correction; haemoglobin, haematocrit, urea and creatinine did not differ significantly, so anaemia and azotaemia were not established; unusual in-house methods (Shteinberg-Dotsenko, Hess, Slutsky) without validation data; text describes leukocytosis although the table shows lower leukocytes in infected dogs; regional journal, peer-review practice not assessed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
