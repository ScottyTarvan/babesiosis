## Abstract-derived summary

A two-year-old local-breed Indian dog was brought in after three days of poor appetite, urinary incontinence and hind-limb weakness. On examination it was febrile (104.1°F), dehydrated and pale, anuric, recumbent and severely debilitated. Red cell count and haemoglobin were reduced, thrombocytopenia was present, and serum creatinine and blood urea nitrogen were elevated. From blood smear findings and haematology the authors diagnosed babesiosis complicated by acute kidney injury. Treatment with diminazene aceturate plus doxycycline, together with supportive renal care, was reported to be successful. The abstract gives no numerical laboratory values, parasite species, urinalysis, imaging or follow-up details, so the reported success rests on the authors' narrative alone.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The dog had canine babesiosis with acute kidney injury and was successfully treated with diminazene aceturate, doxycycline and renal support.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates the recognised association of babesiosis with anuric AKI, anaemia and thrombocytopenia (a syndrome-level observation). It does not show mechanism: haemoglobin-pigment injury, hypoperfusion/hypoxia, inflammation and microthrombosis are all plausible but none was investigated in the abstract. Mechanistic depth is low; the value is as a documented clinical presentation and survival outcome.
