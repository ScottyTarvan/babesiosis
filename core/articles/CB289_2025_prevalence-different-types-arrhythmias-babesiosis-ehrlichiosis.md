## Abstract-derived summary

Figures and attributions beyond the abstract (design details, pathogen counts, ECG-case split, arrhythmia percentages, age range and the authors' proposed mechanisms) come from the full text. A hospital-based survey from Palampur, India, between April 2024 and August 2025. Of 2,755 dogs presented, 465 (16.9%) had haemoparasites on stained blood smears: B. gibsoni 320, E. canis 126, B. canis 6, Hepatozoon canis 2 and mixed infection 11. Six-lead ECGs from positive dogs showed abnormalities in 108 (23.2%); babesiosis accounted for 58 of these, ehrlichiosis 35 and mixed infection 13. The most frequent findings were tachycardia (44.4%), sinus arrhythmia (23.1%), low-voltage QRS (20.3%), QRS notching (19.4%), atrial standstill (16.6%) and ST-segment deviation (14.8-17.6%). Males and dogs aged 2 to 6 years predominated. The authors ascribe the changes to anaemic hypoxia, inflammatory myocardial injury and metabolic disturbance, and recommend routine ECG in tick-borne disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Tick-borne infections, especially babesiosis and ehrlichiosis, carry substantial cardiac involvement, and routine ECG should be part of their work-up.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Gives a frequency of ECG abnormalities in smear-positive dogs, mostly B. gibsoni, supporting a cardiac phenotype in complicated disease. It does not establish cause: sinus tachycardia (commonest) is non-specific in anaemic or febrile dogs, and no troponin, echocardiography, electrolytes or PCV adjustment link ECG changes to myocardial injury. Babesia and Ehrlichia effects cannot be separated, and "conclusively demonstrates" overstates a no-control survey.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no healthy control or reference ECG group; smear-only species identification and likely missed co-infections; Babesia cases not split by species for ECG; internal inconsistencies (58+35+13=106, not 108; 13 co-infected cardiac cases versus 11 co-infected dogs overall); no severity, haematology-ECG association or outcome data; referral-hospital selection; rapid review (33 days from receipt to acceptance) in a broad-scope journal, peer-review rigour not verified.
