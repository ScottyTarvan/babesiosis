## Abstract-derived summary

This single-author narrative review introduces canine babesiosis as an infection of red cells by tick-borne protozoa, listing eight species that infect dogs, each tied to particular regions. It notes persistence of the parasite in tick populations via transstadial and transovarial passage, so foci continue without a vertebrate host. Usual clinical signs are fever, lethargy, weakness, pallor, jaundice and haemoglobinuria, with peracute, acute, subacute and subclinical courses described. The author states that severity depends on species and strain virulence and on host factors such as age, immune status and concurrent disease. Treatment relies on antiprotozoal drugs, some antibiotics or combinations, with species-specific differences; clinical cure of the acute phase often leaves the parasite in the host.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** the author concludes that therapy differs by Babesia species and that dogs often recover from the acute phase while the parasite persists.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Peripheral to pathophysiology: the abstract mentions only strain virulence and host response as determinants of clinical variability, with no mechanistic content on haemolysis, inflammation, coagulopathy or organ injury. Retained as a canine review because screening adjudicated it eligible, but expected yield for the mechanistic synthesis is low. The persistence statement bears on chronic carrier state rather than acute pathogenesis.
