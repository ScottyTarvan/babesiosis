## Abstract-derived summary

Diminazene aceturate is widely used against canine babesiosis, and it had been suggested that it might lower blood pressure and worsen the hypotension seen in affected dogs. The authors tested the drug in six healthy German shepherds aged 18 to 24 months (mean weight about 30 kg). Each received 4.2 mg/kg intramuscularly, and arterial pressure was measured directly at 14 time points from 5 minutes before dosing to 120 minutes after. Blood pressure did not differ significantly between any time points. Heart rate rose briefly at 5 minutes. The authors conclude that the current antipyrine-containing formulation does not change blood pressure in healthy adult dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Small experimental pharmacodynamic study in healthy dogs.
- **Relevance:** For Q5, it argues against a direct haemodynamic (blood pressure) effect of diminazene as an explanation for early post-treatment deterioration, at least in uninfected dogs. It says nothing about inflammatory surge, haematocrit or organ function after treatment in infected dogs.
- **Key limitations:** Six dogs of one breed and age range; healthy animals, so it cannot show how the drug behaves in dogs that are hypotensive, anaemic or inflamed; 2 h observation window; single dose and formulation; no inflammatory markers; no control group described in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
