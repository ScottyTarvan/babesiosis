## Abstract-derived summary

The authors report a rapidly progressive tick-borne disease in an elderly male Bernese Mountain Dog that ended in death, using the case to show what babesiosis can mean for the health and survival of dogs. They give particular weight to the possibility of autoimmune anaemia and thrombocytopenia as complications. The report covers the presenting signs, the diagnostic methods used, the clinical course and how the animal responded to treatment. They conclude that infection with B. canis produced advanced haematological changes that disturbed haemostasis throughout the body. The abstract gives no numerical results, and the immune-mediated component is raised as a risk rather than demonstrated by a named test.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis infection caused advanced blood-system changes with disrupted haemostasis, with autoimmune anaemia and thrombocytopenia a risk.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single-patient illustration of severe haemolytic anaemia, marked thrombocytopenia and multi-organ decline (ERY, COA). Immune-mediated destruction is plausible but not shown: no direct antiglobulin test or PCR is reported, and anaemia plus thrombocytopenia are explained by babesiosis itself. Treatment (glucocorticoids, transfusions) confounds interpretation of the course.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no necropsy; species not molecularly confirmed; age inconsistent between title, abstract and text; no Coombs test; haematology table unclear; confounded by treatment.
