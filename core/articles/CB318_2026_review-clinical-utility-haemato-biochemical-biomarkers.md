## Abstract-derived summary

This narrative review argues that routine haematology and serum biochemistry could provide affordable severity markers in canine Babesia gibsoni infection, a parasite increasingly reported outside its traditional endemic zones. The authors describe a clinical spectrum from subclinical or asymptomatic infection through to severe multiorgan dysfunction and death, note that no specific anti-babesial drug effective against it is available, and that relapse is common. They propose that biomarkers help gauge which organs are involved, how severe disease is, whether treatment is working and how illness evolves. The abstract reports no quantitative results; it calls for identifying clinically meaningful, cost-effective indicators among standard blood parameters that reveal involvement of one or several organ systems. Per the full text, sections cover red cell indices, platelets, immune-mediated haemolysis, urea and creatinine, cardiac and liver enzymes, bilirubin, albumin and glucose.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that clinically relevant, financially sustainable biomarkers among standard haematological and biochemical parameters are needed to detect single or multiple organ involvement.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A narrative map of reported abnormalities (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotemia, hepatic enzyme and bilirubin changes, hypoalbuminaemia, hypoglycaemia) in B. gibsoni infection, relevant to syndrome description and severity biomarkers. It pools no data, proposes no validated thresholds and does not establish mechanism; biomarker associations should not be read as causal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no search strategy was evident in the page extraction; relies on secondary citation of mostly small, older or regional studies; at least one quoted diagnostic accuracy figure (cardiac troponin) was taken from a human myocardial infarction study rather than canine babesiosis; no article-level peer-review statement or received/accepted dates found (journal-level "Peer Reviewed" banner only), so peer-review quality is unverified.
