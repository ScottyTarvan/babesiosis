## Abstract-derived summary
Adults with severe sepsis and acute respiratory failure from a prospective cohort were assessed for links between endothelial injury and kidney damage. Plasma angiopoietin-2, soluble VE-cadherin, endocan and syndecan-1 were measured at enrolment. Of 228 patients, 141 developed severe AKI (KDIGO stage 2 or 3). All four markers were higher in these patients, but only angiopoietin-2 remained independently associated (odds ratio 6.07 per log increase, 95% CI 2.34-15.78). Angiopoietin-2 was also raised with hepatic, coagulation and circulatory failure, was higher in non-pulmonary than pulmonary sepsis, and related to death. The authors positioned the work as evidence that endothelial injury, rather than tubular damage alone, helps explain kidney failure in sepsis.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** prospective observational human biomarker cohort, non-parasitic disease.
- **Authors' conclusion:** early endothelial dysfunction, best captured by angiopoietin-2, is involved in sepsis-induced AKI and wider organ dysfunction.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** generic soluble endothelial activation is rated directly demonstrated in both hosts, but only at class level: CB004 (n=30 vs 10 controls) found raised thrombomodulin, VCAM-1 and HMGB-1; CB145 and CB087 add thromboinflammation and endothelial desquamation. Ang-2/Tie2 is mechanistically plausible only, as no canine study measured it. The syndecan-1 and glycocalyx link is speculative (CB080, CB146, CB111). Microvascular dysfunction with circulatory and coagulation failure is plausible (CB041, CB145). Differences: sepsis has no parasite, Ang-2 is non-specific to critical illness, and human assays need canine validation.
- **Key limitations:** adults with respiratory failure only; enrolment-time sampling; association, not causation.
