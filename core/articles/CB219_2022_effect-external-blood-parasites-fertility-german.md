## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether brown dog tick (Rhipicephalus sanguineus) infestation and Babesia infection affect reproductive hormones in German Shepherds. Eighty clinic dogs were examined; 21 (26.5%) carried ticks, and 12 (15%) were febrile, anorexic, pale and weak with tachycardia, with Babesia seen on blood films (17% of males, 12.8% of females). Testosterone in tick-only males was 2.01-3.2 ng/ml, in males with ticks plus Babesia 0.75-1.35 ng/ml, and in healthy males 3.04-4.1 ng/ml. Progesterone in estrous bitches was 2.5-3.4 ng/ml with ticks alone, 0.2-0.79 ng/ml with ticks plus Babesia, and 2.9-3.4 ng/ml in healthy bitches. The author concludes that ticks alone have little effect, whereas persistent infestation together with babesiosis may affect fertility.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Tick infestation alone has little fertility impact, but prolonged infestation combined with babesiosis can affect fertility.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests lower sex-steroid concentrations in acutely ill, Babesia-positive dogs, an endocrine facet of the systemic disease phenotype. It does not show that Babesia impairs fertility: no fertility outcomes (mating, conception, semen quality) are reported in the abstract, and systemic illness, fever, anaemia and stress can independently suppress hormones.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Species not identified (blood film only); ranges rather than group statistics reported; group sizes, assay method and estrous staging not given in abstract; co-infections not excluded; clinic-based sample; peer-review practice of the journal not verified.
