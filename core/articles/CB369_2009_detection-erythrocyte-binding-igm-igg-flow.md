## Abstract-derived summary

Flow cytometry immunophenotyping was used to test whether sick dogs infected with Babesia canis canis or B. canis vogeli had IgG or IgM bound to erythrocyte membranes at diagnosis. Infection was confirmed by blood smear and PCR-RFLP in 30 dogs (24 B. c. canis, 6 B. c. vogeli). Anaemia affected 92% of B. c. canis dogs and was mostly non-regenerative (94%); 67% of B. c. vogeli dogs had regenerative anaemia. Eccentrocytes were seen in 33% of B. c. canis cases. Four of six B. c. vogeli dogs carried membrane-bound antibodies, one was equivocal and one negative, whereas no B. c. canis dog was positive. Mean percentages of IgG- and IgM-coated erythrocytes were significantly lower in B. c. canis. Antibody binding to red cells seems absent in B. c. canis but common in B. c. vogeli at diagnosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** At the time of diagnosis, immune-mediated formation of erythrocyte membrane-binding IgG and IgM does not appear to be involved in B. c. canis infection, but it is present in most B. c. vogeli infections.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The two Babesia groups differed in both the type of anaemia and the presence of erythrocyte-bound immunoglobulins. Bound IgG and IgM were found in most B. c. vogeli dogs and in none of the B. c. canis dogs. This is consistent with an immune-mediated component of erythrocyte injury in B. c. vogeli infection, but the design cannot show that the antibodies produced the regenerative anaemia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small B. c. vogeli group (6 dogs) against 24 B. c. canis dogs; single time point at diagnosis; no uninfected control group mentioned in the abstract; study setting and recruitment period not stated in the abstract; bound immunoglobulin shows antibody coating but not its target or its effect on red cell survival; full text not read.
