## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective review of hospital records from a Nigerian teaching hospital covered January 2018 to December 2021 and included 230 dogs, of which 19 had confirmed hemoparasitic infection, mainly babesiosis and ehrlichiosis. Complete blood count data from the infected dogs were examined for changes linked to these diseases. The authors report that anaemia, shown by lowered packed cell volume and haemoglobin, was common, and that eosinopenia appeared consistently, which they interpret as a change in immune response. They conclude that routine haematology is useful for early screening of dogs with compatible signs. The abstract gives no numerical results, no breakdown by pathogen, no description of how infection was confirmed and no comparison group.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis and ehrlichiosis are associated with notable haematological changes, chiefly anaemia and eosinopenia, supporting routine haematology.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds low-weight descriptive data on anaemia in tick-borne infections; it cannot separate Babesia from Ehrlichia effects in the abstract. The "consistent eosinopenia" claim is doubtful for babesiosis: the publisher page lists a Babesia-group mean eosinophil count (0.24 ×10³/µL), which may not be low against usual canine reference intervals (reference ranges not checked), so the eosinopenia may instead reflect the Ehrlichia subgroup; this is an unverified inference from group means.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** 12 Babesia dogs; no healthy control group; diagnostic method not stated; retrospective records; SEM-based summaries; no species-level molecular confirmation. Journal/publisher caution: 10 days from receipt to acceptance and a publisher (Science Publishing Group) that has been questioned in the literature (background knowledge, not verified here); peer-review rigour uncertain.
