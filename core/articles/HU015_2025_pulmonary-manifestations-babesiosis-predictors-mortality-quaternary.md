## Abstract-derived summary
Single-centre retrospective review of 147 hospitalised adults with confirmed babesiosis at a New York referral hospital, describing lung involvement. Sixty-three cases were severe, 41 needed intensive care and 10 died. Respiratory symptoms affected 42%, and 55 patients had an abnormal chest radiograph. Among severe cases, 75% had parasitaemia of 10% or more. Logistic regression linked severity to age 70 or older, severe dyspnoea, abnormal radiograph, higher white cell count, haemoglobin of 8 g/dL or less and raised creatinine. Mortality was linked to age, encephalopathy, AKI, dyspnoea, abnormal radiograph and intubation. Lyme seropositivity was 28%, and exchange transfusion did not track with outcome.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** descriptive retrospective cohort with univariable and regression associations.
- **Authors' conclusion:** pulmonary symptoms and abnormal radiographs are not uncommon and signal worse outcomes.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** parasitaemia-severity and AKI/respiratory failure-mortality links are rated directly demonstrated in both hosts (CB141, CB123; CB025, CB061, CB043), with CB073 and CB040 as caveats. ARDS with alveolar injury is plausible (CB111 diffuse-alveolar-damage-like histology; CB102, CB025). Haemolysis and bilirubin severity markers are plausible (CB040, CB099, CB067). Pulmonary endothelial activation, post-treatment lung injury, and age/co-infection effects are speculative (CB080, CB123, CB060, CB126). Differences: B. microti in older comorbid adults vs B. rossi, B. canis, B. gibsoni, B. vogeli in dogs; Lyme co-infection has no canine counterpart.
- **Key limitations:** only 10 deaths, so predictors are unlikely independent; confounding by age and co-infection; no mechanistic lung data.
