## Abstract-derived summary

The authors hypothesised that failure of the haem-haemopexin scavenging pathway contributes to severe and fatal malaria. They measured plasma haemin, haemopexin, haptoglobin and haemoglobin in 87 Ugandan children with falciparum malaria, comparing uncomplicated disease with severe malarial anaemia and cerebral malaria. Haemin was higher (p<0.01) and haemopexin lower (p<0.0001) in severe malarial anaemia and cerebral malaria than in uncomplicated disease. Among 58 severe cases, higher haemin and cell-free haemoglobin at presentation went with later death (p<0.05); no haptoglobin result is reported in the abstract. In mice, cerebral-malaria-susceptible C57BL/6 animals had less circulating haemopexin than resistant BALB/c animals, and haemopexin-gene deletion raised mortality versus wild-type littermates (p<0.05).

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** human case-control study (observational) plus murine experiments.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Presenting haemin and haemopexin track severity, haemin and cell-free haemoglobin predict outcome, and the mouse data support a causal haem-haemopexin role.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** ERY (free haem and scavenger depletion) rated speculative. No canine study measured haem or haemopexin; CB117 (3 vs 3 dogs) found haemopexin higher in infected dogs, the opposite direction. Indirect haemolysis and oxidative evidence: CB099, CB082, CB006, CB114. Differences: P. falciparum sequestration, paediatric hosts, possible absence of haemozoin in Babesia (unverified).
- **Key limitations:** modest sample; case-control design; mouse model of cerebral malaria not directly transferable; mouse and outcome subgroups small.
