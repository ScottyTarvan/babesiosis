## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined whether serum cystatin-C could indicate kidney function in dogs with natural infection by Babesia canis vogeli or Ehrlichia canis. Forty-six dogs were allocated to three groups: 16 with uncomplicated B. c. vogeli babesiosis, 10 with canine monocytic ehrlichiosis, and 20 healthy controls negative for both agents. Urea, creatinine and the urea-to-creatinine ratio were measured, and cystatin-C was quantified with a validated species-specific ELISA used as the reference. Mean cystatin-C was 5.28 mg/L in the babesiosis group, 3.02 mg/L in the ehrlichiosis group and 2.30 mg/L in controls. The authors read these values as suggesting that kidney function is affected in uncomplicated babesiosis but not in ehrlichiosis, and they propose cystatin-C for early detection of kidney injury in both diseases.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Renal function appears to be affected in uncomplicated canine babesiosis caused by B. c. vogeli, judged by serum cystatin-C, whereas dogs with ehrlichiosis showed no cystatin-C elevation. Cystatin-C might serve for early detection of renal injury in both diseases.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Serum cystatin-C was higher in dogs with uncomplicated B. c. vogeli babesiosis than in E. canis-infected dogs and healthy controls, which fits reduced glomerular filtration but does not measure it directly. Creatinine did not differ between groups (full text), so the study supports cystatin-C as a candidate marker rather than showing renal injury directly. Without follow-up, the data do not show whether the changes predict later azotemia or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small groups (16, 10 and 20 dogs); case-control design with no follow-up; no direct measure of glomerular filtration rate or renal histology (full text); healthy controls had unexplained high urea (full text); no outcome data; preliminary study by the authors' own description (full text).

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Dogs were mixed-breed, aged 1 to 6 years, weighing 16 to 40 kg, and were described as client-owned (full text)
  - Babesiosis was confirmed by blood smear, and the species by PCR and reverse line blot; co-infection with E. canis was excluded by PCR, and anaplasmosis, leishmaniasis and Lyme disease were excluded by rapid ELISA (SNAP 4Dx), smear and cytology, and available IFAT/PCR results (full text)
  - Uncomplicated babesiosis was defined as a presentation attributable to haemolytic anaemia only (full text)
  - Mean serum urea was 35.99 mg/dl in babesiosis, 40.87 mg/dl in ehrlichiosis and 71.85 mg/dl in controls (full text)
  - Mean serum creatinine was 1.78, 1.53 and 1.45 mg/dl in the babesiosis, ehrlichiosis and control groups, with no significant difference (P=0.209) (full text)
  - Mean urea:creatinine ratio was 20.45, 29.38 and 59.46 in the babesiosis, ehrlichiosis and control groups (full text)
  - Cystatin-C in the babesiosis group differed from both other groups at P<0.001; analysis was ANOVA with Tukey test, with significance set at P<0.001 (full text)
  - Cystatin-C was measured with a BioVendor canine ELISA kit; the authors note that haemolysis and icterus are reported not to interfere with cystatin-C measurement (full text)
  - Kidney biopsies were not performed, as invasive procedures were judged not ethically possible in client-owned dogs under clinical conditions (full text)
