## Abstract-derived summary

This hospital series described 102 dogs with natural Babesia gibsoni infection, diagnosed by examining blood smears from ear tips, to characterise multisystem effects and propose a management protocol for critical cases. The disease occurred in as many as 14 breeds. Clinical signs covered gastrointestinal, cardiorespiratory, haemorrhagic, neurological and mucosal changes, among others. The mean clinical score was 9.2 ± 0.34 (range 5 to 16), and case fatality was 9.7%. Fever was inconsistent, no dog had haemoglobinuria, and five showed jaundice. Haemoglobin ranged from below 3.0 to 16.4 g/dl. Severely ill dogs received fluids, antiparasitic drugs, steroids, haematinics and other supportive agents, with 80.0% reported as managed successfully. The authors suggest that pomegranate juice coincided with improved haemoglobin, that no single sign is specific, and that combinations such as irregular fever, weight loss, depression, pale mucous membranes and splenomegaly raise suspicion most.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** No single sign is specific for babesiosis, but certain physical findings in combination raise suspicion most. Computing a clinical score may improve cure rates under field conditions.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series documents a heterogeneous multisystem clinical picture, with a wide haemoglobin range and a 9.7% case fatality in one hospital population. Treatment observations were uncontrolled, so they do not show that any agent, including pomegranate juice, changed haemoglobin or outcome. The figures describe one hospital's cases and should not be generalised without controls.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no control group; diagnosis and species by blood smear without PCR; clinical score not defined in the abstract; number of critical cases not reported; laboratory data other than haemoglobin not reported; uncontrolled treatment observations; single hospital; the OpenAlex abstract repeats part of the treatment passage.
