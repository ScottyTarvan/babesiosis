## Abstract-derived summary

The authors profiled clinical, haematobiochemical and oxidative changes in dogs infected with B. gibsoni and tested N-acetylcysteine (NAC) as an add-on to standard triple therapy. Nineteen dogs were smear- and PCR-positive; twelve similarly aged Labrador Retrievers were enrolled for treatment, with six healthy dogs as controls. Infected dogs differed from controls in white cell count, red cell indices, platelet count and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPC). The twelve were randomised to doxycycline, clindamycin and metronidazole alone (Group I) or with oral NAC at 70 mg/kg for five days (Group II) and reassessed on day 21. Both groups improved; Group II showed numerically better red cell, haemoglobin and platelet recovery and lower bilirubin and UPC, but only antioxidant capacity and MCHC differed significantly (p < 0.05, Mann-Whitney). The authors suggest NAC aids recovery and call for larger trials.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (infected vs control comparison) plus a small interventional pilot; mechanistic inference is limited
- **Authors' conclusion:** NAC supplementation is said to improve haematological recovery, lessen oxidative stress and support renal function in babesiosis dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The infected-versus-control comparison offers a small descriptive signal for altered erythrocyte indices, platelet count and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio in B. gibsoni infection; the abstract does not report an infected-versus-control oxidative stress difference as significant. The NAC arm is a treatment-efficacy question; with only two endpoints significant, it does not show that oxidative injury drives the anaemia or that NAC acts through that route. Eligible mainly for the oxidative and renal observations.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 12 treated dogs across two arms (per-arm split not stated in the abstract) and 6 controls; single breed; many comparisons without evident correction; most outcomes non-significant; no placebo or blinding described in abstract; concurrent antimicrobials confound; day-21 single time point; country and PCR method not reported in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
