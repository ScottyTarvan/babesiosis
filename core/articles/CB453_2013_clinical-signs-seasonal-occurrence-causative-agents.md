## Abstract-derived summary

This French survey followed canine babesiosis cases reported across several regions from October 2006 to December 2007. Veterinarians recorded 836 cases on a multiple-choice questionnaire, and blood from 70 dogs was tested by PCR followed by restriction fragment length polymorphism to identify the parasite. Lethargy and anorexia were each reported in 98% of cases, and fever of at least 39 °C in 80%. Pale mucous membranes (54%), altered urine appearance (45%) and splenomegaly (33%) were less common. Affected dogs were mostly young and lived in rural areas. Of the 70 samples, 25 (36%) contained Babesia/Theileria DNA, and every positive sample gave a pattern resembling Babesia canis. The authors offer the findings as a baseline for later molecular studies of species and vectors in France and the Mediterranean basin.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors say the results give a baseline for later molecular studies of parasite species and vectors in France and across the Mediterranean basin.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study describes the clinical picture reported for 836 cases recorded as canine babesiosis in France. The frequent pallor and altered urine appearance are in line with a haemolytic presentation, and the frequency of splenomegaly adds to the multisystem clinical phenotype. The clinical signs come from veterinarian questionnaires and are not linked to laboratory markers or outcome in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** questionnaire-based reporting by veterinarians; most cases not tested by PCR (only 70 of 836 tested); only 25 of 70 samples positive, so some reported cases may not have been babesiosis; species inferred from RFLP profile similarity (sequencing not described in abstract); no control group described in abstract; no haematology, biochemistry or outcome data in the abstract; full text not read.
