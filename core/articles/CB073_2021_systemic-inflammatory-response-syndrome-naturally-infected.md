## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared dogs with acute B. canis infection who met criteria for systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) against those who did not, looking at demographics, haematology, biochemistry, acute-phase proteins (serum amyloid A, ceruloplasmin, paraoxonase-1), serology and PCR-based parasite load, in 54 client-owned dogs. SIRS occurred in 59% of dogs. SIRS was more frequent in males and in seronegative dogs, but not linked to age or parasite load. SIRS dogs had lower leukocyte, neutrophil, lymphocyte and monocyte counts, and lower iron and bilirubin; acute-phase proteins did not differ. Seronegative dogs had higher parasite load and fewer lymphocytes and monocytes. Logistic regression retained leukopenia (<6 x 10^9/L) and monocytopenia (<0.2 x 10^9/L) as independent correlates of SIRS.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Leukopenia and monocytopenia are independent associates of SIRS in B. canis infection and could serve as routine markers of SIRS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Describes a SIRS phenotype in canine babesiosis characterised by leukopenia rather than leukocytosis, apparently independent of parasite burden and of the measured acute-phase proteins. The seronegative/higher-parasitaemia/lymphopenia-monocytopenia pattern hints at early-infection or immune-hyporesponsive states, but this is hypothesis-generating. Cross-sectional design cannot show temporality or causation; the markers are correlates of SIRS classification (which itself includes WBC criteria, so some circularity is possible), not mechanisms or outcome predictors.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time point; no uninfected controls; SIRS-free comparator group small (about 22 dogs, derived from the reported 0.59 frequency); SIRS criteria, PCR target, recruitment period and setting not available from the abstract; no outcome data; co-infections not addressed in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
