## Abstract-derived summary

A seven-year-old crossbred male dog presented with inappetence, lethargy, weakness and abrupt bleeding from the urogenital orifice. Examination found pallor, enlarged popliteal lymph nodes, anaemia and marked thrombocytopenia. Babesia gibsoni infection was established by blood smear, a commercial antibody kit and PCR with sequencing. A positive saline agglutination test, spherocytes and ghost cells were taken as evidence of concurrent immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA). The dog was stabilised with an intravenous plasma volume expander and given doxycycline, clindamycin and metronidazole for 28 days. Prednisolone plus romiplostim, a thrombopoietin-receptor agonist, was added to address the IMHA and immune thrombocytopenia (ITP). Recovery was described as uneventful. The authors frame the bleeding as a consequence of B. gibsoni-triggered ITP.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Urogenital haemorrhage in this dog arose secondary to B. gibsoni-induced ITP and responded to romiplostim plus prednisolone alongside antiprotozoal therapy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates the overlap of IMHA and thrombocytopenia with mucosal bleeding in B. gibsoni infection. It does not show that platelet loss was antibody-mediated: the abstract reports no platelet count, platelet antibody test or coagulation profile, and ITP was inferred. The clinical response cannot be assigned to romiplostim because antiprotozoal drugs and prednisolone were given concurrently. Case-level syndrome observation, not mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; no platelet count or coagulation data in abstract; co-infections and other causes of haemorrhage not stated as excluded; treatment confounding; country and cohort not stated in abstract.
