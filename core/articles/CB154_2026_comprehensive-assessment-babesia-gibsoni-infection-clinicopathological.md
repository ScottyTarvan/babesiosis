## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers characterised naturally acquired Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs from Mizoram, India, screening 138 samples by rapid antibody test, blood smear and PCR (43, 70 and 85 positive respectively). Infected dogs typically showed lethargy, anorexia and fever, with splenomegaly and splenic lesions on abdominal ultrasound. Laboratory changes included anaemia, neutrophilic leukocytosis with eosinophilia, thrombocytopenia, low protein and albumin, raised bilirubin, azotaemia, and elevated ALT, ALP and CRP. Lipid peroxide was higher, and total antioxidant capacity and reduced glutathione lower, in infected dogs (P<0.001). RBC count correlated inversely with lipid peroxide (r=-0.741) and positively with glutathione (r=0.714) and antioxidant capacity (r=0.828). The authors propose multi-organ involvement and a contributory role for oxidative stress in anaemia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile) with associational oxidative stress data
- **Authors' conclusion:** Findings indicate multiorgan involvement and suggest oxidative stress may contribute to the pathogenesis of B. gibsoni-induced anaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful descriptive profile of a naturally infected B. gibsoni population in Mizoram, India: anaemia and thrombocytopenia with an inflammatory signature (CRP, neutrophilia), hepatic enzyme rise, azotaemia and splenic change on imaging. The strong RBC-count correlations with LPO, GSH and T-AOC show oxidant-antioxidant imbalance tracks anaemia severity, but cannot separate cause from consequence (haemolysis itself, reduced RBC mass and inflammation can all shift these markers). Not mechanistic evidence for oxidative haemolysis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time point; control group composition and size not stated in abstract; unclear which dogs entered the biochemical/oxidative analyses (85 PCR-positive vs smaller subset); co-infections and prior treatment not described; azotaemia not staged or distinguished as pre-renal vs renal; correlation only.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
