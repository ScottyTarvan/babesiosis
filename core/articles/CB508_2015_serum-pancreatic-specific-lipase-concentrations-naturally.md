## Abstract-derived summary

Babesia rossi causes a severe, multisystem illness that is considered a dependable model for systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS). This observational study measured serum canine pancreatic-specific lipase (cPL) in 87 dogs with natural B. rossi infection, with daily sampling until discharge or death, and examined links with SIRS status and death. Median values were 124.0 µg/L at admission (n = 87) and 145.5 µg/L on hospital day two (n = 40). Twenty-four dogs (28%) exceeded the 400 µg/L cut-off used for pancreatitis on admission, and 13 dogs (32.5%) did so on day two. Dogs classified as having SIRS (n = 53) had a median of 158 µg/L, higher than the 75 µg/L in dogs without SIRS (n = 32; P = 0.018). The authors regarded the share of dogs in the pancreatitis range as unexpectedly large.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** An unexpectedly high number of dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis had serum cPL within the range diagnostic for acute pancreatitis, and cPL was significantly higher in dogs classified as having SIRS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Raised cPL was associated with SIRS status in B. rossi infection, which fits a possible pancreatic contribution to the systemic syndrome. The design cannot show whether the rise reflects pancreatic inflammation, leakage related to vasculitis or another cause, because pancreatitis was not confirmed by imaging or pathology (full text). The full text reports no association with death, but only six dogs died (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no uninfected or non-infectious SIRS comparison group (full text); pancreatitis not confirmed by ultrasound or histopathology (full text); few deaths, limiting the outcome analysis (full text); single hospital setting (full text); day-two cPL available for 40 dogs (abstract); no adjustment for multiple comparisons reported (full text).

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - 98 dogs with acute B. rossi infection were recruited; 11 were excluded (incomplete records, insufficient data, insufficient stored serum or serum spilled in transport), leaving 87 for analysis (full text)
  - Inclusion required morphological demonstration of Babesia on thin capillary blood films; B. rossi was confirmed by PCR reverse line blot; dogs with B. vogeli, Ehrlichia canis or Theileria infection, or other concurrent infection, were excluded (full text)
  - Of the 87 dogs, 54 were male and 33 female; median age was 14 months (range 2-156 months) and median body mass 16.4 kg (range 1.9-54.3 kg) (full text)
  - Samples came from a 2005 prospective study of biomarkers and outcome at the Onderstepoort Veterinary Academic Hospital; blood was taken before treatment and cPL was measured with the Spec cPL assay at Texas A&M University, with less than 200 µg/L as the reference interval and more than 400 µg/L as consistent with pancreatitis (full text)
  - SIRS was defined as two or more of four criteria (white cell count, rectal temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate), using canine criteria from Okano et al.; 53 of 87 dogs (61%) met them (full text)
  - Six dogs (6.9%) died or were euthanised for poor prognosis; serum cPL did not differ between non-survivors and survivors (P = 0.74), and three of the six had cPL above 400 µg/L (full text)
  - Twenty of 87 dogs (22.9%) had vomiting, diarrhoea or abdominal pain; five of them had cPL above 400 µg/L, and cPL did not differ between dogs with and without these signs (P = 0.51) (full text)
  - cPL correlated positively with immature neutrophil count, band cell percentage, amylase and lipase activity, and negatively with rectal temperature and free thyroxine; it did not correlate with creatinine or ALT (full text)
  - The authors list the lack of consistent abdominal ultrasound, the lack of pathology records for all non-survivors, and the possibility of leakage from babesiosis-related vasculitis as limitations (full text)
