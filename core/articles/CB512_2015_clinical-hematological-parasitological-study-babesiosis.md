## Abstract-derived summary

Between March 2014 and February 2015, 200 dogs were examined for babesiosis, and the authors recorded clinical signs, the tick species present, blood count changes and blood-film findings. Dogs with acute disease showed fever (39.3 to 41.5 °C), lethargy, jaundice, an enlarged spleen, vomiting, congested mucous membranes and blood in the urine. Chronic cases had intermittent fever, reduced appetite, weight loss and pale membranes, while subclinical dogs had no signs. Blood films showed Babesia canis trophozoites in two shapes, pear-shaped and amoeboid. Seventy-nine dogs (39.5%) carried hard ticks, identified as Rhipicephalus sanguineus. Infected dogs had significantly lower red cell, platelet and granulocyte counts, haematocrit and haemoglobin than healthy dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract gives no separate conclusion statement. It reports that infected dogs had significantly lower RBC, platelet, granulocyte, HCT and HGB values than healthy dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a descriptive clinicopathological profile in which blood-film-positive dogs show lower red cell and platelet indices than healthy dogs, a pattern compatible with anaemia and thrombocytopenia. The abstract does not give group sizes, values or test statistics, so the size and precision of the differences cannot be judged. The findings show associations with infection status only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; diagnosis by blood film without PCR, so species is unconfirmed; numbers of infected and healthy dogs not reported; haematological values and P values not given; definitions of acute, chronic and subclinical forms not described; control selection not described; cross-sectional design.
