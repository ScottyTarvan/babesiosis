## Abstract-derived summary

Lobetti measured serum urea, creatinine, the derived urea to creatinine (UC) ratio, haemoglobin and glomerular filtration rate (GFR) in dogs with haemolytic anaemia of different origins. The groups were 25 dogs with babesiosis, 13 control animals, six dogs with induced haemoglobinaemia plus anaemia, six with induced haemoglobinaemia alone, and 14 with immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA). Median urea was 11.18 mmol/L in the babesiosis group, against 4.3 mmol/L in controls and 8.5 mmol/L in IMHA. The median UC ratio was 41.35 in babesiosis and between 13.6 and 15.36 in the other four groups. Median creatinine ranged from 67 to 84 μmol/L across groups, and GFR was normal in every group. Urea and the UC ratio were significantly higher in babesiosis than in IMHA and in the experimental anaemia or haemoglobinaemia groups.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum urea concentration and the UC ratio were significantly greater in dogs with babesiosis than in dogs with IMHA or with experimentally induced anaemia and/or haemoglobinaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Urea and the UC ratio were higher in babesiosis while GFR was normal and creatinine medians were 67 to 84 μmol/L across groups. The raised values were not seen in the induced haemoglobinaemia groups or in IMHA to the same degree. The comparison describes a larger urea rise in babesiosis than in these groups, but the design does not test why, and the abstract does not show whether infection, haemolysis or haemoglobinaemia is responsible.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; Babesia species and identification method not stated; small group sizes; abstract gives medians without dispersion or test statistics; GFR method not described in the abstract; babesiosis severity and complications not described; the experimental groups are not infected dogs; comparison of babesiosis with the controls is not reported as a significance result in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
