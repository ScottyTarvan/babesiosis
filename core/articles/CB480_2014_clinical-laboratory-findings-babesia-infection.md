## Abstract-derived summary

The authors screened dogs presenting with fever, poor appetite and enlarged lymph nodes at T.V.C.C., C.V.Sc., Proddatur over one year, and found six with babesiosis on stained blood smears. All six had ticks on the body, congested conjunctival membranes, dullness, fever, a fast heart rate and fast breathing; some also showed jaundice, enlarged lymph nodes and haemoglobin in the urine. Blood tests showed lower haemoglobin concentration, red cell count and platelet count, together with lower total serum protein, albumin and glucose. Serum SGPT, creatinine and blood urea nitrogen were higher. The dogs received a single dose of diminazene aceturate at 5 mg/kg, with supportive and symptomatic therapy chosen case by case.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract states no explicit conclusion. It reports the clinical and laboratory findings of six smear-positive dogs and their treatment with diminazene aceturate.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a small descriptive case series. The reported pattern (lower red cell indices and platelets, lower albumin and glucose, higher SGPT, creatinine and BUN) fits red cell, haemostatic, hepatic and renal involvement in these dogs. With no control group reported, no values and no statistics in the abstract, the findings cannot be tested for association with severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** six dogs only; no control group reported in the abstract; diagnosis by blood smear only, with the species not identified; no numeric values, reference ranges or statistics in the abstract; co-infections not addressed in the abstract; outcome after treatment not reported.
