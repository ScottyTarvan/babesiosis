## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs living near Aboriginal communities in north-west New South Wales and the Northern Territory were screened to relate vector-borne pathogens to red cell and platelet counts. Ninety-six dogs underwent haematology and qPCR for Anaplasma platys, Babesia vogeli, two haemotropic mycoplasmas and Bartonella, plus serology for Coxiella burnetii and Bartonella (and Brucella suis in the New South Wales dogs). B. vogeli was detected in 7 dogs and was associated with anaemia (OR 11.8, 95% CI 2.3-61.6, P=0.003); A. platys (26 dogs) was associated with both anaemia and thrombocytopenia. Pathogen DNA load did not track positively with cytopenias, haemotropic mycoplasmas showed no haematological association, and Bartonella DNA was never detected. The authors attribute cytopenias mainly to A. platys and B. vogeli, with nutritional, parasitic, lactational and environmental contributions likely.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (screening-eligible as a descriptive clinicopathological association; borderline for pathophysiology because it is primarily a multi-pathogen prevalence survey)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia and thrombocytopenia were the main haematological abnormalities, likely attributable to A. platys and B. vogeli but probably also affected by nutritional, parasitic, lactational and environmental stressors.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Offers a weak, confounded B. vogeli-anaemia association in largely clinically healthy dogs under heavy ecological stress. The load-versus-cytopenia analysis is the only mechanism-adjacent element; with so few positive dogs it cannot distinguish parasite-driven haemolysis from co-pathogen or nutritional effects, and it does not demonstrate causality.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 7 B. vogeli-positive dogs, wide confidence interval; co-infection with A. platys common among them; no sequencing-based species confirmation described; cross-sectional single time point; undernutrition, ectoparasites and lactation as confounders; no outcome data; qPCR load is only a semi-quantitative proxy.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
