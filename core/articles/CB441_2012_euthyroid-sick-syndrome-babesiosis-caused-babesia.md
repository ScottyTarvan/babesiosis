## Abstract-derived summary

The authors measured total and free thyroxine (TT4 and FT4) by immunoassay in 23 dogs infected with Babesia canis. Their aims were to describe thyroid hormone status and to test whether it relates to azotaemia. Nine dogs were azotaemic and fourteen were not. A high proportion of the infected dogs had low TT4 and FT4 values, which the title frames as euthyroid sick syndrome. Hormone levels did not differ significantly between azotaemic and non-azotaemic groups on Mann-Whitney testing. However, both thyroxine measures showed statistically significant correlations with serum urea and creatinine. As a small association study of infected dogs, it says nothing direct about mechanism.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** A high proportion of B. canis-infected dogs had decreased TT4 and FT4. The two hormones did not differ between azotaemic and non-azotaemic dogs, but both correlated with serum urea and creatinine.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Low thyroxine is common in dogs with B. canis infection and fits the nonthyroidal illness pattern described for B. rossi in the introduction (full text). The negative correlations with urea and creatinine show an association with the degree of azotaemia, not its cause. The study has no healthy control group and does not test the authors' hypothesis of reduced renal blood flow.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample (23 dogs); no healthy or non-infected control group (full text); single clinic (full text); samples taken during 1-4 days of illness (full text); TT4 comparison of the azotaemic and non-azotaemic groups was borderline (P = 0.0508, full text); no outcome or severity data (full text); no triiodothyronine or TSH measurement (full text).
