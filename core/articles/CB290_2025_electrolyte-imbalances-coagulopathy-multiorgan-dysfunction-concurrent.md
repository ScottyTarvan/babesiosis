## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a two-year-old male Doberman with acute fever, lethargy, inappetence, progressive weakness and jaundice, in which co-infection with Babesia gibsoni and Leptospira spp. was identified. Examination found marked pallor and icterus, a fast heart rate and dehydration. Laboratory work-up showed anaemia, low platelet counts, azotaemia with raised creatinine, increased liver enzymes, hyperbilirubinaemia and notable electrolyte disturbance. A multi-antigen PCR assay performed on day 1 detected both pathogens. In spite of early diagnosis and treatment, the dog deteriorated quickly and died. The authors argue that the two infections acted synergistically to cause multi-organ involvement and irreversible organ damage, and that this uncommon co-infection is a diagnostic and therapeutic challenge.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that leptospiral and babesial co-infection acted synergistically to produce multisystem involvement and irreversible organ damage.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a fatal multi-organ dysfunction phenotype (progressive anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia, hepatobiliary enzyme rise, hyperkalaemia/hyponatraemia, prolonged PT/aPTT) in a dog PCR-positive for both agents. AKI, hepatopathy and coagulopathy occur in each infection alone, so injury cannot be apportioned between agents and "synergy" is a hypothesis, not a finding. Useful as a severity/complication syndrome observation and a reminder of co-infection confounding, not as mechanistic evidence.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no necropsy or histopathology; Leptospira not typed (no MAT reported); no parasitaemia or parasite load; no antiprotozoal drug listed in treatment; coagulation measured on day 0 only (no D-dimer, smear review or viscoelastic testing); bilirubin values in the text disagree with Table 1; peer-review process not verifiable (about 6 weeks from receipt to acceptance).
