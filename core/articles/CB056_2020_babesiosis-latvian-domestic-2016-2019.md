## Abstract-derived summary

Between 2016 and 2019, blood from tick-exposed dogs in Latvia was screened by nested PCR to establish how often Babesia causes disease and where outbreaks occur. Of 262 samples, 43 were positive for B. canis, and 98% of positives were submitted between April and June, with a single October case. Around 46.5% of cases came from Riga. Clinical presentation matched typical acute babesiosis; thrombocytopenia (89%) and normocytic normochromic anaemia (69%) were the commonest haematological changes, and smear microscopy detected 79% of cases. Two 18S rRNA genotypes differing at positions 610/611 (GA versus AG) were found, without any link to disease severity. The authors conclude that canine babesiosis is endemic in southern and western Latvia and caused solely by B. canis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with a null associational genotype-severity comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis is now endemic in parts of Latvia, caused only by the large species B. canis, and veterinary and owner awareness should increase.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly epidemiological, but the clinicopathological profile (thrombocytopenia and normocytic normochromic anaemia) adds a descriptive syndrome and the genotype comparison addresses parasite virulence. The absence of a genotype-severity association does not exclude one, given 43 cases split across two genotypes. It does not inform mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Route of sample submission and reason for testing not described in abstract (possible selection bias); severity definition not reported in abstract; co-infections not described; no controls; small genotype subgroups; haematology availability per dog not stated.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
