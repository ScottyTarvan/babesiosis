## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a five-year-old neutered male Scottish Terrier presented to the emergency department of a French veterinary school with worsening breathing difficulty and signs pointing to central nervous system disease. Chest radiographs showed a marked, widespread interstitial and alveolar pattern. Examination of a blood smear led to a diagnosis of babesiosis. The dog died from cardiopulmonary arrest six hours after presentation. Necropsy confirmed both cerebral babesiosis and acute respiratory distress syndrome, with clumps of red cells seen in lung, liver and brain tissue. The authors describe the presentation as unusual for Babesia canis canis, the usual European species, and state that this is, as far as they know, the first histopathology-confirmed Babesia-associated ARDS in a dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The case shows an unusual presentation of B. canis canis infection, with cerebral involvement and ARDS confirmed at necropsy. The authors consider it the first histopathologically confirmed Babesia-associated ARDS in a dog.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The necropsy findings of red cell aggregation in lung, liver and brain are consistent with microvascular involvement in both the cerebral and pulmonary syndromes, as the keywords also suggest. A single fatal case can show that the combination occurs but cannot show how often it occurs or by what mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; species based on blood smear evaluation as stated in the abstract; no controls; no quantitative laboratory data in the abstract; abstract-level information only.
