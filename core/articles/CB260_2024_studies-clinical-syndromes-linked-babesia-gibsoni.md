## Abstract-derived summary

This short communication asks which organ-level syndromes accompany Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs presented to an Indian teaching hospital. Twenty-four dogs with PCR-confirmed infection had serum ALT, ALP, BUN, creatinine, amylase and lipase measured, and syndromes were assigned using published biochemical cut-offs. Nine dogs (37.5%) met at least one definition: hepatopathy in four, acute kidney failure in two, pancreatitis in two and acute respiratory distress syndrome in one. Cohort means sat near normal limits (ALT 73.6 U/L, BUN 25.0 mg/dL, creatinine 0.90 mg/dL, amylase 639 U/L, lipase 380 U/L). German Shepherds were the commonest breed among the nine syndromic dogs. The authors conclude that these four syndromes are linked with B. gibsoni infection, hepatopathy being the most frequent.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hepatopathy, acute renal failure, pancreatitis and ARDS are clinical syndromes linked with B. gibsoni infection in dogs, hepatopathy being most prevalent.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports organ involvement (liver, kidney, pancreas, lung) as a recognised phenotype of B. gibsoni infection, but is a syndrome catalogue rather than a mechanistic or prognostic study. Enzyme thresholds identify biochemical injury, not its cause (haemolysis, hypoxia, inflammation and co-infection are not distinguished).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no healthy or non-syndromic comparison; no haematology, outcome or treatment data; co-infections not reported; the abstract's "2 cases" without syndromes does not reconcile with 15 of 24, and syndrome counts overlap across dogs (the full text describes two dogs with acute kidney failure also meeting hepatopathy or pancreatitis criteria), so unique dog counts are unclear; the breed-predisposition inference lacks breed denominators; peer-review policy not verified (journal not MEDLINE-indexed, about 5 weeks from submission to acceptance).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
