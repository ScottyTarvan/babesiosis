## Abstract-derived summary

Acute kidney injury is the commonest complication of canine babesiosis, so the authors profiled urine proteins in naturally infected dogs to look for early, pre-azotaemic markers. Urine from 10 B. canis-infected dogs and 10 healthy dogs was pooled by group, resolved by two-dimensional electrophoresis and identified by mass spectrometry. 403 proteins were identified in the infected pool versus 176 in controls, with only 30 shared. Bioinformatic analysis highlighted eight proteins linked to ten pathways of immune and inflammatory response. The authors proposed that epithelial-mesenchymal transition may play a role in renal pathology, and suggested interleukin-13, BMP7, collagen alpha-2(I) and tyrosine kinase Fer as candidate early-injury biomarkers.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (exploratory omics, hypothesis-generating)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Epithelial-mesenchymal transition may underlie renal tissue changes in babesiosis, and four proteins are potential early markers of kidney injury.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive of urinary protein composition in early (non-azotaemic) babesial kidney injury; the EMT hypothesis is inferred from pathway enrichment, not demonstrated in tissue. Presence or absence of a protein in a pooled gel is qualitative and cannot establish cause or biomarker performance.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single pooled sample per group without replicates (authors acknowledge this), so no inter-dog variance or statistics on protein abundance; n=10 per group; no validation cohort or orthogonal assay; haemoglobinuria and proteinuria confound proteome content; controls not matched for urine concentration; no histology.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
