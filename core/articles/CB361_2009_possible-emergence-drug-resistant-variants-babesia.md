## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective case study followed eight dogs with naturally acquired Babesia gibsoni infection and acute clinical signs, treated at Kagoshima University Veterinary Teaching Hospital in 2007-2008. All received atovaquone combined with azithromycin as first-line therapy, and the authors reviewed clinical courses and clinicopathological parameters. Alterations in the parasite cytochrome b gene were examined by PCR and DNA sequencing. Every dog showed fast improvement in clinical condition and haematological values, yet five of the eight relapsed after treatment. The cytochrome b analysis strongly suggested that atovaquone-resistant parasite variants emerged after therapy. The authors conclude that the drug combination is usable as first-line treatment, although relapse occurs and in vivo selection of resistant variants deserves attention.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (retrospective case series)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Atovaquone with azithromycin can serve as first-line treatment for canine babesiosis, but relapses occur and in vivo selection of drug-resistant variants needs attention.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The host-side content is limited to the clinical course and haematological recovery of eight dogs, followed by relapse in five; the main focus is treatment response and parasite resistance. The work offers little on mechanism. The cytochrome b analysis is described as strongly suggesting resistant variants after treatment, but the abstract does not show that these variants caused the five relapses, and the title frames the finding as possible. It is included as a clinicopathological series of infected dogs under the review criteria.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small sample; retrospective design; no control group; treatment and parasite-genetics focus with few host pathophysiological data in the abstract; single teaching hospital; haematological values not available at abstract level.
