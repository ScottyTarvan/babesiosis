## Abstract-derived summary

This case-control study examined the heart in dogs with babesiosis (n=50) versus healthy controls (n=20), using blood counts, electrocardiography, echocardiography, and serum troponin I and CK-MB. Every infected dog was thrombocytopenic; 52% had reduced haematocrit and 46% were anaemic. The commonest rhythm and structural findings were T-wave morphology or amplitude changes (34%), increased fractional shortening (24%), faster sinus rhythm (14%) and axis deviation (10%). Troponin I was raised in 19 of 50 dogs and was markedly high in the two fatalities, which also showed sinus tachycardia and a ventricular premature beat. CK-MB was elevated in all infected dogs (23.17-369.62 U/L), peaking at 367.33 and 369.62 U/L in the dogs that died. The authors judge cardiac changes frequent but mostly nonspecific and of limited clinical importance, proposing troponin I and CK-MB as possible prognostic markers.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cardiac changes are common in canine babesiosis but mostly nonspecific; high troponin I and CK-MB may indicate poor prognosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests myocardial injury (troponin I) in a subset, possibly secondary to anaemia, hypoxia, tachycardia or systemic inflammation, but the design cannot separate direct parasite effects from these. CK-MB is not cardiac-specific in dogs (skeletal muscle contributes), so universal elevation is hard to interpret. The prognostic claim rests on two deaths and is hypothesis-generating only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 2 non-survivors; no multivariable or survival analysis in the abstract; diagnostic confirmation and species identification not stated; reference intervals, treatment timing and renal function (a troponin confounder) not reported in the abstract; single time point; prior cardiac disease not described.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
