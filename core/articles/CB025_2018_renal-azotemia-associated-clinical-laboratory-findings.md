## Abstract-derived summary

The study tested whether renal azotemia is under-recognised in B. rossi babesiosis, using urine osmolality rather than urine specific gravity (USG), which is unreliable when haemoglobinuria is present. Records from three earlier prospective collections were reviewed for client-owned dogs with a complete blood count, biochemistry and urinalysis at admission. Of 152 dogs, 26 (17%) were azotemic and 21 (14% of all dogs) had renal azotemia, so 81% of azotemic dogs were renal in origin. A USG cut-off below 1.030 would have classed only 6 of 26 as renal. Renal azotemia was associated with older age, collapse, hypoglycaemia, hyperphosphataemia, cerebral babesiosis and acute respiratory distress syndrome, and with lower survival to discharge. The authors advise measuring osmolality where possible.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (with a prognostic element)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Renal azotemia is more common in B. rossi infection than previously reported, and USG is not a dependable indicator of concentrating ability in azotemic dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Positions renal injury as a frequent component of severe B. rossi disease that clusters with other complications (hypoglycaemia, cerebral, ARDS), consistent with a multi-organ syndrome. The associations do not establish causal direction or mechanism, and renal azotemia classification depends on osmolality thresholds applied to admission samples.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Retrospective pooling of three data collections; single admission time point; treatment and timing confounding; multiple univariable comparisons; species confirmation method and setting not stated in abstract; no histopathology.
