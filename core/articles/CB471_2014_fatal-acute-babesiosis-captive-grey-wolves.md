## Abstract-derived summary

Two adult male Eurasian grey wolves from a group of 12 kept in a large open-air enclosure near Veresegyháza in Hungary were found dead in September 2002. Two further wolves died in the same period but could not be examined. At necropsy the two wolves were in good body condition, yet the mucous membranes, eyes and subcutaneous tissues were markedly yellow. Liver, gall bladder and spleen were all swollen, the kidneys looked paler than expected, and small pinpoint bleeds lay beneath the fascia. Spleen impression smears contained abundant small round piroplasm-like organisms about 1.5 to 2 micrometres across. PCR and sequencing identified Babesia canis. The authors note that babesiosis is rarely reported in grey wolves and suggest the parasite may matter for wolf conservation, in captivity and in the wild.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single outbreak case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis is seldom documented in grey wolves, and the authors see B. canis as a possible threat to weigh in future wolf conservation work, both in captivity and in the wild.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The necropsy picture (jaundice, enlarged liver, gall bladder and spleen, pale kidneys, petechiae) in wolves with a heavy splenic parasite burden is compatible with a severe acute multisystem illness, possibly with haemolytic features. The findings are associated with B. canis infection but no clinical pathology, histology or outcome comparison is reported in the abstract, so mechanisms cannot be inferred. Canis lupus counts as canine evidence under the review criteria.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only two animals examined; two further deaths not examined in the laboratory; no haematology, biochemistry or histopathology reported in the abstract; no control animals; no ante-mortem clinical data; abstract only, so co-infections and other causes of death cannot be judged.
