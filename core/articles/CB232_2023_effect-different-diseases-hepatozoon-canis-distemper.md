## Abstract-derived summary

Serum haptoglobin, ceruloplasmin and albumin were measured in 60 dogs from a Turkish animal hospital. Fifteen each had Babesia canis canis or Hepatozoon canis (both confirmed by PCR) or canine distemper (rapid diagnosis kit), and 15 were healthy controls. Ceruloplasmin was higher in the diseased groups than in healthy dogs (P<0.05); in babesiosis the mean was 7.85 versus 2.97 mg/dL. Haptoglobin (about 1.87 mg/mL in every group) and albumin (babesiosis 3.25 versus 2.96 g/dL in healthy dogs) showed no significant differences. Each disease group was compared with the healthy controls. The authors conclude that ceruloplasmin rose in all three infections while the other two analytes did not change.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Ceruloplasmin is raised in H. canis, distemper and B. c. canis infection, whereas haptoglobin and albumin are unchanged.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests a modest positive acute-phase response in natural B. c. canis infection, but ceruloplasmin is non-specific (also raised in the other two diseases) and is not linked to anaemia, severity or outcome. The absent haptoglobin change is unexpected in a haemolytic disease and may reflect assay or sampling issues.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=15 per group; no signalment, parasitaemia, haematology or severity data; co-infection exclusion not described; single time point; wide ceruloplasmin variance (maximum 25.43 mg/dL listed for both H. canis and B. c. canis, possible transcription error; full text); haptoglobin kit validation for dogs not described; pairwise P values not reported. Journal is Zenodo-hosted; peer-review policy not verified (about 3 months from receipt to acceptance; ethics approval stated).
