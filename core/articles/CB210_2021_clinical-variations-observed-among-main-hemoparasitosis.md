## Abstract-derived summary

This narrative review addresses three tick-borne haemoparasitic diseases of dogs that are commonly encountered in Brazilian small-animal practice and are associated with the brown dog tick, Rhipicephalus sanguineus: ehrlichiosis (Ehrlichia canis), babesiosis (Babesia canis and B. gibsoni) and anaplasmosis (Anaplasma platys). The authors compare how these infections differ in clinical signs and laboratory abnormalities, with particular attention to the types of anaemia that underlie the clinical picture in each. They also observe that diagnostic approaches overlap and that tetracyclines, most often doxycycline, form the treatment basis for all three. The authors close by calling for further research on this topic. The abstract reports no quantitative results.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** the three infections differ in clinical and haematological presentation but share diagnostic and treatment approaches, and more studies are needed.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Offers a comparative, differential-diagnosis view of anaemia types across co-endemic infections rather than new pathophysiological data. Babesia-specific mechanisms (haemolysis, immune-mediated destruction) are not detailed in the accessible record. Reviewer comment (outside the abstract): the species named (B. canis, B. gibsoni) are not the ones classically linked to R. sanguineus (B. vogeli is), so the tick-vector framing is imprecise.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no review methodology or search strategy stated; only 22 references; secondary literature; Babesia content depth unknown (full text not read).
