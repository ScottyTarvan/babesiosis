## Abstract-derived summary

This post-mortem study compared five dogs dying of babesiosis with five dying of leptospirosis. Both diseases can progress to septic shock and are hard to tell apart clinically or pathologically. Hepatomegaly with pulmonary oedema was the commonest gross finding in both groups. In babesiosis dogs, non-congestive splenic enlargement was invariably present, anaemia and mild icterus were more marked, and oedema with transudate in body cavities dominated the gross findings. Leptospirosis dogs had renal enlargement from acute cortical necrosis, stronger icterus without anaemia, and a greater tendency to coagulation disorders with haemorrhage. Under the microscope, babesiosis was marked by B. canis merozoites in red cells of untreated dogs, intravascular monocytic-histiocytic leukostasis and more extravascular haemolysis. Leptospirosis showed a lymphocytic-histiocytic infiltrate at the corticomedullary junction. The remaining organ lesions were similar and were attributed to blood redistribution in septic shock, hypoxic necrosis, disseminated intravascular coagulopathy and bleeding.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Apart from merozoites in red cells, leukostasis and extravascular haemolysis in babesiosis, and the renal lesions of leptospirosis, the organ lesions of the two diseases are very similar. They reflect septic shock with hypoxic necrosis and disseminated intravascular coagulopathy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives necropsy and histology of fatal canine babesiosis next to a leptospirosis comparison group. The findings describe lesion patterns in dogs that died and do not show mechanism or causation. The link to septic shock, hypoxia and DIC is the authors' interpretation of the pattern.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** five dogs per group; fatal cases only, so survivors and milder disease are not represented; species identified only as B. canis, with no molecular confirmation stated in the abstract; no controls without disease; treatment history unclear; publication type and journal not identified; full text not available.
