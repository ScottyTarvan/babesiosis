## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether the strong acute phase response of acute Babesia canis infection is accompanied by dyslipidaemia. Twenty-nine client-owned dogs with acute infection were compared with 10 healthy controls in a cross-sectional design. Serum amyloid A (SAA), cholesterol, phospholipids and triglycerides were measured, lipoprotein classes were separated electrophoretically, HDL particle diameter was estimated by gradient gel electrophoresis, and apolipoprotein A-1 (ApoA-1) was quantified by radioimmunoassay. Infected dogs had much higher median SAA (168.3 vs 3.2 μg/mL, P < .001), lower cholesterol (4.79 vs 6.15 mmol/L), phospholipids and alpha-lipoprotein fraction, but higher ApoA-1. SAA correlated with HDL diameter (rho 0.43) and ApoA-1 (rho 0.63). The authors infer altered HDL composition and reverse cholesterol transport, and call the parallel ApoA-1 and SAA rise unexplained.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis-associated acute phase response is accompanied by altered HDL concentration, composition and size, suggesting disturbed reverse cholesterol transport.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A host-response (acute phase/lipid metabolism) study that characterises a syndrome-level feature of acute babesiosis; it does not show that lipoprotein change contributes to tissue injury, severity or outcome. Correlations between SAA and HDL variables are associational and do not establish causality. Relevance to mechanism is hypothesis-generating (HDL remodelling during inflammation).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; single time point with variable time since infection; no severity or survival analysis (all dogs survived); controls were presumably recruited from a different source (staff-owned dogs awaiting neutering); co-infections not covered by the SNAP panel and comorbidities such as chronic kidney disease not excluded.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
