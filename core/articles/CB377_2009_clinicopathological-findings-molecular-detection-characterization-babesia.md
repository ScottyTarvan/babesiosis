## Abstract-derived summary

An intact 4-year-old female American Pit Bull Terrier from Italy, descended from an American-born dam, was presented with anorexia, lethargy, weakness and intermittent vomiting. Examination found dehydration, pallor, a hunched stance and abdominal pain, and a moderate anaemia was noted. Ultrasound showed an enlarged spleen with hyperechoic areas suspected to be infarcts, and splenectomy was done for suspected torsion. The dog worsened afterwards. Repeat blood work showed severe macrocytic normochromic anaemia with marked regeneration, neutrophilia with a left shift, monocytosis and marked thrombocytopenia. Smears showed small intra-erythrocytic piroplasms, 1-3 micrometres across, compatible with a small Babesia or Theileria. Sequencing of a partial 18S rRNA gene product gave a sequence identical to B. gibsoni in GenBank. The authors regard this as a first: molecular detection and characterisation of B. gibsoni in a sick dog in Italy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** This is the first molecular detection and characterisation of B. gibsoni infection in a sick dog from Italy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In this one dog, B. gibsoni infection was found together with regenerative macrocytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly and ultrasound changes suspected to be splenic infarcts. The clinical state worsened after splenectomy, which had been done for suspected torsion. The report describes these findings alongside one another; it cannot show whether the infection was responsible for the splenic changes or for the deterioration.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case, so no comparison group and no generalisability; abstract-level information only (full text not retrieved); outcome after deterioration not stated in the abstract; testing for co-infections not described in the abstract; route of infection not established; no numerical haematology values in the abstract.
