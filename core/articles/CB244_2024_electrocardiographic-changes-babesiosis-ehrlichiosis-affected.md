## Abstract-derived summary

The stated aim was to examine haematobiochemical changes in dogs with babesiosis and ehrlichiosis, but the reported results concern electrocardiography only. Fifteen dogs attending a small animal outpatient clinic and PCR-positive for either infection, or both, underwent ECG. The commonest abnormalities were reduced QRS amplitude and tall T waves (6/15 each, 40%), widened T waves (5/15), ST coving (4/15), and absent P waves and Q dips (3/15 each). Less frequent were widened P waves, tall QRS complexes and absent T waves (2/15 each). No inferential statistics, controls or pathogen-specific breakdown are given in the abstract. Results are given as counts and percentages, and the abstract does not describe the ECG method.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** not stated beyond listing the frequencies of ECG abnormalities.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that ECG changes occur in PCR-confirmed tick-borne infection, in line with myocardial involvement described in severe canine babesiosis, but the mixed cohort prevents attributing any finding to Babesia. Findings such as low QRS voltage, ST coving and T-wave changes are non-specific and may reflect anaemia, hypoxia, electrolyte disturbance or pericardial effusion; none was assessed. It is a syndrome-level observation, not a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 15; Babesia, Ehrlichia and co-infected dogs pooled; no healthy controls; no cardiac biomarkers, echocardiography, electrolytes or severity data; ECG method and interpretation criteria not described; aim does not match reported results; journal peer-review policy not verified (Crossref lists AkiNik Publications; record has zero references deposited).
