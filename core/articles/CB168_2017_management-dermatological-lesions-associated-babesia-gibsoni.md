## Abstract-derived summary

The report describes skin disease in dogs infected with Babesia gibsoni and its response to treatment. Three dogs presented with recurrent skin problems together with fever, digestive upset, lethargy and progressive weight loss. Skin changes included dry scaling, hair loss, haemorrhagic spots on the abdomen and groin, ulcers between the toes, thickened digital pads, brittle claws and pododermatitis. Stained blood smears showed B. gibsoni inside erythrocytes. Laboratory findings were anaemia, leucopaenia, thrombocytopenia, raised globulin and low albumin. Two intramuscular doses of diminazene aceturate (7 mg/kg) plus oral clindamycin (25 mg/kg twice daily) led to improvement within three days and full resolution of the skin signs after two months.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract reports uneventful recovery and complete dermatological cure after combined diminazene and clindamycin therapy; it states no explicit mechanistic conclusion.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a cutaneous and digital phenotype in B. gibsoni infection alongside cytopenias and a hyperglobulinaemia/hypoalbuminaemia pattern. Haemorrhagic spots are compatible with the reported thrombocytopenia; the pathogenesis of ulceration, hyperkeratosis and pododermatitis (e.g. immune-complex or vascular injury) is not addressed and not established. Syndrome description only, not a mechanistic or severity-biomarker study.
