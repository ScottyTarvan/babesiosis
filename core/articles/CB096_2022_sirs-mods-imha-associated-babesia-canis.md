## Abstract-derived summary

_The packet holds no abstract (the Crossref record is empty). The figures below come from the publisher PDF full text and could not be checked against the packet record._

A two-month-old German Shepherd pup was seen at a referral polyclinic in northern India after ten days of fever, inappetence, lethargy, nasal discharge and abdominal fluid. Examination showed marked pallor, a temperature of 103 F, enlarged popliteal nodes and lung crackles. Admission bloods showed haemoglobin 5.2 g/dl, platelets 0.54 lakh/mm3 as tabulated, 86% neutrophils, 12% lymphocytes, albumin 1.8 g/dl, urea nitrogen 47 mg/dl, creatinine 4.9 mg/dl and ALT 178 IU/l. Smears contained large piroplasms, spherocytes and agglutinated cells, and the direct Coombs test was positive. Ultrasound revealed an enlarged liver, ascites and mild splenomegaly. Diminazine, diuretics and multimodal supportive care were followed by recovery: no parasites microscopically by day 14, haemoglobin 9 g/dl, creatinine 1.8 mg/dl.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia canis infection in this pup produced SIRS and multi-organ dysfunction, and early diagnosis and treatment are important to prevent progression.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates a juvenile case combining regenerative-type haemolytic anaemia with Coombs-positive immune-mediated features and azotaemia/hepatic enzyme elevation. It is a syndrome description, not mechanistic evidence. Causality of the organ changes is not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species by morphology only; co-infections (Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon, viral or helminth causes of hypoproteinaemia and ascites) not tested; SIRS criteria (heart/respiratory rate) not reported; azotaemia not distinguished from prerenal causes; reference-range column in the table is unreliable; steroids and other drugs confound recovery attribution.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
