## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective case-control study used records from the Onderstepoort Veterinary Academic Hospital in South Africa. Dogs with a white cell count of 35 x 10^9/L or higher (182 dogs) were compared with age-matched dogs that had counts of 30 x 10^9/L or lower and few band neutrophils (179 dogs). Infections, babesiosis, immune-mediated blood disease and tissue necrosis were more common in the leukocytosis group. These dogs also stayed longer in hospital, had higher neutrophil, lymphocyte and monocyte counts, and had lower haematocrit, eosinophil and platelet counts and lower serum albumin. Glucocorticoid treatment was not linked to differences in white cell counts. Survival did not differ between the two groups, but the total neutrophil count, mature plus band, was related to survival. The authors link marked leukocytosis with complicated babesiosis, among other conditions.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with leukocytosis of 35 x 10^9/L or more were more likely to have bacterial and fungal infections, complicated babesiosis, immune-mediated haematologic disease and necrosis. The authors state that total neutrophil count has a significant impact on outcome; the abstract reports only a significant relationship with survival (P=.01).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Babesiosis appears here as one of several diagnoses that were over-represented among dogs with marked leukocytosis, and the conclusion describes it as complicated babesiosis. The abstract does not give the number of babesiosis dogs or their separate haematology and outcome, so the Babesia-specific contribution cannot be separated from the pooled comparison. The finding is an association between leukocytosis and disease severity in a hospital population; it does not show a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective record review; single hospital (referral status not stated in the abstract); Babesia species and diagnostic method not stated in the abstract; number of babesiosis dogs not given; leukocytosis and control groups defined by white cell thresholds, so diagnoses are not the grouping variable; mixed diagnoses not analysed separately in the abstract

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
