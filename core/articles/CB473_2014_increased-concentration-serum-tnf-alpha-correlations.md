## Abstract-derived summary

This study measured serum tumour necrosis factor alpha in 11 dogs infected with Babesia canis and examined how it related to arterial blood pressure and to markers of kidney damage. The authors describe the cytokine as raised during the disease. Rank correlations were negative between the cytokine and systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure, with coefficients of -0.7246, -0.6642 and -0.7151 respectively. The cytokine was also positively correlated with fractional sodium excretion and with disease duration, and negatively correlated with the urinary-to-serum creatinine ratio and urine specific gravity. The authors propose that the cytokine contributes to low blood pressure and kidney failure, possibly through nitric oxide-driven vasodilation that leads to renal ischaemia, hypoxia and tubular necrosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum TNF-alpha is increased in canine babesiosis, and the authors state that it influences the development of hypotension and renal failure, probably through nitric oxide production and vasodilation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The data show that higher TNF-alpha values went together with lower blood pressure and with changes in several renal indices in a small group of infected dogs. Correlations from a single time point cannot show that the cytokine produced the hypotension or the renal injury; the mechanism offered is a hypothesis. The study is useful as a link between inflammation, haemodynamics and renal function in B. canis infection.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 11 infected dogs; correlations only, at one time point; no adjustment for confounders; no causal test; five of the 11 dogs had undetectable TNF-alpha (full text); pulse pressure, renal failure index and urinary protein to creatinine ratio were not significantly correlated (full text).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Samples (blood, serum, urine) and clinical examination, including blood pressure, were taken at the first clinic visit, before treatment (full text)
  - Infection was diagnosed by blood smear and confirmed by PCR, using a method described in the authors' earlier work; 8 clinically healthy dogs served as controls (full text)
  - Blood pressure was measured by the non-invasive oscillometric technique, and TNF-alpha by a canine ELISA kit (full text)
  - Detectable TNF-alpha was present in 6 of the 11 infected dogs, with a median of 14.7 pg/mL in those dogs; the median was significantly higher in infected dogs than in the 8 healthy dogs (Mann-Whitney U test) (full text)
  - The 14.7 pg/mL median given for the six detectable dogs matches the median of all 11 dogs in Table 1 (zeros included), not of the six detectable values alone (full text)
  - TNF-alpha was also positively correlated with serum urea (r = 0.7232) and serum creatinine (r = 0.7075); there were no significant correlations with pulse pressure, renal failure index or urinary protein to creatinine ratio (full text)
  - Urinary protein to creatinine ratio was raised in 7 of 11 dogs, and haemoglobinuria or bacteriuria in these dogs may have affected urinary protein (full text)
  - The dogs were of various breeds; apathy, anorexia and fever were the most common clinical signs; disease duration before admission ranged from 1 to 4 days (full text)
  - Individual-dog values for TNF-alpha, blood pressure and renal indices are tabulated in Table 1 (full text)
