## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe what they present as the first report of Babesia merozoites in peritoneal fluid. A seven-month-old crossbred male dog at a Brazilian university hospital presented with emaciation, apathy and abdominal distension. Examination found pale mucous membranes, ascites, a grade IV/V systolic murmur and tachypnoea. Bloodwork showed normocytic normochromic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and hypoproteinaemia, and a blood smear contained intra-erythrocytic forms compatible with Babesia spp. The ascitic fluid was a modified transudate containing numerous intracellular and extracellular merozoite-like structures. The authors suggest that organisms outside cells may reflect splenic injury with leakage of contents into the peritoneal cavity, and propose that sampling peritoneal fluid could aid diagnosis when a suspected case has ascites.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Merozoite-like forms in ascitic fluid may arise from splenic lesion and leakage, so peritoneal fluid can be an alternative diagnostic sample in babesiosis with ascites.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single unusual cytological observation; it is a syndrome-level curiosity, not mechanistic evidence. Splenic leakage is hypothesised but no splenic imaging or necropsy finding is reported. Ascites has plausible competing explanations (hypoalbuminaemia, increased vascular permeability, possible right-sided heart disease given the murmur), none of which the case separates. Free organisms in effusion could also reflect haemorrhagic fluid contamination by parasitised blood (reviewer comment, not made in the abstract).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; species not molecularly confirmed; co-infections (e.g., Ehrlichia, Anaplasma) not excluded; suspected cardiac disease not characterised; no necropsy; early death limits outcome interpretation; juvenile dog with possible congenital disease confounds the picture.
