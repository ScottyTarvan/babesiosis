## Abstract-derived summary

The study characterised vascular-platelet haemostasis and circulating volume in dogs with acute spontaneous babesiosis, then compared infusion therapies. Affected dogs had a marked circulating blood volume deficit (lower haematocrit, plasma volume and red cell volume), thrombocytopenia with increased spontaneous aggregation of platelets and erythrocytes, and a higher vascular wall wetting index. The authors interpret this as subcompensated shock with the thrombogenic phase of DIC and platelet consumption in clots. Two regimens were compared: Rheosorbylact alone, and Rheosorbylact (100 ml) mixed with 4 ml of 0.5% dipyridamole, the mixture given at 5 ml/kg daily for three days; the dose for Rheosorbylact alone is not stated. The mixture reportedly returned haemodynamic and platelet-vascular indices to physiological values within 48 hours, outperforming the crystalloid alone. Group sizes and statistics are not given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with a treatment comparison of unstated design)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Rheosorbylact plus dipyridamole corrects shock and thrombogenic haemostatic changes faster than Rheosorbylact alone.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant to the COA and END axes: it describes hypovolaemia, platelet consumption and enhanced aggregation as a thrombogenic, shock-associated state. The treatment arm is of limited pathophysiological value and its response is not proof that platelet aggregation drives the disease. Whether the "wetting index" and aggregation assays are validated is not established from the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n, randomisation, blinding, controls and statistics not reported in abstract; species and diagnostic method not stated; co-treatments (antiparasitics) not described; possible overlap with the same group's cohort in CB212; regional journal, peer-review practice not verified.
