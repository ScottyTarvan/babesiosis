## Abstract-derived summary

An elderly (12-year-old) intact female Satsuma dog, locally bred, was presented with generalised alopecia, with erythema, crusts and scaling mainly over the trunk, papules and erosions on the ears, and several areas of skin necrosis on the right forelimb. Earlier treatment with systemic antibiotics and prednisolone had not helped. The dog was also progressively anaemic. Babesia gibsoni was identified in blood, and antiprotozoal drugs were given. Both the skin disease and the anaemia got better, then returned once therapy was stopped. Skin biopsies were examined microscopically and showed changes that suggest either early leukocytoclastic vasculitis or ischaemic vasculopathy. The report describes the cutaneous presentation and histology of a dog with B. gibsoni infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The skin lesions were considered cutaneous vasculitis or vasculopathy associated with babesiosis, and chronic babesiosis should be a differential diagnosis for suspected cutaneous vasculitis in endemic areas (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case adds a histological description of skin involvement in B. gibsoni infection, with perivascular neutrophilic infiltration and fibrin deposition (full text), consistent with a microvascular lesion. The link between infection and skin disease rests on the timing of improvement, relapse and renewed improvement in relation to antiprotozoal treatment. This pattern is associational and does not show mechanism in a single animal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case with no control; skin biopsy taken only at relapse, not at first presentation (full text); prior and concurrent prednisolone exposure may have altered clinical and histological findings (full text); Coombs' test not done (full text); no demonstration of parasite, immune complexes or vessel-wall injury in the skin; mechanism is proposed from the literature, not tested.
