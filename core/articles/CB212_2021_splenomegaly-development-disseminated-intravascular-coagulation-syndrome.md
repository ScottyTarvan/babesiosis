## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined whether disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) drives splenic damage in dogs after acute spontaneous babesiosis. Dogs had clinical, haematological, biochemical and haemodynamic work-up, and removed spleens were examined histologically. Affected dogs showed cytopenias, normochromic anaemia, a raised erythrocyte sedimentation rate, conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, raised transaminases and low albumin, read as hepatitis with hepatic failure. Low fibrinogen, raised degradation products (including D-dimer), soluble fibrin monomer complexes and discordant APTT/prothrombin time were classed as the consumption stage of DIC, alongside a marked circulating-volume deficit consistent with stage II shock. Spleens showed stromal proliferation, vessel-sinus complexes plugged with clots, parenchymal dystrophy, older fibrin deposits and vascular hyalinosis. The authors conclude DIC underlies post-babesiosis splenomegaly. No numerical results are given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** DIC is the basis of splenomegaly in dogs after acute spontaneous babesiosis, supported by laboratory markers and fibrin thrombi in splenic tissue.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Splenic fibrin deposition and vascular hyalinosis support microthrombosis/organ-level coagulopathy as a feature of severe canine babesiosis, linking the COA, END and haemodynamic axes. The data show co-occurrence of consumption coagulopathy and splenic change; they do not isolate DIC as the cause of splenomegaly, because congestion, immune-mediated hyperplasia and extramedullary haematopoiesis are not addressed in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n and controls not reported in abstract; species not identified; timing relative to infection unclear; dogs with spleens removed are probably a severe, selected subset; shock staging and "hypersplenism" use non-standard criteria; co-infections not mentioned; regional journal, peer-review practice not verified.
