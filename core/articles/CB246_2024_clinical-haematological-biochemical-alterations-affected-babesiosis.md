## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs presenting with fever, poor appetite, enlarged lymph nodes, ticks and pale membranes at a veterinary college in Proddatur were screened for blood parasites. Twenty-seven dogs diagnosed with Babesia infection between October 2023 and September 2024 are described. Frequent findings included inappetence, fever, lymphadenopathy, lethargy, pallor, altered urine colour, mucosal congestion, dependent oedema and icterus. Relative to apparently healthy dogs, infected animals had lower haemoglobin, packed cell volume, erythrocyte and platelet counts. ALT, ALP, urea nitrogen and creatinine were significantly higher and albumin significantly lower. Urine ranged from red-brown to yellow, with raised pH and specific gravity, proteinuria and bilirubinuria. No overall conclusion is stated in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Haemato-biochemical changes in local dogs help show visceral organ involvement and guide therapy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single-site profile suggesting haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatocellular or cholestatic injury, azotaemia and hypoalbuminaemia, consistent with multisystem babesiosis. It cannot attribute organ injury to Babesia versus co-infection, dehydration or pre-renal causes, and gives no outcome data. Pathophysiological statements in the discussion (immune-mediated destruction, splenic sequestration) are drawn from literature, not tested here.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species not confirmed (smear only, species not named); selection of 27 of 136 positive dogs unexplained; healthy comparators small (n=10) and possibly historical (values cited to an earlier study); co-infections not excluded; units and tables inconsistent (for example platelets reported as 1.24 x10^3/L, albumin in mg/dL, abstract versus text on urinalysis abnormalities, in three dogs only); no ethics or data statement. Journal peer review rigour unverified (see verification notes).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
