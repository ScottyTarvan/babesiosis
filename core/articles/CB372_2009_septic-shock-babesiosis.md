## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective study reviewed the records of 86 dogs with babesiosis admitted to a university veterinary clinic in Zagreb during 2007. Ten of them met criteria for septic shock, defined as a systemic inflammatory response followed by multiple organ dysfunction and hypotension that did not respond to treatment. Seven had parasitaemia above 1%, with a maximum of 20.2%. Seven were anaemic, three were leucopoenic, nine had low platelet counts and nine had raised bilirubin; two were hypoglycaemic. Four dogs had two organs affected, five had three and one had four. The kidney was involved most often, in nine dogs, and the central nervous system least often, in one. All ten dogs died within one to four days of admission, whereas mortality among babesiosis cases without septic shock was 2.6%.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** When the inflammatory response progresses to septic shock in canine babesiosis, an unfavourable outcome should be expected.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series describes the clinicopathological picture and organ involvement of the sickest babesiosis cases seen at one clinic in one year. Death in all ten dogs is a descriptive finding in a small group, and the design cannot show what drove the outcome. Renal involvement was the most frequent organ dysfunction, and thrombocytopenia and anaemia were common, which fits the multisystem severity phenotype.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective single-centre design; only 10 septic shock dogs; one-year admission window (2007); Babesia species not stated; septic shock cases not compared statistically with other babesiosis cases beyond a mortality figure; no mechanistic or biomarker data in the abstract.
