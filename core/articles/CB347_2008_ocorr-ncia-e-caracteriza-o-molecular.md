## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked how often Babesia infection occurs in dogs attending a hospital in Londrina, Brazil, and which species are involved. The authors selected 282 dogs that had anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, or a combination of these at consultation. Babesia genus DNA was sought by PCR, and Giemsa-stained blood smears were also examined. Smears were positive in 38 dogs (13.5%), whereas PCR detected infection in 105 (37.2%). Restriction analysis with Hinf I of the positive samples gave a profile compatible with B. canis vogeli in 66 dogs (23.4%) and with B. gibsoni in 39 (13.8%). The authors concluded that babesiosis is an important differential diagnosis in dogs with anaemia, leukopenia and thrombocytopenia, that B. canis vogeli predominates, and that B. gibsoni is also present in the region.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis is an important differential for dogs with anaemia, leukopenia and thrombocytopenia. B. canis vogeli was the subspecies found in most cases, and B. gibsoni was also present in dogs of the Londrina region.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is mainly a molecular occurrence survey, but the full text also tabulates the cytopenia patterns of the PCR-positive dogs, which gives descriptive haematological evidence for the anaemia and thrombocytopenia seen with both species. Because dogs were enrolled for having cytopenias, the cytopenia frequencies among infected dogs partly reflect the selection rule. The authors found no statistical association between the inclusion criteria and PCR or PCR-RFLP results.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** dogs selected on the basis of cytopenias, with no healthy control group; species assigned by restriction profile without sequencing; haematology limited to packed cell volume, white cell and platelet counts; no clinical severity, organ-injury or outcome data; single hospital population; Portuguese-language regional journal.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

  - Among the 105 PCR-positive dogs, 57 (54.3%) had anaemia, 37 (35.2%) leukopenia and 61 (58.1%) thrombocytopenia. Combinations: 17 anaemia with thrombocytopenia, 5 anaemia with leukopenia, 6 leukopenia with thrombocytopenia, and 11 with all three (full text).

  - Against PCR, the Giemsa smear had a sensitivity of 36.2% and specificity of 100% (kappa 0.41) (full text).
  - Sex, breed and age were not significantly associated with PCR or PCR-RFLP results (full text).
