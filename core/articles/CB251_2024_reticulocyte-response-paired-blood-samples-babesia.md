## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether dogs with acute Babesia canis infection mount a regenerative erythroid response, and how apolipoprotein A-I (ApoA-I) behaves as an acute-phase marker. Eleven young and eleven adult naturally infected dogs were sampled at admission and again 14 days after imidocarb dipropionate therapy. Blood counts and biochemistry were run on automated analysers, reticulocytes were counted manually after supravital staining, and ApoA-I was measured by radioimmunoassay. At admission both age groups had anaemia without a regenerative reticulocyte response, with no between-group difference. By day 14 the anaemia had resolved and reticulocyte counts were high (p<0.05). Vital signs, leukocyte count and triglycerides pointed to a stronger acute-phase reaction in young dogs. ApoA-I fell in both groups by day 14 (p<0.01), which the authors read as positive acute-phase behaviour.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive, paired before/after design)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The erythroid regenerative response after treatment was efficient in young and adult dogs, and ApoA-I behaves as a positive acute-phase protein in acute B. canis infection, though its link to erythroid regeneration needs further study.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Non-regenerative anaemia at presentation, followed by reticulocytosis after treatment, suggests the early marrow response is blunted during acute disease (inflammation-related suppression is plausible but not tested here). The ApoA-I result is an inference from a fall after recovery, not a demonstrated mechanism; it may be consistent with related work by the same group, which was not checked against this packet. Relevant to anaemia kinetics and acute-phase biology, not to haemolysis mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=11 per group; no healthy controls in abstract; B. canis identification method and co-infection screening not stated; day-14 comparison is confounded by imidocarb and any supportive treatment and by time since admission; single post-treatment time point; manual reticulocyte counting; no numeric effect sizes in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
