## Abstract-derived summary

Malaria-associated ARDS is poorly understood, and because endothelial activation contributes to other malaria complications, the authors examined two markers, von Willebrand factor (VWF) and angiopoietin-2 (ANG-2), in lung tissue taken after death. Three groups were stained and scored semi-quantitatively: *P. falciparum* patients without alveolar oedema, *P. falciparum* patients with oedema (MA-ARDS), and lung tissue from uninfected individuals killed in accidents, showing no lung pathology. Within oedematous alveoli both markers were markedly raised in MA-ARDS, as was VWF inside vessels, relative to both other groups. Septal and large-vessel-wall VWF was reduced against controls. Septal ANG-2 was elevated in infected patients without oedema, and alveolar leukocytes staining for ANG-2 were more frequent in both infected groups. The authors infer marked endothelial activation with Weibel-Palade body release.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Post-mortem immunohistochemistry with semi-quantitative scoring; cross-sectional and associational only.
- **Authors' conclusion:** MA-ARDS lungs show high VWF and ANG-2 in oedematous alveoli, and reduced vessel-lining VWF with more intravascular VWF suggests endothelial activation and Weibel-Palade body release.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category PUL; the pulmonary endothelial-activation pathway is rated **speculative**. The only canine lung measurement is CB111 (*B. rossi*, necropsy), showing more macrophages and T cells with fibrin and partial diffuse-alveolar-damage overlap; CB080 (n=6) favoured circulatory failure over lung injury. No canine study measured VWF, ANG-2 or other endothelial markers in lung, and no human babesiosis paper did either. Differences: *P. falciparum* sequesters in the microvasculature, which Babesia does not do to the same extent, so malaria is a comparator rather than an equivalent; the canine and human readouts are different cell types and not like-for-like.
- **Key limitations:** Group sizes not stated in the abstract; semi-quantitative scoring; post-mortem tissue cannot show cause and effect; single Thai population.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
