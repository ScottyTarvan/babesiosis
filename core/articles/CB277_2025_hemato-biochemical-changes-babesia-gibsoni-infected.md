## Abstract-derived summary

A four-year-old German shepherd seen at a private clinic in Odisha, India, had passed haemoglobinuria for three days. Blood smear microscopy indicated B. gibsoni. Haematology showed reduced haemoglobin, haematocrit, red cell, platelet and total leukocyte counts, with neutrophilia and lymphopenia. Biochemistry showed increases in bilirubin, AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, GGT, total protein, globulin and albumin:globulin ratio, while creatinine stayed normal. Imidocarb was given on presentation without benefit, and the dog died within four days of the onset of haemoglobinuria. The authors attributed death to haem accumulation in renal parenchyma and glomerulonephritis driven by immune-mediated proteinuria. No numerical values appear in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors attribute the fatal outcome to haem toxicity in the kidney and immune-mediated glomerulonephritis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates fulminant intravascular haemolysis with haemoglobinuria, cytopenias and hepatobiliary enzyme changes in a presumed B. gibsoni infection. The renal mechanism (pigment nephropathy plus immune-complex glomerulonephritis) is a hypothesis: creatinine was normal, and no urinalysis values, histopathology or necropsy are reported in the abstract or on the landing page. Normal creatinine argues against established azotaemic kidney injury at sampling, although the keywords state acute kidney failure. The descriptive laboratory profile fits a haemolytic syndrome; causality for death is not demonstrated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no necropsy, histopathology or urinalysis; species by smear only; co-infections not excluded; no numerical values; treatment delay and imidocarb as a questionable agent for B. gibsoni confound outcome; the abstract states death occurred without immediate treatment although imidocarb was given on presentation. Indian Veterinary Association journal with no peer-review policy seen on the landing page; citation metadata inconsistencies (DOI, dates) noted above.
