## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective observational study characterised kidney injury in client-owned dogs with B. rossi babesiosis using injury and function markers. Thirty-five infected dogs and 10 healthy controls were enrolled; urine and blood were taken at presentation, then in subsets at 24 hours (n = 11) and one month (n = 9). Urinary markers of glomerular (IgG, CRP) and tubular (retinol-binding protein, NGAL) damage, urine protein:creatinine ratio, serum creatinine and SDMA were measured, and post-mortem kidney tissue underwent light and electron microscopy. At presentation all injury markers exceeded control values (P < 0.001) whereas serum creatinine and SDMA did not differ. Tubular markers and UPC fell within a day; all urinary markers normalised by one month. Complicated cases had higher glomerular markers, UPC and SDMA than uncomplicated cases, but tubular markers and creatinine were similar.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. rossi infection causes transient kidney injury detected by injury biomarkers but not by functional markers; tubular injury was similar across severity groups, while functional loss (SDMA) was more frequent in complicated disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports early, subclinical glomerular and tubular injury as a common feature of B. rossi infection, with glomerular involvement and reduced filtration tracking disease severity. Biomarker data show injury and its reversibility, not its cause (haemoglobinuria, hypoxia/hypoperfusion, inflammation and immune-complex mechanisms remain untested); histology findings are not summarised in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample; follow-up in small subsets (n = 11, 9), likely survivors only; 10 controls; case definition of "complicated" not given in abstract; treatment effects confounded with time; country/setting and species confirmation method not stated in abstract; number of biopsied dogs not reported.
