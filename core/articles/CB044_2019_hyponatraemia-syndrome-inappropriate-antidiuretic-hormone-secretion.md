## Abstract-derived summary

Earlier work suggested that dogs with babesiosis have low serum tonicity, possibly reflecting inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion, a disturbance also described in human malaria. The authors measured serum and urinary sodium and urine specific gravity in infected dogs, recorded mean arterial pressure (MAP) at presentation, and calculated serum tonicity and osmolality. In non-azotaemic dogs, MAP correlated significantly with tonicity, calculated osmolality, specific gravity and both serum and urinary sodium, and three dogs met criteria for SIADH. The authors conclude that SIADH occurs in non-azotaemic babesiosis, probably related to lower blood pressure, and suggest vasopressin release may be an appropriate protective response to hypotension that would otherwise lead to hypoxia and renal failure.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** SIADH develops in non-azotaemic dogs with babesiosis, probably linked to decreased blood pressure, possibly as a protective vasopressin response.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating link between haemodynamics (MAP) and water-sodium handling. Correlations are moderate (|r| about 0.46-0.52, full text) within the 21-dog non-azotaemic subgroup. Vasopressin was not measured, so the "appropriate secretion" mechanism is speculative; only one of three SIADH dogs was hypotensive (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small subgroups (three SIADH cases); osmolality calculated, not measured; urine osmolality proxied by specific gravity; no hormone assays; correlations only in one subgroup without stated multiplicity correction; no ethics statement.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
