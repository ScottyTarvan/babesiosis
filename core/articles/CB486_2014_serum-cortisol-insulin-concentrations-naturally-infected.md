## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined the endocrine anti-inflammatory response of dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis, the main cause of canine babesiosis in Europe. The authors measured serum cortisol, insulin and glucose in healthy dogs and in infected dogs that later survived or died, both before and on the day after antibabesial treatment. Before treatment, all three variables differed between the groups, with higher cortisol and insulin in infected dogs. One day after treatment, cortisol and glucose still differed between healthy dogs and both infected groups, whereas insulin did not. Median glucose was within the reference range in every group, although one surviving dog was hypoglycaemic before treatment. Cortisol and insulin correlated positively. The authors concluded that both hormones could be used to predict mortality in B. canis babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that cortisol and insulin concentrations can be used to predict death in B. canis babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Cortisol and insulin were higher in infected dogs than in healthy dogs, and the hormone values were associated with outcome group. A positive cortisol-insulin correlation fits a coordinated endocrine response, but the design cannot show that the hormones drive survival. Group sizes and effect sizes are not given in the abstract, so the strength of the prognostic claim cannot be judged.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes not reported in the abstract; species identification method not stated; setting not stated; no cut-off values or discrimination statistics given in the abstract; full text not read; single-centre or multicentre origin unknown
