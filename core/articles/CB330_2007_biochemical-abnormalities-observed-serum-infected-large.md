## Abstract-derived summary

The authors measured serum biochemistry in dogs from Warsaw, Poland, that were naturally infected with large Babesia. From 2023 positive samples, 202 were randomly chosen and analysed for ALT, AST, ALP, creatinine, BUN, total serum protein, albumin and glucose. Raised AST was the most frequent finding (92.6% of dogs), followed by raised ALT (64.9%) and raised BUN (62.4%). Raised ALP and raised creatinine were found in 31.7% and 30.7% of dogs. Lower albumin was seen in 32.7%, lower total protein in 19.8% and low glucose in 18.3%, while only 1.5% had reduced BUN. The authors report that transaminase and ALP elevation, higher creatinine, hypoalbuminaemia and hypoglycaemia were the commonest abnormalities, and they attribute these to liver disease, kidney failure and fasting.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The commonest biochemical abnormalities in infected dogs were raised transaminases and ALP, raised creatinine, hypoalbuminaemia and hypoglycaemia, which the authors attribute to hepatopathy, renal failure and fasting.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives frequencies of abnormal liver, renal and glucose values in a large series of infected dogs, which supports hepatic and renal involvement as common features of canine babesiosis. The attribution to hepatopathy, renal failure and fasting is an inference; with no control group or histology in the abstract, the findings show an association only. The percentages are shares of dogs with values reported as elevated or decreased; the abstract gives no reference intervals, and the percentages do not show severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no control group described; no molecular method for the species reported in the abstract; 202 of 2023 positive samples were chosen at random, and the abstract does not describe the sampling source or period; no severity grading, outcome or treatment data in the abstract; reference intervals and the statistical tests are not given in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
