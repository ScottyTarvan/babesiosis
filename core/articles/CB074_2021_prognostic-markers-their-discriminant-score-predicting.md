## Abstract-derived summary

The study sought laboratory predictors of death in canine B. gibsoni infection. Blood from 108 client-owned dogs with compatible signs was assessed for haematology, biochemistry, haemostasis, antioxidant status and lipid peroxidation (thiobarbituric acid reactive substances), and screened by microscopy and molecular methods. Infected dogs were split into survivors and non-survivors and compared with 30 healthy controls. Haemoglobin, platelets, catalase, urea, creatinine, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, lactate and reticulocytes correlated strongly with survival. Receiver operating characteristic analysis highlighted urea, ALT and lactate as markers with specific prognostic value, and a discriminant formula with a cut-off of 0.141 reached 79% sensitivity and 83% specificity for fatal outcome. The authors suggest it could flag dogs needing intensive care at presentation.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** A discriminant score built from prognostic markers can predict the outcome of B. gibsoni infection at presentation, with scores above 0.141 indicating need for intensive care.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a severity signature of renal dysfunction (urea, creatinine), hepatic injury (ALT, ALP), hyperlactataemia (hypoperfusion/hypoxia), anaemia with reduced regenerative response, thrombocytopenia and lowered catalase with oxidative markers, consistent with multi-organ involvement in fatal cases. These are prognostic associations, not mechanisms. The score was derived and tested in the same data as far as the abstract shows, so performance is likely optimistic.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no external validation reported; survivor/non-survivor numbers and treatment protocols not given in abstract; treatment confounding; co-infections not mentioned; setting and recruitment period not available.
