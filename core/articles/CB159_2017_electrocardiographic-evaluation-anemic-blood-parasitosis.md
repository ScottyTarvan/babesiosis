## Abstract-derived summary

Electrocardiograms were recorded in 23 dogs with haemolytic anaemia attending the medicine outpatient service of a veterinary teaching hospital in Ludhiana: 12 had Ehrlichia canis and 11 had Babesia spp. Sinus arrhythmia was the commonest rhythm finding, present in about 83% of the dogs. Sinus tachycardia affected 20 dogs (87%). Deviations of the ST segment, depression or elevation, were recorded in 5 dogs (22%), and a further 3 dogs showed ST slurring. The abstract does not break results down by causative organism, and it describes no outcome data, control group or comparison between the two infections. The findings are therefore a descriptive snapshot of cardiac findings in a single hospital sample.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** not stated in the abstract; the findings are presented as the ECG changes seen in anaemic dogs with blood parasitosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests that tachycardia and repolarisation changes accompany haemolytic anaemia in dogs, but the cause cannot be separated (anaemic hypoxia, fever, volume status, myocardial injury or electrolyte disturbance). Sinus arrhythmia is often a normal canine variant, so its frequency here is not itself evidence of cardiac pathology. Babesia-specific cardiac pathophysiology is not established, since the two infections are pooled.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small n; no healthy or non-anaemic control group; results pooled across Babesia and Ehrlichia; Babesia species and diagnostic method not stated in the abstract; no cardiac biomarkers or echocardiography reported; no outcome data; peer-review process of the journal not verified.
