## Abstract-derived summary

Diminazene had been suggested to cause a faster fall in haematocrit than trypan blue after treatment of dogs with heavy Babesia canis parasitaemia. To test this, 19 dogs with mild to moderate, uncomplicated disease were sorted into low, moderate or high parasitaemia groups and randomly given one of the two drugs. Haematocrit and parasitaemia were recorded before treatment and over 24 hours. The two drugs did not differ in haematocrit or parasite clearance, and starting parasitaemia did not correlate with haematocrit. Haematocrit dropped in every dog, by a mean maximum of 0.046 L/L, usually at 6 or 12 hours. All dogs improved clinically, so either drug was judged safe in uncomplicated cases.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Small randomised comparative clinical study (older, drug comparison, uncomplicated cases only).
- **Relevance:** Supports the haematocrit component of the Q5 hypothesis: a modest fall (mean maximum 0.046 L/L at about 6-12 h) occurred after treatment in all dogs, regardless of drug. It did not measure cytokines or organ function, and enrolled only uncomplicated cases, so it cannot show whether the fall relates to inflammation or later deterioration.
- **Key limitations:** Only 19 dogs split across parasitaemia and drug groups, so very few per cell and low power to detect a difference; 24 h follow-up; complicated or severe cases excluded; the abstract does not say whether fluids or transfusions were given, which could affect haematocrit; trypan blue is no longer a usual treatment; no inflammatory markers.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
