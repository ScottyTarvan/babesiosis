## Abstract-derived summary

This review addresses canine babesiosis, a tick-transmitted disease caused by intraerythrocytic Babesia protozoa that long affected livestock and is now regarded as emerging in dogs around the world. The authors note that the disease mechanisms in dogs have been studied at length, yet important gaps persist, particularly about why clinical presentation differs between European countries. They propose that comparing how host and parasite interact across these countries would improve understanding of how the disease develops. Such work could, in turn, help clinicians forecast which dogs will do well or poorly and could open the way to new therapies. Because the European situation is the focus, the authors argue that this research should concentrate on Babesia canis as the relevant parasite.
