## Abstract-derived summary

This methodical-guidelines paper from the Ryazan region of central Russia describes how canine babesiosis occurs and presents, and how it may be diagnosed, treated and prevented. The authors used blood-smear microscopy and a serological test, together with tick counts on dogs and regional veterinary records. Seasonal activity of Dermacentor reticulatus was described, and the maximal extensity of invasion with Babesia spp. was 75%. Parasitaemia varied with season and with the dog's age, being higher in young and old animals than in dogs aged three to seven years. In severe disease, haematology and biochemistry showed fewer red cells, lower haemoglobin, haematocrit and platelets, higher white cell counts, raised bilirubin, urea and creatinine, and lower glucose; milder cases showed little change. The paper also gives efficacy figures for several babesiosis drugs, an antioxidant adjunct and tick repellents.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Epizootiological monitoring should guide treatment and prevention. Timely imidocarb (Babesan) is said to prevent deaths in severe cases, and the antioxidant, antihypoxic drug Emidonol is said to help prevent cardiac and respiratory failure and shorten recovery (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The paper reports that severe disease was accompanied by anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukocytosis, raised bilirubin, urea and creatinine, and lower glucose, and that these changes were less marked in mild and moderate disease. These are descriptive clinicopathological observations by severity group. The text gives no group sizes, reference ranges or statistical tests, so the size and consistency of the changes cannot be judged. The treatment and prevention claims are outside the pathophysiological scope and are not supported by a described trial design.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes and sampling not reported; no statistical analysis; fold-changes given without a comparator group; species identified by smear and serology only; mixed infections possible; severity grading criteria not defined; guidelines format rather than a full research report; full text read through an unreviewed machine translation.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
