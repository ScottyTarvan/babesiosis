## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether age, sex and breed shape the haematological, biochemical, acute phase protein (APP), serological and parasitaemia profile of dogs with natural B. canis infection. Sixty-one client-owned dogs were compared by age (under versus over one year), sex and breed type (purebred versus mixed). Methods comprised IFAT serology, real-time PCR parasitaemia, automated haematology and biochemistry, SAA ELISA, fibrinogen, ceruloplasmin, paraoxonase-1 and gel electrophoresis. Overall, fever, pancytopenia and altered APPs predominated. Young dogs were seropositive far less often (about one-third versus 96% of adults, P<0.001), had higher parasitaemia, lower erythroid and leukocyte values, lower beta- and gamma-globulins and higher alpha-globulins. Males had lower neutrophil counts than females; purebreds had more band neutrophils than mixed breeds. The authors concluded that young dogs develop more severe anaemia and acute phase response.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive / associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Young dogs with natural B. canis infection have more severe anaemia and acute phase response with leukopenia than adults, and male and purebred dogs may have a more marked myeloid-lineage response.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Host-factor modifiers of the clinicopathological profile and acute phase response, not a mechanism. Low seropositivity with higher parasitaemia in young dogs is compatible with immunologically naive or early infection, but timing of sampling relative to infection is not established. "Severity" is inferred from laboratory values; no outcome data are reported in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** modest n (61) split into small subgroups; no uninfected control group in the abstract; adjustment for confounders (age, sex and breed overlap, time since infection) not described; species not stated as molecularly confirmed; co-infections not mentioned; single time point.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
