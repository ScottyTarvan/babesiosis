## Abstract-derived summary

Twelve dogs seen at Saint Petersburg clinics between April and October 2023 were diagnosed with babesiosis by species-specific real-time PCR: ten with B. canis and two with B. gibsoni, four infections being judged locally acquired. Presentations ranged from fever with rapidly evolving multiple organ dysfunction to relapsing, prolonged anaemia of undetermined cause. Laboratory abnormalities included raised ALT, AST, creatinine, urea, bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase, low total protein, hyperchromic anaemia, neutrophilic leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia and increased ESR. The two species produced similar pictures, and B. gibsoni infection could be acute or chronic. The authors advise PCR testing, including for B. gibsoni, in compatible cases, and report imidocarb for B. canis, diminazene for B. gibsoni and plasmapheresis for multisystem intoxication.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis and B. gibsoni cause a similar clinical picture, and PCR species identification is decisive for diagnosis and treatment monitoring.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Contributes a clinicopathological profile (hepatic, renal, haematological changes) and a "multiple organ dysfunction" phenotype from a northern region where the authors diagnosed four locally acquired cases, plus chronic/relapsing B. gibsoni anaemia. It does not establish mechanisms. "Hyperchromic" anaemia in dogs more plausibly reflects haemolysis or measurement artefact (raised MCHC) than true haemoglobin overload. Treatment and plasmapheresis statements come from uncontrolled observations.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=12 with only 2 B. gibsoni; no control group; summary values rather than per-dog data in abstract; co-infections and prior treatment not described in abstract; no outcome or mortality figures in abstract; severity not graded; locally acquired status presumably based on travel history (not verified).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
