## Abstract-derived summary

The authors prospectively enrolled 45 dogs from eastern Slovakia with suspected babesiosis and characterised clinical signs and haematology. B. canis was confirmed by PCR in 34 dogs, of which 24 (70.6%) also had parasites on blood smear. Other tick-borne pathogens were screened by PCR. Common signs were lethargy (91%), fever and anorexia (59% each), pigmenturia (47%) and icterus (18%); mortality was 6%. All infected dogs were thrombocytopenic; lymphopenia occurred in 82% and anaemia in 68%. No co-infections were found, and the single Anaplasma phagocytophilum case was not co-infected with B. canis. Roughly 30% of infected dogs lacked demonstrable parasites on smear, so PCR is advised when disease is suspected.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** PCR should be used for suspected cases because about 30% of infected dogs were smear-negative, and lymphopenia is more frequent than anaemia although thrombocytopenia remains the most common finding.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds a clinicopathological profile of European B. canis infection with uniform thrombocytopenia and frequent lymphopenia. It does not examine the cause of the lymphopenia and does not test mechanisms, severity biomarkers or outcome predictors. The diagnostic-sensitivity remark is secondary to the clinicopathological data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; no control group or reference intervals described in the abstract; percentages apparently based on 34 PCR-positive dogs; mortality 6% (about 2 dogs if based on the 34 PCR-positive dogs); single time point; species method and treatment not described in abstract; recruitment setting and period not stated.
