## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined thyroid function in dogs with babesiosis attributed to Babesia canis. Serum concentrations of interleukin-6, total triiodothyronine, total and free thyroxine, thyrotropin, urea and creatinine were measured in 13 affected dogs and 10 healthy dogs. Affected animals had lower total T3, total T4, free T4 and TSH, and higher IL-6, urea and creatinine, than the healthy dogs. Within the infected group, IL-6 showed inverse correlations with total T3 and TSH, while total T3 showed inverse correlations with urea and creatinine. The authors interpret this pattern as low T3 syndrome (non-thyroidal illness) in canine babesiosis and describe the T3 level as associated with IL-6. The background notes that earlier work described a similar lowering of thyroid hormones in babesiosis caused by B. rossi.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Low T3 syndrome is present in canine babesiosis, confirmed by measuring T3, and the T3 concentration is associated with serum IL-6 concentration.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Dogs with babesiosis had lower thyroid hormones and TSH and higher IL-6 and azotaemia markers than healthy dogs, and the correlations associate IL-6 and renal markers with T3. The design is case-control with correlation analysis, so the correlations show association only and do not show that IL-6 or azotaemia lowers T3. The findings fit a non-thyroidal illness pattern that spans endocrine, inflammatory and renal change.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample (13 infected, 10 controls); single comparison with healthy dogs, no sick non-babesia controls reported in the abstract; species identification method not stated in the abstract; country and setting not stated in the abstract; disease severity grading not reported in the abstract; correlations do not show direction of effect; full text not read.
