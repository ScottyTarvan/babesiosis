## Abstract-derived summary

This descriptive study characterised haematological and serum biochemical changes in dogs presented to the college clinics at Anand, Gujarat. Dogs with natural infection, identified by Babesia on direct blood smear (n=24), were compared with healthy dogs (n=8). Infected dogs had significantly lower haemoglobin, packed cell volume, platelets, total leucocytes and lymphocytes, with higher neutrophils and eosinophils, which the authors read as anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutrophilia, lymphopenia and eosinophilia. Serum ALP, ALT, AST, globulin and BUN were raised, whereas albumin and the albumin:globulin ratio were reduced. Effect sizes and exact p-values are not given in the abstract. The authors conclude that babesiosis should be considered in any anaemic dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis is associated with significant haemato-biochemical alterations, so it should be ruled out in all anaemic dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a multisystem clinicopathological profile (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatocellular or hepatobiliary enzyme rises, raised BUN, hypoalbuminaemia with hyperglobulinaemia). It is a syndrome-level description; it does not separate mechanisms (haemolysis, consumption, inflammation, hepatic or renal injury) or relate findings to severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small sample, unequal groups; species not identified (microscopy only); co-infections not mentioned; no severity or outcome stratification; no hydration or treatment data; effect sizes not reported in abstract; full text not accessed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
