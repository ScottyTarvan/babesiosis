## Abstract-derived summary

The authors measured serum cytokines in 97 dogs naturally infected with Babesia rossi and 15 healthy controls, using blood taken at admission before treatment. Infection was confirmed by PCR and reverse line blot, and seven cytokines or chemokines were quantified with a canine multiplex assay. Compared with controls, infected dogs had higher IL-10 and MCP-1 but lower IL-8. Twelve infected dogs (12%) died; these non-survivors had higher IL-6 and MCP-1 than survivors. Dogs seen within 48 hours of the onset of signs showed higher IL-2, IL-6, IL-18 and GM-CSF than those seen later. The authors interpret the findings as a mixed cytokine response in B. rossi babesiosis, with an excessive pro-inflammatory component that may be linked to a poor outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (admission cytokine concentrations compared across infected vs control, survivor vs non-survivor and early vs late presentation groups)
- **Authors' conclusion:** A complex network of inflammatory and modulating cytokines exists in dogs infected with B. rossi. Only IL-6 and MCP-1 marked a poor outcome, which suggests that an excessive pro-inflammatory host response may be an important factor in understanding the cause of death.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is the first cytokine profile in B. rossi infection (full text) and it shows a mixed picture: IL-10 and MCP-1 higher, IL-8 lower, IL-6 not different from controls overall (full text). IL-6 and MCP-1 were higher in the dogs that died, so they are candidate markers of a poor outcome, but the design cannot show that the cytokines drive death. Acute phase proteins (CRP, SAA) were raised in infected dogs without differing by outcome (full text), so in this cohort IL-6 and MCP-1 separated the outcome groups where CRP and SAA did not.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 12 non-survivors, so outcome comparisons are underpowered (full text); univariate Mann-Whitney tests with no stated adjustment for multiple comparisons or confounders (full text); one admission sample, so kinetics are not captured; only 15 controls, with a different sex ratio from the infected dogs (full text); days sick known for 84 of 97 infected dogs (full text), based on the recorded days of illness before presentation; TNF and IFN-gamma not measured (full text); treatment decided by different clinicians (full text); serum concentrations may not reflect bioactive or local cytokine activity (full text).

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Client-owned dogs presented to the Onderstepoort Veterinary Academic Hospital between October 2011 and April 2013; ethics approval from the University of Pretoria Animal Ethics Committee (full text)
  - Eligible dogs were over 12 weeks of age and over 5 kg with demonstrable parasitaemia; dogs with B. vogeli or Ehrlichia canis infection by PCR/RLB, concurrent inflammatory, cardiac or neoplastic disease, or recent anti-inflammatory treatment were excluded (full text)
  - Controls were 15 healthy client-owned dogs admitted for ovariohysterectomy, castration or blood donation, confirmed healthy by examination, smear, CBC, biochemistry and PCR/RLB (full text)
  - Infected dogs received diminazene aceturate, packed red cell transfusion and fluids as needed; outcome was survival to discharge or death/euthanasia for poor prognosis (full text)
  - Cytokines were measured by a Millipore canine multiplex panel on a Luminex 200 at the University of Copenhagen; values below the detection limit were set to the minimum detectable concentration (full text)
  - Days sick were known for 84 dogs: 44 were sick for 48 hours or less and 40 for more than 48 hours; median 2 days (full text)
  - Median admission IL-8 was 1066.3 pg/mL in infected dogs and 3316.7 pg/mL in controls (P = 0.005); IL-10 was 620.3 vs 44.6 pg/mL and MCP-1 544.2 vs 211.7 pg/mL (both P < 0.001) (full text)
  - IL-6 (P = 0.041) and MCP-1 (P = 0.009) were higher in non-survivors than survivors; IL-6 did not differ significantly between infected dogs and controls (full text)
  - CRP and SAA were raised in infected dogs (P < 0.001) with no difference between survivors and non-survivors; WBC, neutrophil, lymphocyte and eosinophil counts were lower in infected dogs than controls (full text)
  - IL-8 correlated weakly and positively with WBC, neutrophil and lymphocyte counts, and IL-10 correlated weakly and negatively with WBC, neutrophil and monocyte counts (full text)
  - Abnormalities in non-survivors, alone or combined, included severe anaemia, hyperlactataemia, icterus, hypoglycaemia, neurological signs, pulmonary oedema, acute kidney disease and secondary IMHA (full text)
  - Male:female ratio differed between infected dogs and controls (P = 0.02) (full text)
  - Data availability statement: all relevant data are within the paper and its Supporting Information files (full text)
