## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined blood samples from 248 dogs naturally infected with large Babesia in Warsaw, Poland. They assessed red cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit, red cell indices, leucocyte and platelet counts, mean platelet volume, erythrocyte morphology and the differential leucogram. Thrombocytopenia and anisocytosis were the most frequent abnormalities, with low platelet counts in nearly every dog (99.5%). Reduced red cell count affected 26.2% of dogs, low haematocrit 31.4% and low haemoglobin 29%. Raised MCHC occurred in 21% and low MCV in 2%. Anisocytosis was seen in 60.5%, poikilocytosis in 25%, polychromasia in 23.8%, hypochromia in 19.7% and erythroblastosis in 4.4%. Only 15.3% had raised MPV, which the authors read as suggesting a bone marrow response. Neutropenia occurred in 36.3%, a left shift in 21.8%, lymphocytosis in 14.9% and lymphopenia in 7.2%.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Thrombocytopenia and anisocytosis were the commonest haematological abnormalities in dogs infected with large Babesia. The authors read raised MPV in a minority of dogs (15.3%) as suggesting a bone marrow response.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a large clinicopathological profile of naturally infected dogs. It shows that thrombocytopenia was almost universal, whereas anaemia indices were abnormal in roughly a quarter to a third of dogs. The red cell morphology findings and the leucogram add descriptive evidence of erythrocyte and leucocyte changes. The study does not link these findings to severity, outcome or mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; no control group or reference population reported; species not confirmed by a molecular method; no severity or outcome analysis; reference intervals and per-parameter denominators not given in the abstract; single-region sample.
