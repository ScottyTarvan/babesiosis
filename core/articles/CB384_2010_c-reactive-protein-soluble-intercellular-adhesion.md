## Abstract-derived summary

The study tested whether inflammation in canine babesiosis accompanies a change in soluble intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (sICAM-1), a marker of endothelial disturbance. Twelve dogs with signs of acute babesiosis were studied prospectively, with blood taken before imidocarb dipropionate treatment and six days later. Plasma C-reactive protein (CRP) and sICAM-1 were measured by ELISA and compared with healthy dogs using t-tests (p < 0.05). Before treatment, CRP was significantly higher in infected dogs than in healthy dogs. It fell by day six but remained significantly raised. sICAM-1 did not differ significantly from its pre-treatment value at day six, yet was significantly lower than in healthy dogs. The authors described systemic CRP elevation in canine babesiosis, followed by a fall in sICAM-1 after treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** CRP was systemically elevated in canine babesiosis, and sICAM-1 decreased after treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** CRP was higher in dogs with acute babesiosis than in healthy dogs and fell, without normalising, six days after imidocarb treatment. sICAM-1 changed little from baseline, so the link between inflammation and endothelial disturbance that the authors set out to test is not clearly supported by the abstract. The design shows association over time only and cannot show that treatment or inflammation drove the sICAM-1 change.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small sample (12 dogs); healthy group size not reported; no stated parasite species or confirmation method; single post-treatment time point; no untreated infected control group; simple t-tests only; publication venue and peer-review status not confirmed
