## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with natural B. canis infection presented to a university internal medicine clinic in Türkiye were evaluated for four serum proteins: haptoglobin (Hp), serum amyloid A (SAA), ceruloplasmin (Cp) and albumin (Alb). Twenty dogs diagnosed by Giemsa-stained blood smear were compared with ten healthy dogs. Infected dogs had much higher SAA (mean 43.18 vs 1.18 µg/mL) and Cp (10.75 vs 4.80 mg/dL), and lower Hp (0.44 vs 1.84 mg/mL) and Alb (2.74 vs 3.01 g/dL) than controls. Differences were significant at P < 0.001, except albumin (P < 0.01). The authors conclude that infection raises SAA and ceruloplasmin and lowers haptoglobin and albumin. Dispersion is reported as mean ± SD in the source.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (case-control biomarker comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis infection increases serum SAA and ceruloplasmin and decreases haptoglobin and albumin.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents the acute phase response in clinical canine babesiosis: SAA and Cp as positive and albumin as negative acute phase proteins. The Hp decrease is not a typical positive-APP pattern and may reflect consumption by haemoglobin release, but haemolysis was not measured in the available information, so this is hypothesis only. The data describe an inflammatory signature, not mechanism, severity grading or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n; species by microscopy only; clinical severity, haematology, parasitaemia, co-infections and treatment status not reported; recruitment period not reported; single comparison with healthy controls rather than other inflammatory diseases.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
