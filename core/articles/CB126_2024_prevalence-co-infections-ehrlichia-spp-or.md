## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked how common co-infection with Ehrlichia canis or Theileria equi is among dogs with babesiosis in South Africa's Eastern Cape, and whether demographic, haematological or biochemical variables flagged such co-infection. They enrolled 150 dogs with natural babesiosis seen at one state veterinary clinic during 2021, using quantitative PCR to determine the Babesia species and to detect co-infecting agents. Babesia rossi accounted for 149 dogs and B. vogeli for one. Only three dogs (2.0%, 3/149; 95% CI 0.4-5.7) carried a second agent, all E. canis alongside B. rossi; no Theileria co-infection was reported. None of the variables examined, including monocyte, platelet, band neutrophil and reticulocyte indices and monocytosis, was significantly associated with co-infection. The authors suggested B. rossi might confer some protection against other tick-borne infections.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (prevalence-oriented, with associational analysis)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Co-infection is uncommon in dogs with babesiosis in this region, raising the possibility of a protective effect of B. rossi.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly a co-infection prevalence study; its pathophysiological value is indirect. Because almost all dogs had single-agent B. rossi infection, the study says little about whether the leukocyte, platelet and reticulocyte indices tested here reflect Ehrlichia co-infection rather than babesiosis itself. With only 3 co-infected dogs, the absence of significant associations is uninformative (very low power). The "protective effect" idea is speculative; no mechanism or uninfected comparator was studied.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very few co-infected dogs; single clinic and single year; no babesiosis-negative controls; co-infecting agents beyond E. canis/T. equi apparently not covered (not stated in abstract); B. vogeli represented by one dog; severity and outcome not reported in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
