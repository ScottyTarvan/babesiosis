## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared seven dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis, diagnosed on clinical signs plus a parasitological/ELISA work-up, against seven healthy controls. Serum total and lipid-bound sialic acid were measured spectrophotometrically, routine chemistry and macro-elements on an autoanalyser, and trace elements by ICP-MS. Infected dogs had markedly higher sialic acid fractions, together with raised AST, ALP, LDH, CK, CRP, glucose, globulin, bilirubin, urea, uric acid, creatinine and BUN, and lower total protein. Zinc, copper, magnesium, sodium and potassium were reduced, whereas iron and chloride were elevated. ALT, lipid fractions and ferritin did not differ significantly. The authors concluded that babesiosis alters sialic acid, biochemical variables and element status.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive / associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis produces significant changes in sialic acid levels, biochemical parameters and trace and macro-element concentrations.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds an unusual panel (sialic acids, trace elements) to the clinicopathological profile of canine babesiosis. Sialic acids behave as acute-phase glycoconjugate markers, so the rise fits systemic inflammation (with CRP and globulins) and tissue injury (AST, LDH, CK), but no mechanism is shown. Element shifts (low Zn, Cu, Mg; high Fe) are compatible with acute-phase redistribution and haemolysis, yet this was not tested. Azotaemia is reported without distinguishing pre-renal from renal causes.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 7 per group; Babesia species not stated; "ELISA" is serology, so the parasitological confirmation is unclear; no severity stratification, co-infection screen, treatment status or timing; control matching not reported; many univariate comparisons without stated correction; packet abstract was truncated (completed from the publisher page).
