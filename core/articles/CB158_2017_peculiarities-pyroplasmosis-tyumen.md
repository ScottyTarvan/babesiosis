## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe canine pyroplasmosis (babesiosis, attributed to Babesia canis) in the city of Tyumen, using data from a private veterinary clinic between 2012 and 2016. They examined haematological and biochemical blood indices and looked for relationships between disease occurrence and age, sex, breed, season and year. The abstract states that incidence peaked in May and August, that dogs younger than one year were the most affected, and that ornamental, hunting and guard breeds were over-represented among pedigree dogs. Changes in blood count and serum chemistry values were noted, but the abstract gives no figures. The article is written in Russian; the abstract is a Russian original with an English version appended to the PDF.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Seasonal peaks, susceptible age and breed groups, and a pattern of blood count and chemistry abnormalities characterise the disease locally.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinic-level clinicopathological profile supporting the multisystem phenotype of canine babesiosis (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatocellular and cholestatic enzyme rises, hypoglycaemia). It shows association only; organ-level attributions (e.g. myocardial injury from the AST/ALT ratio) are the authors' inference and are not demonstrated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no healthy control group; the number of dogs is absent from the abstract; species not molecularly confirmed; co-infections not excluded; single sampling at presentation with variable disease stage; treatment and outcome detail minimal; the abstract and the body disagree on the most affected age group (see below); no evidence of an explicit peer-review policy was seen on the publisher page.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
