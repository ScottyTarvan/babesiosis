## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined two inflammatory markers, procalcitonin (PCT) and the amino-terminal portion of C-type pronatriuretic peptide (NT-pCNP), in dogs with babesiosis attributed to Babesia canis. The authors regard the disease as a sepsis-like multisystemic condition for which veterinary medicine lacks specific biomarkers. Of 97 dogs enrolled, 72 had babesiosis and 25 were healthy controls. Both markers were measured in every dog, and comparisons were made before and after antibabesial treatment and between survivors and dogs that died. PCT differed between healthy and infected dogs at both time points but did not separate survivors from non-survivors. NT-pCNP showed the reverse pattern: no difference between healthy and infected groups, yet a difference between survivors and non-survivors. The authors propose that PCT may help detect and grade bacterially driven systemic inflammatory complications, whereas NT-pCNP may serve as a prognostic marker of outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors judge PCT probably useful for detecting and grading SIRS complications that arise solely from bacterial systemic infection, and NT-pCNP a prognostic, pro-inflammatory marker of outcome in dogs with babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The two markers behaved differently: PCT was associated with the presence of babesiosis but not with survival, and NT-pCNP was associated with survival but not with the presence of disease. The abstract gives no concentrations, test statistics or numbers of survivors and non-survivors, so effect sizes and discriminatory performance cannot be judged. The statement that PCT reflects bacterial complications is an interpretation and is not tested in the reported comparisons.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-only appraisal; no concentrations, p values or group sizes for survivors and non-survivors reported; Babesia species identification method not stated; country, clinic and recruitment period not stated; the abstract text in the record is duplicated and garbled, and the full text was not retrieved.
