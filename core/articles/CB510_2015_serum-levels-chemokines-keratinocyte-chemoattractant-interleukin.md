## Abstract-derived summary

The study measured serum concentrations of two CXC chemokines, keratinocyte chemoattractant (KC-like) and interleukin-8 (IL-8), in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis canis in Croatia and compared them with healthy dogs. The measurements came from a larger research project and used a canine-specific multiplex assay read on a Luminex analyser. The authors also asked whether the chemokine levels relate to the development of complicated disease and to outcome. Higher KC-like levels were linked with more severe anaemia, leukocytosis and the complicated form of babesiosis, while IL-8 correlated positively with the granulocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio. Both chemokines were higher in dogs with complicated disease. The authors suggest that these chemokines may contribute to the pathogenesis of canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum KC-like and IL-8 concentrations are higher in dogs with complicated babesiosis, and the authors suggest they could be an important factor in the pathogenesis of the disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The findings suggest that raised chemokine concentrations were associated with anaemia, leukocytosis and complicated disease, which is consistent with an inflammatory contribution to severity. The design cannot show that the chemokines drive these changes. The abstract also states an aim of testing an effect on outcome but reports no outcome result.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** sample size and group sizes not reported in the abstract; method of species confirmation not stated; single comparison of infected and healthy dogs, with no causal inference; no outcome result reported in the abstract; chemokine data come from a larger study whose overlap with other papers is not stated.
