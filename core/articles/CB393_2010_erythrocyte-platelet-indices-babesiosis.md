## Abstract-derived summary

The authors measured red cell and platelet indices in blood from 30 dogs naturally infected with large Babesia, sampled before and after imidocarb dipropionate treatment, and compared the values with 50 healthy dogs. An automated analyser (Abbott Cell-Dyn CD 3500) provided red cell count, mean corpuscular volume, haematocrit, red cell distribution width, platelet count, mean platelet volume and plateletcrit. Thrombocytopenia was the commonest abnormality, accompanied by lower plateletcrit and higher mean platelet volume, which the authors suggested reflected platelet activation. Red cell distribution width was unchanged, which they took to mean a uniform red cell population, while red cell count, mean corpuscular volume and haematocrit were reduced in most infected dogs both before and after treatment. They propose that these indices may help clarify the causes of anaemia and thrombocytopenia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Erythrocyte and platelet indices could provide clinical information about the conditions underlying anaemia and thrombocytopenia in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a descriptive haematology comparison of infected and healthy dogs. A low plateletcrit with a raised mean platelet volume was seen with thrombocytopenia and was read by the authors as platelet activation, but activation was not measured directly. The unchanged red cell distribution width and reduced red cell indices that persisted after imidocarb are described, and the abstract does not test their mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; no numerical results in the abstract; Babesia species and identification method not stated; recruitment setting and period not stated; no severity or outcome stratification; effect of treatment on the indices not quantified in the abstract; the source record is a reference-list entry with duplicated abstract text.
