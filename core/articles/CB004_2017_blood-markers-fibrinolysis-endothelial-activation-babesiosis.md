## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether endothelial activation and fibrinolytic change accompany canine babesiosis. In a prospective cohort of 30 dogs with naturally acquired B. canis infection, blood was drawn at admission and on day 6 after imidocarb treatment. Plasminogen activity was measured chromogenically; HMGB-1, ICAM-1, VCAM-1, suPAR, TAFI, soluble thrombomodulin and PAI-1 were measured by canine ELISA. At admission, thrombomodulin, HMGB-1, VCAM-1 and suPAR exceeded values in healthy dogs. Relative to day 6, admission samples showed higher thrombomodulin, ICAM-1 and HMGB-1 and lower plasminogen and PAI-1. Thrombomodulin fell below control values after treatment. In the complicated subgroup, thrombomodulin, HMGB-1 and TAFI differed between admission and day 6. The authors conclude that endothelial and fibrinolytic markers are altered and merit evaluation as severity or prognostic biomarkers.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Endothelial activation and fibrinolysis biomarkers are altered in babesiosis, and their value for severity or prognosis needs further study.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Marker shifts that resolve by day 6 are compatible with endothelial activation and a hypofibrinolytic-to-recovering state during acute infection (a descriptive pathophysiological signal), but the data do not show mechanism or causality. Complicated and uncomplicated dogs were largely indistinguishable at admission, so utility as severity markers is not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n (11 complicated); no non-babesial disease controls; supportive therapy given mostly to complicated dogs and assumed, not tested, to be non-influential; imidocarb confounds the day 6 comparison; recruitment period not stated; the abstract and Results text give the TAFI direction in complicated dogs as higher at admission, while Table 3 shows lower (440.7 vs 561.6 ng/ml).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
