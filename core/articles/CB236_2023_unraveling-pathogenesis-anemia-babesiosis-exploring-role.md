## Abstract-derived summary

A single-author review sets out to explain how anaemia arises in canine babesiosis, concentrating on whether inflammatory mediators contribute. The full text says PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science were searched for English-language animal, clinical and in vitro work, but gives no search terms, dates, study counts or appraisal results. The abstract states that IL-1, TNF-α, IFN-γ and various chemokines are implicated in anaemia, promoting red cell destruction, altered erythropoiesis and disturbed iron handling. The full text names CXCL8 and CCL5 and adds that these effects are partly mediated by reactive oxygen species. It concludes these mediators contribute substantially and may be therapeutic targets. No quantitative findings are presented. The full text (four pages) lists ten references, mostly on Babesia taxonomy and general babesiosis rather than cytokine measurements in dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The author concludes that dysregulated pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines contribute significantly to anaemia in canine babesiosis through haemolysis, impaired erythropoiesis and altered iron metabolism.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The mechanisms named (cytokine-driven haemolysis, anaemia of inflammation, hepcidin-type iron restriction) are biologically plausible and match the wider literature, but this article does not substantiate them for dogs. No primary canine cytokine study is cited in the text, so it cannot be used as evidence of mechanism or as a guide to the primary literature; treat it as a low-reliability secondary source.
