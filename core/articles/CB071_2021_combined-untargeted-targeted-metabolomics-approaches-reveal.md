## Abstract-derived summary

Urine from 22 dogs naturally infected with B. canis, split by kidney function, and 12 healthy dogs was profiled by untargeted and targeted mass-spectrometry metabolomics. The untargeted workflow found 601 features differing in abundance among groups, 27 of which matched known standards; the targeted kit produced 17 metabolites with significant between-group differences. Together the changes pointed to an inflammatory host response, oxidative stress and altered energy metabolism. Renal dysfunction accompanying babesiosis was linked to shifts in amino acid, energy and fatty acid metabolism and in urea cycle and ammonia detoxification pathways. The authors propose that urinary markers could support detection and monitoring of kidney damage in babesiosis and comparable diseases.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Urinary metabolite changes reflect kidney dysfunction in canine babesiosis and could yield markers for detecting and monitoring renal damage.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Exploratory omics evidence supporting renal and metabolic involvement (amino acid excretion, acylcarnitines, urea cycle) in babesial AKI/proteinuria. It shows association with creatinine/UPC-defined groups, not mechanism, and cannot say whether changes are tubular injury, Babesia-specific or generic to AKI.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small groups (6, 10, 6 versus 12 controls), cross-sectional single time point, groups defined by creatinine and UPC only (no SDMA/IRIS), no external validation or non-babesial AKI comparator, recruitment period not reported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
