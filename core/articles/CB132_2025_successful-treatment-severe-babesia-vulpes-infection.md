## Abstract-derived summary

A 12-year-old spayed crossbreed dog had PCR-confirmed Babesia vulpes infection diagnosed three weeks after elective splenectomy. Antiprotozoal drugs, glucocorticoids and three transfusions did not correct the anaemia, which became non-regenerative, prompting referral. The dog was pale and febrile, with regenerative anaemia, low platelets, and raised bilirubin, triglycerides, ferritin and liver enzymes. Coombs' testing was negative, imaging unremarkable, repeat PCR still positive and other infectious screening negative. Marrow cytology and histology showed hyperplasia with macrophages at or above 3% of nucleated cells, actively engulfing red cells and, less so, neutrophils, platelets and nucleated erythroid cells, supporting haemophagocytic syndrome. Dual immunosuppression, antiprotozoals and three further transfusions followed. Platelets recovered quickly and anaemia improved by day ten; therapy was withdrawn without relapse and PCR turned negative.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** This is presented as the first report of successful treatment of a splenectomised dog with severe B. vulpes infection complicated by haemophagocytic syndrome.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Raises the hypothesis that macrophage haemophagocytosis (hyperferritinaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, cytopenias, marrow erythrophagia) may contribute to anaemia and thrombocytopenia in severe babesiosis, possibly unmasked by loss of splenic clearance. A mechanistic link to splenectomy or to the parasite is not established; the observation is a syndrome description from one animal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no control; temporal rather than causal link to splenectomy; recovery coincided with combined immunosuppression, antiprotozoal therapy and transfusion, so the contribution of each is unclear; diagnostic criteria for haemophagocytic syndrome applied by authors only.
