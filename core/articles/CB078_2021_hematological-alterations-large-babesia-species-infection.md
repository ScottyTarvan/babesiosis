## Abstract-derived summary

The authors reviewed haematology records from 4039 dogs seen at a district veterinary centre in Kerala between December 2018 and October 2020, aiming to describe blood changes in natural infection with large-form Babesia, for which local data were scarce. Using a retrospective design, the authors identified 35 dogs (0.87%) with large intraerythrocytic piroplasms on Giemsa-stained blood smears. The commonest findings were mild-to-moderate regenerative anaemia with normal red cell indices, neutrophil counts that were low or normal, and thrombocytopenia. Thrombocytopenia was more frequent than haemolytic anaemia and was accompanied by increased numbers of large, activated platelets (macroplatelets). Overall, the authors describe platelet changes as the most frequent haematological abnormality in this regional dog population, more so than haemolytic anaemia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Thrombocytopenia with macroplatelets was the most frequent clinicopathological abnormality in large-Babesia infection, more so than anaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports platelet consumption or activation as a common feature of large-Babesia infection, with macroplatelets plausibly reflecting thrombopoietic response. It is a haematological profile only. It does not show mechanism (immune-mediated destruction, sequestration, or consumption), and does not link platelet changes to severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Species assigned by morphology alone; no PCR, so B. vogeli is assumed from regional context rather than shown. Retrospective, single centre, no co-infection testing described. Small control group (n=10, demographics not described). Mostly mild or asymptomatic dogs, with no outcome data. Text inconsistency over whether the 4039 dogs were babesiosis suspects.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
