## Abstract-derived summary

This small retrospective study measured three acute phase proteins in serum from dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis. Samples came from six dogs with uncomplicated disease and one with complicated disease, and were compared with six healthy dogs. Infected dogs had significantly higher C-reactive protein and ceruloplasmin, whereas haptoglobin was significantly lower than in controls (P < 0.01). The authors propose that these proteins could help with diagnosing babesiosis and judging its severity. They describe the work as preliminary, and state that published information on the acute phase response in natural canine babesiosis was limited at the time.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** retrospective observational case-control study; preliminary, very small.
- **Relevance:** Cited in Q1 as prior work on haem scavengers: it reports low serum haptoglobin in B. canis infection, which fits haptoglobin consumption during haemolysis but was not paired with cell-free haemoglobin, haemopexin, or endothelial or tubular injury markers. It therefore does not close the Q1 gap. The reduced haptoglobin, against raised C-reactive protein, also illustrates that haptoglobin behaves differently from the positive acute phase proteins in this disease.
- **Key limitations:** Only 7 infected dogs (one complicated), so no severity analysis is possible; retrospective use of stored samples; a single sampling point; the abstract gives no effect sizes, breed, age, parasitaemia or treatment details; the lower haptoglobin may reflect haemolysis as much as an acute phase response (an interpretation, not tested in the abstract).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
