## Abstract-derived summary
In hospitalised Malaysian adults with knowlesi malaria (48 severe, 154 non-severe) and 50 healthy controls, investigators measured cell-free haemoglobin as an index of intravascular haemolysis, alongside angiopoietin-2 and osteoprotegerin (both Weibel-Palade body products), endothelial and microvascular function, and severity markers. Cell-free haemoglobin rose in step with severity, exceeded levels earlier seen in falciparum patients of the same cohort, and related to parasitaemia and, independently, to both endothelial markers. Osteoprotegerin was independently linked to creatinine, lactate, IL-6, ICAM-1, E-selectin, impaired microvascular reactivity and nitric oxide-dependent endothelial dysfunction. Acute kidney injury (AKI) affected 88% of severe cases, with each marker an independent risk factor. The authors infer haemolysis-driven endothelial activation contributes to organ dysfunction.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** primary human observational cohort; associations, no effect sizes in the abstract.
- **Authors' conclusion:** haemolysis-mediated endothelial activation and Weibel-Palade release likely contribute to end-organ dysfunction, including AKI.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** soluble endothelial activation is **directly demonstrated in both** (CB004, B. canis, 30 dogs vs 10 controls: thrombomodulin, VCAM-1, HMGB-1, suPAR; CB145; CB087), at class level only. The Ang-2 axis is **mechanistically plausible** (no canine Ang-2; CB122 vesicle proteomics). Haemolysis and nitric oxide loss is **speculative**: canine work shows anaemia and oxidative stress (CB099, CB006, CB107) but no cell-free haemoglobin or NO function. Microvascular dysfunction is **mechanistically plausible** (CB041, CB145). Differences: P. knowlesi sequesters little, dog assays need validation, and Ang-2 is non-specific.
- **Key limitations:** cross-sectional associations; single cohort; no canine measurement of these analytes.
