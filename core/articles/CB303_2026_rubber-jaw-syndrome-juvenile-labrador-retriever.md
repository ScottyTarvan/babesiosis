## Abstract-derived summary

A seven-month-old male Labrador Retriever was brought to a university clinic with worsening facial swelling, inappetence, vomiting and weakness. Examination found enlarged, pliable jaws and loose teeth. Blood work showed macrocytic hypochromic anaemia, and Giemsa smears showed B. gibsoni inside red cells. Chemistry revealed marked increases in urea nitrogen, creatinine, phosphorus and parathyroid hormone, with low calcium and slightly raised liver enzymes. Skull radiographs showed bone loss in the facial bones; ultrasound found an enlarged spleen and thickened renal cortex with lost corticomedullary definition. The authors diagnosed fibrous osteodystrophy from chronic kidney disease with renal secondary hyperparathyroidism, attributing the renal insufficiency to babesiosis, and present it as a rare complication.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The dog had fibrous osteodystrophy secondary to chronic kidney disease complicated by babesiosis-induced renal insufficiency, a rare metabolic bone presentation of a vector-borne haemoprotozoan infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a distinct renal-endocrine-skeletal syndrome (CKD, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia, raised PTH) in a Babesia-positive juvenile dog. Causation is not established: the abstract gives no renal biopsy or histology, and juvenile renal disease (congenital, developmental) or other causes of CKD are not excluded; coexisting babesiosis may be incidental or a contributor. The case is hypothesis-generating only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no histopathology; no baseline renal data or timeline linking infection to CKD; no PTH figure in the text (table provided as an image); treatment response and outcome not recorded in the abstract; the PCR described on the publisher page was gel-based without reported sequencing.
