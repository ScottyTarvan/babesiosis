## Abstract-derived summary

The packet abstract was empty, so this summary draws on the publisher landing page and the checked full text (all figures are from full text). A 4-year-old male Labrador in Kolkata presented with fever, inappetence and coughing up blood, and was dull, with marked pyrexia, tachycardia and tachypnoea. A blood smear indicated clinical babesiosis. Haemoglobin was low (6.3 g/dL; PCV 20.5%), with neutrophilic leucocytosis (41,600/uL) and raised bilirubin, ALT and ALP. Treatment was one diminazene injection followed by 21 days of doxycycline, enrofloxacin and metronidazole, with supportive care. The smear cleared and values normalised by day 21. The authors conclude that the regimen was effective and well tolerated. The discussion proposes thrombocytopenia, DIC, cytokine-driven capillary injury and red-cell sequestration as possible routes to haemoptysis, drawing on cited literature rather than case data.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Diminazene combined with doxycycline, enrofloxacin and metronidazole plus supportive care was very effective against B. gibsoni in this dog.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Haemoptysis in babesiosis is plausible, but this report cannot show a mechanism. No thoracic imaging, coagulation testing, airway sampling or exclusion of other causes is reported. The pathophysiology section is generic literature rather than case data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species by morphology only; internal inconsistencies (abstract gives 106 F, HR 145 and haemoptysis as the complaint; the case history gives paraplegia, 105 F and HR 158; text says creatinine was elevated but the table shows 1.3 mg/dL, within the printed range; text cites imidocarb, which was not given; the differential count sums above 100%). Treatment confounding. Short-turnaround, regional journal outside MEDLINE; reliability of the record is doubtful.
