## Abstract-derived summary

Blood from five dogs with severe babesiosis was compared with blood from five control dogs for carboxyhaemoglobin. The fraction was significantly higher in the infected dogs (P < 0.05). The authors argue that haemolysis increases the breakdown of haem by haem oxygenase, the only known endogenous source of carbon monoxide, and that the gas then binds haemoglobin. They propose that carboxyhaemoglobin on top of severe anaemia further impairs oxygen status in severe disease and may contribute to the hypoxic tissue damage seen in this condition. The abstract gives no figures for the carboxyhaemoglobin fractions themselves, and the mechanism is presented as a proposal, not as a demonstrated pathway.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** small comparative observational study with a hypothesis-generating interpretation.
- **Relevance:** Cited in Q1 (haemolysis and free haem) as prior work showing that haem catabolism has a measurable downstream effect in babesiosis. It supports the idea that haem turnover is raised in severe disease and may add to hypoxia, but it measured carboxyhaemoglobin, not cell-free haemoglobin, haem scavengers, or endothelial or kidney injury markers, so it does not address the Q1 gap directly. Hypoxia is also the co-factor that Q1's counter-hypothesis (see BG002) says may be needed for any haemoglobin-related kidney injury.
- **Key limitations:** Five dogs per group; no effect size or carboxyhaemoglobin values in the abstract; control group characteristics are not described; the link from carbon monoxide to tissue hypoxia and damage is inferred, not measured; no survival, organ-function or kidney outcomes in the abstract; the study predates current taxonomy, so the large Babesia named in the title may correspond to a different species today (inference, not stated in the record).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
