## Abstract-derived summary

Few studies have asked whether different Babesia species provoke different serum protein responses. The authors compared protein electrophoresis fractions in 17 dogs naturally infected with B. gibsoni and 40 with B. canis, and also split the B. canis dogs by clinical form. B. gibsoni dogs had higher total protein, β-globulin and γ-globulin values (P<0.05 to P<0.001), while relative albumin, α1- and α2-globulins and the albumin/globulin ratio were lower (P<0.001). More fractions differed when B. gibsoni was compared with the complicated form of B. canis, and red cell indices also differed between those two groups. The authors conclude that electrophoretic patterns, and so the host response, differ between the two species.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum protein electrophoretic patterns differ between B. gibsoni and B. canis infection, suggesting species-specific host responses.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive evidence that B. gibsoni is associated with a stronger polyclonal gamma-globulin rise and relative albumin/α-globulin fall than B. canis, consistent with more chronic antigenic stimulation; the pattern in B. canis fits an acute-phase response. It cannot separate species effect from disease stage, signalment or sampling setting, and gives no mechanism. The abstract is internally unclear on β-globulins.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No healthy controls; groups differ in breed, age and setting (clinic admission versus kennel); no recruitment period; B. canis not molecularly confirmed; co-infection exclusion not described; haemolysis in 10/40 B. canis samples; unequal and small groups.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
