## Abstract-derived summary

The two-sentence abstract says canine babesiosis is a tick-borne protozoal disease of red blood cells and that anaemia reflects several interacting factors that complicate treatment; it reports no data. The full text (read, in Romanian) lists proposed contributors to anaemia: immune recognition of parasitised cells, erythrophagocytosis, reactive oxygen species damage, complement activation, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, and splenic sequestration of infected and uninfected erythrocytes. It then outlines diagnosis (history, Giemsa-stained smears, PCR, serology) and management (anti-parasitic therapy, transfusion, supportive care, with a transfusion trigger of haematocrit below 20%). No quantitative canine data are presented. Its six references are mostly textbooks, and none of the listed mechanisms is tied to a canine primary study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The parasite does not itself directly cause anaemia; it triggers immune and cellular responses that together produce it (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a checklist of recognised anaemia mechanisms (immune-mediated, oxidative, complement, splenic), but it is textbook-level and none of the mechanisms is linked to a canine primary study. The claim that the parasite does not directly cause anaemia is questionable, since intraerythrocytic replication and rupture also destroy cells. A sentence on endothelial adherence refers to B. bovis, not dogs. A haemoglobin threshold is given as "8 mg/dl", which is probably a units error for g/dl.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** non-systematic; six references, mostly textbooks (one from 1992); no primary canine citations; species unspecified; no peer-review policy located on the article page (peer-review status not established; journal is not MEDLINE-indexed).
