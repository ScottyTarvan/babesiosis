## Abstract-derived summary

The study looked at electrolyte and acid-base changes in dogs in the early stage of babesiosis. It also asked whether the infecting 18S rRNA genotype of Babesia canis canis, type A or type B, was linked to how marked these changes were. Forty sick dogs were compared with forty healthy dogs, with blood pH, potassium, chloride, calcium and sodium measured and the parasites typed by molecular methods. Most sick dogs were alkalaemic, a few were acidaemic, and low chloride affected nearly all of them. Potassium and sodium were low in some dogs, and sodium was high in a small number. Twenty-nine dogs carried genotype A and eleven genotype B. No relationship was found between genotype and the electrolyte disturbances, and the authors called for larger studies.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study could not show that the type of electrolyte disturbance in babesiosis depends on the infecting parasite genotype. Further work in a larger group of animals is needed.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Dogs with early babesiosis often had low chloride and altered blood pH, and some had low potassium or sodium, compared with healthy controls reported to have no abnormalities. The genotype comparison was made by listing counts per genotype, with no test statistics given in the abstract, so a genotype effect cannot be excluded or confirmed. The findings describe a pattern of disturbances at an early stage and do not show their cause.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no statistical tests or reference intervals in the abstract; uneven genotype groups (29 vs 11); the abstract states calcium was normal in all 40 dogs yet also reports hypocalaemia in 10 genotype A and 6 genotype B dogs; severity and outcome are not reported; setting and recruitment are not stated; sampling timing relative to infection is not stated.
