## Abstract-derived summary

A 6-year-old female Lagotto Romagnolo in Germany was found on routine ultrasonography to have an enlarged spleen with a diffusely heterogeneous parenchyma. Fine-needle aspiration cytology showed reactive lymphoid hyperplasia together with extramedullary haematopoiesis. Further investigation established babesiosis, with high anti-Babesia antibody titres and evidence of B. canis, as the explanation for the splenic changes. The dog never exhibited clinical signs or clinicopathological abnormalities attributable to infection, even though the infection was judged active. The authors suggest the high antibody level indicates either earlier exposure or a subacute course. They describe this as one of few reports of babesiosis presenting solely as structural splenopathy with extramedullary haematopoiesis and no accompanying clinicopathological changes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Active B. canis infection can present as splenopathy (reactive hyperplasia, extramedullary haematopoiesis) alone, without clinical or clinicopathological abnormalities.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates a subclinical/subacute phenotype in which splenic immune and haematopoietic activation is the only detectable host response. It is a syndrome-level observation, not mechanistic; it cannot show that the parasite caused the splenic change, nor how the dog avoided anaemia or thrombocytopenia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no longitudinal data in the abstract; causality inferred from exclusion of other causes; species identification method and treatment response not described in the abstract; serology cannot distinguish current from past exposure.
