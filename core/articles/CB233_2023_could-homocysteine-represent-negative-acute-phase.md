## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether homocysteine (Hcy) falls as systemic inflammation intensifies in dogs, as a negative acute-phase marker would. They measured serum Hcy, serum amyloid A (SAA) and routine haematology and biochemistry in six healthy dogs and in dogs with dirofilariosis (n=31, mild inflammation), babesiosis (n=12, moderate) or pyometra (n=8, severe). Hcy was lower in babesiosis and pyometra than in healthy dogs, and lower in pyometra than in dirofilariosis; SAA followed the opposite order. Across groups Hcy and SAA were inversely correlated (rho -0.502, P<0.001). Both correlated, in opposite directions, with red cell indices, haemoglobin and neutrophil count, and haemoglobin alone independently predicted Hcy. The authors concluded that Hcy declines as the acute-phase response strengthens and tracks haematological change.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hcy concentration fell as the acute-phase reaction intensified and was related to the accompanying haematological changes; clinical value remains to be established.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Babesiosis is one of four groups, so this informs host response (high SAA, low Hcy) rather than babesiosis-specific mechanism. Haemoglobin was the only independent predictor, so the Hcy fall may reflect anaemia, anorexia or hypoalbuminaemia (Hcy is albumin-bound) instead of a regulated acute-phase response; the data show association only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small, unequal groups; aetiology confounded with inflammation grade; different sample numbers per analysis (clotted EDTA samples, insufficient serum); no folate/B12; single time point; no outcome data; possible overlap of babesiosis dogs with an earlier publication from the same group (not stated).
