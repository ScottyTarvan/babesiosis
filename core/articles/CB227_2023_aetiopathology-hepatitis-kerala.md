## Abstract-derived summary

Forty dogs from Thrissur, Kerala, presenting with signs compatible with hepatitis were screened for three infectious causes. Canine adenovirus 1 was found by PCR in one dog, leptospirosis by microscopic agglutination test in eight, and babesiosis by blood smear in nine (22.5%). Each aetiology showed its own haematological and biochemical pattern. Dogs with babesiosis had marked anaemia, low platelet counts, a mild leucocytosis, raised alkaline phosphatase and total bilirubin, and low total protein; the abstract gives no numerical values for this group. The authors treat babesia as one of several infectious contributors to canine hepatic disease in this region, alongside leptospira and adenovirus.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis, leptospirosis and CAV-1 contribute at differing frequencies to hepatitis presentations, each with a characteristic clinicopathological profile.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that babesia-positive dogs selected for hepatic signs had cholestatic-pattern enzyme and bilirubin changes alongside anaemia and thrombocytopenia. Hyperbilirubinaemia may reflect haemolysis as well as hepatocellular or cholestatic injury, and no histopathology is reported, so hepatic pathogenesis is not established. A syndrome-level descriptor, not a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 9 babesia cases, selected by clinical suspicion of hepatitis (selection bias); smear-only diagnosis without species identification; no controls; co-infection not reported; no numerical results for the babesiosis group in the abstract; recruitment period and site not stated on the page read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
