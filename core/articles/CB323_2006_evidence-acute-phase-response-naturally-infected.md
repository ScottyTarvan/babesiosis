## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis were compared with healthy dogs for sedimentation rate, white cell count, haematocrit, platelet count and acute phase proteins. Both groups received imidocarb dipropionate on admission and were sampled on admission and on days one, two, three, four and seven. Infected dogs showed an acute phase response, with significant increases in the major acute phase proteins. C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A and sedimentation rate declined daily after treatment, nearing reference values by day eight, whereas platelets and haematocrit rose, nearing reference values by day four. White cell count and haptoglobin rose first and then fell. C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A and platelets were more sensitive diagnostically than the other markers, and the two acute phase proteins were useful for treatment monitoring.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** An acute phase response occurs in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis, and C-reactive protein and serum amyloid A are of clinical use for monitoring the response to antibabesial treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study describes how acute phase proteins and haematological variables differ between infected and healthy dogs and how they change after imidocarb treatment in uncomplicated disease. Increased acute phase proteins were associated with infection; the design does not show which component of the disease or treatment drove the later changes. It reports acute phase proteins and haematology, not cytokines or organ function, so it says little about mechanism or about complicated disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes not given in the abstract; healthy dogs also received imidocarb, so changes after treatment cannot be separated cleanly from the effect of the drug; uncomplicated cases only, as the abstract describes, so severe or fatal disease is not described; sampling on admission and then daily or less often cannot resolve changes within the first hours; species identification method not stated in the abstract; cut-offs behind the sensitivity comparisons not given in the abstract; full text not read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
