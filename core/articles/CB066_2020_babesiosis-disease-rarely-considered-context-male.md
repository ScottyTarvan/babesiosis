## Abstract-derived summary

Little is known about how babesiosis affects semen quality and fertility in male dogs. The authors report four male dogs that became infertile after Babesia canis infection. Sperm quality was poor in every dog. Two dogs were castrated, pharmacologically or surgically, whereas fertility returned in two others after selenium and vitamin E supplementation. The authors discuss several candidate explanations for impaired spermatogenesis: adverse effects of imidocarb used to treat the infection, fever, and disturbed testicular microcirculation. They conclude that clinicians evaluating a male dog with fertility problems should routinely ask about previous babesiosis. No controls, hormone data or testicular histology are described in the abstract, so the cases suggest an association rather than a demonstrated mechanism.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Past babesiosis should be a standing item in the clinical history of male dogs presenting with fertility problems.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a possible post-infection reproductive sequela, not an acute-phase mechanism. Fever, testicular hyperthermia, hypoxia from anaemia/microcirculatory disturbance, cytokine damage and drug effects are proposed but none was tested; causality is not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 4, no control group, species by microscopy only, no pre-infection semen or hormone data, no testicular histology, multiple concurrent drugs (imidocarb, enrofloxacin, NSAID), unblinded supplementation, owner-reported history, incomplete follow-up.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
