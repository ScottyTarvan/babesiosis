## Abstract-derived summary

The study describes serum monovalent ion changes in 70 dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis, a species occurring in Europe, and compares them with earlier descriptions of B. rossi infection in South Africa. Low sodium, low potassium and high chloride were among the commonest findings, alongside reduced chloride gap, strong ion gap and sodium-minus-chloride difference and raised chloride-to-sodium and sodium-to-potassium ratios. Hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia and hyperchloraemia were less frequent than with B. rossi, but similar in severity. Azotaemic dogs had significantly lower sodium than non-azotaemic dogs; anaemic and non-anaemic dogs were also compared. The authors suggest that hyperchloraemic acidosis may be developing and that aldosterone probably contributes to low potassium, and they call for blood gas and hormone studies.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The results point to a possible development of hyperchloraemic acidosis and a probable contribution of aldosterone to hypokalaemia in B. canis infection. Further work on blood gas, aldosterone and antidiuretic hormone in these dogs is needed.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In naturally infected dogs, serum sodium, potassium and chloride abnormalities and altered ion-gap indices were common, and lower sodium was associated with azotaemia. The acid-base and hormonal mechanisms are inferred from electrolyte patterns, not measured directly in the abstract. The comparison with B. rossi relies on earlier reports, not on dogs studied side by side.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; no healthy control group reported in the abstract; acid-base status, aldosterone and antidiuretic hormone not measured; species identification method not stated in the abstract; single-species sample; setting and recruitment period not stated in the abstract; comparison with B. rossi is indirect.
