## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective survey reviewed 3174 anaemic dogs (haematocrit below 37%) seen at a Taiwanese university veterinary hospital from January 2008 to December 2012. About half of the dogs had mild anaemia; the rest were graded moderate, severe or very severe by haematocrit, and 147 fell in the very severe grade. A cause was identified in 2037 dogs, mostly a single cause. Cancer, infection, kidney disease and surgery or trauma were the leading single causes. Among the 43 dogs whose very severe anaemia was attributed to infectious disease, Babesia gibsoni was the pathogen diagnosed most often (36 dogs), ahead of Ehrlichia canis, Babesia canis and Leptospira spp. The authors concluded that cancer, infection and renal failure are the commonest causes of canine anaemia in Taiwan, with B. gibsoni the main infectious agent in very severe cases.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cancer, infectious disease and renal failure were the most frequent causes of canine anaemia in Taiwan, and B. gibsoni appeared to be the most important infectious pathogen in very severe anaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series reports B. gibsoni as the most frequent pathogen among infectious cases in the most severe haematocrit grade of a large hospital population, which is useful descriptive evidence on anaemia severity in infected dogs. It reports no infected-versus-uninfected comparison and no mechanism, so it cannot show how infection relates to the degree of anaemia. Babesia-specific data come only from the very severe infectious subgroup.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; retrospective single-centre hospital series; diagnostic methods for pathogens not stated in the abstract; Babesia numbers given only for the 43 very severe infectious cases; counts of severe and very severe dogs differ between the haematocrit grading and the cause analysis and are not reconciled in the abstract; no clinicopathological or outcome data for infected dogs beyond haematocrit grade.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
