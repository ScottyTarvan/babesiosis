## Abstract-derived summary

The authors searched necropsy records (2003 to 2017) of a Brazilian university pathology laboratory for dogs with cerebral babesiosis, defined as parasitised erythrocytes in cerebral vessels plus central nervous system lesions. Seven dogs qualified. Disease was hyperacute, with sudden death within about 24 hours in four dogs and a course of roughly 12 hours in the seventh. Signs included vocalisation, anorexia, jaundice and recumbency. At necropsy the liver was orange and the spleen enlarged. Brain histology showed endothelial hypertrophy, monocyte leukostasis, non-suppurative meningitis, congestion, thrombosis, haemorrhage, vasculitis and perivascular cuffing. Only one dog had a heavy cerebral parasite burden, so the authors suggest death arose from systemic responses to parasite antigens rather than parasite load.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cerebral parasite burden did not explain death; antigen-driven systemic responses probably did, implying high pathogenicity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides histological support for an endothelial and inflammatory (leukostasis, vasculitis, thrombosis) component of cerebral babesiosis, loosely analogous to sequestration-type microvascular pathology. The "systemic response" explanation is a hypothesis; no immunological, coagulation or cytokine data are given. Species identity matters for interpretation but was not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 7; retrospective, 15-year necropsy selection biased to fatal hyperacute cases; species by morphology only (no PCR or IHC in abstract); co-infections and concurrent disease not excluded; no controls or parasite-load quantification method in abstract; the OpenAlex abstract may omit the journal's conclusion sentence.
