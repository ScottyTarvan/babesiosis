## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined whether uncomplicated B. canis infection is linked to changes in whole-blood zinc and copper and in erythrocyte antioxidant defences (glutathione, superoxide dismutase and catalase). Fifteen naturally infected dogs showing anorexia, fever, depression, pallor, splenomegaly and dark urine were compared with seven apparently healthy dogs presented for check-up or vaccination. Infection was confirmed by blood smear and PCR. Superoxide dismutase and catalase activities were significantly higher in infected dogs, while zinc (0.478 vs 1.241 μg/mL) and copper (0.722 vs 1.392 μg/mL) were significantly lower. The authors proposed oxidative stress as one mechanism of anaemia and suggested antioxidant enzymes and trace metals as severity and prognostic indicators.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Oxidative stress may contribute to anaemia in canine babesiosis, so antioxidant markers and zinc/copper could serve as severity and prognostic indicators.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows a compensatory rise in erythrocyte SOD and CAT with depletion of zinc and copper in acute B. canis infection, compatible with an oxidant burden. Oxidative damage itself (e.g. lipid peroxidation) was not measured, and no outcome or severity gradient was analysed, so the prognostic claim and the anaemia mechanism are inferred rather than demonstrated. Zinc and copper fall may also reflect acute phase redistribution or anorexia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n (15 vs 7); single pre-treatment time point; co-infections not addressed; no outcome data; GSH not different; haematology reported for infected dogs only; no ethics statement ("none required"); assay matrix described inconsistently.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
