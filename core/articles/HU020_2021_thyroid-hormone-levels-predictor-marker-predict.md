## Abstract-derived summary

Drawing on the MIMIC-III critical-care database, the authors identified septic patients to examine how thyroid hormone concentrations relate to outcome. Variables with P below 0.1 on univariate testing entered a binary logistic regression, and AUROC measured discrimination. Of 929 eligible patients, 703 had a good outcome and 226 a poor one. Thyroxine (T4) was significantly lower in the poor-outcome group (P<0.01), and lower admission T4 was linked to worse outcome (OR 0.556; 95% CI 0.41-0.75). A combined model of free T4, T4 and SAPS II reached an AUROC of 0.82 for ICU survival, exceeding free T4 (0.65), T4 (0.71) and SAPS II (0.70-0.72) individually. The authors conclude that low T4 predicts short-term sepsis outcome and adds information to SAPS II.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Retrospective analysis of a public ICU database; prognostic association with multivariable adjustment.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Low serum thyroxine is a predictive marker of short-term sepsis outcome, and a combined model improves on SAPS II.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category MET; low T4 with severity or death (non-thyroidal illness) is rated **directly demonstrated in both** (association only). CB040 (*B. rossi*, n=320) found low T4 among the best discriminators of death without independent multivariable significance; CB105 (experimental *B. rossi*, n=5) showed T4 falling with severity and dose while TSH stayed in range. The abstract here does not report TSH, so that feature rests on CB105. A *B. canis* thyroid study (unresolved, title-only record; see search log) was not used. Differences: human host is sepsis, not a haemoparasitic disease; dogs have different thyroid hormone binding; canine data include a five-dog model; low T4 may mark illness severity rather than cause it, and thyroid changes in malaria are not uniform.
- **Key limitations:** Retrospective, with unclear selection of who had thyroid testing; abstract omits TSH, confidence intervals for AUROCs and external validation; no parasitic infection.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
