## Abstract-derived summary

Using data gathered over six years (2008-2014) from an owned, free-roaming dog population in Zenzele, South Africa, originally monitored for a rabies-control study, the authors describe the natural course of B. rossi exposure where the parasite is endemic and tick prevention is not routine. Of 109 randomly chosen dogs sampled in early 2010, 34 (31%) were strongly seropositive by indirect fluorescent antibody test; all 34 remained clinically healthy adults on regular examination, and repeated smears were uniformly negative. A separate set of 18 smear-positive dogs, confirmed as B. rossi by PCR with reverse line blot, mostly had subclinical or mild illness and were smear-positive on one occasion only. The authors interpret this as acquired immunity from repeated low-level exposure and advise caution over tick control in adult free-roaming dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The observations are consistent with immunity acquired through repeated low-level exposure, producing transient subclinical infection or mild disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that B. rossi, generally considered to cause severe babesiosis, can produce subclinical or mild infection in endemic free-roaming dogs, so the severity spectrum may reflect host factors (prior exposure, adaptive immunity) that were not measured. It does not demonstrate protective immunity or identify any mechanism; this is outcome-spectrum evidence, not pathophysiology of severe disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control or challenge group; immunity is inferred, not tested; IFAT cross-reactivity and specificity not addressed in abstract; seropositive dogs assessed by smear only (low sensitivity), not PCR; survivor bias (dogs that died of babesiosis cannot appear as seropositive survivors); single serological time point; definition of "mild disease" not given in abstract; relationship between the 34 seropositive and 18 smear-positive groups not stated beyond "separate".

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
