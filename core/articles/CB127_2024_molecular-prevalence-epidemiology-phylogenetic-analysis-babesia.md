## Abstract-derived summary

The study estimated the molecular prevalence of Babesia microti in 654 apparently healthy dogs of 55 breeds sampled across five districts of Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, and also examined risk factors, haematology and phylogeny. PCR detected the parasite in 29 dogs (4%), and partial 18S rRNA sequencing confirmed the species identity. Sequences clustered with isolates reported from several countries across Europe, Asia and North America. Prevalence did not differ by site, breed, sex or ectoparasite presence. Among haematological indices, infected dogs had a higher red cell distribution width (coefficient of variation) than uninfected dogs. The authors framed the data as updated prevalence information useful for control planning, noting that ticks feeding on infected dogs could pass the parasite to people.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (prevalence survey; weak associational haematology)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The work provides updated B. microti prevalence data in Pakistani dogs to inform control, given possible zoonotic tick transmission.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Pathophysiological content is minimal and borderline for inclusion: the only reported host-response finding is a higher RDW-CV in PCR-positive, apparently healthy dogs, compatible with mild anisocytosis in subclinical infection with no haemolysis, anaemia or clinical disease reported in the abstract. Sequence-based "B. microti" labelling in dogs may correspond to the B. microti-like taxon (B. vulpes); this is a reviewer note, not stated in the abstract. Does not inform syndrome, severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** cross-sectional design; only 29 infected dogs; no clinical disease; co-infections not reported as excluded; other haematological indices and multiplicity handling not reported in abstract; causality of RDW difference not established.
