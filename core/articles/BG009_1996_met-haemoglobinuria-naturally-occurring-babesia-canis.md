## Abstract-derived summary

Urine from six dogs with natural B. canis infection contained more methaemoglobin than urine from five control dogs. Total urinary haemoglobin in the infected dogs lay between 1 and 4 g/l, and 28 to 95 percent of this was in the methaemoglobin form. The authors point out that methaemoglobin is toxic to the canine kidney, particularly when urine is acidic, and the infected dogs also had acidic urine, which could worsen the effect. They conclude that infected dogs with haemoglobinuria may carry substantial methaemoglobin, which could predispose them to kidney disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Small comparative laboratory/clinical study of urinary haemoglobin species.
- **Relevance:** Cited under Q1 (haemolysis, free haem, endothelial activation and tubular injury) as the canine paper on methaemoglobinuria with aciduria (search 4). It supports the plausibility of a pigment-related renal injury route and the need to record urine haem and pH as confounders, but it paired urine pigment with no kidney injury marker, no plasma cell-free haemoglobin and no endothelial measures. The companion experimental haemoglobinaemia study (PMID 9284030) cited alongside it found no significant nephropathy, so the two together argue against a simple haemoglobin-causes-injury model.
- **Key limitations:** 6 infected and 5 control dogs; the renal risk is inferred from other work, and the abstract reports no renal function or outcome data; control group composition not described in the abstract; no timing relative to treatment; 1996 methods and species nomenclature.
