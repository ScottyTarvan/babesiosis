## Abstract-derived summary

Using a canine-specific ELISA, the authors measured serum erythropoietin (EPO) in dogs with PCR-confirmed Babesia infection and in healthy comparison dogs, motivated by the anaemia and tissue hypoxia that the disease causes. Infected dogs had a mean of 87.0 mU/mL (±2.68), several-fold above the control mean of about 16.4 mU/mL (±0.63), a difference reported as significant (p<0.05). Within the infected group, 68.18% had raised EPO, 22.73% had values in the normal range and 9.09% had very low or undetectable concentrations. The authors interpret the rise as a compensatory erythropoietic response to anaemia. The abstract available here is truncated before the p-value, so the group means, percentages and significance figures here are taken from the publisher landing page.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia infection chiefly drives EPO production as a compensatory response to anaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Elevated EPO is the expected endocrine response to anaemia and is consistent with an intact hypoxia-sensing response in most dogs; it does not show a Babesia-specific mechanism, and the authors' wording that infection "drives" EPO production goes beyond a single-time-point comparison. The roughly one-in-ten dogs with low or undetectable EPO is the more interesting observation (an inappropriate response, possibly relevant to non-regenerative or renal-affected cases), but the abstract offers no explanation or outcome link, so this remains speculative.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Group sizes, packed cell volume or haemoglobin, regeneration indices and renal markers not reported, so EPO cannot be interpreted against anaemia severity; no species identification or co-infection screening reported; no outcome data; single time point; very short two-page article with the dispersion measure not defined. The journal is not indexed in MEDLINE and its peer-review process could not be verified from the available pages.
