## Abstract-derived summary

This hospital study at a Chennai veterinary teaching hospital examined how often vector-borne infections underlie bleeding tendencies in dogs and what haematological changes accompany them. Over 2018 to 2020, 16,529 of 76,051 dogs presented with bleeding signs, and 1,593 of those had a vector-borne disease and received haematology, biochemistry and coagulation testing. Within this subset, ehrlichiosis accounted for 52.7%, babesiosis 41.87% and hepatozoonosis 5.5%. Dogs were then sorted into three grades of bleeding disorder. Among B. gibsoni cases, 47.7% had anaemia with thrombocytopenia, 28.5% pancytopenia and 23.8% pancytopenia together with DIC; Ehrlichia canis cases showed comparable proportions. The abstract states no explicit conclusion.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** not stated in the abstract; the aim was to document incidence and clinical features of bleeding disorders in canine vector-borne disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive syndrome data: in hospitalised B. gibsoni cases with bleeding, roughly a quarter met the authors' criteria for DIC alongside pancytopenia. The three groups are defined by the cytopenia/DIC pattern, so the percentages describe the grouping, not a risk estimate. "Incidence" here is a proportion of hospital presentations, not a population rate. Nothing addresses mechanism, and pancytopenia in B. gibsoni is not explained.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** referral hospital population; DIC criteria and co-infection exclusion not visible; internal inconsistencies in the publisher text (study period "two years" vs 2018-2020; E. canis age groups sum to 768 not 839); no outcome data seen; peer-review process not documented on the page.
