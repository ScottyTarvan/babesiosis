## Abstract-derived summary

Infection of dogs with virulent Babesia canis is characterised by rapid onset and high mortality, with similarities to complicated human malaria. The authors investigated which parasite factors are secreted during acute canine disease, since such factors may drive pathogenicity. They first produced a fully annotated genome for a virulent Hungarian field isolate, BcH-CHIPZ, supported by genome-wide RNA-seq. They then combined in silico prediction of the parasite's exportome with experimental analysis of secreted proteins. The results point to conserved apicomplexan families linked to immune evasion, including VESA, and to proteins released across the infected-cell membrane into the bloodstream, such as the SA and Bc28 families. Potential moonlighting proteins (profilin and histones) and uncharacterised antigens present during acute crisis were also identified. The authors present this as a first, partly validated set of candidate virulence factors that could support future diagnosis and management.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental), hypothesis-generating
- **Authors' conclusion:** The data provide a first predicted and partially validated set of potential virulence factors exported during fatal infection, useful for diagnosis and management strategies.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides parasite-side molecular context (VESA-mediated antigenic variation/cytoadhesion-like remodelling, SA and Bc28 families) relevant to virulence and immune evasion. It does not show that any factor causes organ dysfunction, haemolysis or coagulopathy, and no host response is quantified. Candidates are detections, not functionally validated pathogenicity factors.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single strain; one RNA-seq sample from a splenectomised dog (parasitaemia not representative of natural disease); three pooled Beagles for proteomics; no replicate numbers stated; no avirulent comparator strain; many candidates hypothetical.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
