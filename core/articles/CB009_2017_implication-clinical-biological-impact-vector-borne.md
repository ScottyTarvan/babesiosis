## Abstract-derived summary

The authors prospectively enrolled 134 client-owned dogs presenting anaemic to a French veterinary teaching hospital in Lyon, aiming to define how vector-borne haemopathogens contribute to anaemia and haemolysis. Each dog was screened by PCR for a haemopathogen panel, plus point-of-care tests for Leishmania and tick-borne antigens (SNAP 4Dx Plus). Pathogen-associated anaemia was found in 23 dogs (17.2%). Babesia PCR was positive in 11, Mycoplasma in 10, Anaplasma platys in 2, Ehrlichia canis in 1 and Leishmania serology in 1; two dogs were co-positive for Babesia and Mycoplasma. Every dog with Mycoplasma alone had neoplasia. These pathogens ranked second among causes of haemolysis (7/23; 30.4%). In multivariable analysis, male sex (OR 2.82) and haemolysis (OR 3.31) were more frequent in the pathogen-associated group. The authors conclude that these pathogens commonly cause anaemia locally.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with associational multivariable comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Vector-borne haemopathogens are a common cause of anaemia in the study region.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Babesia was the most frequent pathogen among pathogen-associated anaemias (11/23), and haemolysis was associated with that group, which fits a haemolytic phenotype. However, the abstract pools Babesia with Mycoplasma, Anaplasma, Ehrlichia and Leishmania, so Babesia-specific haemolysis, severity or mechanism cannot be separated. This is an aetiological/clinicopathological profile of anaemia, not a mechanistic study; whether haemolysis was immune-mediated or parasite-driven is not addressed in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** pooled pathogens; only 11 Babesia-positive dogs; Babesia species not stated; comparator is other anaemic dogs, not healthy controls; two Babesia/Mycoplasma co-infections; single referral centre; haemolysis definition, recruitment period and outcome data not reported in abstract; PCR positivity does not prove causality of the anaemia.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
