## Abstract-derived summary

This short Brazilian review describes canine babesiosis as a disease caused by Babesia parasites, with B. canis and B. gibsoni cited as the species most often diagnosed in the country. It states that Rhipicephalus ticks transmit the organism while feeding, and that the parasite enters erythrocytes and multiplies asexually, producing intravascular haemolysis. Depending on where the parasite localises, the authors say dogs may develop either marked anaemia or cerebral infarction. The stated aim is a concise overview that highlights features of typical and atypical presentations in small-animal practice. No quantitative data, case numbers or diagnostic and treatment details are reported in the abstract, which is also all that the publisher page displays.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Not stated beyond the aim of summarising key points for recognising characteristic and atypical clinical cases.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Offers only a textbook-level haemolysis-versus-cerebral-syndrome framing; the mechanism of cerebral infarction is not explained in the abstract. Reviewer notes that Rhipicephalus-transmitted infections in Brazil are usually attributed to B. vogeli, and that the abstract calls the dog the definitive host (the tick is the definitive host), so taxonomic and biological accuracy is questionable.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** three pages; Crossref lists 0 references and the publisher page shows none; no species confirmation; secondary source; author list differs between Crossref (two) and publisher page (three); non-MEDLINE regional journal, peer-review process not verified.
