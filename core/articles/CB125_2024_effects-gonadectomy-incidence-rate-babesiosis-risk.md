## Abstract-derived summary

Using hospital records from a South African academic veterinary hospital (2013-2020), the authors estimated how gonadectomy affects two outcomes in dogs aged six months or older: the incidence rate of a babesiosis diagnosis and the risk of severe disease once diagnosed. Incidence was addressed with a case-control design using time-matched controls from primary care (811 cases, 3244 controls); severity with a retrospective cohort of 923 diagnosed dogs, including 112 referrals. Both analyses accounted for sex, age, breed category and weight. Gonadectomy was linked to an incidence rate ratio of 0.5 (95% CI 0.41-0.60) and a severe-disease risk ratio of 0.72 (0.60-0.86), robust in a tipping-point sensitivity analysis. Males had higher incidence (IRR 1.74) and a non-significantly higher severity risk (RR 1.12; CI 0.98-1.28). No evidence that sex modified the effect of gonadectomy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (observational, with causal-inference framing)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Gonadectomy has a robust protective effect on both the incidence and severity of babesiosis in male and female dogs aged six months or more.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A candidate host-level factor associated with severity, not a mechanism. Lower incidence may reflect exposure differences (tick contact, roaming) as well as biology, and the abstract offers no data on hormonal effects on immunity, haemostasis or erythrocyte injury. The male severity estimate is not significant.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single referral hospital population; severity definition and species identification not given in abstract; causal interpretation depends on unmeasured-confounding assumptions despite the sensitivity analysis; treatment and timing of presentation not addressed.
