## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe a single dog imported from north-western Spain, an area where Babesia vulpes occurs, and resident in Germany for three years. After splenic torsion and splenectomy, the dog received immunosuppressive treatment for an autoimmune condition linked to haemotropic Mycoplasma infection. During clinical decline, small intra-erythrocytic Babesia were found on a blood smear and molecular testing identified B. vulpes. Laboratory abnormalities comprised anaemia, thrombocytosis, raised liver enzymes and abnormal renal parameters. Atovaquone, proguanil and azithromycin lowered the real-time PCR signal and cleared parasites from the smear, yet the dog worsened and was euthanised. The authors advise screening imported dogs for pathogens and highlight how splenectomy shaped the course of infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Imported dogs should be screened for pathogens, and splenectomy influences the clinical course of B. vulpes infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates a severe phenotype associated with host factors: asplenia plus immunosuppression with a concurrent haemotropic Mycoplasma infection may have reduced splenic clearance and immune control of a small piroplasm, but this single case cannot establish that. It supports, but cannot isolate, the contribution of splenectomy; thrombocytosis (rather than thrombocytopenia) is atypical for severe canine babesiosis and may reflect asplenia. Death despite falling parasite signal suggests host-driven pathology, but this is not tested.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no control; confounded by immunosuppressive drugs and Mycoplasma co-infection; treatment regimen unvalidated; necropsy or histopathology findings not established from abstract.
