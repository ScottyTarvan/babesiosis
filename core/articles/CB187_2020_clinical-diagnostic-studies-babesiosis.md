## Abstract-derived summary

The authors described the clinical picture and blood changes of dogs with babesiosis seen at one private clinic in Kharkiv, Ukraine, during 2019. The Ukrainian abstract reports diagnosis in 102 dogs, with haemoglobinuria (65.7%), icteric mucosae (58.8%) and fever (67.6%) most frequent, and a further 59.8% showing other signs. Blood changes included lower haemoglobin (down 45.3%), sedimentation rate up to 22.43±2.94 mm/h, leukocytes down to 4.15±0.30 thousand/L (as reported) with monocytosis, total bilirubin more than tenfold higher (18.77±3.62 µmol/L) and AST more than threefold higher (112.48±6.73 U/L). The publisher English summary (not in the packet abstract) adds that the erythrocyte decrease was 18.1% against healthy animals, that diagnosis used thin capillary-blood smears with venous blood for biochemistry, that segmented neutrophils fell, that ALT, creatinine and urea were normal, and that Babesia species and pathogenicity need further study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis in these dogs was marked by lower erythrocyte and haemoglobin values, raised ESR, monocytosis with leukopenia, and markedly increased bilirubin and AST.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Consistent with intravascular/extravascular haemolysis (haemoglobinuria, icterus, hyperbilirubinaemia) with possible hepatocellular or muscle contribution to AST rise; ALT, creatinine and urea in the normal range argue against marked hepatic or azotaemic renal injury at the group-mean level. It is a syndrome description, not a mechanistic or prognostic study; no outcome, severity-stratified biomarker analysis or causal inference is offered.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single clinic; species not identified (microscopy only); no stated comparator n or selection of controls; reporting of sampling time relative to treatment unclear; group means only, no individual-dog data in the abstract; co-infections and breed/age effects not addressed; an implausible-looking unit in the Ukrainian abstract (leukocytes "thousand/L") suggests unit-reporting care is needed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
