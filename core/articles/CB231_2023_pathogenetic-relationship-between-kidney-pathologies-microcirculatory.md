## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with acute spontaneous babesiosis, presumed to be Babesia canis, underwent clinical, haematological, biochemical, coagulation, haemodynamic and immunoassay evaluation to relate renal injury to microvascular damage. Affected dogs showed fever, anaemia, haemoglobinuria, systemic inflammation and a prothrombotic state. The coagulogram was read as disseminated intravascular coagulation, and haemodynamics as decompensating shock. Azotaemia with hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperphosphataemia and hyperuricaemia accompanied nephropathy, with chemistry suggesting hepatic and pancreatic involvement. The authors describe two nephropathy stages, haemoglobinuric then oliguric or anuric, and propose that DIC-driven shock underlies glomerulonephritis and acute renal failure leading to death. Group sizes and numeric results are not reported in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (mechanistic interpretation proposed but not tested by the design)
- **Authors' conclusion:** DIC in response to the pathogen causes shock, forming a vicious circle that drives glomerulonephritis and renal failure.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant to the DIC, microcirculation, shock and AKI axis, but the sequence is inferred from laboratory associations. The abstract does not show histology, urinary protein quantification or causal testing, so glomerulonephritis versus pigment or ischaemic tubular injury is not distinguished.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n, controls and recruitment not reported in abstract; species method unknown; co-infections, treatment and outcome data not described; no histopathology evident. Regional journal not indexed in MEDLINE; peer-review policy not verified.
