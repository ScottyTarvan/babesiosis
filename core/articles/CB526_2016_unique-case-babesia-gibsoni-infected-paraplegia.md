## Abstract-derived summary

A four-year-old male Rottweiler was brought to the Teaching Veterinary Clinics of GADVASU because of paraplegia, fever and poor appetite. The dog was dull, with a rectal temperature of 105 °F, a heart rate of 158 per minute and a respiratory rate of 57 per minute. Microscopy of a blood smear and a PCR assay identified Babesia gibsoni. Laboratory results showed raised bilirubin, alanine aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase and creatinine, higher total leucocyte and neutrophil counts, and low blood glucose. Treatment consisted of one dose of diminazene aceturate plus doxycycline, enrofloxacin and metronidazole over 21 days. Follow-up recorded full clinical recovery within those 21 days.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors describe a dog with paraplegia and B. gibsoni infection that recovered completely within 21 days after combined antiprotozoal and antibiotic treatment, and title the report a unique case.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report describes a neurological presentation, paraplegia, in a dog with confirmed B. gibsoni infection, together with hepatic enzyme and bilirubin elevation, raised creatinine and hypoglycaemia. The abstract does not state how the paraplegia arose, so the link between infection and the neurological sign is not established. Recovery after multi-drug therapy cannot show which component, if any, resolved the paraplegia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single animal; no cause of the paraplegia reported in the abstract (no neurological work-up or imaging described); concurrent disease not excluded in the abstract; treatment combined four drugs, so effects cannot be separated; no reference values or controls stated in the abstract
