## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective study reviewed clinical records of 452 dogs in Brazil with a blood-smear diagnosis of Babesia (n = 124) or Hepatozoon (n = 328) infection. Dogs under one year and females were most often affected for both parasites. In the Babesia group, anaemia occurred in 83.87% (37.9% normocytic normochromic), leukopenia in 45.16%, lymphopenia in 44.35%, eosinopenia in 71.77% and thrombocytopenia in 86.03%. In the Hepatozoon group, anaemia was 76.52%, leukocytosis 26.22% and thrombocytopenia 54.27%. Thrombocytopenia was the most frequent reported change in the Babesia group, and anaemia the most frequent in the Hepatozoon group. The authors recommend keeping puppies away from vectors and consider the blood count valuable for diagnosis, treatment and prognosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The complete blood count is important for diagnosing, treating and prognosticating these haemoparasitoses, and vector exposure in puppies should be prevented.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile only. It shows that thrombocytopenia, anaemia, eosinopenia and lymphopenia are frequent in smear-positive dogs, which is consistent with haemolytic and consumptive processes, but does not establish mechanism, severity or prognostic value (no outcome data in the abstract). Prognostic claims are not supported by the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective; no healthy controls; Babesia species not identified and no PCR; smear diagnosis misses low parasitaemia; co-infections and treatment status not described; the reported 86.03% thrombocytopenia does not correspond to a whole-number count of 124 and should be checked against the source.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
