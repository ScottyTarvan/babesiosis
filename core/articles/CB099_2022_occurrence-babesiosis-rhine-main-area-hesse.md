## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective study reviewed dogs tested by Babesia PCR at a veterinary hospital between October 2018 and December 2020 to describe canine babesiosis (tick vector Dermacentor reticulatus) in the Rhine-Main area. Of 697 dogs tested, 81 (12%) were positive; sequencing in 14 identified B. canis in 13 and B. vulpes in one, and two dogs had concurrent anaplasmosis. Cases occurred year-round with peaks in spring and October. Anaemia affected 82%, thrombocytopenia 94%, pancytopenia 54% and hyperbilirubinaemia 90% of those measured. All received imidocarb twice, 14 days apart; follow-up PCR in 37 dogs was negative in 92%. Six dogs (7%) were euthanised for progressive renal disease, severe intravascular haemolysis requiring repeated transfusion, or splenic and renal abscesses.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis should be a differential in dogs with apathy, fever, haemoglobinuria or anaemia, thrombocytopenia or pancytopenia, with testing year-round and especially in spring and autumn.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Describes the clinicopathological syndrome of European B. canis infection (haemolytic anaemia, near-universal thrombocytopenia, hyperbilirubinaemia) and lists terminal complications (renal disease, haemolysis, abscesses). It does not establish mechanisms or prognostic markers; the euthanasia reasons hint at renal and haemolytic severity pathways only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective single-centre design; selection by PCR testing (referral bias); no control group; species confirmed in only 14 of 81 dogs; euthanasia confounds mortality; treatment uniform, so no outcome comparison by therapy.
