## Abstract-derived summary

A young female Rottweiler in India was presented with fever, anorexia, lethargy, nasal bleeding (spelled "epitasis" in the source), congested mucous membranes and lymphadenopathy. Blood smear showed anaemia and B. gibsoni piroplasms (graded ++). Serum biochemistry showed raised urea nitrogen, creatinine and phosphorus, which the authors read as acute renal failure; no numerical values were available on the journal page. Treatment combined intramuscular imidocarb, intravenous fluids to correct acid-base and electrolyte disturbance, and a 14-day oral course of clindamycin, metronidazole and doxycycline. The dog was reported as fully recovered on day 15 with a negative smear. The authors present this combined protocol as effective for B. gibsoni infection complicated by kidney injury.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The triple-drug regimen plus imidocarb and supportive fluids led to complete recovery of a dog with B. gibsoni-associated kidney injury.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents azotaemia with hyperphosphataemia in a dog with B. gibsoni parasitaemia (renal syndrome, consistent with AKI as a complication). It says nothing about mechanism (haemoglobinuric, hypoxic, immune-complex or other injury) and cannot show that the parasite caused the kidney injury, nor that the regimen caused recovery.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species not molecularly confirmed; no numerical laboratory values, urinalysis, urine output, IRIS staging or histology reported on the journal page; co-infections and other AKI causes not excluded; multi-drug treatment confounded with supportive care; no follow-up beyond day 15.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
