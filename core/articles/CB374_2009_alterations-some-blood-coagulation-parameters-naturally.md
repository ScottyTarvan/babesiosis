## Abstract-derived summary

Haemostasis was studied in 30 dogs with natural Babesia canis canis infection and in 29 healthy dogs. Platelet count, thrombin-antithrombin complexes (TAT), prothrombin time, APTT and antithrombin III activity were taken before imidocarb treatment and on days 1 to 3 afterwards. TAT was higher in infected dogs on days 0 and 2 (means 49.7 and 87.7 microg/L versus 7.2 microg/L in controls), whereas AT III activity was lower at every time point. Prothrombin time did not differ. APTT was shorter than in controls on days 2 and 3 (21.3 and 19.2 s versus 30.0 s). Platelet counts were low throughout, with a day 0 mean of 29 x 10^9/L against 259 x 10^9/L in controls. The authors suggested that haemostasis is compromised and that DIC occurs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors suggest that babesiosis compromises primary and secondary haemostasis and that disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) occurs in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Infected dogs showed higher TAT on days 0 and 2, lower AT III, shorter APTT on days 2 and 3 and significant thrombocytopenia than healthy dogs, a pattern consistent with activated coagulation and consumption. The abstract states that no earlier study had examined TAT dynamics in canine babesiosis. The DIC conclusion rests on these laboratory markers; the abstract does not report a formal DIC scoring system or clinical outcome data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; no species-confirmation method stated; no clinical severity or outcome data in the abstract; time-point changes after imidocarb cannot be separated from the natural course of disease; control group is healthy dogs only, with no non-babesial sick dogs.
