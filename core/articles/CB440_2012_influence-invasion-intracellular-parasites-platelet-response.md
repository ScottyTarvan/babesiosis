## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined blood morphology in dogs infected with the intracellular parasites Babesia and Ehrlichia. Parasites were identified in peripheral blood smears stained with the May-Grünwald-Giemsa method. While the parasites were present, platelet counts were lower than expected, and this was accompanied by the formation of platelet clumps. The proportion of unusually large platelets rose at the same time. The abstract does not state the number of dogs, the study groups, the species of Babesia or Ehrlichia involved, or whether results for the two genera are reported separately. The title describes the work as based on clinical cases and concerns platelet behaviour during intracellular parasite infection in dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (small clinical series with healthy controls and paired sampling)
- **Authors' conclusion:** In diseases caused by intracellular parasites, platelet counts fall slightly, with platelet aggregates and giant platelets, which the authors suggest may lead to abnormal clotting and coagulation disorders.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The Babesia sp. dogs can be separated from the Ehrlichia sp. dogs, with per-dog platelet count and mean platelet volume in Table 1 (full text). Thrombocytopenia, platelet aggregates and giant platelets were observed in the Babesia dogs at the first sampling. No coagulation test is described, so the statement about clotting disorders and DIC is the authors' interpretation, not a measured outcome. Timing relative to treatment confounds the second sampling.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=8 per group; Babesia identified to genus on smear and clinical signs only, with no species or molecular identification; the group sizes are given but the recruitment period, inclusion criteria and co-infection screening of the Babesia group are not stated; aggregates and giant platelets were described in qualitative terms on smears, not reported as measured counts; no between-group statistical comparison reported; second sampling followed imidocarb treatment; Table 1 and the text are inconsistent in places (see Information beyond the abstract); the text speaks of anaplasmosis in the Ehrlichia group; the "slight decrease" in the conclusion does not match the reported Babesia means

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - 24 dogs of various breeds and ages were divided into three equal groups: clinically healthy controls with normal haematology and no intracellular parasites in blood, dogs naturally infected with Babesia sp., and dogs naturally infected with Ehrlichia sp. (full text)
  - The Babesia group was diagnosed from clinical signs, laboratory findings (lower red cell counts, haemoglobin and haematocrit, and a significant drop in thrombocyte counts) and trophozoites seen on May-Grünwald-Giemsa smears, in droplet and ameboid forms (full text)
  - Clinical findings in the Babesia group included fever, raised respiratory and pulse rates, jaundice, pallor, apathy and weakness; untreated progression was accompanied by haemoglobinuria, leukopenia and lymphopenia; two dogs had non-regenerative anaemia attributed to bone marrow hypoplasia; five dogs with advanced disease had weakened kidney function and inhibited urination despite treatment (full text)
  - Blood was sampled 2-3 days after clinical signs appeared and again a week later; babesiosis was treated with imidocarb and ehrlichiosis with oxytetracyclines; platelets were counted on an ADVIA 2120i analyser, and groups were compared with Student's t-test (full text)
  - Table 1: at the first collection the Babesia group had a mean MPV of 21.5 fl and mean PLT of 33 x10^3/mm^3, against 9.5 fl and 306 x10^3/mm^3 in controls (full text)
  - The text reports that PLT rose significantly (P<=0.01) and MPV fell non-significantly at the second sampling in the Babesia group, and that platelet counts did not reach the reference values (full text)
  - Mild babesiosis showed many giant platelets with an insignificant drop in counts; ameboid forms came with few aggregates; abundant trophozoites came with more aggregates and a lower count (full text)
  - In the Ehrlichia group PLT was above the reference range at first sampling (mean 495) and fell significantly at the second (mean 84, P<=0.01), with MPV rising (mean 19.0, P<=0.01) (full text)
  - Table 1 inconsistencies noted on checking: six of the eight second-collection PLT values for the Babesia group are identical to the control group's second-collection values, and the printed Babesia second-collection mean (307) equals the control mean; the asterisk for P<=0.01 appears only on the Ehrlichia mean although the text reports a significant PLT rise in the Babesia group; several other printed second-collection means (control and Babesia MPV) do not match the listed values (full text; reviewer check)
  - The authors state that fewer platelets reduce coagulation efficiency and may contribute to DIC; no coagulation test is described in the Methods or Results (full text)
