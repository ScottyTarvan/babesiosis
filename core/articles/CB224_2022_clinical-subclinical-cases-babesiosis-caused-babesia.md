## Abstract-derived summary

This Korean study screened 400 blood samples from dogs with a presumptive tick-borne disease diagnosis, using 18S rRNA PCR for Babesia and a rapid antibody test. Babesia DNA was found in 36 dogs (9.0%), but only 24 (6.0%) reacted serologically. Acute, chronic and subclinical courses were all represented, acute disease being most frequent. Acute cases usually showed reduced appetite, lethargy, fever and gastrointestinal signs, occasionally haematuria, together with thrombocytopenia and anaemia, and less often raised bilirubin, low albumin and increased liver enzymes. The authors concluded that B. gibsoni is endemic in Korea and causes varied presentations, and that diagnosis depends on history, haematology, biochemistry and a reliable PCR assay.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni is an endemic haemoparasite in Korea with diverse clinical manifestations, requiring combined clinical, laboratory and PCR assessment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a molecularly confirmed clinicopathological profile (frequent thrombocytopenia and anaemia, less frequent cholestatic or hepatocellular changes) that is compatible with a haemolytic syndrome with thrombocytopenia; platelet consumption is not measured. It is a syndrome description only; no mechanism (immune-mediated haemolysis, coagulation, oxidative injury) and no severity or outcome analysis is reported.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** referral population with presumptive tick-borne disease, so 9.0% is not population prevalence; no uninfected controls; only 4 subclinical dogs; CBC and chemistry available for a subset (about 26 dogs); co-infections and prior treatment not described in the abstract; PCR-positive but seronegative dogs suggest timing or assay effects.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
