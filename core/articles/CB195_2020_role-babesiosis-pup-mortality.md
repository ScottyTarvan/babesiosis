## Abstract-derived summary

Babesiosis is considered potentially fatal in dogs, particularly in young pups. The authors necropsied and examined pups from a series of 77 pup deaths, and described their clinical and pathological findings. Babesiosis was found in three of them (3.9%): crossbred, Labrador Retriever and Rottweiler males aged 40, 20 and 45 days. All showed anorexia, dullness and depression, with anaemic changes, and piroplasms were seen in heart-blood and spleen impression smears. Post-mortem findings included catarrhal gastroenteritis, hepatitis, pulmonary congestion with oedema and haemorrhage, thymic lymphoid cell depletion and lymphadenitis. Lymph node phagocytes had engulfed parasitised erythrocytes, parasitised cells lay in cerebral capillaries, and the brain showed spongiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Neonatal immaturity and poor thermoregulation predispose pups to infection, and babesiosis is a major contributor to pup mortality (stated in the full text; the abstract gives no conclusion).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a tissue-level description of fatal neonatal babesiosis: multi-organ lesions (hepatocellular necrosis with bile stasis, proximal tubular necrosis, myocardial degeneration, pulmonary oedema, cerebral congestion with parasitised capillary erythrocytes) fit the multisystem picture, and capillary parasite sequestration is relevant to cerebral involvement. It does not establish which lesions are babesia-driven, because co-pathology confounds attribution.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** three cases; no controls; species not identified; helminth co-infection in one pup and bacterial culture positivity in two of three; haematology and biochemistry listed in methods but no values reported; study period not stated; non-specialist journal (chemistry title), special issue, ~5 weeks from receipt to acceptance, publisher AkiNik; peer-review rigour unverified.
