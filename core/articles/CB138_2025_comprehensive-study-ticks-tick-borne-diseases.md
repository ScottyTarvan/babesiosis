## Abstract-derived summary

The authors surveyed 341 dogs, mostly strays (full text), from Kathmandu, Pokhara and Chitwan in Nepal, combining haematology, conventional PCR for tick-borne pathogens and morphological typing of 219 ticks removed from the dogs. Babesia spp. were the commonest agent (26.09% of dogs), ahead of E. canis (5.87%), H. canis (3.52%) and A. platys (2.93%); 31.09% carried at least one pathogen. Babesia positivity was significantly associated with anaemia (p < 0.01) and thrombocytopenia (p < 0.05), whereas E. canis was linked to thrombocytopenia and H. canis to lymphocytosis. Rhipicephalus predominated among ticks, with 27.86% of dogs infested. Multiple logistic regression flagged geographic site, stray versus pet status and body condition score as risk factors for infection. The authors advocate stronger treatment and prevention.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathological profile)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia spp. was the most prevalent canine tick-borne pathogen, associated with anaemia and thrombocytopenia, and robust treatment and control measures are warranted.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports the usual haematological phenotype of canine babesiosis (anaemia plus thrombocytopenia) in a naturally infected, stray-dominated population, but is a prevalence-led survey. It gives no mechanism, severity grading or outcome. Associations are pathogen-level, not causal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Babesia not sequenced, so species unknown (B. gibsoni versus large forms matters for pathophysiology); co-infections (E. canis, H. canis, A. platys) were tabulated but not statistically adjusted; no clinical severity data; single time point; text and table percentages for low haematocrit/platelets appear inconsistent (automated read; verify against the article).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
