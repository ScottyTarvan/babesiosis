## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with anorexia, fever, lethargy, pallor, lymphadenopathy, ascites, vomiting, diarrhoea, icterus or haematuria were screened for B. gibsoni by smear and PCR. Eighteen infected dogs were then tested for immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) by saline agglutination, smear spherocytosis and Coombs test. All received 21 days of doxycycline, metronidazole and clindamycin. Group I had no further drugs, Group II added tapering prednisolone, and Group III added tapering azathioprine plus prednisolone. Every regimen was judged effective. The authors rated antibiotics plus prednisolone best, because all its dogs were Coombs-negative afterwards and showed no abnormal biochemical rise. The abstract gives no group sizes, test counts or statistics.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (treatment comparison with baseline clinicopathology)
- **Authors' conclusion:** All regimens worked, but antibiotics with prednisolone was the most efficacious.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Principally a treatment study, retained because it documents Coombs-positive, spherocytic, agglutinating IMHA in PCR-confirmed B. gibsoni infection, with severe anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukocytosis, azotaemia, raised ALT/ALP and hypoalbuminaemia. Haematological recovery in the antibiotic-only arm suggests parasite control may suffice for resolution in some dogs; the design cannot separate parasite clearance from immunosuppression. It does not establish mechanism or causality.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=6 per group; randomisation method and blinding not described; no placebo arm; shared 6-dog control group; Coombs conversion counts for Groups I and III not stated; Group III showed rising ALT and ALP after therapy, possibly azathioprine hepatotoxicity, confounding "no abnormal rise" claims; table, unit and citation errors (including unrelated references, so reference accuracy is doubtful). Journal peer review rigour unverified (special issue supplement).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
