## Abstract-derived summary

This Turkish case series reports three naturally infected dogs in which Babesia canis canis was confirmed by Giemsa-stained blood smears and PCR. The authors describe the clinical, haematological and biochemical picture in these dogs, and the illness ranged from mild to severe. Across the group, the findings included haemolytic anaemia, jaundice, haemoglobinuria, low platelet counts, liver damage and myositis. One dog also had cholecystitis, and another had renal damage. One dog died. The title frames the report as the first clinical and parasitological detection of this subspecies in dogs in Turkey. The report is descriptive, with three dogs and no control group. Treatment details are not given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (case series)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis canis was identified clinically and parasitologically in dogs in Turkey for the first time.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report documents a spectrum from mild to fatal disease in three dogs, with haemolysis, thrombocytopenia, hepatic and muscular injury, and in single dogs cholecystitis and renal damage. It is useful as descriptive evidence of multisystem complications. With three dogs and no comparison group, it cannot show how common these complications are or what drove the fatal outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** three dogs only; no controls; no numerical values available in the abstract; treatment details and the cause of death not reported in the abstract; full text not read.
