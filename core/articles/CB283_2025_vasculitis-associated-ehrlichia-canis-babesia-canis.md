## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a dog in the Netherlands that developed abrupt, severe and generalised vasculitis while simultaneously infected with Ehrlichia canis and Babesia canis. After a diagnostic work-up, the clinicians favoured an immune-mediated vasculitis, which they thought might have been provoked by either organism alone or by the two infections together. The lesions resolved completely with a combined regimen of prednisolone, ciclosporin, pentoxifylline, doxycycline and imidocarb dipropionate. Because the abstract gives no signalment, laboratory values, diagnostic methods, histopathology or follow-up duration, the strength of the evidence for each infection, and for the immune-mediated label, cannot be judged from it. The report is therefore best read as a hypothesis-generating description of one patient rather than as evidence about the frequency or mechanism of this syndrome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The dog was suspected to have immune-mediated vasculitis, possibly triggered by E. canis, B. canis or both, which resolved after multimodal treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Reports a distinct vascular syndrome in B. canis co-infection, relevant to endothelial injury and immune-mediated pathology as a hypothesis. It does not show that B. canis (rather than E. canis, which is itself linked to vasculitis) caused the lesions, and combined immunosuppressive and antimicrobial therapy prevents attributing recovery to any single mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; co-infection confounds attribution; "suspected" immune-mediated aetiology without a stated mechanistic test; simultaneous multi-drug treatment; follow-up and histology details not available from the abstract; no controls.
