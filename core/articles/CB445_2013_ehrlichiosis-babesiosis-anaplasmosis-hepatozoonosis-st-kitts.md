## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers tested blood from 372 dogs on the Caribbean island of St Kitts for Ehrlichia, Babesia, Anaplasma and Hepatozoon using PCR, and tested for antibodies to E. canis and Anaplasma with commercial immunochromatography tests. Clinical and laboratory data were reviewed retrospectively. Babesia DNA was found in 24% of dogs (90/372), mostly B. canis vogeli (43 dogs) and B. gibsoni (36 dogs), and nine dogs carried a Babesia that could not be identified. Dual infections with E. canis and either Babesia species were the most common multiple infections. Of 67 Babesia-positive dogs with complete records, 4 showed clinical signs and 9 had laboratory abnormalities. Dogs with A. platys or H. canis were also largely apparently healthy. The authors conclude that dogs in the Caribbean are commonly infected with tick-borne pathogens, and that most have no clinical signs or laboratory abnormalities.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs in the Caribbean are commonly infected with tick-borne pathogens, and most have no clinical signs or laboratory abnormalities.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The Babesia-specific host data are small descriptive subsets: dogs with a single Babesia infection and full records (12 with B. canis vogeli and 12 with B. gibsoni, full text) were mostly healthy, with occasional anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia or pancytopenia (full text). Because most dogs were sampled at routine health checks (full text), the findings describe subclinical carriage and cannot show which laboratory changes were associated with infection. The authors attribute the laboratory abnormalities seen in dogs co-infected with E. canis to that infection (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** convenience sample of dogs; no clinical data for the community clinic dogs; full records for only part of the hospital dogs; no uninfected comparison group or statistical comparison of laboratory values by Babesia status; very small subgroups; species identification from a short amplicon; no serology for Babesia; retrospective record review; the paper is mainly a prevalence study.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Blood was collected between December 2009 and November 2011 from 372 dogs: 93 from the VIDA community clinics and 279 from the Ross University teaching hospital; Babesia PCR was run on all 372 (full text).
  - No Babesia serology was performed (full text).
  - Full clinical, CBC and biochemistry data were available for 159 of the 279 teaching-hospital dogs, and none for the VIDA dogs (full text).
  - Babesia was identified by FRET-PCR melting temperature and confirmed by partial 18S rRNA sequencing (221 nucleotides) in 27 dogs (B. canis vogeli n = 12; B. gibsoni n = 15); two dogs carried both species (full text).
  - Of 25 dogs with B. canis vogeli only, 12 had full data; 4 were unwell, one of them suspected of tick-borne disease with pancytopenia, and 3 of the 8 routine-check dogs had a single abnormality (leukopenia, anaemia or thrombocytopenia) (full text).
  - Of 23 dogs with B. gibsoni only, 12 had full data; 3 were ill without signs of tick-borne disease, and 2 of the 9 routine-check dogs had mild thrombocytopenia or mild anaemia (full text).
  - No Babesia organisms were reported on the CBCs of the single-infection B. canis vogeli or B. gibsoni dogs (full text).
  - Among dogs co-infected with B. canis vogeli and E. canis, both dogs with full data were suspected of tick-borne disease and had pancytopenia; among dogs co-infected with B. gibsoni and E. canis, 3 of the 5 dogs with full data had laboratory abnormalities (full text). The authors attribute the abnormalities seen in dogs coinfected with E. canis to that infection (full text).
  - Pit bulls and their crosses made up 4% (2/46) of the dogs infected with B. gibsoni in the Discussion (full text); the Results give 2 of 36 B. gibsoni dogs with breed data, so the denominator differs between sections (full text).
  - Table 1 gives Babesia spp. as 22% (90/372), while the abstract and text give 24% (full text).
