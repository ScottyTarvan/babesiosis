## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs (n=152) seen at a private practice in Varkala, Kerala, were screened by Giemsa-stained blood smear for small Babesia. Microscopic prevalence was 15.13% (23/152), morphologically consistent with B. gibsoni. Age, pyrexia, tick infestation and haemoglobinuria were associated with infection (p<0.05), whereas sex, breed, anorexia, pallor, dyspnoea and epistaxis were not. Compared with a clinically healthy, non-infected group, infected dogs had lower erythrocyte count, haemoglobin, haematocrit, MCH, eosinophils, platelets, albumin and albumin:globulin ratio, and higher leukocyte, neutrophil and monocyte counts, AST, ALT, bilirubin, urea nitrogen, total protein and globulin. The authors suggest integrated clinical, haematological and biochemical assessment may aid diagnosis and prognosis, and note that microscopy alone may underestimate prevalence.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with associational risk-factor analysis)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Small-form canine babesiosis occurs in the study population, and combined clinico-haemato-biochemical assessment may have diagnostic and prognostic value.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds a clinicopathological profile of presumptive B. gibsoni infection: anaemia, thrombocytopaenia, neutrophilia/monocytosis, eosinopaenia, hypoalbuminaemia with hyperglobulinaemia, and raised hepatic enzymes, bilirubin and urea nitrogen. These are syndrome-level associations; they do not separate haemolysis from hepatic or renal injury or show mechanism. No outcome data are in the abstract, so the prognostic claim is untested. The prevalence component is outside pathophysiology scope.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** microscopy-only diagnosis with presumptive species; control group size not stated in the text; no testing for co-infections described; clinic-attending dogs versus healthy controls (selection bias); unpaired t-tests across many variables without stated multiplicity correction; ethics limited to owner consent with no committee approval number; creatinine and ALP differences non-significant.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
