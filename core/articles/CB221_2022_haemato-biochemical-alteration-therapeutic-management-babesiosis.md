## Abstract-derived summary

A short communication describes one adult female Spitz presented with five days of inappetence, tick infestation, fever (103.4 °F), tachycardia, pale mucous membranes, dehydration and lymph node enlargement. Small pyriform intra-erythrocytic parasites on a blood smear were taken as B. gibsoni. Haematology showed anaemia, and serum chemistry showed raised urea nitrogen, creatinine and ALT with low total protein (full text), leading the authors to diagnose babesiosis with acute renal failure. The dog received fluids, furosemide, antiemetic and gastric protection (full text), plus 14 days of doxycycline, clindamycin and metronidazole, ivermectin and a haematinic. A repeat smear after two weeks was negative and the blood profile had improved. The authors present the regimen as fully effective (full text).

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia and thrombocytopenia with biochemical complications dominate canine babesiosis, and the triple-drug regimen with supportive care was judged 100% effective.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single dog with marked azotaemia (creatinine reported as 11.84 mg/dL) on a background of haemolytic anaemia. It illustrates the renal-injury syndrome associated with B. gibsoni but offers no mechanistic data (no urinalysis, histology or haemodynamics), so pigment nephropathy, hypoxic or ischaemic injury and pre-renal causes cannot be separated. Elevated ALT is attributed by the authors to hepatic hypoxia, without supporting data. The treatment claim is not supportable from one animal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species by morphology only; no co-infection screening reported; no urinalysis, urine output or follow-up renal values in the text; pre-treatment only partial data table; the text claims thrombocytopenia although the tabulated platelet count (234 x10^3/uL) lies within the authors' own reference range; MCHC (21.15) is inconsistent with the reported Hb and PCV; confounded by concurrent supportive therapy; no stated peer-review model beyond handling-editor information, and the journal's peer-review practice was not assessed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
