## Abstract-derived summary
Babesiosis is caused by parasites living inside red cells, and its range of neurological effects, underlying mechanisms and risk factors are poorly characterised. The authors reviewed records of adults admitted to Yale-New Haven Hospital with laboratory-confirmed babesiosis between January 2011 and October 2021. Of 163 patients, more than half had at least one neurological symptom during admission, most often headache, confusion or delirium, and reduced consciousness. These symptoms were associated with high-grade parasitaemia, renal failure and diabetes mellitus. The authors advise clinicians in endemic regions to recognise the wide symptom spectrum, including neurological features. The abstract reports frequencies and associations only, with no imaging, sequestration or barrier data.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** descriptive retrospective cohort; associational, non-mechanistic
- **Authors' conclusion:** Clinicians in endemic areas should recognise the range of babesiosis symptoms, including neurological ones.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Shared pathway is neurological involvement alongside high parasite burden and renal failure. Canine studies: CB025 (B. rossi, azotaemia associated with cerebral babesiosis and collapse) and CB141 (parasitaemia tracked haematological severity, not neurological signs). Rated speculative: both links are associational, neither isolates a brain mechanism, and only the human study links parasitaemia to neurological symptoms. Differences: adult, comorbid, hospitalised humans (diabetes) versus dogs with a clinically defined 'cerebral babesiosis'; uraemia and metabolic disturbance may explain encephalopathy.
- **Key limitations:** Single centre, retrospective and confounded by overall severity; species not stated in abstract; shares its 163-patient cohort with HU024, so the two are not independent.
