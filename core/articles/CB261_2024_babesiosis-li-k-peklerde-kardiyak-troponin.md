## Abstract-derived summary

Cardiac biomarkers help detect myocardial injury and judge prognosis in dogs, but data from canine babesiosis are sparse. At a Turkish university animal hospital, 15 dogs aged 3-4 years with PCR-confirmed Babesia canis infection were compared with 10 healthy dogs of the same age band. Cephalic-vein serum was stored at -20 °C and assayed within 30 days for high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I and T. Infected dogs had higher concentrations of both markers than controls (hs-cTnI p<0.01; hs-cTnT p<0.05), although absolute values are not given in the abstract. The authors read this as suggesting that B. canis may damage the myocardium, and they call for work on troponins as prognostic and treatment-monitoring tools.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis infection probably causes myocardial damage, and further research is needed on troponin use for prognosis and monitoring.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows higher circulating troponins in infected dogs, consistent with myocardial injury, but troponin elevation is not organ-specific evidence of cause: hypoxia from anaemia, renal dysfunction, systemic inflammation and sepsis-like states can raise it. It is a biomarker association, not a demonstration of babesial myocarditis or of prognostic value.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n (15 vs 10); single time point; no concentrations or effect sizes in the abstract; no echocardiography, ECG or histology reported in the abstract; severity, outcome, anaemia, renal function and co-infections not addressed; treatment status at sampling unknown; recruitment period not stated; PCR method not described; Turkish-language report in a regional journal (peer-review policy not verified).
