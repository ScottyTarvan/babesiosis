## Abstract-derived summary

Serum protein electrophoresis was performed in 37 dogs naturally infected with B. canis and showing compatible signs, and in 25 healthy dogs. Infected dogs were divided into those with normal and those with reduced red cell values. Total protein, albumin and the albumin/globulin ratio were lower in infected dogs than in controls (P < 0.001). Alpha-1, beta-1 and beta-2 fractions were higher (P < 0.01), alpha-2 and gamma were numerically higher, and six dogs showed a split alpha-2 zone. In the reduced-red-cell subgroup, albumin and the A/G ratio were lower (P < 0.05), while alpha-1 and beta-1 fractions were higher (alpha-1 P < 0.05; beta-1 P < 0.01). The authors propose electrophoresis for assessing pathophysiological change and severity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Marked electrophoretic changes support its use for evaluating disease pathophysiology and severity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Pattern is compatible with a negative acute-phase albumin response plus positive acute-phase alpha/beta globulins, and anaemia severity tracks it. Proteinuria, hepatic synthesis and dehydration were not measured, so the mechanisms discussed are speculative. Albumin and A/G are severity-associated markers, not causal evidence.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single clinic; smear-only diagnosis, no co-infection testing; 9/37 sera haemolysed (can distort fractions); control age not given; unadjusted multiple t-tests; severity defined by RBC count only; outcome not analysed against fractions.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
