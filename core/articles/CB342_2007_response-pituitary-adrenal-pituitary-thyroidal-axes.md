## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective cross-sectional interventional study examined pituitary-adrenal and pituitary-thyroid hormones in naturally infected dogs with Babesia canis rossi babesiosis, relating them to blood glucose. Thirty-six dogs were sampled at admission before treatment for ACTH, cortisol, thyroxine, free thyroxine and TSH, then given tetracosactrin and sampled again an hour later for cortisol. Twelve dogs each fell into low, normal and high glucose groups. Dogs with low glucose had higher cortisol before and after stimulation, and lower body temperature, thyroxine and free thyroxine. Haematocrit was lower in both the low- and high-glucose groups than in the normal-glucose group. The authors concluded that low glucose was associated with raised cortisol and reduced thyroxine concentrations in these dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Low blood glucose was associated with high basal and post-ACTH cortisol and with low thyroxine and free thyroxine in dogs with B. canis rossi babesiosis. In the discussion, they judged that the pituitary-adrenal axis responded appropriately, so adrenal failure did not appear to explain the hypoglycaemia (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The data describe an endocrine profile that accompanies hypoglycaemia in infected dogs: preserved, even raised, adrenal responsiveness and a low-thyroxine pattern that the authors liken to non-thyroidal illness. Cross-sectional sampling at one time point cannot show whether the hormone changes preceded or followed the glucose changes. The hypoglycaemic dogs were also more often collapsed and hypothermic, so the hormone differences may reflect general illness severity as well as glycaemic state.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** 12 dogs per group; no healthy or uninfected control group; survivors only, so the most severe cases that died were excluded; single admission time point; median age and weight differed between groups (Table 1), but the between-group tests were not significant (P = 0.12 and P = 0.15; full text); species attribution rests on smear microscopy with PCR-RLB used mainly as an exclusion screen; single hospital.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
