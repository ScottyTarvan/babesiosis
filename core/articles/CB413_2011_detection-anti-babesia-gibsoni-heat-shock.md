## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined whether dogs infected with Babesia gibsoni make antibodies to a 70-kDa heat shock protein of the parasite and to its canine counterpart on red cells. Sera from acutely and chronically infected dogs contained antibodies recognising 70-kDa proteins from both the parasite and canine erythrocytes, and these reacted with recombinant versions of the two proteins. Antibody amounts rose after infection. Rabbits immunised with the recombinant parasite protein produced antibodies that also bound the canine protein, consistent with the high sequence similarity (70.8%). Immunofluorescence did not detect the canine protein on the erythrocyte surface, whereas only free parasites outside red cells were stained by the anti-parasite antibody. The authors concluded that the parasite protein is strongly immunogenic in canine infection and that free parasites may be antibody targets.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The B. gibsoni Hsp70 is a highly immunogenic protein in canine infection and could induce antibodies that react with both the parasite and canine Hsp70 (full text). The authors state that these antibodies could not enhance haemolytic anaemia (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Antibody reactivity to parasite and canine Hsp70 increased in experimentally infected dogs, and rabbit antiserum showed that cross-reactivity is possible. Canine Hsp70 was not detected on the red cell surface and rabbit antibody did not significantly change haemolysis in vitro (full text), so the findings do not support a role for these antibodies as anti-erythrocyte autoantibodies. They do not rule out other immune effects in naturally infected dogs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only seven infected dogs (full text), with no separate uninfected control group (pre-infection sera were the comparison); semi-quantitative immunoblot densitometry; only day-16 sera analysed for acute dogs, although sera were collected daily to day 30 (full text); chronic dogs were splenectomised; the haemolysis assay used rabbit antibody on cultured cells (n = 3; full text), not dog antibody; a laboratory-adapted strain; no clinical or haematological outcome data; the Discussion mentions antibodies in four dogs while the Results cover seven (full text).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Two chronically infected adult male dogs (inoculated with 1 x 10^9 parasites and splenectomised years earlier, with mild anaemia) and five acutely infected adult dogs (two male, three female) were used; all were mongrels (full text)
  - The acute dogs were inoculated with 1 x 10^9 parasites from one of the chronic dogs, parasitaemia was followed by daily blood smear, and sera from day 16 after inoculation were analysed (full text)
  - The strain came from a naturally infected dog in Nagasaki, Japan, in 1973 and is kept in dogs and in vitro culture (full text)
  - Canine Hsp70 cloned from reticulocytes was 2,027 bp and encoded 642 amino acids, with 67.4% nucleotide and 70.8% amino acid identity to BgHsp70; identity was 76.5% over the first 529 residues and 44.8% in the 113-residue C-terminal domain (full text)
  - Anti-rBgHsp70 antibody band intensity was higher in post-infection than pre-infection sera from all seven dogs (P < 0.001), and anti-rcHsp70 intensity was also higher (P < 0.05); some pre-infection sera already had reactivity (full text)
  - Extracellular haemoglobin of infected and uninfected red cell suspensions did not differ significantly after 1 h with 1.2 mg/mL rabbit anti-rBgHsp70 antibody, compared with no antibody or an irrelevant antibody (n = 3) (full text)
  - Serum from a dog with idiopathic immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia stained some canine red cell surface antigens in the immunofluorescence positive control (full text)
