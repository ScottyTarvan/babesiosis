## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs presenting to a South Indian teaching hospital with thrombocytopenia and spontaneous bleeding were evaluated, roughly 180 in total, to see whether automated platelet indices help distinguish ehrlichiosis, babesiosis and leptospirosis. Mean platelet volume, platelet distribution width and plateletcrit were measured alongside smear assessment of platelet size, shape and activation, and compared with healthy controls. Indices did not discriminate ehrlichiosis or leptospirosis. In babesiosis, mean platelet volume was raised, which the author proposes as a screening aid. The paper argues that platelet indices, like red cell indices, merit attention in thrombocytopenic dogs. No effect sizes are given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Platelet indices have no diagnostic role for ehrlichiosis or leptospirosis, but increased MPV in babesiosis can serve as a screening approach.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A raised MPV is compatible with accelerated platelet turnover or larger, younger platelets in the thrombocytopenia of babesiosis, but this study does not establish a mechanism or platelet activation state. It is mainly a diagnostic-indices study with limited pathophysiological content.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** infection confirmation and Babesia species not described; co-infection not excluded; no numeric MPV or p-values in the text read; the MPV rise was in the severe group per the publisher page; inconsistent PCT statements between Results and Discussion; single author; submission to publication about 5 weeks and review process undescribed, so peer-review rigour is uncertain.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
