## Abstract-derived summary

This single case report describes a young male Labrador in which babesiosis was accompanied by chronic renal failure, an unusual complication. Renal dysfunction was identified from the clinical picture, serum biochemical markers and ultrasonography. Management combined chemotherapy, blood transfusion, haemodialysis and dietary modification. Biochemical indices initially improved for a short period and then plateaued in a refractory phase. The author states that the dog's quality of life and survival were extended by this multimodal approach, but it ultimately died from multiple organ failure. The abstract gives no numerical laboratory values, no parasite species, no confirmatory parasitological or molecular test, and no dialysis schedule or drug details.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The author concludes that holistic management (chemotherapy, transfusion, haemodialysis, diet) improved quality of life and longevity despite eventual multiple organ failure.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates babesiosis co-occurring with persistent renal failure and multi-organ failure in one dog, a severity/outcome observation within the REN and SEV domains. It cannot show whether the renal lesion was babesiosis-induced (for example, hypoxic or pigment-related tubular injury, immune-complex glomerular disease) or pre-existing or coincidental. Chronicity (IRIS-type staging, duration, histology) is not established from the abstract. It is management-focused and contributes no mechanistic data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; no controls; causal link between Babesia and chronic renal disease not demonstrated; Babesia species and diagnostic confirmation unreported; co-infections not addressed; treatment confounding; full text not accessible; peer-review process of the journal not stated on the publisher page.
