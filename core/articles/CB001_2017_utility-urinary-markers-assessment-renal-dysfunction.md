## Abstract-derived summary

Acute kidney injury is a frequent complication of canine babesiosis, but the site of nephron damage is poorly defined. The authors compared urinary markers that map to different nephron segments: immunoglobulin G for glomerular leakage, retinol-binding protein for proximal tubular dysfunction and Tamm-Horsfall protein (uromodulin) for distal tubular dysfunction. Concentrations were measured by commercial ELISA in 10 dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis and 10 healthy controls. All three markers were higher in every infected dog than in controls (numerical values and statistics are not given in the abstract). The authors infer combined glomerular and tubular injury and advise histopathology to confirm the extent and nature of kidney damage.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that the findings improve understanding of pathogenesis, indicating glomerular and tubular damage, while noting that histopathology is needed to define its extent and nature.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a multi-segment renal lesion (glomerular plus proximal and distal tubular) as a feature of B. canis infection and is a segment-localising biomarker study rather than a mechanistic one. It does not show cause, timing, reversibility or whether the markers predict azotaemia or outcome. Urinary protein markers can rise from functional overflow, haemoglobinuria or inflammation without structural injury, so "damage" is an inference.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Very small n (10 vs 10); no effect sizes, p values or ranges in abstract; species and infection confirmation method unstated; no control for azotaemia status, parasitaemia, co-infection, hydration or prior treatment; urine normalisation (e.g. to creatinine) not reported; no histopathology; single sampling point assumed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
