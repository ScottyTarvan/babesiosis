## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether the raised MCHC seen in common canine inflammatory diseases is accompanied by greater red cell osmotic fragility. They measured fragility with a saline dilution test (mean corpuscular fragility, MCF) in 73 diseased dogs with babesiosis, dirofilariosis, parvovirosis, pyometra or distemper, and in 8 healthy dogs, and related it to haematology, biochemistry and lipoprotein fractions. MCF was higher than in controls in dirofilariosis, pyometra and distemper, but not in babesiosis or parvovirosis, while MCHC was raised in every diseased group. HDL was lower in babesiosis, pyometra and distemper. Fragility did not correlate with MCHC or lipoproteins; within groups it tracked disease-typical variables, such as ALP in babesiosis (r=0.415, P=0.035). The authors concluded that MCHC and fragility are governed by different mechanisms.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Elevated MCHC indicates haemolysis in all infected groups, yet fragility rose only in some diseases, implying distinct underlying mechanisms.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In babesiosis the study shows no increase in osmotic fragility despite anaemia, severe thrombocytopenia, raised AST and ALP, and low HDL. This argues against a generalised membrane-fragility defect in the (non-immune-mediated) cases sampled, but does not exclude other haemolytic mechanisms. The ALP correlation is a weak association, not evidence of cholestasis-driven fragility.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small groups (8 controls, 6 pyometra); babesiosis dogs were young (mean 23 months) versus controls (83 months) and age affected fragility in healthy dogs; dogs with reticulocytosis or positive agglutination were excluded, a selection bias against immune-mediated haemolysis; species not molecularly confirmed; many correlations tested; babesiosis N differs between text (29) and table (27); timing within disease course unreported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
