## Abstract-derived summary

This report describes difficulties in diagnosing and managing B. gibsoni infection in a single dog. The animal presented with sudden anorexia and weakness, and had severe anaemia and thrombocytopenia. Atovaquone plus azithromycin produced early improvement. Later, the dog developed disseminated intravascular coagulation, non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema and fluid in more than one body cavity. Blood and plasma transfusions were needed to control the anaemia and coagulopathy. A further, novel antiprotozoal drug was then given, leading to full recovery and loss of detectable parasite DNA on PCR. The authors state that this combination of complications had not previously been documented in B. gibsoni infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni infection can progress to a spectrum of serious complications, including DIC, non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema and bicavitary effusion, that the authors believe are novel for this species.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A hypothesis-generating syndrome description pointing to a severe multisystem phenotype (coagulopathy, increased pulmonary vascular permeability, effusions) in a small-form Babesia infection. It cannot establish mechanism, frequency or causation. Complications could reflect parasite-driven inflammation or endothelial injury, but also transfusion, fluids or drug effects.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** One animal; no comparison group. Co-infection testing, treatment confounding and the degree of immune-mediated component are not reported in the abstract. The "novel therapy" cannot be linked to recovery with any confidence.
