## Abstract-derived summary

Investigators described infectious diseases in 269 pit bull-type dogs seized during a multi-state dogfighting investigation, to inform medical protocols for rescued animals. At intake the dogs underwent packed cell volume measurement, serology and PCR for vector-borne agents, and faecal examination. Fleas were common while ticks were seldom seen. Babesia gibsoni was the most frequent infection, found in 39% of dogs, followed by two haemotropic mycoplasmas at 32% and 30%, heartworm at 12% and Ancylostoma at 23%. Anaemia was linked with B. gibsoni infection, present in 63% of infected dogs (odds ratio 2.5), whereas it was not linked with haemotropic mycoplasmas or Ancylostoma. The authors recommend broad-spectrum parasite control, anaemia monitoring, screening and treatment programmes, and exclusion of former fighting dogs as blood donors.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs from dogfighting cases should receive empirical broad-spectrum parasite control and be monitored for anaemia, and should be prepared for mass screening and treatment of B. gibsoni and heartworm. Former fighting dogs should not be used as blood donors.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study is mainly a prevalence survey, but it adds host haematology: anaemia was associated with B. gibsoni infection, with an odds ratio of 2.5. The association fits haemolytic or erythrocyte-injury pathophysiology, but the design cannot show that the infection caused the anaemia. Haemotropic mycoplasmas and other parasites were also found in this population; the abstract does not report how often they occurred together with B. gibsoni, and anaemia was not associated with those agents.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; cross-sectional design; no PCV cut-off, adjustment for co-infection or per-dog haematology reported in the abstract; unusual population (seized fighting dogs) limits generalisation; institution and recruitment period not stated in the abstract.
