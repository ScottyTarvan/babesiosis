## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective study described the clinical and laboratory picture of Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs, the most common arthropod-borne infection affecting dogs in Hong Kong. Records of 108 PCR-positive dogs seen at an emergency and specialty hospital between 2012 and 2022, each with a complete blood count and serum biochemistry, were analysed alongside urinalysis and coagulation data where available. Most dogs had moderate to marked thrombocytopenia and anaemia. Other frequent findings were regenerative anaemia with altered red cell indices, monocytosis, eosinopenia, prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time, hyperglobulinaemia, raised ALP and GGT, proteinuria and bilirubinuria. The authors propose these patterns help clinicians decide when to test for B. gibsoni by molecular methods and begin specific treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The clinicopathological pattern should prompt consideration of B. gibsoni and molecular testing so that specific therapy can start.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a large single-centre phenotype of B. gibsoni infection: regenerative anaemia, marked thrombocytopenia, prolonged APTT with normal PT (suggesting intrinsic-pathway or consumptive change, tested in few dogs), cholestatic enzyme and bilirubin changes, and proteinuria. It is a syndrome description, not a mechanistic or prognostic study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective; no control group; single hospital; selection by record completeness and exclusion of dogs under 1 or over 9 years; co-infection results not reported; small subsets for coagulation (APTT n=17), urinalysis (n=22) and blood smears (n=58); no inferential statistics; no outcome or survival data.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
