## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective randomised pilot trial tested whether adding N-acetylcysteine (NAC) to standard care changes haematological, biochemical, acute-phase and oxidative-stress variables in dogs with clinical Babesia gibsoni infection. Twenty-four outpatient dogs were allocated equally to standard therapy alone (doxycycline, clindamycin and metronidazole plus supportive care) or the same regimen with oral NAC at 70 mg/kg once daily for 14 days. Clinical score was the primary endpoint; blood counts, serum chemistry including C-reactive protein, and oxidative markers (lipid peroxide, reduced glutathione, total antioxidant capacity) were followed from day 0 to day 28. By day 14 the NAC arm showed earlier recovery from anaemia, a weaker acute-phase response and less hepatic injury, with lower lipid peroxidation and higher glutathione and antioxidant capacity. The authors recommend larger confirmatory trials.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (randomised adjunct-treatment pilot with biomarker outcomes; not mechanistic proof)
- **Authors' conclusion:** NAC as an adjunct ameliorates oxidative stress and attenuates anaemia, acute phase response and hepatic injury in B. gibsoni infection, pending larger trials.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study is chiefly treatment-efficacy work, retained because it reports oxidative (LPO, GSH, T-AOC), acute-phase (CRP) and hepatic indices in clinical B. gibsoni cases. Faster biomarker recovery under NAC is compatible with oxidative injury contributing to anaemia and hepatic damage, but cannot show it: NAC is a glutathione precursor with other effects, and parasitaemia or clearance was not reported as an outcome in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** 12 dogs per arm; no effect sizes, p-values or baseline comparability in abstract; primary outcome (clinical score) result not stated; blinding not stated; species confirmation method not stated; day-28 persistence of effects not stated; concurrent triple antimicrobial therapy.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
