## Abstract-derived summary

The authors aimed to characterise kidney involvement in canine babesiosis caused by Babesia rossi, a complication described as common but poorly documented. Because serum urea and creatinine respond poorly to early damage and are affected by haemolysis, they measured three urinary proteins by commercial ELISA and expressed each relative to urinary creatinine. Immunoglobulin G and C-reactive protein served as glomerular indicators, and retinol-binding protein as a tubular indicator. Eighteen dogs with uncomplicated babesiosis were compared with eight clinically healthy dogs. All three urinary markers were significantly higher in infected dogs. Among routine measures, only the urinary protein to creatinine ratio differed between groups; specific gravity, serum urea and serum creatinine did not. The three markers correlated positively with one another and with the protein to creatinine ratio. The authors interpret the findings as evidence of glomerular and tubular dysfunction detected earlier than by routine tests.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The data support both glomerular and tubular dysfunction in dogs with uncomplicated B. rossi infection. Urinary markers were superior to urine specific gravity, serum urea and serum creatinine for early detection of renal dysfunction.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Urinary IgG, CRP and retinol-binding protein concentrations were higher in infected dogs than in healthy dogs, while routine serum and urine markers other than the protein to creatinine ratio did not differ. This was associated with infection, but the design does not show what drives the renal changes (for example haemolysis, inflammation or hypoxia). The finding is relevant to renal pathophysiology because it points to renal involvement in dogs that were classed as uncomplicated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample (18 infected, 8 controls); only uncomplicated cases, so severe disease is not covered; no outcome or histology data reported in the abstract; cause of the renal changes not tested; markers indexed to urinary creatinine; country, setting and recruitment period not stated in the abstract; full text not read
