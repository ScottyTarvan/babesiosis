## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe six Labrador dogs, aged 3 to 5 years, with acute babesiosis, using haematology, serum biochemistry, urinalysis and sediment microscopy. Blood findings comprised leukopenia with fewer band neutrophils alongside basophilia and monocytosis, plus low red cell counts, hypochromia, low haematocrit and thrombocytopenia. The largest biochemical change was in liver transaminases, with AST about five-fold and ALT about three-fold the control values; total protein and albumin were reduced and azotaemia was noted. Urine was dark and turbid, containing erythrocytes, haemoglobin, leukocytes and protein, which the authors read as concurrent nephropathy. They propose that these early-stage patterns help explain pathogenesis and could guide diagnosis, prevention of complications and therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute babesiosis produces characteristic haematological, hepatic and urinary changes indicating multi-organ involvement, useful for understanding pathogenesis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile suggesting haemolysis, hepatocellular injury or leakage, hypoalbuminaemia and glomerular/tubular injury or haemoglobinuric nephropathy. It describes syndrome-level associations only; it does not establish mechanism or causality, and the pathogenic claims (catabolism, liver primacy) are inferential.
