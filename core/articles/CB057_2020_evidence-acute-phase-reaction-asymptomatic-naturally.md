## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether apparently healthy outdoor dogs that carry Babesia canis mount a measurable acute phase response. They compared 9 PCR-positive dogs and 20 PCR-negative but seroreactive dogs with 10 Babesia-naïve controls, measuring serum amyloid A (SAA), paraoxonase-1 (PON-1), haematology, routine biochemistry, protein electrophoresis and lipoprotein particle size. Non-parametric tests and ROC analysis were applied. PCR-positive dogs had higher SAA (median 39.0 µg/mL), total protein (median 74.7 g/L) and larger α-lipoprotein diameter (median 13.31 nm) than controls and than seroreactive PCR-negative dogs (p < 0.001). Both exposed groups had lower PON-1 activity and an electrophoretic pattern compatible with low-grade chronic inflammation; mild to moderate anaemia (4/29) and thrombocytopenia (7/29) occurred in exposed dogs. The authors conclude that carriers with detectable parasite DNA show an acute phase reaction.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (cross-sectional group comparison; descriptive of a subclinical state)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Asymptomatic B. canis carriers with detectable DNA in blood show an acute phase reaction, with higher SAA, total protein and α-lipoprotein diameter than seroreactive PCR-negative dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Informs the inflammatory component of subclinical infection and the host response that precedes or accompanies clinical disease. SAA, PON-1 (a negative acute phase marker) and lipoprotein remodelling are inflammation markers, not parasite-specific mechanisms. The data do not show that inflammation is caused by B. canis, nor that it predicts progression to clinical babesiosis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 9 PCR-positive dogs; seroreactive PCR-negative status may reflect past exposure, sub-detection parasitaemia or serological cross-reactivity; co-infections and other inflammatory conditions in outdoor dogs not addressed in the abstract; control matching, sampling time and ROC performance (AUC) not reported in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
