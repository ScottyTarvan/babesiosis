## Abstract-derived summary

Over one year, the authors examined anaemia among dogs diagnosed with babesiosis at a Nigerian teaching hospital. Of 103 smear-positive dogs, 80 with anaemia were analysed. Severe anaemia was stated to be the most frequent grade, and prevalence was highest in dogs over three years old and in small breeds. Eighteen dogs (22.5%) died, with deaths concentrated among severe and microcytic hypochromic cases (the abstract reports these as mortality rates of 61.% and 38.9%). Non-survivors had higher body temperature and pulse rate and significantly lower PCV, haemoglobin and red cell counts than survivors. The authors conclude that anaemia was very common and that severe and microcytic hypochromic anaemia accounted for most deaths.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Most deaths are attributed to severe and microcytic hypochromic anaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** PCV, haemoglobin and RBC count were lower in dogs that died, consistent with anaemia severity as a crude severity marker in babesiosis; the design cannot show that anaemia caused death, since sicker dogs may have lower PCV for other reasons. The cited 61.1% is the share of deaths that had severe anaemia (11/18), not case fatality; case fatality in severe anaemia from the paper's table is 11/24 (about 46%).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Smear-only diagnosis, species unknown; anaemic dogs only, so no non-anaemic comparison for mortality; no controls; treatment and follow-up not described in abstract; abstract inconsistencies (severity ranking, denominators); univariate statistics only; peer-review rigour not assessed.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
