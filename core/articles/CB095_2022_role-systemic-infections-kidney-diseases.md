## Abstract-derived summary

This German-language review considers infectious agents as causes of canine kidney disease, which is common and a frequent cause of death. It notes that leishmaniosis, dirofilariosis and ehrlichiosis, linked to immune-complex glomerulonephritis across central and southern Europe, matter in Germany mainly as travel-associated diseases. Anaplasmosis, hepatozoonosis, Lyme disease and babesiosis due to small Babesia species are reported only rarely as causes of such glomerulonephritis there. In contrast, leptospirosis, infectious canine hepatitis and babesiosis due to large Babesia species may cause acute kidney injury with tubulointerstitial nephritis. The authors argue that searching for a causative infectious agent in dogs with renal disease matters for both prognosis and therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Identifying potentially causative infections in dogs with renal disease is important for prognosis and therapy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Frames two renal phenotypes for Babesia: large species with acute kidney injury and tubulointerstitial nephritis, and small species with rare immune-complex glomerulonephritis. Babesiosis is one of many agents here, so this is a differential-diagnosis framework, not a mechanistic account of babesial nephropathy.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; evidence base, case numbers and species-level detail not visible; no search methods reported in abstract; Babesia is a minor part of the scope.
