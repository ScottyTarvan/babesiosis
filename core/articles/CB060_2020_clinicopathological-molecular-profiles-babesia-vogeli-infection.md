## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared haematological and biochemical findings in dogs from a Thai teaching hospital with PCR-confirmed Babesia vogeli, with or without Ehrlichia canis coinfection. Of the samples analysed, 33 carried B. vogeli alone and 11 were also coinfected; Hepatozoon canis and Anaplasma platys coinfections were excluded by PCR. Blood smears, complete blood counts and chemistry results were reviewed. Red cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit and red cell distribution width differed significantly between the groups (p=0.001), with the coinfected dogs more anaemic. Sex, breed, recorded clinical signs, geographic origin of the dog and 18S rRNA sequence results were not linked to infection status. The authors conclude that E. canis coinfection worsens disease severity and that the mechanisms of red cell destruction merit further study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile) with group comparison; associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Coinfection with E. canis increased the severity of babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that coinfected dogs presented with lower RBC mass than dogs with B. vogeli alone; this is a presentation-time difference in anaemia, not evidence of mechanism. Anaemia severity is a plausible severity proxy, but no outcome, treatment or disease-duration data are described, so "increased severity" is inferred from red cell indices only. Additive marrow suppression or haemolysis from E. canis cannot be separated from longer undetected disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small, unequal groups (n=11 vs 33); single hospital; no uninfected controls; no outcome data; timing of presentation and prior treatment not controlled; no mechanistic measurements.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
