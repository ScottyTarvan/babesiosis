## Abstract-derived summary

A five-year-old female Labrador with recurrent tick infestations and earlier episodes of anaemia and low platelets was presented with two days of dullness and anorexia. Examination showed pale mucous membranes, petechiae and hepatosplenomegaly on ultrasound. Haematology showed anaemia and thrombocytopenia, and blood smears were negative for haemoparasites. Flow cytometry pointed to immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia and immune-mediated thrombocytopenia, and PCR identified B. canis. The authors' diagnosis was Evans syndrome, coexisting with B. canis infection. The dog received diminazene aceturate, a corticosteroid, doxycycline and supportive therapy, plus a haematinic syrup, and improved clinically and haematologically. The report illustrates a combined immune-mediated cytopenia alongside active Babesia infection in a single dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The dog had Evans syndrome with concurrent B. canis infection, the latter identified by molecular (PCR) diagnosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates the overlap between babesiosis and immune-mediated cytopenias (combined IMHA and IMTP), a distinct syndrome; a single case cannot establish whether B. canis was involved in its onset. Prior anaemic and thrombocytopenic episodes raise the possibility of pre-existing immune dysregulation, with B. canis present concurrently. Smear-negative, PCR-positive status suggests low parasitaemia, so the report does not allow parasite-mediated haemolysis to be assessed.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; combined antiparasitic and immunosuppressive treatment confounds outcome; other tick-borne co-infections not documented in the accessible record; PCR product sequencing not confirmed; no follow-up duration; full text not read.
