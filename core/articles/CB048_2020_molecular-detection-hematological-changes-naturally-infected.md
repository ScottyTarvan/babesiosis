## Abstract-derived summary

The study aimed to estimate piroplasm infection among dogs attending a university small-animal hospital in the Rio de Janeiro metropolitan region and to describe the blood-count changes in infected animals. Blood from 250 randomly selected dogs was screened by conventional PCR of the 18S rRNA gene, followed by sequencing. Nine dogs (3.6%) were positive, and all nine were identified as Babesia vogeli. Among these nine, anaemia was reported in 66.7% and thrombocytopenia in 89%, the latter presumably 8 of 9 animals (inferred; the denominator is not stated on the landing page). The authors present anaemia and thrombocytopenia as the principal haematological alterations.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report anaemia and thrombocytopenia as the main haematological changes in PCR-confirmed B. vogeli infection (wider conclusion text not reviewed).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds sequence-confirmed B. vogeli clinicopathology (regenerative status, severity and outcome not available at abstract level). Thrombocytopenia more frequent than anaemia fits the usual mild-to-moderate B. vogeli picture, but nine dogs cannot support mechanism, syndrome definition or severity inference.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 9 infected, no control or comparison group, single time point, co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) not addressed in the information reviewed, clinical status and treatment not known, incidental-prevalence design rather than case-driven enrolment.
