## Abstract-derived summary

This Ukrainian clinic-based study described clinical and routine laboratory findings in 36 dogs diagnosed with babesiosis in 2023, with diagnosis resting on blood smear examination and clinical signs. Half the dogs were aged 1 to 5 years. Frequent signs were anorexia (97.2%), fever (91.7%), lethargy (77.8%), abdominal pain (72.2%), haemoglobinuria (69.4%), pallor (58.3%) and vomiting (47.2%). Erythropenia affected 83.3% and low haemoglobin (labelled haemoglobinaemia in the abstract) 88.9%, with a neutrophil left shift in 55.6%. Raised bilirubin (52.8%), AST and ALT (58.3%) and total protein (63.9%) were reported. The authors concluded that haemolytic anaemia and hepatic involvement characterise canine babesiosis and plan to study treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis in dogs presents with typical clinical signs, haemolytic anaemia, leukocyte changes and liver enzyme and bilirubin elevation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A syndrome-level description supporting intravascular or extravascular haemolysis with hepatic enzyme changes. The pathogenesis discussion (toxins, allergic reaction, myocardial and renal failure) is drawn from the literature; no cardiac, renal, coagulation or immune markers were measured, so no mechanism is demonstrated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no controls; diagnosis by smear only, species assumed; sampling timing and treatment status unreported; small n; no ethics statement; internal inconsistencies (reported MCH of 47.2 pg is incompatible with the stated mean RBC 2.83 T/L and Hb 41.8 g/L, which imply about 15 pg; very small SEs, e.g. AST 81.62 ± 0.41 U/L, are implausible for n=36). The abstract's "haemoglobinaemia" appears to mean low haemoglobin. Peer-review process for the article is not described; not excluded on that basis.
