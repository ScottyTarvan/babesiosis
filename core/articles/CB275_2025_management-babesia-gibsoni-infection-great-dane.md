## Abstract-derived summary

A two-year-old male Great Dane was brought in after ten days of poor appetite, dullness and partial loss of sight. On examination the dog was lethargic, had a raised rectal temperature and a slightly fast heart rate, and showed bleeding inside the front of the eye (hyphema). Blood work revealed low platelet numbers, anaemia, raised liver enzymes, a high white cell count and increased blood urea nitrogen. A blood smear showed intra-erythrocytic piroplasms, and the authors identified them as B. gibsoni. Treatment combined atovaquone with azithromycin, with haematinic, antioxidant, platelet-supporting and haemostatic supportive medicines given twice a day for five days. The dog recovered without complications. The abstract gives no numerical laboratory values.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report that atovaquone plus azithromycin with supportive care led to uneventful recovery in this dog.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents hyphema and visual impairment co-occurring with thrombocytopenia and anaemia in B. gibsoni infection, an uncommon haemorrhagic ocular presentation. It suggests, but cannot show, a thrombocytopenic or vasculitic contribution; no ocular examination findings, coagulation tests or retinal assessment are reported in the abstract.
