## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether vitamin D status differs in dogs with B. rossi babesiosis and relates to disease severity. Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] was measured in 34 infected dogs and 24 healthy controls. Recruitment invited owners of dogs with a confirmed B. rossi diagnosis and owners of healthy dogs to enrol. Infected dogs had significantly lower mean concentrations (37.76 ± 21.25 vs 74.2 ± 20.28 nmol/L), and the difference persisted after adjustment for age, body weight and sex. Within infected dogs, 25(OH)D was inversely related to a clinical severity score, but not to creatinine, ALT or time since last meal. The authors suggest hypovitaminosis D may serve as an additional indicator of severity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Infected dogs had lower 25(OH)D than healthy dogs, and the inverse relation with severity suggests hypovitaminosis D may be a useful severity indicator.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A severity biomarker association, not a mechanism. Low 25(OH)D could be a cause, consequence or bystander effect of acute inflammation or anorexia (negative acute-phase behaviour of vitamin D-binding protein), and the authors acknowledge causality is unclear. The severity score is built partly from laboratory variables (e.g. cortisol, albumin, creatinine), so overlap with metabolic derangement is likely.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Dietary vitamin D intake not assessed, healthy controls were blood donors, diet timing by owner recall, no recruitment dates, species confirmation method incomplete, no outcome data, severity-model reporting ambiguous.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
