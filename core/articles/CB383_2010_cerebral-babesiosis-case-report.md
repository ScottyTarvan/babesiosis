## Abstract-derived summary

The abstract introduces babesiosis as a tick-transmitted disease caused by intraerythrocytic protozoa of the genus Babesia, affecting many domestic and wild animals and, rarely, people, with worldwide distribution. It describes Babesia gibsoni as a small ring-shaped parasite, about 2.5 micrometres across, that typically produces a chronic illness with progressive anaemia in most infected adult dogs and is spread by the brown dog tick, Rhipicephalus sanguineus, which is widespread in Africa and Asia. The authors state that a cerebral presentation is uncommon and is accompanied by failure of several organ systems. The report then presents the clinical management of one dog with cerebral babesiosis.
