## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with naturally acquired Babesia canis canis infection in Croatia were studied at a university veterinary clinic. Sixty dogs were divided into two groups of 30: uncomplicated disease and complicated disease. Blood was sampled on admission and again on days 2 and 7 after treatment with imidocarb dipropionate. The white cell count, neutrophil and lymphocyte percentages, the neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio and parasitaemia were compared between the groups. Dogs with complicated disease had higher white cell counts on day 7, higher neutrophil percentages on days 2 and 7, lower lymphocyte percentages on day 2, a higher neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio on day 2 and higher parasitaemia. The authors propose the ratio as a possible severity and prognostic marker.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors propose the neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio as a marker of severity and prognosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Within the sampled dogs, complicated disease was associated with higher neutrophil percentages on days 2 and 7, lower lymphocyte percentages on day 2 and higher parasitaemia. Because groups were defined by the presence of complications, the data show an association with severity, not that the white cell changes drive complications. Prognostic use is proposed by the authors; the abstract reports no outcome-based accuracy analysis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; exact values and p-values not reported in the abstract; species identification method not stated; groups defined by complications, so confounding by parasitaemia is possible; single clinic, recruitment period not stated; no outcome or accuracy analysis reported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
