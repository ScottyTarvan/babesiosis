## Abstract-derived summary

This case report concerns a five-year-old male Labrador brought to a teaching veterinary clinic with a swollen abdomen, fever and loss of appetite. On examination the dog was dull, and its rectal temperature, heart rate and respiratory rate were all raised. A blood smear and a PCR assay both indicated infection with Babesia gibsoni. Laboratory work showed higher bilirubin, liver enzymes (ALT and ALP), creatinine, blood urea nitrogen and total leucocyte count, alongside low albumin and low total protein. Radiographs showed a ground-glass appearance of the abdomen, and ultrasound showed an anechoic area in the abdomen. The report's title names peritoneal effusion, and the imaging findings sit alongside hepatic and renal biochemical changes and hypoproteinaemia in this infected dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract states no explicit conclusion. It reports B. gibsoni infection confirmed by smear and PCR in a dog with abdominal distension, imaging changes that the title calls peritoneal effusion, and the listed haematobiochemical changes.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case reports abdominal imaging changes, titled as peritoneal effusion, alongside hypoalbuminaemia, hypoproteinaemia and raised hepatic and renal markers in a B. gibsoni-infected dog. These findings were seen together; the abstract does not test any mechanism for the fluid accumulation, so the role of low protein, hepatic injury or vascular permeability remains unproven. It is a single observation and cannot show how often the presentation occurs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single dog; no control or comparison group; values and reference ranges not given in the abstract; no effusion fluid analysis reported in the abstract; no treatment or outcome in the abstract; concurrent infections not described in the abstract; full text not read.
