## Abstract-derived summary

This descriptive study from a state veterinary hospital in Agartala, Tripura, examined blood profiles of 50 dogs diagnosed with Babesia gibsoni infection on the basis of parasite morphology in Giemsa-stained smears. Haemoglobin concentration, erythrocyte count and packed cell volume all declined significantly, consistent with anaemia, and platelet counts were also reduced, indicating thrombocytopenia. Total and differential leukocyte counts stayed within reference intervals, which the authors took to mean that the infection has little effect on these variables. They suggest the pattern supports accurate diagnosis and timely management of the disease. The abstract gives no numerical effect sizes, p values, control group, recruitment period or outcome data.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that the haematological findings offer useful insight into the impact of canine babesiosis and can aid diagnosis and timely management.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents the expected anaemia (regenerative status not reported) and thrombocytopenia of B. gibsoni infection, a clinical-pathology profile rather than a mechanism. Haemolytic versus immune-mediated contributions, platelet consumption versus sequestration, and severity or outcome relationships are not addressed. The normal leukogram claim rests on comparison with reference ranges and may not generalise across disease stages.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Species identified by microscopy alone (small piroplasms can be confused with other species); no healthy control group stated; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) not reported as excluded; single time point; no data on treatment, severity or outcome; small single-centre sample; publisher and journal not MEDLINE-indexed and peer-review process could not be verified.
