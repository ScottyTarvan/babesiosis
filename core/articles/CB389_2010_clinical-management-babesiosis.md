## Abstract-derived summary

This review covers the epidemiology, pathogenesis and pathophysiology behind the clinical syndromes of canine babesiosis, as well as diagnosis, treatment, prevention and zoonotic aspects. It notes that many Babesia species occur worldwide and that an increased incidence has been described, especially in North America. The parasite spends most of its life cycle inside red blood cells, and haemolysis is described as the result, with or without systemic complications. Confirmation relies on seeing the organism on a blood smear or by PCR, whereas a positive antibody test signals exposure, with or without active infection. Treatment rests on antiprotozoal drugs, antimicrobials and supportive care. Prognosis is said to depend on disease severity, reflecting organism and host factors. Syndromes linked to a worse outlook are red biliary disease, acute kidney failure, acute respiratory distress, neurological signs, acute pancreatitis, cardiac dysfunction and low blood glucose.
