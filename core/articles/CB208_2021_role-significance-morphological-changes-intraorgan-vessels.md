## Abstract-derived summary

The study aimed to characterise structural changes in small intra-organ blood vessels and their part in babesiosis pathogenesis. Tissue came from domestic dogs with clinically and laboratory confirmed babesiosis and from wild mouse-like rodents from latent foci across several Ukrainian regions; 91 infected animals were compared with 36 healthy controls, regardless of species. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded 5 µm sections were stained with haematoxylin and eosin and by Van Gieson. In infected animals, microvessels were branched, varied in size and spasmodic, with wall delamination and full-thickness defects, erythrocyte diapedesis into perivascular tissue and small focal haemorrhages. The author concludes that wall defects, endothelial vesiculation and desquamation are central, with intravascular coagulation, thrombosis, stasis and ischaemia as additional features.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Vessel-wall defects with dissection, haemorrhage and endothelial injury play a critical role in babesiosis pathogenesis, alongside intravascular coagulation, thrombosis and microcirculatory ischaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports an endothelial/microvascular injury and haemorrhagic phenotype, but dogs and rodents are pooled, so canine-specific findings cannot be isolated; descriptive histology shows lesions, not mechanism, and coagulation claims (DIC, thrombosis) appear unsupported by coagulation tests in the abstract. Key lesion terms (diapedesis, delamination) lack quantification.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Pooled host species with unreported dog numbers; Babesia species and confirmation method not stated; organs sampled not specified in abstract; no semi-quantitative scoring, blinding or statistics reported; Babesia-negative control tissue origin unclear; Crossref lists no references and peer-review procedure was not verified (publisher page returned HTTP 403).
