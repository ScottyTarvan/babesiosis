## Abstract-derived summary

A 7-year-old female German Shepherd from Assam, India, was presented with two months of inappetence and incoordination, fever of 41.2 °C, pallor, enlarged prescapular lymph nodes and ocular discharge. Blood smears showed intra-erythrocytic Babesia, and haematology indicated normocytic, normochromic anaemia. The dog died during work-up and a necropsy was done. Findings included splenomegaly, hepatic fatty change, renal and gastric haemorrhage, pulmonary oedema with interstitial pneumonia, focal myocardial haemorrhage with inflammatory cells, renal tubular necrosis with casts and subacute glomerulonephritis. Molecular testing confirmed B. gibsoni, as the title indicates. The authors considered the lesion pattern in keeping with a chronic course of babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The clinicopathological changes were consistent with chronic babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single fatal B. gibsoni case showing multi-organ lesions (lung, kidney, heart, liver, spleen, stomach). It documents a syndrome-level phenotype and is compatible with anaemic hypoxia, systemic inflammation and haemoglobin-associated tubular injury, but it does not test any mechanism. "Chronic" is an authors' inference; parasitaemia was about 5% and no chronicity criteria are given.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no controls; no tissue PCR, IHC or special stains; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon, leptospirosis) not tested; treatment confounding; timeline and tables internally inconsistent (death 3 days after sampling vs day 7 of treatment; text and biochemistry table disagree). Journal peer-review rigour is unverified (see Evidence basis).
