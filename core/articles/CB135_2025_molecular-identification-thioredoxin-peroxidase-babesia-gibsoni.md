## Abstract-derived summary

The authors characterised a 2-Cys peroxiredoxin (thioredoxin peroxidase) from Babesia gibsoni to ask how this intraerythrocytic parasite withstands the oxidant-rich red cell environment. The gene was cloned, and its 255-residue, 27.7 kDa product, named BgTPx-2, was expressed as a recombinant protein. Antibodies raised against it placed the native protein in parasitised erythrocytes by immunofluorescence. In a mixed-function oxidation assay the recombinant protein shielded plasmid DNA from oxidative nicking, which the authors read as antioxidant activity. Serum from experimentally infected beagles reacted with the protein by Western blot and ELISA, with a detectable antibody response on day 4 and a significant one by day 9 post-infection. The work proposes BgTPx-2 as a parasite antioxidant that may support survival inside red cells.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental), parasite-side and in vitro
- **Authors' conclusion:** BgTPx-2 has antioxidant activity that may help B. gibsoni resist oxidative and host immune attack and survive in erythrocytes, and it is immunogenic in dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant to host-parasite interaction and oxidative stress (OXO) rather than to canine clinical pathophysiology. It shows a cell-free antioxidant function of a recombinant parasite enzyme and early seroconversion; it does not show that the enzyme protects the parasite in situ, contributes to virulence, or alters host erythrocyte oxidative injury or anaemia. The link to haemolysis is inferential.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Antioxidant activity shown only in a plasmid-nicking assay; no parasite knockdown or in vivo test; 3 infected dogs; healthy-dog controls not quantified and none described for ELISA; no statistical test described despite "significant" wording; several internal inconsistencies in methods and figure captions (concentrations, secondary antibodies, fusion-protein size).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
