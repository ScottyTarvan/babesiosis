## Abstract-derived summary

This short communication from Mizoram, north-east India, estimated how often immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) accompanies haemoparasitic infection in dogs around Aizawl, a region lacking systematic data. Dogs were enrolled for signs compatible with haemolysis (icterus, pallor, lethargy, anorexia, fever). IMHA and the associated infection were established using blood smear examination, an antibody detection kit, Coombs' testing, haemato-biochemical profiling and urinalysis. IMHA linked to haemoparasites was found in 34.29% of the dogs studied, and Babesia gibsoni accounted for 75% of these cases. The authors present B. gibsoni as the most frequent causative agent of haemoparasite-associated IMHA in the area. Because the dogs were selected for clinical signs, the 34.29% figure describes this group rather than population prevalence.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni was the commonest cause of haemoparasite-associated IMHA in dogs in and around Aizawl.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests an immune-mediated haemolytic component in B. gibsoni infection in this region. A positive Coombs test in a Babesia-infected dog does not separate secondary immune-mediated destruction from antibody coating of parasite-damaged red cells, and the abstract gives no quantitative haematology. Mainly syndrome-level observation, not mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** denominators not reported; clinically selected dogs, so 34.29% is not a population prevalence; species by smear/serology without PCR; Ehrlichia canis co-infection not disentangled; full text inaccessible; abstract-level only.
