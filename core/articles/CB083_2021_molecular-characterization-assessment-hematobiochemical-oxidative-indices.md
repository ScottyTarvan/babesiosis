## Abstract-derived summary

The authors screened 239 dogs by 18S rRNA PCR and sequenced the positives, which clustered tightly with B. canis vogeli from India and other countries. PCR found more infections (7.95%) than microscopy (3.76%). In infected dogs, haemoglobin, red cells, haematocrit and white cells were lower, while MCHC, lymphocytes, eosinophils, monocytes and platelets were higher. ALT, AST, total protein, creatinine and urea were raised and glucose was reduced. Malondialdehyde was elevated and superoxide dismutase activity reduced, suggesting a pro-oxidant shift. Plasma cortisol was about 11.1 versus 2.8 nmol/L in infected versus uninfected dogs (p<0.01). The study frames these indices as biomarkers of natural canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Not stated as a separate conclusion in the abstract; the aim was molecular detection and assessment of important biomarkers in natural cases.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive evidence of anaemia, hepatic and renal enzyme/azotaemia changes, hypoglycaemia, oxidant-antioxidant imbalance and raised cortisol in B. vogeli dogs, supporting a multisystem and stress-axis involvement. Associations only; no mechanism, severity grading or outcome data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small and ambiguously described comparison groups; no ethics statement or data availability statement found; some reported summary statistics look internally inconsistent (e.g. SOD mean 0.015 with dispersion 3.91; total protein 10.56 g/dL), so effect sizes need cautious reading; co-infections and treatment status not described in the abstract; single time point at presentation.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
