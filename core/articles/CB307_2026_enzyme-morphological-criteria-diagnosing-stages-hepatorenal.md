## Abstract-derived summary

The authors characterised how acute canine babesiosis progresses in 45 spontaneously infected dogs of three breeds, aged 1.5–8 years, from Zhytomyr, Ukraine. Cases were assigned to three clinical stages (16, 13 and 16 dogs). The reported incubation period was 5–8 days, a little shorter in autumn than in spring. Liver enzymes, urea and creatinine were measured, and liver and kidney were examined grossly and histologically. Livers showed disorganised cords, focal haemorrhage, capillary congestion, dilated bile-filled ducts, granular hepatocyte dystrophy with pigment accumulation and intrahepatic cholestasis; gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase at stage III was about four times normal. Kidneys showed tubular epithelial dystrophy and necrosis with cortical and medullary haemorrhage. The authors offer these enzymatic and structural changes as staging criteria.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The staged enzymatic and morphological changes characterise the acute course of babesiosis and clarify the functional importance of the affected organ structures.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful descriptive pathology of hepatic cholestatic injury and renal tubular damage in natural infection, stratified by clinical stage. The abstract gives no data supporting a hepatorenal syndrome in the strict sense (functional renal failure secondary to liver disease), so the title term looks like a label for combined liver and kidney injury. Haemolysis, hypoxia and haemoglobin or bile pigment toxicity are plausible contributors that the abstract does not test. Severity-parasitaemia claims are not quantified.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no controls or reference intervals reported in the abstract; diagnostic confirmation and species identification not described; how stages were defined and how histology samples were obtained (necropsy, survival bias) not stated; treatment and co-infections not addressed; no multivariable or causal analysis; peer-review policy of the journal not assessed.
