## Abstract-derived summary

This Croatian-language original article from the University of Zagreb measured serum concentrations of two CXC chemokines, keratinocyte chemoattractant (KC) and interleukin-8 (IL-8), in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis canis, and compared them with healthy dogs. According to the English abstract on the journal repository page, higher KC-like concentrations were linked with more severe anaemia, leukocytosis and complicated disease. IL-8 showed a positive correlation with the ratio of granulocytes to lymphocytes. Both chemokines were raised in dogs with complicated babesiosis. The authors propose that the two chemokines may take part in the pathogenesis of the disease. The journal page, as read, gave no group sizes, species confirmation method or assay details, so the quantitative results are not reproduced here.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors suggest that the chemokines KC and IL-8 may contribute to the pathogenesis of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study links two neutrophil-related chemokines to markers of severity (anaemia, leukocytosis, complicated disease) in naturally infected dogs. The design is observational, so the findings show association and not cause. They add host inflammatory data from naturally infected dogs in Croatia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only a summary of the abstract was read, with no group sizes or effect sizes; species confirmation method not stated; sampling schedule not stated; no full text read; possible overlap of dogs with other papers from the same group not determined.
