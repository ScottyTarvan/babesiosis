## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared markers of oxidative stress in seven dogs diagnosed with babesiosis and seven healthy controls. Measures included total antioxidant and oxidant capacity, an oxidative stress index (OSI), thiol-disulphide parameters, albumin, and ischaemia-modified albumin (IMA). Infected dogs had lower antioxidant capacity and albumin, and higher oxidant capacity and OSI (p<0.05). Thiol and disulphide variables and IMA did not differ between groups. Several internal correlations among thiol indices were reported. Areas under the ROC curve were 0.918 for antioxidant capacity, 0.796 for oxidant capacity, 0.980 for OSI, and 1.0 for albumin. The authors concluded these four variables discriminate babesiosis dogs and indicate raised oxidative stress.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** TAC, TOC, OSI and albumin have diagnostic value for assessing elevated oxidative stress in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a shift in redox balance (reduced antioxidant capacity, higher OSI) in seropositive sick dogs, in line with oxidative injury as a contributor to erythrocyte damage, but cannot show causation. Thiol-disulphide balance and IMA were unchanged, so not all redox axes shift. Albumin is a negative acute-phase protein, so its fall may reflect inflammation rather than redox change specifically.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Seven dogs per group; AUCs (including a perfect 1.0) are likely overfitted; case definition by IgG ELISA indicates exposure, not confirmed active infection or species; healthy controls only, with no non-babesial diseased comparators; co-infection and parasitaemia not assessed; severity not related to markers.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
