## Abstract-derived summary

This study screened 216 dogs from northern India for Babesia vogeli and B. gibsoni by blood smear and conventional PCR, and related infection to risk factors and haemato-biochemical findings. Microscopy detected infection in 12.5% and PCR in 27.78%; B. vogeli predominated (22.22% by PCR) over B. gibsoni (5.55%). Non-descript breed (OR 27.5, very wide confidence interval) and lymph node enlargement (OR 3.3) were linked to B. vogeli, whereas B. gibsoni was linked to age over four years and male sex. Infected dogs of both species had lower haemoglobin, erythrocyte count, PCV and platelets, higher ALT, bilirubin fractions and BUN, and lower albumin. B. gibsoni cases had more severe erythroid, platelet, BUN and bilirubin changes than B. vogeli cases. Species-directed treatment was described as promising.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile), with cross-sectional associations
- **Authors' conclusion:** Accurate diagnosis and species-specific treatment matter, since the two species differ in prevalence, risk factors and clinical impact in northern India.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mostly a prevalence study, retained for its species-comparative clinicopathology against controls. Anaemia with thrombocytopenia, raised bilirubin (higher indirect fraction in B. gibsoni) and raised ALT, BUN and low albumin describe a haemolytic, multisystem phenotype and suggest B. gibsoni infection is more severe, but no mechanism (immune-mediated haemolysis, consumption, hepatic or renal injury) was tested; BUN without creatinine or urinalysis cannot localise renal involvement.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 10 controls and 12 B. gibsoni cases (authors note imprecise ORs); cross-sectional design; suspected-case sampling inflates prevalence; co-infections not reported in cases; disease duration and prior treatment as confounders not addressed in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
