## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a 13-year-old female Doberman with severe regenerative anaemia in which immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) coexisted with PCR-confirmed Babesia gibsoni infection. Blood films showed spherocytes, many nucleated red cells (metarubricytes) and marked erythroid dysplasia: binucleate and multinucleate cells, nuclear fragmentation, and nuclear-cytoplasmic maturation asynchrony. Bone marrow showed erythroid hyperplasia with a reduced myeloid-to-erythroid ratio, and early erythroid precursors were not meaningfully dysplastic, which the authors read as arguing against primary myelodysplasia. After antimicrobial plus immunosuppressive treatment, the blood abnormalities and dysplasia improved considerably. The authors concluded that reactive erythroid dysplasia can appear on blood smears of dogs with a haemoparasitic infection and concurrent immune-mediated haemolysis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Reactive (secondary) erythroid dysplasia may be seen in peripheral blood of dogs with concurrent haemoparasitic infection and immune-mediated haemolysis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A distinct morphological observation within the erythrocyte-injury axis. It is consistent with dysplastic-looking erythroid cells occurring alongside intense regenerative drive, with or without immune-mediated haemolysis, and such cells should not be mistaken for myelodysplastic syndrome. It does not separate the contributions of B. gibsoni and IMHA, and it shows no mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; Babesia-versus-IMHA attribution impossible; antimicrobial and immunosuppressive treatment given together, so the response cannot be assigned to either; numerical haematology, PCR target and IMHA criteria not available to this review; follow-up duration not stated; age-related marrow changes not excluded.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
