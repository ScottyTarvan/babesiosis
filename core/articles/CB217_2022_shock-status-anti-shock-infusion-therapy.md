## Abstract-derived summary

The authors studied 100 dogs of different breeds (20-40 kg, 2-4 years) with acute spontaneous babesiosis to determine whether shock is present, grade it, and trial anti-shock infusion. Measurements covered oscillometric blood pressure and pulse, platelet count and spontaneous aggregation, PT, aPTT, fibrinogen, FDP and D-dimer by enzyme immunoassay, soluble fibrin-monomer complexes, circulating blood volume and haematocrit. Affected dogs showed sub-compensated grade II shock, supported by lower specific circulating blood volume (p<0.001), lower average blood pressure (p<0.05) and a higher Allgöwer shock index (p<0.05). Consumption-phase DIC was inferred from raised soluble fibrin-monomer complexes (p<0.001), FDP and D-dimer (p<0.05), hypofibrinogenaemia (p<0.001) and inconsistent PT/aPTT shifts. A dextran (Rheopolyglucine) plus dipyridamole (Persantine) infusion, 5 mL/kg, normalised these variables for 48 hours and outperformed either agent alone.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with an interventional component of unclear control design)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that acute babesiosis produces sub-compensated shock with consumption coagulopathy/DIC that needs urgent anti-shock therapy, best given as the combined dextran-dipyridamole infusion.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Frames canine babesiosis as a hypovolaemic/haemodynamic and consumptive coagulopathy syndrome, relevant to the perfusion and coagulation axes. Shock grading rests on blood volume, pressure and shock index, so it is a clinical-physiological phenotype rather than a demonstrated mechanism. Haematocrit-based volume estimates are confounded by anaemia, and DIC is inferred from marker patterns rather than a validated score.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Babesia species and confirmation method not stated; unclear whether a healthy control group was used; allocation to therapy arms, blinding and outcome (survival) not reported in abstract; 48-hour follow-up only; no ethics or data statement seen; no full text read.
