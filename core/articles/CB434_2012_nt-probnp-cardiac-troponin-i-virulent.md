## Abstract-derived summary

Cardiac involvement is hard to recognise clinically in canine babesiosis, although cardiac pathology and raised troponin have been described. This study asked whether plasma N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) is raised in affected dogs and whether it tracks cardiac troponin I (cTnI). Forty-five dogs with babesiosis, split into mild uncomplicated, severe uncomplicated and complicated groups of 15 each, were compared with 15 controls. Renal indices, cystatin-C, lactate, fractional shortening and blood pressure were also recorded. Median NT-proBNP was 246, 650 and 638 pmol/l in the three disease groups versus 106 pmol/l in controls, with the two more severe groups above the mild group. Median cTnI was 0.39, 0.4 and 1.45 ng/ml, and only the complicated group differed from controls, whose values were under the 0.2 ng/ml detection limit. The authors concluded that NT-proBNP rose with severity, earlier than or independently of cTnI.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with babesiosis had elevated NT-proBNP, and the elevation was greater with more severe disease. The rise appears earlier than, or independent of, the rise in cTnI.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** NT-proBNP was associated with disease severity grade and separated even mild cases from controls, whereas cTnI separated only the complicated group. The authors' report that the NT-proBNP rise is earlier than, or independent of, the cTnI rise cannot be tested by a between-group comparison, so timing and cause remain open. The study supports NT-proBNP as a candidate severity-linked cardiac biomarker.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; one comparison of 15 dogs per group, with no repeated sampling described in the abstract; how the severity grades were defined is not given in the abstract, and overlap with renal dysfunction or anaemia as drivers of NT-proBNP is not resolved in the abstract; species not named in the abstract; echocardiographic and blood pressure results are not reported in the abstract

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
