## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined 286 blood smears from Chippiparai dogs over one year for haemoprotozoan infection and found 12 dogs carrying Babesia gibsoni, the small canine piroplasm. These dogs presented with tick infestation, pale or icteric mucous membranes, lethargy and fever. Haematology showed lower haemoglobin, packed cell volume and red cell count, while total white cell counts ranged from leucocytosis to leucopenia, and thrombocytopenia was also recorded. The blood film showed marked anaemic changes with neutrophilia and signet-ring-shaped parasites inside erythrocytes. The paper presents this as the haematological profile of B. gibsoni infection in the breed; no numerical values are given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors describe B. gibsoni as causing severe haematological change, with marked anaemia, thrombocytopenia and variable leukocyte findings in the 12 positive dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports the familiar picture of anaemia plus thrombocytopenia with intra-erythrocytic parasites (ERY, with a COA signal). It does not distinguish direct parasite-mediated red cell injury from immune-mediated haemolysis, because no haemolysis, reticulocyte, Coombs or coagulation data are reported in the abstract. It is a clinicopathological profile, not a mechanistic or severity-biomarker study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small n (12) with no control group; species assigned by smear morphology only, so other small Babesia and co-infections (for example Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) are not excluded; no numeric values or reference intervals in the abstract; design (retrospective or prospective) and recruitment setting not stated; single breed; short regional-journal paper whose peer-review quality could not be assessed (journal self-describes as peer reviewed, not MEDLINE-indexed).
