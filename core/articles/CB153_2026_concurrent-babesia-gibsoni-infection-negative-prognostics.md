## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective cohort asked whether concurrent Babesia gibsoni infection worsens survival in dogs with immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA). Fifty-five dogs meeting ACVIM criteria were enrolled: 26 with primary IMHA and 29 with IMHA accompanied by natural B. gibsoni infection verified by PCR. Every dog received immunosuppression (prednisolone or dexamethasone); the infected group was also given imidocarb and doxycycline. Follow-up lasted 60 days, during which 42 dogs died (40 from IMHA, 2 from unrelated causes). Multivariable Cox modelling identified male sex (HR 2.1), marked spherocytosis (HR 2.1) and B. gibsoni co-infection (HR 2.6, p=0.024) as independent predictors of death. The authors conclude that these three factors indicate a poor prognosis in IMHA.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** Male sex, marked spherocytosis and B. gibsoni co-infection are key independent predictors of poor prognosis in dogs with IMHA.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Frames B. gibsoni as one trigger of secondary IMHA and shows an association with shorter survival, an outcome (SEV) marker rather than a mechanism. Marked spherocytosis tracking with death is consistent with an immune-mediated component of haemolysis, but the abstract gives no immunological, parasitaemia or complement data. The reported HR 2.6 with 95% CI 1.2-2.9 looks internally odd (point estimate near the upper bound) and should be checked against the full text. Cause of death within the co-infected group (immune-mediated vs parasite-driven) is not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** modest n (55); treatment confounding (only infected dogs received antibabesials; immunosuppressant drug differed); 60-day window; unclear whether primary IMHA dogs were tested for other haemoparasites beyond PCR; country and recruitment period not reported in abstract; wide CIs; possible overfitting with 42 events and several covariates.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
