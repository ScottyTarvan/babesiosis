## Abstract-derived summary

The authors set out to describe cardiac function in dogs with babesiosis. Dogs admitted to a veterinary college critical care unit between April and December 2021 on suspicion of babesiosis underwent clinical, haematological, biochemical, echocardiographic and electrocardiographic assessment. Of 92 dogs, 31 showed echocardiographic evidence of cardiac dysfunction: 2 systolic, 24 diastolic and 5 combined. The commonest ECG abnormalities in affected dogs were sinus tachycardia, ST-segment coving and atrial fibrillation. Only 8 of the 31 dogs with cardiac dysfunction survived (26%). The abstract concludes by calling cardiac dysfunction a "good" prognostic indicator, wording that conflicts with the low survival it reports.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with a prognostic association)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cardiac dysfunction in canine babesiosis was described as a good prognostic indicator, although the abstract also reports reduced survival (26%) in those dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests diastolic dysfunction is the dominant echocardiographic abnormality in babesiosis (24/31), consistent with myocardial involvement or altered loading, but mechanism (hypoxia, inflammation, anaemia-driven volume change, fluid therapy) is not examined. The "good prognostic" wording is probably an error for "poor", but this cannot be confirmed from the abstract. The survival of dogs without cardiac dysfunction is not given, so the association with outcome is unquantified.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Babesia species and diagnostic confirmation not stated; no control group; single centre; no survival comparator or adjustment for severity or treatment; co-infections not mentioned; no cardiac biomarkers reported in abstract; journal's peer-review policy not verified.
