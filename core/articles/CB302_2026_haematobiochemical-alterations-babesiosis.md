## Abstract-derived summary

The authors characterised blood and serum changes in 24 dogs with naturally acquired Babesia infection, diagnosed by PCR, and contrasted them with apparently healthy dogs (number not stated in the abstract). Infected dogs showed lower haemoglobin, packed cell volume and red cell counts. A raised white cell count was the leucocyte change seen most often, and three quarters of the infected dogs were thrombocytopenic. Chemistry showed higher ALT, ALP, creatinine, urea nitrogen, globulin and bilirubin, whereas albumin was significantly lower. The authors read this pattern as the consequence of intravascular red cell destruction, immune injury, and liver and kidney dysfunction. Effect sizes, parasite species and p-values for individual analytes are not given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis produces considerable haematological and biochemical alterations that reflect haemolysis, immune-mediated injury and hepatic and renal dysfunction.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile that is consistent with anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia and hypoalbuminaemia. It does not separate haemolytic from hepatic causes of hyperbilirubinaemia, prerenal from renal azotaemia, or show any mechanism; the mechanistic wording is the authors' inference.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; species not reported; control group size not stated; no severity or outcome stratification; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) and prior treatment not described; a two-page article with limited methodological detail. The journal's peer-review process is not described on the article page, so peer-review quality is unverified.
