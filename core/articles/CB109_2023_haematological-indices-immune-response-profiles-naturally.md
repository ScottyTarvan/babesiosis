## Abstract-derived summary

The study compared healthy dogs with dogs infected by B. canis, by D. repens, or by both in central Poland, which is endemic for each parasite. Anaemia and kidney and liver markers were analysed, and blood transcripts for Th1 (STAT4, IFN-γ), Th2 (STAT6, GATA3, SOCS3, IL-13) and regulatory (IL-10) pathways were quantified. B. canis alone produced anaemia plus renal and hepatic abnormalities, whereas D. repens alone caused little change. Co-infected dogs showed less marked ALP, urea and creatinine alterations than B. canis-only dogs. In dogs split into mild (Babesia 1) and severe (Babesia 2) babesiosis, GATA3 dominated the mild group and IFN-γ and SOCS3 the severe group. IL-13 was highest in D. repens dogs, and STAT6 and IL-10 in co-infected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** D. repens-only dogs showed mainly Th2-type expression, B. canis dogs showed mixed responses with marked pathology, and co-infected dogs showed Th2 predominance with a milder biochemical picture.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Suggests a Th1/IFN-γ and SOCS3 signal in severe babesiosis versus Th2/GATA3 in milder disease, but this is a descriptive association in blood mRNA; it does not show that Dirofilaria co-infection is protective or that Th2 skewing causes milder disease. Severity classes were defined from the same RBC, enzyme and urea values later compared, which is partly circular. Pathophysiological relevance: immunopathology (INF) with anaemia, renal and hepatic dysfunction.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 7 co-infected dogs and 3 healthy controls for expression normalisation (full text); most expression comparisons not significant (only IFN-γ and SOCS3 differed); amicrofilaraemic D. repens not detectable; mRNA only, no serum; severity scored by laboratory values, not outcome; unequal group sizes and a retrospective, convenience sample.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
