## Abstract-derived summary

This cross-sectional observational study asked whether C-reactive protein (CRP), a major positive acute-phase protein in dogs, relates to outcome in canine babesiosis. Seventy-five dogs with natural Babesia rossi infection, confirmed by PCR and reverse line blot, were studied. Serum CRP was measured with an automated turbidimetric immunoassay validated for dogs, at admission and again 24 hours later. Mean admission CRP was similar in survivors (n = 57; 107.5 mg/l) and non-survivors (n = 11; 122.1 mg/l), and exact logistic regression adjusted for age and sex showed no link with survival. Among survivors, neither admission CRP nor its change over the first 24 hours was related to days of hospitalisation. The authors concluded that CRP is not associated with outcome and that inflammation is probably not the only driver of disease severity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (negative result for outcome)
- **Authors' conclusion:** CRP concentration, as a measure of the acute-phase response, was not associated with outcome in canine babesiosis, and inflammation is unlikely to be the only cause of disease severity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In B. rossi infection, admission CRP did not separate survivors from non-survivors in this hospital sample, so the acute-phase response measured by CRP was not informative for prognosis here. The small number of deaths limits power, and all dogs were already ill at admission, so the finding does not show that inflammation is unimportant in the syndrome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 11 non-survivors; single referral hospital; no uninfected control group; all dogs sampled when the acute-phase response was already established; dogs treated according to complications, so management varied; no formal adjustment beyond age and sex.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

  - No non-survivor had an admission CRP below 63.2 mg/l; with a retrospective cut-off of 60 mg/l, survival was 9/9 below and 48/59 (81.4%) at or above the cut-off, a difference that was not significant (Fisher's exact P = 0.34) (full text)
  - Median hospital stay in survivors was 3 days (range 2-4; n = 9) below the cut-off and 2 days (range 1-6; n = 48) at or above it (Wilcoxon P = 0.25) (full text)
  - Samples were stored at -80 °C and CRP was run in two batches; haemoglobin and bilirubin in all samples were within the manufacturer's interference limits (full text)
  - The authors suggest that death usually occurs within 24 hours of admission, too soon for CRP kinetics to be useful, and that tissue hypoxia and metabolic collapse may matter more than the inflammatory response (full text)
  - The abstract and results text give a P value of 0.65 for admission CRP against hospital stay, while the Figure 2 caption gives 0.64 (full text)
