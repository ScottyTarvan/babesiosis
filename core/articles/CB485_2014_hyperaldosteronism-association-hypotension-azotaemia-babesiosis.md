## Abstract-derived summary

Earlier work on canine babesiosis found low blood potassium and raised renal potassium excretion, which pointed to a possible aldosterone effect. Low blood pressure, a known complication that can lead to kidney failure, was proposed as a possible reason for a secondary rise in this hormone. The authors measured serum aldosterone in 14 dogs infected with Babesia canis and compared aldosterone and blood pressure with 10 healthy dogs using Mann-Whitney tests. They then calculated Spearman correlations between aldosterone and blood pressure, urea, creatinine and potassium. Only 4 of the 14 infected dogs had aldosterone above the reference interval, yet the infected group had higher aldosterone and lower blood pressure than controls. Aldosterone correlated inversely with systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure and with potassium, and positively with urea and creatinine. The authors read this as hyperaldosteronism in some dogs, possibly a response to hypotension that proved inadequate.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Some dogs with B. canis infection showed hyperaldosteronism, possibly a response to hypotension. Together with hypotension and severe azotaemia in infected dogs, this suggests the response is insufficient.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Higher aldosterone and lower blood pressure in infected dogs, with correlations to urea, creatinine and potassium, fit a haemodynamic and renal-endocrine link in canine babesiosis. The design cannot show direction or cause, so the link is associational. Aldosterone was above the reference interval in only 4 of 14 dogs, so the group-level difference may rest on a minority of dogs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample (14 infected, 10 controls); no time course reported in the abstract; correlations only, with no causal test; confirmation method for B. canis not stated in the abstract; no severity or outcome grouping reported in the abstract; institution and setting not described in the abstract.
