## Abstract-derived summary

Five healthy male Beagles were experimentally inoculated with Babesia rossi, two with a low parasite dose (10^4) and three with a high dose (10^8), to chart how endocrine variables shift during disease. Serum cortisol, thyroxine (T4) and TSH were measured every other day by chemiluminescent immunoassay and analysed with linear mixed models. Cortisol rose and T4 fell in both groups, then trended back toward baseline after treatment. Peaks and nadirs came sooner in high-dose dogs (cortisol 96 h, T4 108 h) than low-dose dogs (108 h and 144 h). Faster, larger cortisol increases and faster, deeper T4 declines accompanied greater disease severity and higher inoculum. TSH stayed inside reference limits throughout. The authors conclude that cortisol and T4 follow severity in opposite directions.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cortisol and T4 tracked disease severity during experimental B. rossi infection, in opposite directions.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a time course of HPA-axis activation and falling total T4 with preserved TSH, a pattern compatible with critical-illness (non-thyroidal illness) physiology rather than primary thyroid disease. It is an association with severity and dose; the abstract shows no test of mechanism (no ACTH, free T4 or T3 reported) and no causal inference is warranted. Value is as a severity-tracking biomarker timeline.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Very small n (5; 2 low-dose), male Beagles only, supraphysiological experimental inocula, total hormone assays by a single immunoassay, recovery confounded by treatment, severity definition and uninfected controls not described in abstract.
