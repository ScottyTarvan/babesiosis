## Abstract-derived summary

A six-year-old Labrador Retriever with two days of fever (41 °C), inappetence and dysuria, previously treated by a field practitioner, was referred to a university clinic. Examination found jaundiced mucous membranes, enlarged lymph nodes and splenomegaly confirmed on radiographs. Blood smear, haematology, biochemistry and PCR were performed; PCR identified B. gibsoni, and urine testing excluded Leptospira. Ultrasonography, electrocardiography and cardiac troponin I measurement were used to characterise secondary problems, reported as acute kidney failure, valvular heart disease, hepatitis and splenitis. A 15-day regimen combining three antimicrobials, plus supportive care, did not succeed, and the authors discuss possible reasons. The abstract gives no numerical laboratory values and does not state the final outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report renal, cardiac, hepatic and splenic complications in B. gibsoni infection and discuss why triple-drug therapy failed.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates a multisystem phenotype (icterus, azotaemia-type renal injury, raised troponin) in a PCR-confirmed B. gibsoni dog. It is a syndrome description, not a mechanism: the abstract does not show that Babesia caused the valvular lesion, and troponin elevation indicates myocardial injury without identifying its cause (hypoxia, inflammation, renal disease or pre-existing cardiac disease are all possible).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; no controls; no numeric data in abstract; co-infections beyond Leptospira (e.g. Ehrlichia, Anaplasma, Hepatozoon) not reported as excluded; prior field treatment confounds presentation; 6-year-old dog may have pre-existing degenerative valve disease; outcome not stated; journal peer-review practice not verified.
