## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined kidney tissue from eight dogs that died of naturally acquired Babesia canis infection with nephropathy, using histological and electron microscopic examination. Seven dogs had received imidocarb dipropionate, on average 4.5 days before death, and only two had severe anaemia. The main finding was degeneration and necrosis of proximal tubular epithelial cells, with sloughing from the basement membrane, necrotic casts and occasional whole-tubule necrosis. Glomeruli showed no significant histological change, and haemoglobin casts and droplets were uncommon. Electron microscopy showed damage to nuclei, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum and microvilli, and degenerative change in glomerular and interstitial capillary endothelium. The authors judged the tubular necrosis probably to reflect hypoxic injury, possibly from renal vasoconstriction during systemic hypotension, with anaemia a possible contributor, and advised caution with imidocarb in dogs with renal involvement.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The severe acute tubular necrosis is probably the result of hypoxic renal injury, with systemic hypotension and renal vasoconstriction suggested as possibly the most important cause of renal hypoxia and anaemia as a possible contributor. Imidocarb should be used with caution where renal involvement is possible until more data on its nephrotoxicity are available.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives tissue-level evidence of tubular and capillary endothelial injury in fatal canine babesiosis, which fits a hypoxia-related mechanism. The design is a descriptive series, so the hypoxic and hypotensive explanation is a hypothesis and not a tested cause. Because seven of the eight dogs had been given imidocarb, the lesions cannot be separated from a possible drug effect.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small series of fatal cases only; no control dogs; seven of eight dogs treated with imidocarb before death, which confounds attribution; species identification method not stated in the abstract; no clinical or laboratory data in the abstract; hypotension and hypoxia not reported in the abstract, only inferred.
