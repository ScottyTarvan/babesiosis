## Abstract-derived summary

(Draws on the abstract and the full text, which was read.) A young German Shepherd bitch had constipation, anorexia, apathy, vomiting, pale mucous membranes and abdominal enlargement, with radiographic peritoneal effusion. Initial tests showed normocytic normochromic anaemia, neutrophilia with left shift, monocytosis, thrombocytopenia, low total protein and mildly raised AST; no haemoparasites were seen, and acute liver disease was suspected. She deteriorated after discharge. Ultrasound showed marked effusion, splenomegaly and hepatomegaly. About 5 L of turbid red fluid was drained, and cytology showed Babesia merozoites; a repeat blood smear then showed intraerythrocytic forms. Despite two transfusions, imidocarb, doxycycline and prednisolone, she died, and necropsy found haemoperitoneum, marked icterus, and an enlarged liver and spleen. The authors suggest effusion cytology as a possible diagnostic aid.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The case matches published findings and supports looking for Babesia merozoites in ascitic fluid, so peritoneal fluid cytology merits inclusion as a diagnostic test.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Describes an unusual syndrome of massive, blood-tinged ascites and haemoperitoneum with hepatic enzyme rise and icterus in babesiosis. Mechanism is not established. The authors invoke vasoactive mediators with increased vascular permeability, hypoxic or cytokine-mediated liver injury, and possible splenic infarction or haemorrhage. Parasites in a red effusion with free blood at necropsy may reflect haemorrhage rather than a distinct process.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species not identified (no PCR); co-infections not tested; effusion not classified (no protein, cell counts or PCV); no coagulation tests or histopathology; first smear negative; treatment confounding; date of case not stated; journal with a 10-day receipt-to-approval interval, not MEDLINE-indexed (peer-review rigour uncertain).
