## Abstract-derived summary

The paper uses enzyme measurements to characterise liver involvement in dogs during the acute phase of babesiosis. When clinical signs first appeared, activities of the transaminases AST (GOT) and ALT (GPT) were raised, together with a moderate enzymaemia. Continued rises in AST, LDH and ALT were read by the author as a sign of progressive disease. From the second or third day after clinical onset, markers of cholestasis appeared, with raised blood GGT and a further marker abbreviated LB in the abstract. The abstract gives no numerical values, group sizes, control group or Babesia species, and it does not say how infection was confirmed. The study is therefore a descriptive enzyme-diagnostic account of hepatic injury and cholestasis in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Enzyme diagnostics show liver pathology in acute canine babesiosis. Early increases in AST and ALT, sustained increases in AST, LDH and ALT, and later cholestatic markers track the course of disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The abstract suggests a sequence of hepatocellular enzyme changes followed by cholestatic changes in acute disease. Because no figures, controls or case definitions are given, the findings can be read only as a descriptive pattern. A role for the parasite in these changes cannot be inferred from the abstract.
