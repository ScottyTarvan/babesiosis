## Abstract-derived summary

Eighteen Beagles underwent a staged model: initial E. canis infection, doxycycline or rifampicin treatment, recovery, E. canis reinfection, then B. vogeli superinfection (to day 116). Weight, temperature, haematology, biochemistry and pathogen qPCR were tracked. The first Chinese E. canis isolate caused acute severe ehrlichiosis. Doxycycline cleared E. canis DNA, whereas rifampicin lowered copy number by up to 1.5-fold; doxycycline-cleared dogs were reinfected. Adding B. vogeli produced higher fever, more severe anaemia and fewer platelets. Splenectomised dogs carried more E. canis during recovery and reinfection. Histological lesions occurred in brain, lung, kidney, liver and spleen. The authors present the work as a model informing clinical, haematological and treatment responses in canine ehrlichiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The model clarifies clinical and haematological responses and treatment options for E. canis, and B. vogeli superinfection aggravates disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Primarily an Ehrlichia study; its babesiosis relevance is the worsening of anaemia and thrombocytopenia when B. vogeli is added to dogs with prior E. canis exposure, a co-infection syndrome rather than isolated babesial pathophysiology. Organ histology is not attributed to either pathogen in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no B. vogeli-only group evident, so babesial contribution cannot be separated from E. canis effects; group sizes and numbers receiving B. vogeli not reported in abstract; treatment confounding; borderline eligibility.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
