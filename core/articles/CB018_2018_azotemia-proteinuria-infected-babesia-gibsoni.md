## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective record review from a US vector-borne disease laboratory looked for azotemia and proteinuria in dogs with Babesia infection, and asked how these findings changed after antiprotozoal therapy. Of 35 dogs found in the database, 5 remained after excluding animals with coinfections or comorbidities that could themselves injure glomeruli; four had both pre- and post-treatment results. All five were American pit bull terriers or crosses infected with Babesia gibsoni, and all showed low serum albumin and proteinuria; three were azotemic. After treatment, albumin normalised in three dogs and rose in one, azotemia resolved in one and improved in another, and proteinuria decreased in two. The authors concluded that B. gibsoni infection can produce laboratory findings compatible with glomerular disease, and that therapy may improve azotemia and proteinuria.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Laboratory findings consistent with glomerular disease occur in B. gibsoni-infected dogs, and treatment can improve azotemia and proteinuria.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypoalbuminaemia with proteinuria is compatible with a protein-losing glomerulopathy as a syndrome-level renal complication of B. gibsoni, but no histopathology, urine protein:creatinine values or mechanism (e.g. immune-complex deposition) are reported in the abstract. Improvement after antiprotozoal therapy is suggestive of a link, not proof of causation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 5 dogs of 35 screened, all of one breed group (limits generalisability); no controls; retrospective database selection bias; exclusion of coinfections was limited to those known to cause glomerular injury; concurrent supportive treatment and follow-up intervals not described in abstract; method of species confirmation not stated in abstract.
