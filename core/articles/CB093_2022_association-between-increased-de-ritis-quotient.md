## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether the aspartate-to-alanine aminotransferase ratio (de Ritis quotient) tracks renal azotaemia in dogs with Babesia canis infection. They compared azotaemic with non-azotaemic infected dogs, and pre-renal with renal azotaemia. Azotaemic dogs had higher AST activity and a higher AST/ALT ratio; both values were also higher in renal than in pre-renal azotaemia. AST and the ratio correlated with renal failure index, fractional sodium excretion and urinary-to-serum creatinine ratio. AST and ALT were uncorrelated within azotaemic dogs but correlated when all dogs were pooled. The authors concluded that the AST rise in azotaemic infected dogs may be kidney-derived and that the ratio could serve as a simple renal index for recognising renal azotaemia in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathological comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Raised serum AST in azotaemic B. canis dogs may originate from the kidney, and the AST/ALT ratio may help identify renal azotaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant to the renal syndrome of canine babesiosis as a proposed bedside discriminator of renal versus pre-renal azotaemia, not as a mechanism. AST is not kidney-specific; hepatic, muscle, cardiac and erythrocyte (haemolysis) sources were not excluded, so "renal origin" remains a hypothesis. Because renal azotaemia was itself defined by the indices later correlated with AST, some circularity is likely.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n (9 renal cases); single clinic; no healthy or histopathology reference; no outcome data; cardiac/hepatic AST contribution not excluded; ethics statement "not applicable"; overlap of dogs with the group's earlier papers not stated.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
