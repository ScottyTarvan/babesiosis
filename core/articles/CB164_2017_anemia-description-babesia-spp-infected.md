## Abstract-derived summary

Over 2015-2017, the Iași veterinary faculty identified 21 Babesia-infected dogs among 494 blood samples submitted (4.3%). EDTA blood was assessed for red cell indices, reticulocytes, sedimentation rate, platelets and leukocytes, and parasitaemia was graded on May-Grünwald-Giemsa smears; associations were tested with Fisher's exact test. Anaemia was recorded in 11 dogs (52.4%). Reticulocytosis occurred in 13 (61.9%), peaking at 78.5%. Lowered MCV, MCH or MCHC was found in 17 dogs (81%; p<0.02), anisocytosis in 19 (associated with anaemia, p<0.001), poikilocytosis in 19 (90.5%), and raised ESR in 10 of 11 heavily infected dogs (p<0.02). The authors attribute anaemia to parasite toxins and autoimmune red cell destruction, and read monocytosis as chronic, immune-driven disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia is the dominant abnormality, red cell indices fall with increasing parasitaemia, and monocytosis indicates chronicity with an autoimmune component.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a small regional haematological profile suggesting a regenerative, anisocytic anaemia with fragmentation morphology. It does not demonstrate an autoimmune mechanism: no Coombs test, spherocyte count or antibody data are shown, and the monocytosis and parasitaemia-correlation claims are not supported by data in the full text. The 52.4% anaemia figure and the 81% abnormal-indices figure are not reconciled.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=21; no controls or reference intervals reported; species not molecularly identified; co-infections and prior treatment not addressed; parasitaemia grading method unquantified; inconsistent period descriptions (two years vs 2015-2017); journal and volume unconfirmed; no abstract in OpenAlex.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
