## Abstract-derived summary

In Belgrade the highest number of acute B. canis cases is recorded between February and May, coinciding with favourable conditions for ticks. The authors tested whether the acute phase response is stronger in dogs falling ill in that window than at other times of year. Sixty-three client-owned dogs with acute infection had routine haematology and biochemistry, serum amyloid A, ceruloplasmin, paraoxonase-1, fibrinogen, anti-B. canis IgG and parasitaemia measured. Two composite indices combining positive and negative acute phase proteins with iron or albumin were derived; Kruskal-Wallis and logistic regression were used. In Feb-May, creatinine, albumin, iron and parasitaemia were lower, and higher index values were more likely. The authors read this as a stronger response plus probable haemodilution, implying a more acute course in that season.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Greater acute phase intensity and presumed haemodilution in February-May indicate a more acute course of B. canis infection than in other periods.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Links season of presentation to the magnitude of the systemic inflammatory (acute phase) response and to lower parasitaemia in natural infection. It is an association; the abstract does not show that season causes a different pathophysiology, nor that the indices predict outcome. Lower albumin, iron and creatinine may reflect haemodilution, negative acute phase behaviour or hydration status rather than disease severity per se.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single centre; species not stated to be molecularly confirmed; enrolment period and group sizes per season not in abstract; no healthy controls or outcome data; composite indices not externally validated; haemodilution presumed, not measured; possible confounding by tick stage, dog population and timing of presentation.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
