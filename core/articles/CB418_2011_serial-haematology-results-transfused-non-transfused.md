## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective longitudinal study followed blood counts in 86 dogs naturally infected with Babesia rossi (32 transfused, 54 not). Counts were taken at presentation and again at 24 hours, day 3 and day 6 after diagnosis and diminazene treatment. All dogs showed a mild to moderately regenerative anaemia with normal red cell size and haemoglobin concentration. Transfused dogs were more likely to have an inflammatory leukogram early on, and a left shift often occurred without a raised segmented neutrophil count. Marked thrombocytopenia was common and had resolved within a week. Transfusion was reported to relieve the anaemia but did not measurably change white cell or platelet responses, and age had little influence on the counts. The authors judged the red and white cell responses weaker than the haemolysis and inflammation would predict, and raised the possibility of an immune-mediated mechanism for the low platelet counts.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** During treatment of dogs with B. rossi babesiosis, the anaemia was mildly to moderately regenerative and the inflammatory leukogram was mainly a left shift without neutrophilia. Both responses were weaker than expected, and the quick recovery of platelet counts suggested a possible immune-mediated mechanism for the thrombocytopenia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a large natural-infection series that gives the time course of red cell, white cell and platelet counts over the first 6 days of treatment. Comparisons between the transfused and non-transfused groups are confounded by anaemia severity and age, because the clinician chose who was transfused. The immune-mediated explanation for thrombocytopenia is inferred from how fast platelet counts recovered and was not tested directly. Complicated cases were excluded, so the findings describe less severe disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** transfusion decision was subjective and not randomised, so the two groups overlap in haematocrit and differ in age; dogs with complicated babesiosis were excluded; day of infection unknown; no healthy control group; follow-up limited to 6 days with fewer dogs sampled at later time points; no mechanistic testing of the platelet findings

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - 96 client-owned dogs presenting to the Onderstepoort Veterinary Academic Hospital were considered, and 86 met the inclusion criteria (full text)
  - Diagnosis was made on a thin capillary blood smear and confirmed by PCR; dogs were excluded for E. canis morulae or PCR, B. vogeli PCR positivity, a positive in-saline agglutination test, recent vaccination or cortisone treatment, or severe complicated babesiosis (full text)
  - The decision to transfuse rested with the attending clinician, with PCV below 15 % as a general guideline; whole blood or packed red cells were used, and the type of transfusion had no significant effect on any blood cell parameter (full text)
  - Median haematocrit at presentation was 0.28 in the non-transfused group and 0.09 in the transfused group (full text)
  - Median platelet count at presentation was 3.6 x 10^9/L in the non-transfused group and 4.1 x 10^9/L in the transfused group, and was within the reference interval by day 6 in both groups (full text)
  - The transfused group was significantly younger than the non-transfused group (median 10 vs 21 months, P = 0.003) (full text)
  - 82 dogs survived and 4 died (2 in each group); the cause of death was not established because no post-mortem examinations were done (full text)
  - The number of dogs sampled fell from 84 at presentation to 46 on day 6 (full text)
