## Abstract-derived summary

This descriptive ultrasound study examined the abdomen of 72 dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis and compared them with seven healthy Beagles. Splenomegaly with a diffusely heterogeneous parenchyma and generally reduced echogenicity was the most common finding in the infected dogs. Hepatomegaly with diffuse hypoechogenicity and increased bilateral renal cortical echogenicity were seen more often in the severe uncomplicated and complicated groups. Mean renal resistive indices were 0.66, 0.73 and 0.71, and pulsatility indices 1.35, 1.91 and 1.73, for the mild uncomplicated, severe uncomplicated and complicated groups respectively. A markedly higher pulsatility index in the complicated and severe uncomplicated groups was correlated with anaemia and with the degree of renal damage. The authors suggest ultrasonography could support diagnosis and follow-up of the disease and its systemic complications.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Ultrasonography can support diagnosis and monitoring of canine babesiosis and its systemic complications, and diffuse heterogeneous splenomegaly can support a diagnosis of Babesia infection, given how common it is in these patients.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Splenic changes were described in all infected dogs, and liver and kidney changes across severity groups; the renal pulsatility index was associated with anaemia and with the degree of renal damage. The design is descriptive, so the findings show association with severity, not a mechanism. The only comparator is healthy dogs, so the abstract does not show whether these changes are specific to Babesia infection.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; Babesia species and diagnostic method not stated in the abstract; control group small (7 healthy Beagles) with no matching of the infected dogs described in the abstract; group definitions and statistical tests not available from the abstract; no concurrent-disease exclusion described in the abstract.
