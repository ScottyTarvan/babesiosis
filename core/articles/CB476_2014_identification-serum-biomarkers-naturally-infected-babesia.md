## Abstract-derived summary

This case-control study applied serum proteomics to canine babesiosis. The authors note that the disease's impact on serum proteins had not been studied. Serum came from 25 dogs with naturally acquired Babesia canis canis infection, sampled at admission and again on treatment days 1 and 6, and from healthy dogs. Pooled samples were separated by two-dimensional gel electrophoresis in triplicate. Image analysis flagged 64 spots with differing abundance (p ≤ 0.05), and 49 spots showed at least a twofold change. Six spots were analysed by tandem mass spectrometry, identifying proteins tied to the acute phase response, complement and coagulation pathways, apolipoproteins and vitamin D metabolism. The authors interpret the results as supporting haemolysis and acute phase response as the main pathogenic mechanisms, and propose candidate serum markers for monitoring disease and for further proteomic work.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The findings are taken to confirm haemolysis and the acute phase response as the two dominant pathogenic mechanisms of babesiosis. The identified proteins are offered as candidate serum biomarkers for clinical monitoring.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Differences in protein abundance between infected and healthy dogs were associated with infection; the design does not show that these proteins drive the mechanisms the authors name. Because sera were pooled before electrophoresis, spot-level differences are not available per dog, so the markers remain candidates that need validation in individual animals. The mechanistic reading rests on the known functions of the identified proteins.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** pooled samples run in triplicate rather than individual dogs; only six spots identified by mass spectrometry; low sensitivity of 2DE gels, as the authors note; no severity or outcome comparison in the abstract; single clinic (full text).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Dogs were admitted to the Clinic for Internal Diseases, Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, University of Zagreb, Croatia; 8 were female and 17 male, aged 2 months to 10 years (full text)
  - Diagnosis was by parasites on stained thin blood smears, with the subspecies confirmed by PCR (full text)
  - All infected dogs received one subcutaneous dose of imidocarb (6 mg/kg) on admission, and all survived and recovered (full text)
  - The control group was 10 healthy mixed-breed dogs aged 2 to 10 years (6 males, 4 females), judged healthy by examination, haematology and biochemistry (full text)
  - Table 2 lists 17 proteins identified from the six spots, including alpha-1-acid glycoprotein, haptoglobin, hemopexin, serotransferrin, antithrombin-III, alpha-2-antiplasmin, complement C3, clusterin, apolipoproteins A-I and A-IV and vitamin D-binding protein (full text)
  - Five of the six spots were more abundant in infected dogs; the spot containing antithrombin-III, vitamin D-binding protein and albumin was less abundant (full text)
  - In total, 37 spots met both P ≤ 0.05 and fold change ≥2, with power above 0.8 (full text)
  - CRP and haptoglobin were also measured by automated assays: CRP differed between each time point and from healthy dogs, and haptoglobin was lower on day 1 than on day 6 and than in healthy dogs (full text)
  - The authors state that multiple proteins were identified per spot and that 2DE is insensitive, and suggest depleting abundant proteins in future work (full text)
  - The study was approved by the ethics committee of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, University of Zagreb, with owner consent (full text)
