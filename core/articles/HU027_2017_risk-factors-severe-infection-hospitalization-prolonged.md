## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers reviewed 128 people with *Babesia microti* infection confirmed by blood smear or real-time PCR, gathered across 16 years, to find indicators of serious complications that required admission or extended drug treatment. People without a spleen or with immune-weakening conditions were more likely to have severe disease, to be admitted, and to receive long antimicrobial courses (all P<0.01). Vomiting or nausea, diarrhoea and raised bilirubin were also predictive of severe infection, admission and prolonged therapy (all P<0.01). Co-infection with Lyme disease was associated with fewer admissions, and severity and treatment length resembled those without Lyme disease. The abstract gives P values only, without effect sizes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Human cohort accrued over 16 years; the abstract does not state the setting or whether data collection was retrospective, and reports univariable P values only.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Asplenia, immunocompromise, gastrointestinal symptoms and hyperbilirubinaemia mark patients likely to have severe babesiosis, hospitalisation and prolonged treatment.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category SEV, cited in several connections. Asplenia/immunocompromise: **directly demonstrated in both** for the exposure-outcome relation; experimental splenectomy in *B. vogeli* Beagles gave severe anaemia (CB021), with supporting single case CB120. Immune deficit (CB008, CB141): **mechanistically plausible**. Bilirubin and haemolysis (CB040, CB099, CB074): **mechanistically plausible**, since CB040 found no independent predictor. Lyme co-infection versus canine co-infections (CB060, CB116, CB126): **speculative**. Differences: older adults with comorbidity and *B. microti*, usually mild in immunocompetent hosts, versus young dogs and virulent *B. canis* or *B. rossi* that cause severe disease with an intact spleen; splenic filtration is inferred, not measured.
- **Key limitations:** Small sample; P values without effect sizes; no parasitaemia analysis in the abstract; asplenia pooled with immunocompromise; comorbidity confounding.
