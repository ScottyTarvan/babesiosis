## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined the serum urea to creatinine ratio (UCR) in dogs infected with Babesia canis and related it to arterial blood pressure. The ratio was reported as raised in the infected dogs overall. Of 48 infected dogs, 23 were azotaemic and 25 were not, and the azotaemic group had a significantly higher mean ratio. Across all 48 dogs, the ratio correlated negatively with systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure (r from -0.3182 to -0.3909), although the correlations were not high, and azotaemic dogs analysed alone showed no significant correlation. The authors propose that low blood pressure may contribute to the raised ratio but explains it only in part. They note hyperureagenesis and myocardial injury as other suggested contributors and call for further work on cardiac troponins, ammonia and faecal occult blood.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Falling blood pressure seems to explain only part of the increased UCR in canine babesiosis. Further studies are needed to identify the other causes, including cardiac troponins, ammonia and occult faecal blood.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The negative correlations between UCR and arterial pressure fit a haemodynamic contribution to the raised ratio, but the coefficients are low and the link was absent in azotaemic dogs. The data show an association only and do not show that hypotension raises the ratio. Alternative contributors named by the authors were not measured here.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only, so methods, setting and the comparison group for the overall UCR increase are not visible; modest correlation coefficients; no significant correlation in azotaemic dogs; species identification method not stated; no adjustment for confounders reported in the abstract.
