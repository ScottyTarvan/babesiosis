## Abstract-derived summary

The authors studied 30 dogs with babesiosis in Nanjing, China, to describe how the disease presents and which species is involved. The dogs were assessed by clinical history, physical examination, blood counts, PCR-RFLP of an 18S rRNA fragment and sequencing. Lethargy and anorexia were recorded in every dog, while pale or icteric mucous membranes were seen in 80%, fever in 70% and dark urine in 70%. Blood counts showed anaemia and thrombocytopenia as the main abnormalities. Molecular results pointed to B. gibsoni as the predominant species among the cases during the study period. The authors present the findings as reference information on canine babesiosis in China.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that the results provide valuable information for understanding the epidemiology of canine babesiosis in China, having found anaemia and thrombocytopenia as the main blood abnormalities and B. gibsoni as the main species in the cases.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a clinicopathological case series of hospital-presenting dogs, so it describes the haematological picture of B. gibsoni infection rather than testing mechanisms. It supports anaemia and thrombocytopenia as frequent findings and gives molecular species confirmation for the series. Its epidemiological framing is limited, because the dogs were hospital cases and not a population sample.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group, so haematology was judged only against reference ranges; single hospital; full-length sequencing in only 3 of 30 dogs; no biochemistry, renal, coagulation or outcome data; recruitment method not described; no severity grading or follow-up.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Dogs were of different breeds and ages and were collected at the Animal Hospital of Nanjing Agricultural University between September 2012 and September 2013 (full text)
  - 80% of the dogs were male and 20% female; the median age was 48 months (range 2 to 108) (full text)
  - Diagnosis relied on clinical examination and intra-erythrocytic parasites on Giemsa-stained blood smears (small pear-shaped forms) (full text)
  - All 30 dogs had RBC, haemoglobin and haematocrit below the reference values; 29 dogs had platelet counts below the reference range and one was within it (full text)
  - Mean RBC was 2.2 x 10^6/ul (SD 1.1), mean haemoglobin 4.8 g/dl (SD 2.2), mean haematocrit 15.3% (SD 6.7) and mean platelet count 63.5 x 10^3/ul (SD 60.2); the mean values for MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW and the white cell and differential counts were within reference ranges (full text)
  - The 339 bp PCR product from all 30 dogs was cut by Hinf I into 185 bp and 154 bp fragments, consistent with B. gibsoni (full text)
  - The three sequenced full-length 18S rRNA genes were identical and showed 99 to 100% homology with B. gibsoni isolates from Japan and the USA (full text)
