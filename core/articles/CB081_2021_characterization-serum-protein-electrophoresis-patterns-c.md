## Abstract-derived summary

The authors profiled serum protein fractions and C-reactive protein (CRP) in dogs with single natural infections by Ehrlichia canis, Babesia canis or Hepatozoon canis, compared with healthy dogs. From 650 canine blood samples collected at Bangkok-area clinics, suspected blood parasite cases were screened on buffy coat smears and PCR-confirmed. Serum was fractionated by agarose gel electrophoresis and CRP quantified by fluorescence immunoassay. In all three single-infection groups, relative albumin and the albumin/globulin ratio were lower and β2-globulin higher than in normal dogs (p<0.05). γ-globulin rose in the E. canis and H. canis groups. A monoclonal-type band pattern appeared in some E. canis and B. canis infections, whereas H. canis showed β-γ bridging. CRP was raised in infected dogs. The authors suggest both measures can help monitor infected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum protein electrophoresis patterns and CRP can be used to monitor health status and blood parasite problems in infected dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows a negative acute-phase albumin response with raised β2-globulin (acute phase proteins) and raised CRP in PCR-confirmed B. canis dogs, consistent with systemic inflammation. It does not establish mechanism, severity grading or prognostic value, and the CRP link to pathogenesis is inferential. The abstract does not report confirmation of the monoclonal-type pattern (e.g. immunofixation).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small groups (B. canis n=13; CRP n=6 per group, not measured in normal dogs); subspecies not determined; handling of co-infections only implied by "single infection"; no severity or outcome data; clinical status, disease duration and treatment not described in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
