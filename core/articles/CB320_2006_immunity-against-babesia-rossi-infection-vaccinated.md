## Abstract-derived summary

Earlier work showed that soluble parasite antigens (SPA) from several Babesia species can induce protective immunity, but vaccines based on B. rossi culture supernatant alone had given no or only partial protection. This experimental study vaccinated dogs with a mixture of SPA from B. rossi and B. canis and then challenged them with B. rossi. Vaccinated dogs showed milder clinical signs that resolved without treatment, along with lower parasitaemia and lower SPA levels in blood. Dogs that had received three vaccinations had no infected erythrocytes detectable on smears. Three of four unvaccinated control dogs needed chemotherapy to avoid death, while the fourth had a brief self-limiting parasitaemia. Vaccination did not stop a temporary anaemia from developing. The authors conclude the mixed-antigen vaccine protects against heterologous B. canis challenge and against B. rossi.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** A vaccine containing soluble antigens from in vitro culture supernatants of B. rossi and B. canis induced protection in dogs against challenge with B. rossi, as had been shown earlier for B. canis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In this challenge model, vaccinated dogs had milder disease and lower parasitaemia than unvaccinated controls, which is consistent with vaccine-induced protection; the design does not show which immune mechanism was responsible. A transient anaemia still developed in vaccinated dogs, so vaccination did not prevent it. The report centres on vaccine efficacy, so the host-response data are limited to clinical signs, parasitaemia, soluble antigen and anaemia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small control group (four dogs); size of vaccinated groups not given in the abstract; no quantitative haematology or immune measures in the abstract; efficacy-focused design; full text not read.
