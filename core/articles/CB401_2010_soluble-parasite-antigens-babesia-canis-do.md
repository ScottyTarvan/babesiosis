## Abstract-derived summary

Soluble parasite antigens (SPA) of Babesia canis have been shown to induce protective immunity as a vaccine, so the authors asked what part these antigens play in the disease itself. They focused on the plasma kallikrein system, which earlier work had linked to babesiosis, malaria and trypanosomosis. In dogs experimentally infected with B. canis, prekallikrein concentrations fell around peaks of parasitaemia, while kallikrein concentrations did not change. Infusing dogs with varying doses of SPA, or adding SPA to normal dog plasma in vitro, left (pre-)kallikrein unchanged. Infusion of SPA, and of stroma prepared from normal dog erythrocytes, was followed by a rise in C-reactive protein. The authors conclude that the kallikrein pathway may contribute to the pathology but is not triggered directly by SPA, and that part of the inflammatory response may reflect exposure of self molecules.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The kallikrein pathway may be involved in B. canis-associated pathology, but SPA do not directly activate it. The C-reactive protein response to SPA and to erythrocyte stroma suggests that the inflammatory response may partly come from the release and exposure of self molecules.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In experimentally infected dogs, lower prekallikrein at peak parasitaemia was observed without a change in kallikrein, which could reflect a change in the pathway, but the abstract does not show the mechanism. The negative SPA infusion and in vitro results argue against SPA as a direct trigger, which narrows the possible routes rather than identifying the true one. The C-reactive protein finding links the acute-phase response to antigen and to host erythrocyte material, and it ties the coagulation-related kallikrein question to the inflammatory axis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only, so numbers of dogs, doses, time points and statistics are not known; species identification method not stated; experimental infection and infusion may not mirror natural disease; unchanged kallikrein does not exclude activation that was missed by the assay or timing; the C-reactive protein response cannot be attributed to a single component from the abstract.
