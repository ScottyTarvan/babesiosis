## Abstract-derived summary

The authors aimed to describe clinical signs and haematobiochemical changes in dogs from Gujarat, India, infected with small-form Babesia, and to interpret them pathophysiologically. Of 79 suspect dogs examined by blood smear, 16 showed small intraerythrocytic piroplasms and were classed as B. gibsoni cases on clinical signs plus Giemsa morphology. Presentations were varied and non-specific. Relative to healthy dogs, infected dogs had lower haemoglobin and red cell counts (p<0.01), higher mean neutrophil and eosinophil values with fewer lymphocytes (p<0.01), raised ALP, ALT, AST and globulin (p<0.01), and lower albumin (p<0.05). The authors conclude that B. gibsoni infection has multisystem effects with atypical haematobiochemical changes that may assist diagnosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni causes multisystemic effects with atypical haematobiochemical changes that may aid diagnosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a regenerative-status-unknown anaemia (Hb 9.33 vs 13.23 g/dl, full text), leukogram shift and raised hepatic enzymes in naturally infected dogs, consistent with haemolysis plus hepatic involvement. It describes a clinicopathological profile, not mechanism; hepatic enzyme and globulin changes are associations only. Platelet counts did not differ (full text), which is atypical for small-Babesia infection and merits caution.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Smear-only species identification (other small piroplasms not excluded); 16 cases vs 8 controls; co-infections not tested (authors themselves speculate on concurrent infection); full-text tables and text disagree on albumin direction and some significance markers; no reticulocyte or immune-mediated testing; treatment history not reported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
