## Abstract-derived summary

This narrative review examines four vector-borne diseases of European dogs: anaplasmosis, ehrlichiosis, babesiosis and borreliosis. It frames them against changing climate and ecosystems, rising animal and human movement, and growing resistance to insecticides and acaricides. Ticks, fleas, lice and mosquitoes pick up pathogens when they feed on infected animals and can pass them on through their saliva. The authors aimed to describe disease prevalence and health risks, and to condense recent literature on pathophysiology, epidemiology, diagnostic strengths and weaknesses, treatment and prevention. For babesiosis, the full text gives a short pathophysiology passage: intravascular and extravascular haemolysis with immune-mediated contributions, thrombocytopenia, haemostasis-inflammation crosstalk, and possible lung, kidney, liver, pancreas, heart and cerebral involvement. The authors stress that prompt diagnosis and treatment matter for outcome in affected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia infection ranges from asymptomatic to multiorgan failure, and early diagnosis and treatment are critical for outcome.
