## Abstract-derived summary

Ten naive dogs aged six months to one year, kept in Nigeria, were split into five uninfected controls and five dogs inoculated with Babesia canis-positive blood. The infected group was followed clinically and biochemically. Fever and inappetence appeared by day 2 in all infected dogs, lethargy by day 3, and pallor and emaciation by day 4. Later signs included haemoglobinuria in 80% of infected dogs, respiratory distress, nervousness and drooling in 20% each, ascites and body oedema; one dog died on day 8. Serum ALP and ALT were higher, and creatinine and glucose lower, in infected than in control dogs (p < 0.05). The authors concluded that the disease followed an acute as well as a chronic course and that diminazene aceturate was effective against parasitaemia within about two weeks of treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia canis infection in naive Nigerian dogs runs an acute as well as a chronic course, and diminazene aceturate was effective against parasitaemia within about two weeks of treatment. The full text adds that the course is mostly acute and that the infection is associated with intravascular haemolytic anaemia (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a small experimental infection that describes the clinical course and a short panel of serum biochemistry in infected dogs against controls. The higher ALP and ALT and lower glucose in infected dogs are compatible with hepatic involvement and metabolic disturbance, while the authors' suggested explanations for the lower creatinine and glucose (dilution, sepsis, impaired hepatic function) are untested. The study does not test mechanisms, and several numbers in the text, tables and abstract disagree, which weakens confidence in the exact values.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** five dogs per group; Babesia species or subspecies not confirmed by molecular methods; no haematology reported although haemolytic anaemia is claimed; the sampling day for each group mean is not stated; infected dogs were treated with diminazene during the observation period; standard deviations look implausibly small next to the reported ranges; inconsistent figures between abstract, text and tables; single donor passage in a splenectomised puppy.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - The dogs were mongrels of both sexes, judged free of Babesia by physical and clinical examination before challenge; their source is not stated (full text)
  - The inoculum came from blood of a B. canis-positive dog passaged through a splenectomised puppy used as donor; each infected dog received 1 ml, stated as "104" parasitized erythrocytes per ml (probably 10^4, the exponent being lost in the PDF) (full text)
  - Vital signs were taken on alternate days for 14 days and clinical signs daily; blood for biochemistry was collected daily from the infected group and compared with the controls (full text)
  - Infected dogs had higher temperature (39.4 ± 1.4 vs 38.7 ± 0.81 °C), pulse (129 ± 44.21 vs 76 ± 15.00 beats/min) and respiratory rate (31 ± 12.76 vs 21 ± 4.56 breaths/min) than controls (Table 2, full text)
  - Table 3 gives glucose in infected dogs as 2.86 ± 2.4 mmol/l versus 5.35 ± 2.1 in controls, while the abstract and results text give 3.80 ± 2.3 for the infected group (full text)
  - Respiratory distress is 20% in the abstract and Table 1 but 10% in the results text; the diminazene dose is 3.5 mg/kg in the methods but 0.3 mg/kg in the abstract and conclusion (full text)
  - Ascites occurred in two dogs (day 10), body oedema in one (day 12) and hair erection in one (day 13) (Table 1, full text)
  - The authors report a rise in ALP of two- to four-fold in most infected dogs from day 4, and a fall in blood glucose in most infected dogs; hypoproteinaemia and hypoalbuminaemia are mentioned in the discussion but no values are given (full text)
  - The authors suggest dilution as a possible reason for the lower creatinine, and sepsis, anorexia and impaired hepatic function for the lower glucose; these are their interpretations, not tested (full text)
  - Data were analysed with one-way ANOVA, p < 0.05 taken as significant; ethical approval came from the university's Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (full text)
