## Abstract-derived summary

The authors described laboratory findings in 23 dogs from north-eastern Italy with naturally acquired babesiosis attributed to a large Babesia form. They used history, examination, haematology, biochemistry, haemostasis tests, protein electrophoresis and urinalysis. Most dogs (74%) had returned 5-15 days earlier from hunting trips to Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia or Hungary. Dehydration was universal, and apathy, inappetence and fever were common. Anaemia affected 74% and was mostly mild to moderate, normocytic, normochromic and seldom regenerative; it was classed haemolytic in 70% and non-haemolytic in 30%. Leucopenia occurred in 69%, while every dog had thrombocytopenia and raised fibrinogen. D-dimer and fibrin degradation products rose together in four dogs, antithrombin fell slightly in 11, and aPTT was clearly prolonged in one. Liver enzymes, CK and bilirubin were mildly raised and iron was low.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Descriptive case series, apparently retrospective (PubMed MeSH term Retrospective Studies); publication type Journal Article.
- **Relevance:** Cited in Q2 as a prior study with coagulation tests and lymphocyte morphology but no cytokine, myeloperoxidase or NET measurement, so it did not examine inflammation and coagulation jointly. It provides a descriptive European baseline (thrombocytopenia, hyperfibrinogenaemia, little overt DIC) against which B. rossi coagulopathy can be contrasted, but its different parasite, geography and era limit direct comparison.
- **Key limitations:** Small descriptive series without controls or outcome analysis; retrospective; parasite identified only as a large form (species and molecular confirmation not given in the abstract), so attribution to a single species is uncertain; travel history suggests imported infections; single time point; no inflammatory mediator measurement; findings reported as frequencies without tests of association; 2005 assays and case definitions may not match current practice.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
