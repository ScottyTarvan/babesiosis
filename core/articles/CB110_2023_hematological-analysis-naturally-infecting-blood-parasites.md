## Abstract-derived summary

Blood from 122 dogs at a southern Thai shelter was examined to see how single and multiple blood-parasite infections alter haematology. Infections with Anaplasma platys, Babesia vogeli and Ehrlichia canis were confirmed by PCR, and uninfected, single, double and multiple infection groups were compared with Kruskal-Wallis and Dwass-Steel-Critchlow-Fligner tests. The authors report that all infected dogs had lower red cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit and platelet count than uninfected dogs. Triple-infected dogs had the lowest values, but differences from double and single infections were not significant. They propose that triple infection with A. platys, B. vogeli and E. canis produces more severe disease than fewer infections, and suggest haematology can support health management of subclinically infected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Triple A. platys, B. vogeli and E. canis infection caused more severe disease than double or single infections.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive haematology in subclinical shelter dogs; the Babesia-specific signal is weak and cannot be separated from co-infecting pathogens. In Table 1 (full text) the single B. vogeli group (platelets 89.3 vs 127.7 x10^3/µL; RBC 6.17 vs 6.40 x10^6/µL) shares significance letters with uninfected dogs, so the abstract's blanket "all infected dogs" statement is not supported for B. vogeli alone. The severity inference conflicts with the authors' own non-significant triple-versus-double comparison.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** few single B. vogeli and A. platys infections, no A. platys + B. vogeli doubles, non-random sampling, single time point and site, predominantly female shelter population, means reported despite non-parametric tests, no clinical severity or outcome data, internal text/table inconsistencies.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
