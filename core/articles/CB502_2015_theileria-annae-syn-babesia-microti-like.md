## Abstract-derived summary

Veterinarians in north-western Spain lacked specific diagnostic tools and epidemiological information on piroplasmosis caused by Theileria annae, a small piroplasm also called Babesia microti-like. One hundred and twenty dogs with a clinical suspicion of piroplasmosis were examined by Giemsa-stained blood smear, IFAT and PCR with sequencing. PCR confirmed T. annae in 75 dogs. Microscopy and IFAT each showed moderate agreement with PCR and moderate sensitivity (76% and 73.3%). Among the 75 confirmed dogs, pale mucous membranes, anorexia and apathy were the commonest signs, and blood counts showed severe regenerative anaemia together with low platelet counts. Infection was more frequent in dogs aged three years or less. Antibody titres were low. The authors advise molecular confirmation of the species and report cases in Asturias, next to the known endemic area.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinical case series)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Microscopy had moderate sensitivity in acute T. annae infection and IFAT titres were low, so the infecting species should be confirmed by molecular methods. The authors suggest the disease also affects dogs in Asturias, outside the known endemic area of Galicia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The paper gives a clinicopathological profile of PCR-confirmed T. annae infection in dogs, in which regenerative anaemia and thrombocytopenia were the main haematological findings. These findings were associated with infection status in comparison with non-infected dogs. A large part of the paper concerns diagnostic test agreement, so the pathophysiological content is limited to the descriptive blood count and biochemistry data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no healthy control group, because the comparison dogs were also clinically suspected of piroplasmosis; single time point with no outcome follow-up; enrolment based on clinical suspicion, which shapes the reported clinical signs; platelet counts and liver enzymes missing for some dogs; some figures differ between text and tables; taxonomy of T. annae unresolved.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Dogs were enrolled from several veterinary clinics in NW Spain between June 2012 and January 2014, and large piroplasms (B. canis, B. vogeli) were first ruled out by microscopy in the clinics (full text)
  - Of the 120 dogs, 103 came from Galicia (73 PCR-positive for T. annae) and 17 from Asturias (2 PCR-positive) (full text)
  - PCR detected B. canis in 12 and B. gibsoni in 3 of the 120 dogs (full text); the text does not say explicitly that these were among the 45 T. annae-negative dogs
  - Regenerative anaemia was found in 57 of 72 infected dogs with data (79.6%); anaemia was most often hypochromic and macrocytic; thrombocytopenia was found in 58.3% (full text)
  - Infected versus non-infected means: erythrocytes 3.28 vs 4.9 x10^6/ul, haematocrit 27.12 vs 35.16%, haemoglobin 7.75 vs 11.04 g/dl, platelets 158.5 vs 211.2 x10^3/ul (p = 0.019); MCV was higher and MCHC lower in infected dogs; leukocyte count did not differ significantly (full text)
  - Hyperglobulinaemia was found in 33 of 65 infected dogs, elevated hepatic enzyme activities in 25 of 57, and azotaemia in 7 of 71 (not significantly different from non-infected dogs) (full text)
  - Other signs in infected dogs included fever (29.6%), haematuria (18.5%) and splenomegaly (25%) (full text)
  - Anti-Leishmania infantum antibodies were found in 13 of 120 dogs; no anti-Ehrlichia canis antibodies were found in the 75 confirmed cases (full text)
  - Infection was associated with age of 3 years or less (81.54% vs 46.8% positive), size of 22 kg or less and hunting lifestyle; no association with sex, breed or season (full text)
  - The text gives an IFAT sensitivity of 77.3% whereas the abstract and Table 2 give 73.3% (full text)
