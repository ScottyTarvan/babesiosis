## Abstract-derived summary

Measuring serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) in dogs depends on pyridoxal 5-phosphate (P5P) to activate the enzyme, and the study asked whether omitting P5P from the assay underestimates ALT. The authors examined dogs with babesiosis, dogs with suspected hepatocellular damage, and anorexic dogs whose P5P stores might be depleted. Serum from 105 Babesia-infected dogs and 120 healthy controls was tested with and without added P5P. ALT was higher with P5P in the infected dogs and in the 14 dogs with suspected hepatocellular damage, but the duration of anorexia had no effect on the difference between methods. The authors judged the differences unlikely to cause misdiagnosis of liver disease in infected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (assay method comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Adding P5P gave a statistically significant increase in measured ALT, but it was unlikely to lead to incorrect clinical diagnoses of hepatocellular damage in dogs with babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study reports hepatic enzyme data in infected dogs, but its main purpose is analytical rather than pathophysiological. The ALT difference between methods was similar in infected dogs and controls (full text) and was larger in the subset with high ALT, which the authors link to reduced P5P availability (full text), a suggestion they could not test. It adds little on mechanisms of liver injury in babesiosis beyond showing that ALT values from the routine method are close to those with P5P.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species not identified (blood smear only); hepatocellular damage defined by the same ALT result being tested; anorexia duration reported by owners and limited to about 5 days; controls analysed in a separate, later run; no data on outcome, anaemia grade or other liver tests; P5P or inhibitor explanations not measured; single centre.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

  - Controls were judged clinically healthy on clinical and blood smear evaluation, with no anorexia in the week before sampling (full text)
  - Infected dogs were split by days of anorexia into groups of 31, 30, 10, 21 and 13 dogs (full text)
  - Suspected hepatocellular damage was defined as admission ALT of 80 U/L or more by the method without P5P (reference interval 17-47 U/L): 14 dogs, versus 91 without (full text)
  - Mean ALT in controls was 28.9 U/L with P5P and 25.0 U/L without, a difference of 3.9 U/L (full text)
  - In infected dogs the mean difference was 4.2 U/L, and 90 of 105 (86%) had higher ALT with P5P (full text)
  - In the 14 dogs with suspected hepatocellular damage, mean ALT was 256.1 U/L with P5P and 242.3 U/L without (full text)
  - The mean difference between methods in those 14 dogs was 13.8 U/L, as in Table 1; one sentence of the text gives 15.2 U/L, and 13.8 is used here (full text)
  - One dog had ALT of 1576 U/L with P5P and 1480 U/L without, and had been anorexic for 2 days (full text)
  - The authors report an average 16% increase in ALT with P5P; the difference between methods showed no correlation with days of anorexia (r = 0.0103) (full text)
  - Classification of dogs as with or without suspected hepatocellular damage was the same with both methods (full text)
