## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective case review describes seven immunocompromised dogs infected with a large, unnamed Babesia species. Six dogs had been splenectomised, and the seventh was receiving oncolytic drugs. Lethargy and anorexia were each reported in six dogs, fever in four and dark urine in three. All seven dogs were mildly anaemic, and six had severe thrombocytopenia. A broad PCR for Babesia sensu stricto was positive in every dog, while specific assays for B. canis and B. gibsoni were negative. The 18S rRNA gene sequences matched a large Babesia isolated earlier. Serological reactivity to other Babesia species was inconsistent. Imidocarb was given to five dogs and atovaquone with azithromycin to one, with some favourable responses, and one untreated dog remained a clinically stable carrier. The authors advise PCR testing and suggest that asplenia or chemotherapy could be risk factors for persistent infection or illness.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with dark urine, anaemia and thrombocytopenia should be tested for this Babesia by PCR, because serology is not sufficient for diagnosis. Asplenia, chemotherapy, or both might be risk factors for persistent infection, illness, or both.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series documents anaemia and thrombocytopenia in dogs infected with a large Babesia that is not B. canis or B. gibsoni, almost all of them splenectomized. Because there is no control group and the dogs had other conditions or treatments, the haematological changes cannot be attributed to the infection alone. The link between asplenia or chemotherapy and persistent infection is an association proposed by the authors, not a tested effect.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-only appraisal; very small retrospective series; no control group reported; cases identified through a diagnostic laboratory, so selection is likely; clinical data taken from medical records; treatment responses described informally; setting not stated in the abstract.
