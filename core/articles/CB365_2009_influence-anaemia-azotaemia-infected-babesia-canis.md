## Abstract-derived summary

Blood and serum samples from 127 dogs with Babesia canis infection in Poland were sorted into a non-anaemic group (A) and an anaemic group (B). Group B was split further according to whether mean corpuscular haemoglobin concentration was raised (B1) or not (B2). Red cell count, haematocrit, haemoglobin and MCHC were measured with an automated haematology analyser, and serum creatinine and urea with a clinical chemistry analyser. Group means were compared using Student's t-test. Urea and creatinine did not differ significantly between the anaemic and non-anaemic dogs, nor between the two anaemic subgroups. No correlation emerged between anaemia and azotaemia, or between raised MCHC and azotaemia. The authors concluded that anaemia on its own does not appear to influence azotaemia in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia, as the only factor, has no influence on azotaemia in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In these 127 infected dogs, serum urea and creatinine did not differ between anaemic and non-anaemic animals, or by MCHC status. The result does not show that anaemia drives azotaemia in canine babesiosis, and the abstract does not examine other factors that could contribute to it. A comparison of group means cannot show mechanism.
