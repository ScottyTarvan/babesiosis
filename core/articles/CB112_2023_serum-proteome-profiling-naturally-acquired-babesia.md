## Abstract-derived summary

The authors profiled serum proteomes by label-based shotgun mass spectrometry in dogs naturally infected with B. rossi, the most pathogenic of the large canine Babesia, and in healthy dogs. The cohort comprised 32 infected animals, split into 18 uncomplicated and 14 complicated cases, plus 20 controls. Seventy-eight proteins differed in abundance across the three groups. Annotation of these proteins and their pathways pointed to differences in haemostasis, innate immunity, lipid metabolism and inflammation. Some proteins separated mild from severe disease and are proposed as serum severity biomarker candidates. The authors suggest the data may inform host-parasite interaction research and the search for therapeutic targets.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum proteomic differences implicate haemostasis, innate immunity, lipid metabolism and inflammation, and offer candidate markers of severity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A hypothesis-generating severity-stratified omics dataset; pathway enrichment is an association with disease class, not evidence that a given pathway causes complicated disease. Value lies in a public PRIDE dataset and candidate markers (e.g. acute-phase and lipoprotein-related proteins, CD14, lactoferrin) needing independent validation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n; retrospective with a single sampling time point; illness duration, comorbidities, age, sex and breed not fully adjusted; treatment (diminazene, transfusion, fluids) potentially confounding; serum depth modest (128 proteins quantified); Western blot validation limited to two proteins with partly discordant results.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
