## Abstract-derived summary

The authors inoculated six Beagles, half of them splenectomised, with a Chinese B. vogeli strain to describe clinical and laboratory changes in dogs free of other tick-borne infections. Parasites appeared on smears the day after inoculation, and FRET-qPCR was positive in 77 of 86 samples (90%) over 95 days. All dogs developed fever, reduced appetite and lethargy, more marked in splenectomised animals, which also failed to gain weight for three weeks. Regenerative anaemia, thrombocytopenia and leukopenia occurred in every dog and were worse after splenectomy; two splenectomised dogs became critically ill and were withdrawn for treatment. Re-challenge at day 66 caused no clinical disease and no significant change in parasite copy number. Intact dogs had mild disease followed by prolonged subclinical infection, whereas asplenic dogs could develop severe disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that this B. vogeli strain causes only mild signs followed by asymptomatic infection of at least 95 days in intact dogs, but severe, life-threatening disease in splenectomised dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Controlled, co-infection-free data on the natural history of a typically mild species. The splenectomy contrast is consistent with splenic function (parasite clearance and immune control) contributing to severity, with anaemia, thrombocytopenia and leukopenia worse in that group; with three dogs per group and splenectomy confounded with cohort, this is an association rather than an established determinant. It does not identify the effector mechanism (e.g. immune-mediated erythrocyte destruction, platelet consumption versus sequestration). Persistent low-level parasitaemia and absent response to re-challenge suggest premunition, but this is not tested directly.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Six dogs; splenectomy status confounded with cohort and no uninfected controls described in the abstract; single Chinese strain; two severely affected dogs were removed for treatment, so outcome and later data are censored; no mechanistic assays reported in the abstract.
