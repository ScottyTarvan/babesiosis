## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether pulmonary thromboembolism, a possible cause of sudden breathlessness in complicated canine babesiosis, can be seen on lung perfusion imaging. They compared 14 hospitalised dogs of varied breeds that were naturally infected with Babesia against 9 healthy adult Beagles. Every animal had chest radiographs and an arterial blood gas analysis before pulmonary perfusion scintigraphy. The images were inspected for wedge-shaped, pleura-based perfusion defects of the kind expected with clinically relevant embolism. None of the infected dogs showed such defects, and the overall perfusion pattern did not differ significantly between the infected and control groups (P = 1.00). Within this small sample, the imaging therefore gave no evidence of clinically relevant pulmonary thromboembolism in hospitalised dogs with babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (negative imaging finding against healthy controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** In this limited study, the authors concluded that clinically relevant pulmonary thromboembolism was not a complicating factor in hospitalised dogs with babesiosis (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study is a small prospective comparison that tested one specific mechanism, large-vessel pulmonary embolism, and did not find it. The result does not rule out small or partially occluding emboli, nor microvascular pulmonary pathology. Blood gas oxygenation indices were similar in both groups (full text), which fits the imaging result, but the authors suggested that the lower estimated oxygen content in infected dogs could be explained by anaemia (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample; infected dogs selected within a body-weight range to match Beagles; breed mismatch between groups; visual, non-quantitative scan classification; no ventilation scan; no gold-standard test (CT, angiography) or D-dimer; small or partially occluding emboli could not be excluded; mottled scan pattern also seen in controls; species identified by blood smear only.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
