## Abstract-derived summary

This small descriptive series reports the clinical, haematological, biochemical and ultrasonographic findings in eight dogs naturally infected with Babesia gibsoni between December 2016 and December 2017. Icteric membranes, lethargy and fever predominated, and most dogs had splenomegaly. Compared with healthy dogs, infected animals had significantly lower haemoglobin (about 7.6 g/dl), packed cell volume (about 20%), erythrocyte count (about 4.0 million/µl) and platelet count (about 43,000/µl), and higher globulin (about 4.3 g/dl). The authors note that presentations ranged from anorexia to hepatomegaly, splenomegaly or death, so clinical examination alone cannot give a definitive diagnosis. The abstract does not state the diagnostic method, the number or source of controls, or any treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with associational group comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni infection has highly variable clinical manifestations, so diagnosis cannot rest on clinical examination alone.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents an anaemia phenotype with marked thrombocytopenia and splenomegaly (haemolysis and regeneration are not assessed in the abstract); full text adds azotaemia, raised liver enzymes and sonographic renal change, supporting a multisystem phenotype. It does not establish mechanism, causality or prognostic value.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 8; species not molecularly confirmed; no co-infection screening (e.g. Ehrlichia) reported; control source undefined; mean plus dispersion term inconsistently reported (abstract values labelled as spread are SE per Table 1, with implausibly large SE for Hb); abstract states a significant globulin rise although Table 1 marks it non-significant; treatment not described although a clindamycin keyword and "after treatment" figure appear; no outcome data; the journal's scope is entomology/zoology and peer review was not verified (31 days from receipt to acceptance).
