## Abstract-derived summary

The study compared arterial blood pressure in healthy dogs with that in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis, and in infected dogs with complicated versus uncomplicated disease. It also asked whether hypotension contributes to the development of complicated babesiosis and whether it relates to outcome. Fifty dogs were enrolled: 20 healthy and 30 with babesiosis, the latter split into 12 complicated and 18 uncomplicated cases. Infection was identified from Romanowsky-stained blood smears. Infected dogs overall had lower pressures than healthy dogs (mean arterial pressure 83 versus 106). Uncomplicated cases had higher pressures than healthy dogs (mean arterial pressure 116), whereas complicated cases had markedly lower pressures (mean arterial pressure 60). The authors conclude that low blood pressure accompanies complicated disease and recommend repeated pressure measurements to judge how pressure changes relate to course and outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report low blood pressure in complicated canine babesiosis caused by B. canis, and advise repeated pressure readings to judge how pressure changes bear on course and outcome.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Blood pressure differed across the three groups, with lower values in complicated cases and higher values in uncomplicated cases than in healthy dogs. This supports a haemodynamic component in severe disease. The design cannot show whether hypotension contributes to complications or follows from them, and the abstract reports no outcome analysis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; small groups (12 complicated, 18 uncomplicated, 20 healthy); species identified by blood smear only; reported standard deviations are very large relative to the means, so the precision of the values is unclear; measurement method and units are not given in the abstract; no outcome data are reported in the abstract; the abstract text is duplicated and garbled in the source record.
