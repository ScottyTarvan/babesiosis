## Abstract-derived summary

This single-patient case report describes a mixed-breed dog treated for severe haemolytic anaemia linked to Babesia canis infection. Roughly 14 hours after hospital admission, the animal developed respiratory distress and low blood oxygen, which the authors described as suspected Babesia-induced acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). Management relied on lung-protective mechanical ventilation together with neuromuscular blockade, and the dog improved. It survived to discharge and was reported healthy at three-month follow-up. The authors frame this supportive approach as a successful treatment for the respiratory complication, although the abstract gives no ventilator settings, laboratory values or diagnostic criteria for ARDS. The ARDS label therefore rests on the authors' clinical judgement.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Lung-protective ventilation with neuromuscular blockade was associated with survival in a dog with suspected Babesia-associated ARDS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a distinct pulmonary syndrome (hypoxaemic respiratory failure, P:F 140 on full text) arising after haemolytic anaemia, but cannot establish that Babesia caused the lung injury. Full-text confounders include roughly 42 mL/kg pRBC transfusion (TRALI not excluded), an ehrlichiosis co-infection, and an exudative effusion with bacteria on cytology. It is a management and syndrome observation, not a mechanistic one; the authors discuss only generic ARDS mechanisms.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; no histopathology or full echocardiography; no controls; co-infection and transfusion confounding; ARDS criteria partly inferred; a puppy with prior treatment; internal inconsistencies in the full text (discharge day 4 vs 5; initial FiO2 text vs table).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
