## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether the raised bilirubin and jaundice of complicated B. rossi babesiosis arise from primary liver damage or from systemic consequences of disease. Liver tissue from ten naturally infected dogs that succumbed to infection was compared with tissue from four healthy dogs. Infected dogs had low red cell counts, haematocrit and platelets, with higher ALT and urea. Histologically, the spaces of Disse were widely dilated, bile accumulated in the parenchyma, and enlarged Kupffer cells held bile and haemosiderin. Extramedullary erythropoiesis, congestion and diffuse mononuclear infiltrates (mainly monocyte-macrophages) were also seen. Centrilobular hepatocytes showed coagulative necrosis or marked hydropic change, which the authors attribute to severe haemolysis and hypoxia, comparable to malaria and sepsis. They suggest the findings clarify fatal disease mechanisms and may guide treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (necropsy histopathology, associational with controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hepatic injury in fatal B. rossi infection is attributed mainly to haemolysis- and hypoxia-related damage amplified by an activated macrophage response, rather than primary liver failure.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Is compatible with a pathophysiological model in which the liver is a secondary target (centrilobular hypoxic injury, Kupffer cell erythrophagocytosis, cholestasis) in the complicated syndrome. Histology cannot establish causality or temporal sequence; hypoxia and haemolysis are inferred, not measured in tissue.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Fatal cases only (end-stage snapshot); n=10 vs 4 controls, with controls younger, lighter and of different breeds; no survivors or uncomplicated cases for comparison; semi-quantitative scoring.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
