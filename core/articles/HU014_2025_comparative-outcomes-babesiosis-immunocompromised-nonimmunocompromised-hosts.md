## Abstract-derived summary
Retrospective multicentre comparison of 225 adults with confirmed babesiosis (mean age 66), of whom 112 had immunocompromise or asplenia/hyposplenia. This group showed higher median peak parasitaemia (2.8% vs 0.9%), more AKI (24% vs 11%) and ARDS (11% vs 4%), and more red cell (31% vs 17%) and exchange transfusion (18% vs 6%). Treatment lasted longer (median 27 vs 10 days). Twelve-month mortality (7% vs 1%) and recurrence (8% vs 0%) were higher. Haematological malignancy and B-cell-depleting drugs predicted recurrence. Follow-up smears and PCR did not reliably forecast relapse, so the authors call for better monitoring strategies in these high-risk groups.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** retrospective human cohort; AKI is a reported complication only, with no renal biomarkers or mechanism.
- **Authors' conclusion:** immunocompromised or asplenic patients have more severe disease and complications; better monitoring is needed.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** absent spleen is rated directly demonstrated in both hosts for exposure and severity (CB021 splenectomised B. vogeli Beagles severely anaemic; CB120 case); parasitaemia-severity and AKI/ARDS-mortality links also directly demonstrated (CB141, CB025, CB061); impaired adaptive immunity is plausible (CB008, CB052, CB141); the AKI-with-ARDS link is speculative; haemolysis driving renal injury is plausible. Differences: B. microti in older comorbid adults vs virulent canine species in mostly immunocompetent dogs; spleen effect is pooled with immunosuppression; treatments differ.
- **Key limitations:** retrospective; AKI definition not given in abstract; pooled exposure groups; associations unadjusted in abstract.
