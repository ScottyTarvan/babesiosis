## Abstract-derived summary

The abstract (Ukrainian, reviewer's translation) relates infecting Babesia species to clinical course, haematology, biochemistry and treatment response in dogs from Lviv, studied between September 2023 and December 2025. Of 125 dogs, 122 carried B. canis and 3 B. gibsoni; six (4.8%) were subclinical. Reported signs included anorexia, apathy, pallor, pigmenturia, splenomegaly, fever, vomiting, jaundice, hepatomegaly and diarrhoea, without frequencies. Key laboratory findings were thrombocytopenia, anaemia and leukopenia (bi- or pancytopenia), hyperbilirubinaemia, raised liver enzymes, azotaemia, dysglycaemia and varied electrolyte shifts; many dogs had low albumin:globulin and high urea:creatinine ratios. B. canis cases received imidocarb dipropionate, with diminazene aceturate as starter therapy in complicated cases without kidney pathology; they improved within 2–3 days and cleared parasitaemia by day 14. B. gibsoni cases received combination antimicrobials, clearing by day 30. The authors conclude that species and clinicopathological pattern should guide therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The infecting species and the observed clinical, haematological and biochemical features determine the therapeutic strategy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A descriptive clinicopathological profile (cytopenias, cholestatic/hepatocellular and renal markers, electrolyte disturbance) consistent with a multisystem phenotype; it shows co-occurrence, not mechanism. Only 3 B. gibsoni dogs, so species comparison is essentially anecdotal. The title's "mechanisms of development" is not supported by mechanistic data in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group; B. gibsoni n=3; species confirmed by microscopy (PCR only if doubtful), so B. canis subspecies/co-infections not excluded; mixed treatments and supportive care confound outcome; numerical results not given in abstract.
