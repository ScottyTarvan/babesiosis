## Abstract-derived summary

This study from Croatia examined how often systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) occurred in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis canis. Of 332 infected dogs, 226 (68%) met SIRS criteria; most of these satisfied two criteria (151), fewer satisfied three (66), and only nine satisfied all four. MODS criteria were met by 33 dogs (10%): 22 had two organs involved, 10 had three and one had four. Among MODS dogs, renal dysfunction was most frequent (30/33), followed by liver (20/33), muscle (19/33), lung (16/33) and cerebral involvement (3/33). Twenty-two MODS dogs died and eleven survived. Survival was 45% with two affected organs, 10% with three, and none with four. The authors therefore advise that an unfavourable outcome be considered when MODS develops.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** In canine babesiosis caused by B. canis canis, an unfavourable outcome should be considered when MODS develops.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives incidence figures for SIRS and MODS and the pattern of organ involvement in a large group of naturally infected dogs. Survival fell as the number of affected organs rose, which is an association within infected dogs and does not show that organ dysfunction alone determined the outcome. Renal and hepatic involvement dominated, with muscle, lung and cerebral involvement less often.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no uninfected or non-MODS comparison group reported in the abstract; single-country sample (setting not stated in the abstract); Babesia species identification method not stated in the abstract; SIRS and MODS definitions and study period not given in the abstract; no multivariable analysis reported in the abstract
