## Abstract-derived summary

This short communication describes clinical, physiological and laboratory findings in dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis in Jaipur, India. Sixteen affected dogs were compared with healthy dogs. The abstract reports significant changes in haemoglobin (9.37 ± 0.57 g/dL), erythrocyte count (6.24 ± 0.42 × 10^6/µL), platelet count (1.49 ± 0.17 × 10^5/µL), ALT (84.66 ± 15.15 U/L), urea nitrogen (40.12 ± 9.59 mg/dL) and creatinine (2.74 ± 0.68 mg/dL). It lists fever, anorexia, weight loss, dehydration, diarrhoea, depression and lethargy as the commonest signs and notes that BUN, ALT and creatinine exceeded control values. The authors state that clinical and physiological observations reflected disease severity and that babesiosis is a complex multisystem disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Several organs, including liver, spleen and kidney, appear involved, and the haemato-biochemical changes may help gauge severity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a phenotype of haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia and raised ALT in natural babesiosis, with universal fever, tachycardia and tachypnoea. Mechanisms invoked (hepatic hypoxia, glomerular parasite infiltration, DIC, splenic sequestration) are drawn from cited literature, not tested here, and no severity or outcome analysis was performed.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** 16 cases and 6 controls; species and diagnostic method not stated; co-infections not excluded; statistical test not described; single time point, no outcome or treatment data; abstract calls erythrocyte count and BUN significant but the full text calls them non-significant; short peer-review interval and unclear review policy (see verification notes).
