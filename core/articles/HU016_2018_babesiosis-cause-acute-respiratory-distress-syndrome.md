## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective review covered adults admitted to a medical intensive care unit at a tertiary New York hospital between 2008 and 2016, aiming to characterise ARDS arising during *Babesia microti* infection. ARDS affected 8 of the 22 babesiosis patients (36.4%; 95% CI 19.7-57.0%). In six of these eight it began after antiparasitic drugs were started, but those six had been symptomatic for longer before treatment (mean 7.5 vs 4.5 days; p=0.34). Three of the eight died with unresolved ARDS, compared with 14.3% mortality among the 14 patients without ARDS (p=0.31). Non-survivors were older (74.0 vs 54.6 years; p=0.07) and had much higher SOFA scores (12.3 vs 6.0; p=0.0006). The authors wonder whether parasite death contributes to ARDS but concede confounding by delayed treatment. The abstract's conclusion gives 35.4% rather than 36.4%, inconsistent with 8 of 22.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Small retrospective ICU case series of one hospital; descriptive, with no adjusted analysis in the abstract.
- **Authors' conclusion:** ARDS complicated about a third of critically ill *B. microti* patients, often followed drug initiation, and carried substantial mortality, but the treatment-timing link is confounded.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category PUL. Acute alveolar-capillary injury and ARDS in severe haemoprotozoan disease is rated **mechanistically plausible**. Canine studies describe ARDS or non-cardiogenic oedema: CB025 (*B. rossi*, n=152; ARDS linked to azotaemia and lower survival), CB111 (necropsy with diffuse-alveolar-damage-like lesions), CB102 (single puppy), with CB079, CB097 and CB032 as single-animal reports and CB080 favouring circulatory failure. The post-treatment onset signal is rated **speculative**: no canine study reports timing relative to therapy. Differences: older, comorbid, sometimes asplenic *B. microti* patients versus dogs infected with *B. rossi*, *B. canis*, *B. gibsoni* or *B. vogeli*; imidocarb versus atovaquone-azithromycin or clindamycin-quinine; no shared ARDS definition.
- **Key limitations:** 22 patients; wide confidence intervals; non-significant mortality and timing comparisons; Lyme coinfection and age not controlled; no lung histology or markers.
