## Abstract-derived summary

B. gibsoni infection in dogs is clinically severe, and more than 10% relapse after anti-babesial therapy. The authors followed 145 dogs diagnosed with acute disease for 16 weeks, recording haematology (anaemia, platelets, leukocytes, haemoglobin, RBC count), co-infections, secondary immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA), and signalment. Risk factors were analysed with cumulative incidence functions and Kaplan-Meier methods. Recurrence occurred in 11.8% of dogs. Higher recurrence risk was linked to Rottweiler breed (21.8%), secondary IMHA (28.7%), RBC below 2 x 10^6/microlitre at diagnosis (16%), and anaemia persisting beyond 20 days after treatment (29.1%). Dogs with concurrent haemoparasites were predicted to have higher disease-related mortality (25%). The authors advise vigilance in these groups.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** Rottweilers, dogs with secondary IMHA, concurrent haemoparasitism, low RBC at diagnosis and persistent anaemia need attention to limit relapse.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Links severity of erythrocyte injury and immune-mediated haemolysis to relapse risk; supports an IMHA-associated, anaemia-persistence phenotype but shows association only, not mechanism. Whether "recurrence" is relapse versus reinfection, and how it was confirmed, is not stated in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species identification and recurrence criteria not reported in abstract, breed effect possibly confounded by case mix, treatment protocol not described, co-infection identity not given, multiple comparisons, setting unknown.
