## Abstract-derived summary

A single dog is described: an eight-year-old male Labrador presented with high fever to a university veterinary clinic in Kolkata, India. Blood-smear microscopy identified Babesia gibsoni. The animal died despite therapy, and necropsy showed notable abnormalities in the liver, lungs, kidneys and spleen. Under the microscope, lung, liver and spleen sections showed vascular congestion and oedema, while the renal tubular epithelium showed prominent degenerative and necrotic injury. The abstract gives no haematology, biochemistry, parasitaemia, treatment regimen, time to death or molecular confirmation, so the clinical context of the lesions cannot be judged from it. The report therefore offers a qualitative multi-organ pathology description from one fatal natural infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract states no explicit conclusion beyond reporting the gross and histological lesions.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Pulmonary oedema and congestion, hepatic and splenic congestion, and tubular epithelial necrosis fit the multi-organ picture described for severe canine babesiosis (circulatory/microvascular disturbance, hypoxic or pigment-associated tubular injury). One animal cannot show mechanism, and the lesions are not attributable to B. gibsoni alone: they were not separated from terminal, treatment-related or co-infection effects. Value lies in documenting renal and pulmonary pathology in a B. gibsoni death (a syndrome description, not a mechanistic or biomarker finding).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; species based on smear morphology only; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) not reported as excluded; no antemortem laboratory data in abstract; treatment confounding; possible agonal or post-mortem change; full text not accessible.
