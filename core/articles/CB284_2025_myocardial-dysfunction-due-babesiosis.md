## Abstract-derived summary

This single-dog case report describes a two-year-old intact female Great Dane presented with six days of breathlessness and inappetence, after earlier treatment at a private clinic. Clinical examination, blood counts, serum chemistry and a stained blood film were used to diagnose Babesia gibsoni infection. Echocardiographic examination revealed both systolic and diastolic dysfunctions. The animal received doxycycline, clindamycin and metronidazole. Histology of the heart showed myocardial cell death and bleeding. The abstract gives no echocardiographic values, no cardiac biomarker data, and does not state the clinical outcome or when tissue was collected. The report describes one dog and gives no comparison group for the cardiac findings.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors attribute the systolic and diastolic dysfunction to babesiosis-induced cardiac dysfunction and recommend that cardiac function be assessed in complicated cases.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents myocardial necrosis and haemorrhage at necropsy alongside echocardiographic dysfunction in one fatal, multi-organ case (severe anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia, hypotension). It is a syndrome observation, not a mechanism: hypotension, hypoxaemia, anaemia and azotaemia are competing contributors to myocardial injury, and babesial causation is not isolated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; species not molecularly confirmed; no cardiac troponin; no control or pre-illness cardiac data; pre-treatment elsewhere; histopathology limited to one figure; journal peer-review policy not stated on pages checked.
