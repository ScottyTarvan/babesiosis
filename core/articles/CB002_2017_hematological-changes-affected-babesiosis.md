## Abstract-derived summary

The authors described haematological abnormalities in dogs naturally infected with Babesia. Measured variables were haemoglobin, red cell count, platelets, total and differential white cell counts, packed cell volume (PCV) and the red cell indices MCV, MCH and MCHC. Most affected dogs showed microcytic hypochromic anaemia and reduced platelet counts, and three quarters of cases had a 24% decrease in PCV. The study concludes that canine Babesia infection is accompanied by anaemia and thrombocytopenia. Taken together, anaemia and low platelet counts were the abnormalities most often seen in these dogs. The abstract does not state how many dogs were examined, how infection was confirmed, or how the 24% figure was defined.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that Babesia infection in dogs caused anaemia and thrombocytopenia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Confirms the expected cytopenias (anaemia, thrombocytopenia) in clinical cases, labelled descriptive, and says nothing about mechanism (direct haemolysis vs immune-mediated destruction vs consumption). Microcytic hypochromic indices are not the typical pattern of acute babesial haemolytic anaemia, which is usually normocytic or macrocytic with reticulocytosis; this raises the possibility of iron deficiency, chronic disease or co-infection or ectoparasitism, but that is speculative here. Causal wording ("caused") is not supported by an uncontrolled descriptive design.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Sample size and recruitment not reported in abstract; no control group; no confirmation method for Babesia or species; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, hookworm) not addressed; ambiguous "24% decreased PCV" statement; no reticulocyte or regeneration data; treatment and disease stage unknown.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
