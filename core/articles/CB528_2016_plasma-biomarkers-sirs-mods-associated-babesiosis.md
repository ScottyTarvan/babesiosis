## Abstract-derived summary

This proteomic study compared plasma collected at admission from four groups of five dogs: healthy controls, dogs with uncomplicated babesiosis caused by Babesia canis canis, babesiosis with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), and babesiosis with multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS). Proteins were separated by two-dimensional gel electrophoresis and identified by liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry. Sixty-eight spots differed between groups at P below 0.05. In the SIRS group, paraoxonase 1 and apolipoprotein A-I were higher. In the MODS group, complement inhibitors and vitamin D binding protein were lower, which the authors interpret as reflecting prolonged complement activation, haemolysis and activation of coagulation. The work aims to support early recognition of SIRS and MODS, which inform severity, outcome and therapy decisions.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** SIRS in canine babesiosis was characterised by higher paraoxonase 1 and apoA-I, and MODS by lower complement inhibitors and vitamin D binding protein; the authors link the MODS pattern to complement activation, haemolysis and coagulation activation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study links distinct plasma protein patterns to clinical severity groups at admission. With five dogs per group and a single sampling point, the findings are hypothesis-generating. The mechanistic readings (complement activation, haemolysis, coagulation) are the authors' inferences from protein changes and were not tested directly.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small groups (five dogs each); single time point at admission; gel-based proteomics with no validation cohort reported in the abstract; no outcome data in the abstract; species identification method not stated in the abstract.
