## Abstract-derived summary

Thirty dogs with non-traumatic peripheral oedema were investigated with a clinical, haematological, biochemical, fluid, ultrasound, electrocardiographic, echocardiographic and molecular workup. Conventional PCR screened for six vector-borne and filarial agents, and positive amplicons were sequenced to confirm them. Microscopy of wet films and blood smears revealed sheathed microfilariae and Babesia gibsoni. Molecular testing identified Brugia malayi and B. gibsoni as the main agents, and other pathogens were not detected. The oedema fluid was a modified transudate. Group-level leucocytosis with neutrophilia, hyperglobulinaemia and raised AST were recorded. Treatment cleared parasitaemia and improved clinical signs, but oedema recurred in several cases. The authors conclude that filarial infection, babesiosis and heart disease all contribute to oedema, and they call for vasculitis-directed therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. malayi, B. gibsoni and cardiac disease play significant roles in canine peripheral oedema, and cause-directed treatment alone often gives only temporary benefit.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Babesia-specific pathophysiology is peripheral here: only 4 of 30 dogs were B. gibsoni-positive. The anaemia, thrombocytopenia and endothelial-injury reasoning in the discussion is not supported by Babesia-specific data. Vasculitis rests on Doppler engorgement in 6 dogs, without histology, and was not linked to a pathogen. It is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small Babesia subgroup; no control group; one-sample t-tests of pooled values against reference limits, not by aetiology; microscopy-positive microfilariae (16) exceed PCR-positive B. malayi (13); recurrence not quantified; treatment confounding.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
