## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a bitch that developed babesiosis in the seventh week of pregnancy and her litter of three puppies, which showed signs of disease in weeks 8 and 9 after birth. Infection with Babesia was confirmed by PCR on blood from the bitch and all three puppies. The amplified 18S rRNA gene fragments from the puppies and the bitch were identical in sequence, indicating infection with the same strain of Babesia canis canis. The authors considered other routes of infection in the puppies and judged them unlikely. On this basis they propose that the puppies were possibly infected in utero. The report is a single-litter observation and does not by itself establish the route of transmission.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The identical sequences in the bitch and puppies, together with the exclusion of other likely routes, suggest that the puppies were infected with B. canis canis in utero.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report describes a distinct presentation, clinical babesiosis in young puppies whose infection the authors attribute to in utero transmission. An identical 18S rRNA sequence shows a shared strain but cannot by itself separate in utero infection from infection during birth, through colostrum or later by a tick. The puppies' blood counts showed anaemia, thrombocytopenia and leukopenia (full text), in line with the bitch's findings.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single litter; one gene fragment compared; transmission route inferred by exclusion, not shown directly; no testing of the puppies at birth; limited clinical data; no control animals.

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - The bitch was a four-year-old Russian Terrier admitted in September 2013 to the Department of Infectious Diseases, Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Lublin, in week 7 of pregnancy; the owner had removed three ticks about four days earlier (full text)
  - At admission the bitch had fever (40.2 °C), pale mucous membranes and brown urine, with anaemia (RBC 3.24 x 10^12/l), leukopenia (WBC 5.1 x 10^9/l) and thrombocytopenia (PLT 62 x 10^9/l) (full text)
  - Biochemistry in the bitch showed raised AST, ALT, urea, total bilirubin and creatinine (creatinine 187.2 µmol/l); ultrasound showed splenic enlargement and three live foetuses (full text)
  - A Giemsa-stained smear showed intra-erythrocytic Babesia in the bitch; PCR and sequencing classified the parasite as the 18S RNA-B strain (full text)
  - The bitch received a single subcutaneous dose of imidocarb dipropionate (5 mg/kg) and improved within 24 h; one week later only mild thrombocytopenia remained (PLT 154 x 10^9/l) (full text)
  - Three puppies (one female, two males) were born on day 59 after mating and were weaned and sold in week 7; signs of babesiosis appeared in week 8 (female and one male) and week 9 (other male) (full text)
  - Puppy blood counts (Table 1) showed RBC 4.0 to 4.75 x 10^12/l, haematocrit 33 to 38%, WBC 4.9 to 5.5 x 10^9/l and PLT 81 to 100 x 10^9/l (full text)
  - Smears were positive in two of the three puppies (both males), whereas PCR detected B. canis canis in all three (full text)
  - Fipronil spray had been applied to the puppies, no ticks were found on them, and no treatment other than vaccination and antiparasitic prophylaxis had been given, so an iatrogenic route was considered excluded (full text)
  - The authors judged colostral transmission unlikely, because colostrum passes antibodies but not parasites; they noted that transmission in the birth canal is undocumented, and stated that infection after birth by a tick could not be ruled out completely (full text)
  - After a single imidocarb dose (5 mg/kg) in each puppy, PCR two weeks later did not detect B. canis canis (full text)
