## Abstract-derived summary

The report describes a 12-year-old pot-bellied pig in South Africa that died after two days of poor appetite and reluctance to stand. Blood smear showed many Babesia parasites with a moderate regenerative anaemia, and urine dipstick read 4+ for haem. At necropsy the animal was jaundiced with an enlarged spleen. Histology found haemoglobin casts in renal tubules and collecting ducts, lung oedema, splenic congestion and bile stasis in the liver. Reverse line blot and 18S rRNA sequencing identified Babesia sp. Suis, with a sequence fully identical to isolates previously reported from pigs in Italy. The authors present the case to document the clinical and pathological picture of porcine babesiosis, which is rarely reported.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Case report (n = 1), non-canine host.
- **Relevance:** Cited under Q1 (haemolysis, free haem, endothelial activation and tubular injury) because it surfaced in the haem-related PubMed search. It is not canine evidence and cannot show that a canine gap is filled. At most it illustrates, in another host, that Babesia-associated intravascular haemolysis can coincide with haemoglobinuria and haemoglobin casts in the kidney, which fits the pigment-nephropathy side of the Q1 hypothesis.
- **Key limitations:** Single animal; different host and a different Babesia species; no cell-free haemoglobin, endothelial or tubular injury markers; terminal case, so no temporal sequence; cause of death attribution rests on necropsy findings in one animal; no clinical severity comparison.
