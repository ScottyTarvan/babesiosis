## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with B. rossi babesiosis are markedly thrombocytopenic yet have normal thromboelastograms, possibly because platelets are strongly activated. The authors applied thromboelastographic platelet mapping to separate thrombin-, fibrinogen- and platelet-dependent contributions to clot strength. Thirteen dogs with complicated infection were compared with five healthy dogs, with platelet indices and fibrinogen also measured. Infected dogs had higher fibrin-only clot strength and fibrinogen, and lower platelet counts (P < 0.01 for each). Thrombin-induced strength and arachidonic acid/ADP-induced platelet responses did not differ. Fibrin-only strength tracked fibrinogen (r = 0.735) and mean platelet volume (r = 0.517), and inversely tracked haematocrit (r = -0.685). The authors propose that hyperfibrinogenaemia compensates for thrombocytopenia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hyperfibrinogenaemia appears to offset severe thrombocytopenia, giving normal tracings and no overt bleeding.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Is compatible with an acute-phase, fibrinogen-driven compensation mechanism for haemostasis in B. rossi, rather than demonstrating platelet hyperactivation; this remains a hypothesis from correlational data. Normal agonist-induced responses with low platelet counts are compatible with preserved per-platelet function, but this is not tested directly. Correlations do not establish causation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Very small n (13 vs 5), "complicated" case definition and severity not detailed in abstract, no uncomplicated comparator, treatment and co-infection effects unknown, species confirmation method not reported, single time point.
