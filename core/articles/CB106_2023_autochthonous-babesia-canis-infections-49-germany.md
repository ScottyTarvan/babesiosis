## Abstract-derived summary

The authors characterised autochthonous B. canis infection in Berlin/Brandenburg, evaluating history, signs, laboratory results, treatment and outcome in 49 dogs seen 2015-2021, most between September and January (40 of 49). Lethargy occurred in all dogs; pale membranes in 63%, fever in 50% and pigmenturia in 52%. Every dog was thrombocytopenic; 85% were anaemic, 52% had intravascular haemolysis, 41% pancytopenia and 37% SIRS. Diagnosis rested on smear (40 dogs) and PCR (all 49 dogs), with sequencing. All received imidocarb, yet PCR positivity persisted in 7 dogs after the first injection and one dog after the fourth. Four dogs were euthanised and three died. The authors associate local infection with severe laboratory changes, SIRS and multiorgan involvement.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Autochthonous B. canis infection was associated with severe clinicopathological changes, SIRS and multiorgan involvement, and PCR monitoring after treatment is advisable.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile of a B. canis phenotype (universal thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, inflammatory response, organ involvement), useful for syndrome definition and outcome (7 of 49 dogs euthanised or dead, about 14%, per abstract). It does not identify mechanisms or prognostic markers; thrombocytopenia drivers and organ injury pathways are not tested. Persistent PCR positivity concerns treatment, not pathophysiology.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No control group, mixed retrospective/prospective design, referral bias, variable PCR timing, some dogs with travel history, imidocarb treatment confounding outcomes, no formal severity-risk analysis.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
