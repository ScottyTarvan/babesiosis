## Abstract-derived summary

A male German Boxer was admitted to a Lviv clinic in April 2020 with inappetence, remitting fever, blood in the urine and jaundice after repeated tick attachment during park walks. Giemsa-stained smears showed heavy erythrocyte parasitisation attributed to B. canis. Imidocarb dipropionate did not save the animal, and a necropsy followed. Gross findings included tracheal froth, pulmonary oedema with lobar pneumonia, extensive epicardial and pleural haemorrhages, splenomegaly, venous congestion, degeneration of liver and kidney, pancreatic necrosis, anaemia and cavity transudates. Haematoxylin-eosin histology of lung, kidney, liver, spleen and pancreas showed alveolar oedema with septal congestion, bronchiolar inflammation, extracapillary glomerulonephritis with tubular hyaline-droplet degeneration, hepatocellular granular degeneration with sinusoidal dilatation, red pulp hyperplasia and haemosiderophage accumulation indicating enhanced red cell destruction.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Fatal B. canis infection in this dog produced widespread organ lesions (lung, kidney, liver, spleen, pancreas) with signs of increased erythrocyte breakdown.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a necropsy-level picture of fatal complicated babesiosis (pulmonary oedema, renal glomerular/tubular injury, hepatic congestion, haemolysis-related haemosiderosis) consistent with multisystem involvement. It cannot separate parasite-specific injury from hypoxia, shock, immune complex effects or terminal changes, and says nothing on mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; no molecular species confirmation; no clinicopathological values reported in abstract; co-infections and other causes of nephritis or pneumonia not excluded; routine H&E only (no special stains or immunohistochemistry reported); treatment and delay confounded; tick genus reported by owner history.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
