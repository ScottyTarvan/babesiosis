## Abstract-derived summary

Sixty-eight dogs with signs compatible with babesiosis were seen at a university clinical campus in Puducherry, India, and blood smears identified nine positives. These nine were compared with six clinically healthy dogs for haematology and serum biochemistry using unpaired t-tests. Thrombocytopenia was the standout finding (mean platelets 1.52 vs 3.38 x10^5/uL, p=0.003). Red cell count was also lower (4.59 vs 5.90 x10^6/uL, p=0.013), although mean haemoglobin and PCV did not differ significantly; three dogs were severely anaemic, the lowest haemoglobin being 3.5 g/dL. Glucose was lower and ALT higher in infected dogs (p<0.05), yet the authors noted both stayed within normal limits. Bilirubin, creatinine, albumin and other biochemistry did not differ. The authors identified thrombocytopenia as the most significant abnormality.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that, despite marked changes in individual dogs, thrombocytopenia was the most significant haemato-biochemical abnormality in infected dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile, not a mechanistic study. Thrombocytopenia as the consistent finding agrees with other canine babesiosis series, but the data cannot distinguish consumption, immune-mediated destruction or sequestration. Heterogeneity (three severely anaemic dogs, six not) may partly explain why group-mean haemoglobin and PCV were non-significant, so the data fit variable severity better than uniform absence of anaemia. Small glucose and ALT shifts within reference intervals are of doubtful clinical significance.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=9 infected and 6 controls; species not molecularly confirmed; co-infections not reported as excluded; control matching not stated; no outcome or treatment data; multiple t-tests without correction; selection of dogs under 3 years and smear-positive cases only.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
