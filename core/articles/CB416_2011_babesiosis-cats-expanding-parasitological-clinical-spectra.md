## Abstract-derived summary

This review describes the expanding range of Babesia parasites that infect dogs and cats. Molecular methods showed that dogs can carry several parasites beyond the traditionally recognised large and small forms, including B. conradae, a microti-like piroplasm, Theileria species and an unnamed large Babesia. B. rossi, B. canis and B. vogeli, once treated as subspecies, are morphologically identical but differ in disease severity, tick vectors, genetics and geographic range. Evidence suggests B. gibsoni can pass directly between fighting dogs without needing a tick vector. In cats, disease is uncommon and is largely due to B. felis in South Africa, with anaemia and icterus, plus occasional reports of other species elsewhere. Large forms usually respond well to imidocarb, whereas small forms resist therapy and relapse. The authors stress that accurate species identification guides treatment and prognosis.
