## Abstract-derived summary

Native, unfixed and unstained blood films were examined by scanning electron microscopy to characterise erythrocyte ultrastructure in babesiosis. Eighteen dogs described as having confirmed babesiosis were compared with six healthy dogs and 31 healthy people. Normocytes made up about 99% of cells in controls but 77.3% in infected dogs, and regenerative forms rose from 1% in controls to 1.5% in infected dogs. Infected dogs also had degenerative erythrocytes with abnormal size and shape and membrane defects. The author concludes that the ultramicroscopic erythrocyte profile is a leading element of babesiosis pathogenesis and proposes that SEM can reveal extraerythrocytic parasite forms in cases where anisochromia and inclusions cannot be resolved by routine methods.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Altered erythrocyte ultrastructure is a leading component of babesiosis pathogenesis, and SEM adds detail on extraerythrocytic parasites.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Qualitative morphological evidence of erythrocyte membrane damage and poikilocytosis, consistent with haemolytic injury; it cannot separate parasite-induced damage from immune-mediated, oxidative or preparation artefact, nor does it measure mechanism. The pooled human-plus-dog control group weakens the canine comparison.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n, no per-group cell counts with dispersion or statistical tests reported, mixed-species controls, Babesia species and confirmation methods not specified, air-dried unfixed films prone to artefact, stated 100 kV accelerating voltage is atypical for SEM (possible reporting error), no clinical severity or anaemia data.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
