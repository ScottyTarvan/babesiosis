## Abstract-derived summary

From July to December 2025, dogs attending the medicine outpatient clinic of a veterinary college in Bikaner were screened, and 23 of 1628 (1.41%) were PCR-positive for B. gibsoni. Positives were most often German Shepherds, males, dogs aged over one to five years, and cases seen in July. Fever, inappetence and tick infestation led the clinical signs. Ultrasound showed splenomegaly in just over half, hepatomegaly in about two fifths and renal cortical changes in a minority. Haemoglobin (8.56 g/dl), erythrocyte count, packed cell volume (28.09%) and platelets (82.5 ×10³/µl) were significantly reduced, whereas AST, ALT and bilirubin rose and total protein and albumin fell. The authors present these as the clinicopathological and sonographic profile of canine B. gibsoni infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Natural B. gibsoni infection is associated with anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatic enzyme and bilirubin elevation, hypoalbuminaemia and splenic, hepatic and renal ultrasonographic changes.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A PCR-confirmed descriptive profile consistent with haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and hepatic involvement; it shows association, not mechanism (haemolysis type, immune mediation and hepatic injury cause are not addressed). Ultrasound findings are a useful organ-involvement signal but are non-specific. The reference against which "significant" changes were tested is not stated in the abstract. The reported 52.3% splenomegaly does not correspond exactly to a count out of 23 (12/23 = 52.2%), a minor reporting inconsistency.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=23; no control group described; single centre, six-month window; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) not reported as excluded; no severity or outcome data; sequencing confirmation not stated.
