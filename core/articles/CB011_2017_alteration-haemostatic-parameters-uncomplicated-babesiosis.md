## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined coagulation and anticoagulant pathway markers in 25 dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis attributed to B. canis, compared with 10 healthy controls. Protein C and antithrombin III (AT III) activities were measured chromogenically, whereas thrombin-antithrombin (TAT) complexes, APC and the endothelial protein C receptor were quantified with canine-specific ELISA kits. AT III activity was reduced and TAT was raised, indicating thrombin generation; within the protein C pathway, only APC differed significantly (direction not given in the abstract). The authors attribute the AT III fall to negative acute-phase behaviour, elastase degradation, reduced glycosaminoglycan availability and, chiefly, consumption through thrombin formation. They conclude that uncomplicated babesiosis involves a procoagulant state that largely resolves with treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (biomarker comparison with controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Uncomplicated canine babesiosis shows a procoagulant haemostatic shift towards thrombin formation that is mostly reversed during treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Raised TAT with low AT III supports ongoing thrombin generation and anticoagulant consumption in non-severe disease, consistent with immunothrombosis/early hypercoagulability rather than overt DIC. The proposed contributors (acute-phase suppression, elastase, glycosaminoglycans) are interpretive and were not separately tested per the abstract. Clinical consequence, outcome or severity prediction is not shown.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; infection confirmation method and species identification not stated; sampling time points and treatment details not reported in abstract; mechanisms inferred from marker patterns; uncomplicated cases only, so severe disease not covered.
