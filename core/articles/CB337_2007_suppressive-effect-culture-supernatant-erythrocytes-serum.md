## Abstract-derived summary

This in vitro study asked whether material produced during Babesia gibsoni infection slows the maturation of canine reticulocytes. Culture supernatant from infected erythrocytes and serum from chronically infected dogs both produced significantly broader protein bands on SDS-PAGE. The infected culture supernatant strongly suppressed reticulocyte maturation, and serum-free supernatant also had an inhibitory effect. Fractions above 150 kDa and between 150 and 70 kDa from infected serum and supernatant delayed maturation. The authors suggest that the parasite and/or its metabolites may release proteins into culture fluid and serum. They connect this to earlier work showing that infected serum reduced erythrocyte 5'-nucleotidase activity, and propose that excess proteins or metabolites may accumulate as maturation is inhibited.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental; in vitro)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Culture supernatant of B. gibsoni-infected erythrocytes had a suppressive effect on canine reticulocyte maturation in vitro and might contain factors that retard it. The >150 kDa and 150-70 kDa fractions of infected supernatant and serum retarded maturation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The findings suggest a possible host-side route by which infection could affect reticulocyte maturation and so bear on the anaemia of canine babesiosis. They are in vitro observations with small numbers, and the active factor was not identified. The data show an association between these fractions and delayed reticulocyte maturation in culture, not a mechanism operating in infected dogs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small numbers (3 infected and 4 healthy serum donors; n = 3 to 4 per assay; full text); crude molecular-mass fractions with no protein identification; single laboratory-maintained B. gibsoni strain (full text); culture conditions that may not reflect in vivo plasma; no in vivo or clinical outcome data; comparison with other anaemia sera reported as data not shown (full text)

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Sera came from four clinically healthy dogs and three dogs chronically infected with B. gibsoni; reticulocyte percentage was 0.6 ± 0.3% (mean ± SD) in the healthy dogs and 5.5 ± 3.7% in the infected dogs (full text)
  - The B. gibsoni strain was originally obtained from a naturally infected dog in Nagasaki in 1973 and has been maintained in dogs at Hokkaido University (full text)
  - Reticulocytes were prepared from four healthy dogs after repeated bleeding, by Percoll gradient, giving 70-95% reticulocytes; parasites were cultured for 6 days before the supernatant was collected (full text)
  - Culture supernatant and infected serum were fractionated into >150 kDa, 150-70 kDa and <70 kDa fractions (full text)
  - Supernatant from uninfected, incubated erythrocytes had no effect on maturation; the <70 kDa fractions did not differ from normal dog serum (full text)
  - Each assay used n = 3 or 4 with Student's t-test; reported significance was p < 0.005 or p < 0.001 (full text)
  - The authors report that SDS-PAGE of samples from dogs with immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (acute and recovery), from experimentally anaemic dogs and of 25-30% erythrocyte haemolysates did not show the broader bands, and that samples incubated with reticulocytes had no apparent effect on maturation (data not shown) (full text)
  - The authors speculate that delayed maturation in culture may favour parasite replication, because B. gibsoni replicates preferentially in reticulocytes in vitro (full text)
