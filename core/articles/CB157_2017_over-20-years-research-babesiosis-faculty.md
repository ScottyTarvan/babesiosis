## Abstract-derived summary

The authors look back on more than two decades of canine babesiosis work at a Warsaw veterinary faculty. They report establishing that B. canis is the causative species and Dermacentor reticulatus the sole vector in Poland. Early clinicopathological work matched international findings: thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leucopenia, raised liver enzymes, azotemia, hypoalbuminaemia, hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia and hypokalaemia. Later studies tied azotemia to excess TNF-alpha, hypotension and renal ischaemia and hypoxia. They also tie IL-6 excess to suppression of the hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis (euthyroid sick syndrome), and secondary hyperaldosteronism from reduced renal perfusion to hypokalaemia. The output comprised 27 original papers, 6 case reports and many review articles, which the authors say improved diagnosis and therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The group's research clarified species, vector and several pathogenic mechanisms, and improved diagnostic and therapeutic practice in Poland.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful synthesis of a single group's proposed cytokine-haemodynamic sequence (TNF-alpha, hypotension, renal hypoperfusion, azotemia; IL-6, thyroid axis suppression). These are largely associational findings, and the abstract does not describe the study designs, so causality is not established by this paper. It is a map to the primary studies rather than new evidence.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** self-review with potential selective emphasis; no n, effect sizes or methods in abstract; dogs likely overlap with the group's earlier publications; B. canis only; no controls described.
