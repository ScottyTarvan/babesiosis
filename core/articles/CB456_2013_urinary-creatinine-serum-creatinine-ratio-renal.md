## Abstract-derived summary

The authors measured the urinary creatinine to serum creatinine ratio and the renal failure index in dogs infected with Babesia canis in Poland and compared the values with those of healthy dogs. Infected dogs had a lower urinary-to-serum creatinine ratio than healthy dogs. Within the infected group, dogs with azotaemia had a lower ratio and a higher renal failure index than infected dogs without azotaemia. The renal failure index showed a strong positive correlation with the time between the start of disease and diagnosis and treatment (r = 0.89, p < 0.001). Both indices are used to separate prerenal azotaemia from acute tubular necrosis. The authors concluded that acute tubular necrosis may develop during the course of babesiosis caused by B. canis in Poland.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute tubular necrosis may develop in the course of canine babesiosis caused by B. canis in Poland.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Lower urinary-to-serum creatinine ratios in infected dogs, and higher renal failure index values in azotaemic infected dogs, fit renal tubular injury rather than a purely prerenal cause of azotaemia. The strong correlation between the index and the time before diagnosis and treatment suggests that the index rises with a longer delay, but a correlation cannot show that tubular injury progresses with delay. The abstract does not mention histological or other direct confirmation of tubular necrosis, so the conclusion rests on indirect indices.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only, so group sizes are not known; method of species identification not stated; no detail on dog recruitment, treatment or comorbidities; indices are indirect markers of tubular injury; correlation with disease duration does not show a causal link.
