## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective study asked whether the inflammatory response in canine babesiosis is accompanied by activation of the endothelial adhesion molecule ICAM-1 and of the coagulation system. Twelve dogs with natural infection, diagnosed on blood smear and confirmed by PCR, were sampled before antiparasitic treatment, and ten were sampled again three days later; ten healthy dogs served as controls. C-reactive protein, soluble ICAM-1 and fibrinogen were significantly higher in infected dogs than in controls before treatment and stayed raised at day 3. Von Willebrand factor activity was significantly lower before treatment, while factor VIII activity did not differ from controls. White cell, red cell and platelet counts were significantly reduced at presentation and had returned to normal by day 3.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** A proinflammatory condition in babesiosis appears to influence endothelial dysfunction and haemostatic activity. Sequestered blood cells may obstruct small vessels, promote inflammation and could increase the severity of the disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Infected dogs differed from healthy dogs in an acute-phase protein (CRP), a soluble endothelial adhesion molecule (sICAM-1) and haemostatic variables (fibrinogen raised, vWF activity lowered, FVIII unchanged), which fits a link between inflammation, endothelial activation and haemostatic change. The design compares groups at one time point plus a short follow-up, so it shows association, not mechanism. The sequestration and severity statements are the authors' interpretation, not outcomes measured in the study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** 12 infected dogs, 10 followed up; follow-up of only 3 days; Babesia species not stated; no severity grading or outcome data reported in the abstract; healthy controls only, with no sick non-babesial comparison group; setting and recruitment not described in the abstract; association rather than causal evidence.
