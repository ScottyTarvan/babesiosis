## Abstract-derived summary

A seven-year-old intact male Labrador was referred to an Indian veterinary institute after a prolonged period of treatment by a local veterinarian for poor appetite, black tarry stools and pinpoint skin haemorrhages. Examination found pallor, enlarged peripheral lymph nodes and mild liver enlargement. Haematology showed anaemia, leukopenia and thrombocytopenia; biochemistry showed low albumin and total protein; aPTT and PT were prolonged. A Giemsa-stained smear was read as Babesia vogeli, yet multiplex PCR for B. vogeli, B. gibsoni, Ehrlichia canis and Hepatozoon canis was negative, which the authors suggest could reflect earlier antibiotics. Spherocytosis and a positive saline agglutination test supported concurrent immune-mediated haemolysis. The dog received about 300 mL whole blood, antibiotics and immunosuppressive steroids but died after four weeks.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The dog was tentatively diagnosed with chronic tick fever and concomitant Evans syndrome, and treatment regimens for Evans syndrome need further research because the prognosis is uncertain.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Spherocytes plus saline agglutination support immune-mediated haemolysis, and thrombocytopenia with prolonged PT/aPTT and melena suggests a consumptive or immune-mediated bleeding diathesis. These are syndrome-level observations. Evans syndrome is not demonstrated: no Coombs test, platelet-directed antibody testing, bone marrow assessment or fibrinogen/D-dimer is reported in the abstract, and babesiosis itself can cause secondary IMHA, thrombocytopenia and DIC. The role of Babesia is therefore inferred, not shown.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; Babesia species rests on smear morphology, which cannot speciate, and PCR was negative; co-infection or other triggers not excluded; prior antibiotic and unspecified earlier treatment; combined treatment (transfusion, steroids, antibiotics) prevents attributing the outcome; no antiparasitic therapy stated in abstract; journal peer-review rigour unverified (see below).
