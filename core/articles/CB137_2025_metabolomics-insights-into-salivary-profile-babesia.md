## Abstract-derived summary

Using saliva as a non-invasive biofluid, the authors compared the metabolome of 14 dogs with natural Babesia canis infection against 14 healthy dogs. Untargeted UHPLC-Orbitrap mass spectrometry detected 2257 features, 868 of which differed significantly between groups, and seven were confirmed against reference standards. A targeted panel found seven metabolites with significant differences. Pathway analysis pointed to altered amino acid, nucleotide and lipid metabolism, together with changes in energy production and purine metabolism. The authors frame these changes as evidence of host-parasite metabolic interplay, given that Babesia depends on host-derived nutrients, and suggest saliva may eventually support diagnostic or therapeutic development.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (omics, hypothesis-generating)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis is accompanied by salivary metabolic shifts in amino acid, nucleotide, lipid, energy and purine pathways that could inform future diagnostics and therapy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that natural Babesia canis infection is associated with a measurable salivary metabolic signature, with a purine and urea-cycle emphasis plausibly linked to parasite nutrient scavenging and to altered renal or protein catabolism. It does not establish causality, parasite versus host origin of the metabolites, or any association with severity or outcome. Salivary creatinine tracked serum creatinine (reported r = 0.85, full text), suggesting part of the signal reflects systemic azotaemia rather than a salivary-specific effect.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n; single time point; uncontrolled treatment timing (imidocarb given on admission); clinical severity and co-infection screening not described; a nominal t-test threshold across 868 features with unclear multiplicity handling; saliva-blood concordance incomplete.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
