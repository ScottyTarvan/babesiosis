## Abstract-derived summary

This experimental study tested whether exoantigens released into the supernatant of in vitro Babesia gibsoni cultures could protect dogs against a virulent homologous challenge. An attenuated Oita strain was grown in culture, and the antigen-containing fluid was combined with Quil A saponin and given to two dogs in three subcutaneous doses at 20-day intervals. On day 68 after the first dose, immunised and control dogs received an intravenous challenge of 2 × 10(8) virulent parasites. Immunised dogs had lower parasitaemia and only a mild, transient fall in packed cell volume, whereas controls developed typical acute disease with marked anaemia and fever. Both immunised dogs mounted antibody responses, with peak IFAT titres of 2,560 and 5,120. Western blotting of the immunogen showed five main proteins between 18 and 57 kDa. The authors concluded that the culture-derived exoantigens are candidates for a non-viable vaccine.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Culture-derived B. gibsoni exoantigens induced a high degree of protection against virulent homologous challenge and are candidates for a non-viable vaccine.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives host-side data from experimental infection: parasitaemia, PCV, clinical signs and IFAT antibody titres in immunised versus control dogs. Immunisation was associated with lower parasitaemia and only a slight PCV fall after challenge, compared with severe anaemia in controls, which is consistent with a humoral immune contribution to protection, but the design cannot separate antibody effects from other immune mechanisms. The paper is mainly a vaccine-candidate study, so its pathophysiological content is limited.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only two immunised dogs; number of control dogs not reported in the abstract; no randomisation or statistical testing described in the abstract; single homologous strain challenge; only humoral response (IFAT) reported; preliminary Western blot only; full text not read.
