## Abstract-derived summary

The authors surveyed 100 tick-infested dogs from a semiarid region of Paraíba State, north-eastern Brazil, to look for Ehrlichia, Babesia and Hepatozoon infection. Dogs were tested by haemogram, smear, serology and PCR, and owners completed a questionnaire. All 1,151 ticks collected were Rhipicephalus sanguineus. Ehrlichia canis was detected by serology in 34% of dogs and by PCR in 25%. Babesia-like merozoites appeared on smears from two dogs, and PCR identified Babesia vogeli in ten. B. vogeli positivity was associated with younger age and with a low platelet count. No dog was positive for Hepatozoon. The authors concluded that E. canis was the main tick-borne agent locally and reported B. vogeli for the first time in dogs from this state.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** E. canis was the main tick-borne pathogen of dogs in the study area. B. vogeli is an important haemoparasite to include in the differential diagnosis (full text). This is the first report of B. vogeli in dogs from Paraíba State.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly a prevalence survey, but it gives Babesia-specific host data: PCR-confirmed B. vogeli infection was associated with a lower platelet count. The association rests on a small number of positive dogs. No adjustment for E. canis co-infection is reported for the platelet comparison, and E. canis qPCR positivity was itself associated with thrombocytopenia (full text). The platelet finding is a statistical association and says nothing about the mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 10 B. vogeli-positive dogs; no haematology reported separately for the B. vogeli group (full text); co-infection with E. canis in 3 of the 10 dogs (full text) and no stated adjustment for it in the platelet comparison; tick-infested dogs from one hospital, so not a random sample; single sampling period (April to September 2012, full text); no clinical severity or outcome data.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

  - Reference values used: leukocytes 6-17 x10^3/µl, erythrocytes 5.5-8.5 x10^6/µl, platelets 2-5 x10^5/µl (full text)
  - Across all 100 dogs, 52% were anaemic, 12% leukopenic and 37% had decreased platelets; these were not reported by Babesia status (full text)
  - Babesia 18S rRNA PCR products from 10 dogs were sequenced and were 100% identical to B. vogeli sequences in GenBank (full text)
  - B. vogeli positivity by age: 8 of 36 dogs aged 6-12 months, 1 of 23 aged 12-48 months and 1 of 41 aged over 48 months (univariate p = 0.009 in Table 1; multivariate p = 0.007, OR 0.197) (full text)
  - The B. vogeli-platelet link was reported as a significant correlation (p = 0.01) with no effect size given (full text)
  - Three dogs were co-infected with E. canis and B. vogeli by PCR (full text)
  - Anaemia, leukopenia and thrombocytopenia were associated with E. canis qPCR positivity (p ≤ 0.05) (full text)
  - Mean tick burden did not differ between B. vogeli-positive (14.6) and negative (11.2) dogs; the Results label the positive group "B. canis-PCR positive", read here as B. vogeli (full text)
  - The authors note that the mechanism of platelet damage by B. vogeli is poorly understood and mention splenic platelet pooling as a possible explanation drawn from human babesiosis (full text)
