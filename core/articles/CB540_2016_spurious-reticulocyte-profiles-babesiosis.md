## Abstract-derived summary

A 9-year-old female Maltese was referred to a French veterinary school after two days of anorexia and weakness. Examination found fever, abdominal discomfort and fast breathing. Laboratory work showed pigmented urine, raised bilirubin, creatinine and fibrinogen, an abnormal canine pancreatic lipase snap test, and pancytopenia with a nonregenerative anaemia. The blood smear contained many large Babesia inside red cells and no polychromasia. The Sysmex analyser returned a reticulocyte count of 60 × 10^9/L (reference interval 19.4-150.1 × 10^9/L), whereas manual counting gave 3.6 × 10^9/L. The red cell scattergram showed a separate cluster in the low-fluorescence reticulocyte zone, which the authors attributed to parasitised cells. A comparable discrepancy has been described in people with falciparum malaria. The artefact appeared on the Sysmex XT-2000iV and the ProCyte, both using polymethine dye, but not on the LaserCyte, which uses new methylene blue.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors interpreted the discrepant automated reticulocyte count and the isolated low-fluorescence cluster as erythrocytes parasitised by large Babesia, seen with polymethine-dye analysers but not with a new methylene blue analyser.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report suggests that, in this dog, polymethine-dye analysers may have counted parasitised red cells as reticulocytes, which could mask a nonregenerative anaemia. It is relevant to how regeneration is assessed in canine babesiosis, but it is one dog and the mechanism is inferred from the scattergram and stain behaviour rather than tested directly.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; large Babesia identified on blood smear only, with no molecular identification reported in the abstract; no controls or repeat samples reported in the abstract; clinical course and outcome not reported in the abstract; only three analysers compared; full text not read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
