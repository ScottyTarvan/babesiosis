## Abstract-derived summary

Babesia rossi causes severe canine disease. Clinical presentations are grouped as uncomplicated, with a good prognosis, or complicated, with a poor one. The authors sampled 141 blood samples from infected dogs: 121 from a veterinary academic hospital, where diagnosis was by thin blood smear, and 20 from private clinics in several regions of South Africa. They amplified and sequenced the polymorphic hexapeptide-repeat region of the BrEMA1 gene. PCR products gave 11 gene profiles on gel electrophoresis, and sequencing resolved 12 genotypes with 6 to 31 repeats. The four most frequent genotypes (19, 28, 29 and 11) accounted for 36.2%, 20.6%, 20.6% and 12.7% of parasites typed. Genotypes were compared retrospectively with clinical case data, and these four were also associated with the poorest prognosis. The authors describe this as a preliminary indication that B. rossi genotypes differ in clinical importance.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (parasite genotype versus clinical presentation and prognosis)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The most frequent B. rossi BrEMA1 genotypes were also those associated with the poorest prognosis, which suggests clinically important differences between genotypes. The authors call this a preliminary finding.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study relates parasite-side genetic variation to host clinical outcome, which bears on virulence. The most common genotypes were also the ones with the worst outcomes, so a frequency effect cannot be separated from a genotype effect on the information available. The abstract gives no statistical test, and the association does not show that genotype determines severity.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; described as preliminary; retrospective comparison with clinical data; no statistical analysis or effect sizes in the abstract; no adjustment for treatment delay or host factors stated; no uninfected control group; mostly hospital-presented cases; diagnosis by thin blood smear before PCR.
