## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether low arterial pressure goes together with kidney dysfunction in canine babesiosis. Acute tubular necrosis had been reported in the disease, and hypotension is regarded as one factor that favours hypoxic renal damage. Blood pressure was measured in 48 dogs infected with Babesia canis. Hypotension, defined as a mean arterial pressure below 80 mmHg, was found in seven of these dogs (14.6%). Infected dogs with azotaemia showed lower systolic, diastolic and mean pressures. All three pressure values correlated inversely with serum creatinine and urea, and the correlations were statistically significant. The authors interpret the findings as a possible influence of reduced blood pressure on azotaemia, probably also linked to acute tubular necrosis in this disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Decreased blood pressure influences the development of azotaemia in canine babesiosis and is probably also associated with acute tubular necrosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Lower blood pressure was associated with higher creatinine and urea in infected dogs, which fits a haemodynamic contribution to renal injury. A single pressure reading at the first visit cannot show the order of events, so the direction of the relationship stays open. Acute tubular necrosis was not assessed directly, so that link is inferred.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single measurement before treatment; cross-sectional design; no histology or other direct evidence of tubular necrosis; no adjustment for anaemia, dehydration or other confounders reported; recruitment period and clinic case mix not stated; short report.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
