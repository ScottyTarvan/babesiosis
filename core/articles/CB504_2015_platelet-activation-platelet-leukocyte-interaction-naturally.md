## Abstract-derived summary

Platelet-leukocyte aggregates had been reported in other canine inflammatory and immune-mediated haemolytic conditions, and this study asked whether they form in canine babesiosis and relate to outcome. Using flow cytometry on EDTA blood, the authors measured surface P-selectin (CD62P) on platelets, monocytes and neutrophils from 36 client-owned dogs with Babesia rossi infection (29 survived, seven died) and 15 healthy controls. A larger share of monocytes carried CD62P in infected dogs than in controls (54% versus 35.3%, P = 0.036). Platelet and neutrophil values did not differ between infected dogs and controls. Survivors (58.9%) exceeded controls (P = 0.019), whereas non-survivors (39.2%) did not, and survivors and non-survivors were not significantly different. The authors conclude that infected dogs, particularly survivors, had more platelet-monocyte aggregates than healthy dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia-infected dogs, specifically those that survived, had a significantly higher percentage of platelet-monocyte aggregates than healthy control dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study links B. rossi infection to more P-selectin-positive monocytes, which points to platelet-monocyte interaction as a cellular feature of the inflammation-coagulation interface. Platelet and neutrophil activation did not differ from controls. The outcome comparison is inconclusive: only seven dogs died, so the lack of a survivor versus non-survivor difference cannot be read as evidence of no relation to outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only seven non-survivors, so the outcome comparisons are underpowered; sampling time points not described in the abstract; the non-survivor neutrophil value (5.6%) is far below the control value and is not explained in the abstract; EDTA anticoagulation and sample handling can affect P-selectin detection; no coagulation or cytokine data in the abstract; matching of controls not described; diagnostic method for B. rossi not stated in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
