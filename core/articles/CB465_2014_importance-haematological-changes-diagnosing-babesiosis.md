## Abstract-derived summary

Canine babesiosis has become widespread in Lithuania over the past decade. This retrospective study covered 2003 to 2012 and examined blood from 300 dogs with babesiosis seen at a university veterinary academy and a small animal clinic. The authors assessed red cell count, haematocrit, haemoglobin, platelets, white cells and the leukogram, looking for patterns that could justify suspecting the disease. Of the 300 dogs, 186 had abnormal haematology and 114 had normal findings. Marked thrombocytopenia (30.90±4.97 x 10^9/l) and a leukogram moving from neutrophilic to lymphocytic-plasmocytic, with monocytosis (16.20±1.30 %), were described as characteristic, and anaemia and leukopenia were frequent tendencies. Azotaemia (urea 29.32±3.75 mmol/l) occurred with renal failure, which the authors regard as important for prognosis; liver failure was less common.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis in dogs can be characterised by marked thrombocytopenia, a lymphocytic-plasmocytic leukogram with monocytosis, a tendency to anaemia and leukopenia, and azotaemia with renal failure, which the authors consider important for prognosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series gives a clinicopathological profile of 300 dogs with babesiosis from two institutions in Lithuania, with thrombocytopenia, monocytosis, anaemia and azotaemia reported. The abstract attributes renal failure to haemolysis, but a retrospective descriptive design cannot show that link; the finding is an association only. It is not clear from the abstract what the P values compare, since the groups are split by haematological status and no healthy control group is described.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no full text retrieved; retrospective design; Babesia species and diagnostic method not stated; no healthy control group described; the groups with abnormal (186) and normal (114) haematology are not explained; no clinical severity or outcome data in the abstract; no DOI or indexed record, so the publication details rest on a repository entry.
