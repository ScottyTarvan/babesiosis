## Abstract-derived summary

Between October 2023 and September 2024, 620 dogs with pallor, fever, lymphadenopathy or neurological signs were assessed in Andhra Pradesh. Babesia was identified in 136 dogs (21.9%) by microscopy of Giemsa-stained smears, and PCR produced a B. gibsoni-specific 670 bp product. Eleven dogs (1.8% of all; 8.1% of infected) were classified as cerebral babesiosis, defined by parasites in CSF erythrocytes. Seven of the 11 were male. Labrador Retrievers, German Shepherds and mongrels each contributed two of the 11 cases, and the 0-1 year group had the highest incidence. Signs included ataxia, seizures, paddling, opisthotonus, head pressing, stupor, rigidity, nystagmus, blindness and paraplegia. The neurological examination assessed reflexes and was divided into three primary categories.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cerebral babesiosis occurs in a minority of B. gibsoni-infected dogs and shows varied neurological deficits.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a phenotype description of neurological involvement, not a mechanism study. The sequestration and hypoxia explanation is a literature statement without data. Because dogs were selected for signs, the percentages are not population prevalences.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=11; no controls, MRI/CT, CSF results or outcomes; CSF sampling and the case definition are not described in the methods; PCR run only on the 11 cases and unsequenced; other CNS disease excluded by history rather than testing; breed and age percentages from tiny subsets; no outcome data; minor numerical inconsistencies (8.08% vs 8.09%); peer-review policy not checked.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
