## Abstract-derived summary

Seven dogs with acute B. canis infection (six purebred, one crossbreed; five males, two females; aged 6 months to 8 years, median 5) were enrolled to test whether red-cell indices relate to circulating matrix metalloproteinases. Infection was identified on blood smear and additionally confirmed molecularly. Haematology showed mild to moderate anaemia. Serum gelatinase activity was measured by zymography and associations were tested with Spearman correlation. ProMMP-2 correlated inversely with haematocrit (rho -0.793, 95% CI -0.968 to -0.099, p=0.033) and showed a borderline inverse link with erythrocyte count (rho -0.739, p=0.058). MMP-9 correlated positively with erythrocyte count (rho 0.937, p=0.002) and haematocrit (rho 0.757, p=0.049). The authors read this opposite pattern as indicating that, in acute infection, anaemia severity tracks endothelial injury-driven inflammation more than leukocyte activity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia severity in acute B. canis infection appears more influenced by inflammation from endothelial damage than by leukocyte activity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating link between serum gelatinases (MMP-2 as an endothelial/platelet-derived marker, MMP-9 as leukocyte-derived) and the erythrogram. Neither endothelial injury nor leukocyte activity was measured directly; cellular source is inferred from the literature. It is a biomarker association, not evidence of mechanism. A positive MMP-9/RBC correlation may simply reflect leukopenia or leukocyte kinetics tracking disease stage.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=7, wide confidence intervals (some nearly reaching zero), several uncorrected correlations, no healthy controls, single time point, no leukocyte or platelet counts reported in abstract, anaemia only mild to moderate, conference abstract without methods detail.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
