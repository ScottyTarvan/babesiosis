## Abstract-derived summary

Acute kidney injury is a frequent complication of canine babesiosis, which the authors attribute to Babesia canis. The investigators enrolled 22 naturally infected dogs, stratified by degree of renal dysfunction, and 8 healthy controls, then compared serum and urine proteomes using tandem-mass-tag quantitative proteomics. Relative to controls, 58 serum proteins and 259 urinary proteins differed in abundance across the dysfunction groups. Pathway enrichment analyses pointed to altered biological processes in diseased dogs, and several candidate biomarkers were validated. Urinary NGAL and urinary L-FABP were proposed as early indicators of renal dysfunction because they increased before azotaemia developed. The abstract does not state how groups were defined, how species was identified, or the validation method and performance.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Urinary NGAL and L-FABP are putative early biomarkers of renal dysfunction in canine babesiosis, rising before azotaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating description of the serum and urinary proteome in babesiosis-associated renal dysfunction. It suggests candidate tubular injury markers and pathways but cannot show causation or distinguish Babesia-specific injury (haemoglobin or pigment nephropathy, hypoxia, immune or inflammatory mechanisms) from generic AKI. Biomarker timing claims ("before azotaemia") are cross-sectional associations.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n (22 split across several groups); species not molecularly confirmed in abstract; no non-renal babesiosis comparator described; no treatment or co-infection details; single-centre generalisability unknown; no external validation reported.
