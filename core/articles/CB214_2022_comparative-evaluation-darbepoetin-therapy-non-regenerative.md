## Abstract-derived summary

Of 68 dogs with signs of anaemia presented to a teaching hospital in Mannuthy, India, 20 with non-regenerative anaemia (reticulocytes under 60,000/µL, haematocrit under 30%) and B. gibsoni positivity were enrolled, with 10 healthy dogs as controls. Both infected groups (n=10 each) had significant anaemia and thrombocytopenia. Total protein, albumin and total iron-binding capacity matched controls, while serum iron and transferrin saturation were comparable between infected groups yet higher than in controls. Group I received clindamycin and doxycycline for three weeks; Group II received the same drugs plus weekly darbepoetin (0.5 µg/kg) for three weeks. Clinical signs resolved within 10 days in both groups, and haematological values did not differ between them. The authors saw no added benefit from darbepoetin.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological comparison with controls; treatment comparison secondary)
- **Authors' conclusion:** darbepoetin offers no advantage over conventional antimicrobial therapy for non-regenerative anaemia in B. gibsoni infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The pathophysiologically useful content is the baseline profile: anaemia without reticulocyte response, thrombocytopenia, and raised serum iron and transferrin saturation. This argues against iron deficiency as the limiting factor and is compatible with impaired erythropoiesis or haemolysis-released iron, but marrow data are not described, so mechanism is not established. The treatment result is efficacy evidence, not mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; selection of only non-regenerative cases; B. gibsoni identification method and co-infections not reported; randomisation and blinding not stated; antimicrobials confounded by remission; no marrow cytology or outcome beyond 10 days reported; journal peer-review practice not verified.
