## Abstract-derived summary

The authors reviewed canine babesiosis in Barnaul, Siberia, using laboratory records from 2013 to 2017 held by an agrarian university and the city's central veterinary hospital. Cases were diagnosed from clinical presentation plus microscopy of blood smears. Of 1,258 blood samples from dogs of varied sex, age and breed, 36.3% were reported infected; mongrels, terriers, German Shepherds, Cocker Spaniels, Central Asian Shepherds and dachshunds predominated, as did males, particularly those older than eight years. Cases clustered in three city districts and in two seasonal windows (April-June, August-October). A flagging survey collected 535 ixodid ticks. Liver enzymes, bilirubin fractions, creatinine, urea and alkaline phosphatase were described as discriminating organ-system involvement; calcium and phosphorus were normal, and total protein was low in dogs under two years.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hepatic, renal and bilirubin-related serum variables are significant indicators of organ-system pathology in dogs with babesiosis, alongside defined breed, sex, age, seasonal and district patterns of infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Weakly informative for pathophysiology. It suggests hepatic and renal involvement in natural infection (consistent with the known hepatopathy and azotaemia of canine babesiosis) but supplies no effect sizes in the abstract and cannot separate parasite-driven injury from haemolysis, hypoxia or concurrent disease. It is a syndrome-level description, not a mechanism or a validated severity biomarker.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no numerical biochemistry results in the abstract; no healthy control group described; species not identified; smear/clinical diagnosis only; retrospective records with unclear denominator for the 36.3% figure (likely clinically suspect dogs); co-infections not addressed; tick species not named; regional journal with no verifiable peer-review detail on the article page.
