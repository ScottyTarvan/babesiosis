## Abstract-derived summary

Using serum from 15 healthy dogs and 15 dogs with naturally acquired Babesia canis infection, the authors isolated small extracellular vesicles (under 200 nm) and compared their physical characteristics and proteins by LC-MS/MS. Vesicle size and concentration did not differ between groups, nor did the number of protein identifications. Overall, 211 canine proteins were found, of which 147 were validated as vesicle-associated; 73 were specific to infected dogs and were enriched for immune, adhesion (integrin) and vascular signalling (VEGF, VEGFR, LKB1) annotations. Inferred cellular origins were mainly immune cells and platelets. Few parasite-derived proteins passed the stringent filters. The authors propose that serum vesicles may participate in parasite invasion and the host response, while stating that further experimental work is needed.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (exploratory omics)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum vesicles and their protein cargo may play a role in B. canis invasion and the host response, but more experimental research is warranted.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating host-response omics. Enrichment of immune-cell and platelet-derived vesicle proteins and VEGF/integrin pathway annotations is compatible with immune activation and endothelial or vascular involvement, but it is pathway-annotation evidence only and does not show causation, clinical severity relevance or a mechanism. No difference in vesicle number or size limits use as a biomarker.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; "preliminary" design; clinical severity, co-infections and treatment status not described in abstract; species method not reported in abstract; pathway enrichment on a small protein set; very few parasite proteins detected; sample numbers differ between abstract and repository record.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
