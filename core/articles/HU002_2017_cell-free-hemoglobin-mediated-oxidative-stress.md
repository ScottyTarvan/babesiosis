## Abstract-derived summary

The study tested whether cell-free haemoglobin (CFH) and the oxidative injury it drives contribute to kidney damage in severe falciparum malaria. Acute kidney injury (creatinine at least 1.5 times expected baseline) was present on enrolment in 62 of 107 severe cases (58%). These patients had higher CFH (geometric mean 8.8 vs 5.1 microM, P=0.018), F2-isoprostanes (56.7 vs 27.8 pg/ml) and isofurans (109.2 vs 41.7 pg/ml), both P<0.001. CFH tracked haemolysis markers, PfHRP2 parasite burden and F2-isoprostanes. Lipid-peroxidation markers were higher in those later dialysed and in fatal cases, and over 72 hours several markers, including red cell rigidity, independently predicted rising creatinine.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prospective human observational study; associations, not causal tests.
- **Authors' conclusion:** CFH and lipid peroxidation are linked to kidney injury and severity, suggesting a role in tubular injury; adjunctive therapy targeting them deserves evaluation.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** ERY (haemolysis, free haem, scavenger depletion) rated speculative: no canine study measured CFH, haem, haptoglobin or haemopexin; indirect haemolysis and oxidative signals exist (CB099, CB082, CB006, CB083, CB114), and CB117 (3 vs 3 dogs) found haemopexin higher, opposite to malaria. REN (CFH to oxidative tubular injury) rated mechanistically plausible: CB083 and CB154 show oxidative shifts without renal linkage. Differences: P. falciparum sequestration and parasite-burden-linked haemolysis, immune haemolysis in dogs, cross-sectional canine sampling.
- **Key limitations:** single country; adults only; observational confounding; abstract does not name scavenger proteins.
