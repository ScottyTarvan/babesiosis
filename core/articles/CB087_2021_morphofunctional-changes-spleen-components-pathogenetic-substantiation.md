## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined spleen tissue from pet and guard dogs diagnosed with babesiosis (n=187) and compared it with spleens from 15 control dogs, using gross inspection, haematoxylin-eosin histology and morphometry. Control organs fell within expected normal limits. Affected dogs showed enlarged spleens, some with rupture and oedema. Histologically there was non-specific follicular hyperplasia, leukocyte and macrophage infiltration, loss of normal zonation in lymphoid follicles and germinal centres, softened pulp, shedding of vascular endothelium and local circulatory disturbance. White pulp occupied up to 39.78 ± 1.99% of the section area. The author concluded that the changes were organ-specific, contributed to a "hepatolienal syndrome", and could serve as markers of babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The author states that the macro- and microscopic splenic changes are organotypic, drive a hepatolienal syndrome through the presence of Babesia spp., and are signalling markers of the disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a splenic histopathology/morphometry description (reactive lymphoid hyperplasia, macrophage activity, endothelial desquamation, local circulatory disorder) that is consistent with an immunopathological and microcirculatory component of canine babesiosis. It supports a descriptive syndrome-level picture only; the causal attribution to the parasite and the "hepatolienal syndrome" are not demonstrated by the abstract data, and the changes are described as non-specific.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species not identified; diagnostic criteria, sampling route and timing (necropsy vs splenectomy) not stated in abstract; small control group (n=15) against 187 cases; no statistical testing reported in abstract; no parasite localisation in tissue; co-infections and treatment not addressed; single-author, no abstract-level quantification beyond white pulp area.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
