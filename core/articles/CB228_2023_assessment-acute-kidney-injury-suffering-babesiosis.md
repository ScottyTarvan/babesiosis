## Abstract-derived summary

The study set out to identify the infectious causes of acute kidney injury (AKI) in dogs and to grade its severity, because babesiosis and leptospirosis look alike clinically and the correct diagnosis is often delayed. Babesiosis was established by blood smear and PCR; leptospirosis by PCR and microscopic agglutination testing, with confirmation on paired sera. AKI was defined as a creatinine rise of at least 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours of presentation, and animals were staged by International Renal Interest Society criteria. Every dog with confirmed leptospirosis had AKI, compared with 15% of dogs with babesiosis. The authors suggest that early recognition of AKI allows renal function to recover with short-term treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** AKI is a notable complication of both diseases, and early diagnosis and staging permit recovery of renal function with short-term treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides an incidence estimate for AKI in canine babesiosis (about 15%, much lower than in leptospirosis in this cohort), consistent with renal injury being a complication in a minority rather than a universal feature. The abstract gives no data on mechanism (haemoglobinuria, hypoxia, inflammation), IRIS grade distribution, or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Denominators, species and recruitment period not reported; the AKI definition requires serial creatinine, so dogs presenting with established azotaemia may be misclassified; co-infection and prior treatment not described; the treatment-recovery claim is not supported by reported outcome data; no controls.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
