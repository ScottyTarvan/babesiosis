## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes an Akita Inu living in Belgium that presented with an atypical form of acute canine babesiosis. The unusual features were neurological signs together with pancytopenia, meaning reduced counts in all blood cell lines. The parasite was identified as Babesia canis by examining a blood smear; the abstract mentions no molecular confirmation. After the dog was treated with imidocarb dipropionate, its neurological signs resolved. Normalisation of the haematological abnormalities was gradual, occurring over five months. The report therefore documents a presentation in which neurological signs and pancytopenia occurred together in one naturally infected dog, followed by clinical recovery after antiprotozoal therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract presents the case as an unusual clinical form of acute babesiosis, with neurological signs that resolved after imidocarb treatment and pancytopenia that normalised gradually over 5 months.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report is a distinct-syndrome description (neurological signs with pancytopenia) in a dog with B. canis seen on a blood smear. Neurological signs resolved after antiprotozoal treatment, which is consistent with, but does not show, a link between the parasite and the signs. Haematological recovery was slow, over 5 months. A single case cannot show how common this presentation is.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single animal; species identified on blood smear only; abstract gives no neurological work-up, imaging or cerebrospinal fluid findings; no numeric haematology in the abstract; no control group; full text not read.
