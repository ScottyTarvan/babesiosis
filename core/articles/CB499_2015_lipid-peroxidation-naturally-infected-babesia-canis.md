## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers measured malondialdehyde, a marker of lipid peroxidation, in blood from 30 dogs with babesiosis caused by Babesia canis canis and 25 healthy dogs. Affected dogs were sampled before imidocarb dipropionate treatment, on day one, and again on days two, three and seven. Ten healthy dogs also received imidocarb, which did not alter their values, so all healthy dogs served as controls. On day one, malondialdehyde was higher in affected dogs than in controls, in both uncomplicated and complicated disease. By day seven it had fallen but stayed above control values. On day seven, dogs with complicated disease had higher values than uncomplicated dogs. Dogs with multiple organ dysfunction syndrome had higher values than the uncomplicated and single-complication groups, with the timing not stated. Concentration correlated positively with disease outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Increased lipid peroxidation was still present in affected dogs on day seven, and the authors suggest oxidative stress could have a possible causative role in the clinical severity of the disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Malondialdehyde was higher in infected dogs than in healthy dogs and was associated with complicated disease, multiple organ dysfunction and outcome. The design is observational, so the findings show an association with severity and do not show that oxidative stress drives severity. Persistence of raised values after treatment is a descriptive observation that fits ongoing oxidative burden but does not explain its source.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; species identification method not stated; group sizes for complicated, single-complication and multiple organ dysfunction groups not given in the abstract; treated dogs lack an untreated infected comparison group; no multivariable adjustment described.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
