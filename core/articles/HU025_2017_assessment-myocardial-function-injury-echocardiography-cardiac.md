## Abstract-derived summary
Severe malaria disturbs circulation, and the earlier FEAST fluid trial linked fluid boluses to excess deaths, raising suspicion of heart dysfunction. This prospective observational study of 104 Ugandan children with severe falciparum malaria (61 with severe anaemia) used echocardiography at presentation and 24 hours, plus troponin I and BNP at presentation. Troponin I exceeded 0.1 ng/mL in 48% while median BNP was normal. Cardiac index was higher in severe anaemia (6.89 versus 5.28 L/min/m2), fell to normal by 24 hours and correlated inversely with haemoglobin. Only 3.8% had depressed ejection fraction; six died. The authors conclude systolic function is mostly preserved and that troponin rises did not track dysfunction.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** prospective observational human cohort
- **Authors' conclusion:** High cardiac index is a physiological response to circulatory compromise and anaemia, over 96% retain systolic function, and troponin-evidenced myocardial injury does not correlate with cardiac dysfunction.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Shared pathway is troponin release without overt systolic failure in severe parasitaemic disease. Canine: CB014 (troponin I raised in 19 of 50 dogs, ECG and echo changes nonspecific) and CB080 (necropsy cardiomyocyte necrosis, proposed circulatory failure). Rated mechanistically plausible: both hosts show injury markers, but the canine work did not test troponin against function and neither establishes cause. Differences: malaria comparator, children with severe anaemia and transfusion care, P. falciparum cytoadherence, and different troponin assays.
- **Key limitations:** Single site, no healthy control group; abstract has a unit error (haemoglobin given in mg/dL); associational; cannot attribute the troponin rise to a cause.
