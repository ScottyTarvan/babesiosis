## Abstract-derived summary

This study measured serum malondialdehyde (MDA), a marker of lipid peroxidation, in 35 naturally infected dogs (confirmed as B. canis by blood smear and PCR) and in 14 healthy dogs. Infected dogs were classed as complicated (seven dogs, with renal, hepatic, muscular or respiratory involvement) or uncomplicated (28 dogs), and the uncomplicated dogs were further graded by anaemia severity. Urea, creatinine, bilirubin and several liver enzymes were significantly higher in the complicated group. Mean MDA was significantly higher in infected dogs (36.90 ± 13.95 μmol/l) than in controls (8.13 ± 1.78 μmol/l). MDA did not differ between complicated and uncomplicated dogs, nor across the anaemia grades. The authors concluded that oxidative damage is involved in natural B. canis infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Oxidative damage is involved in natural B. canis infection; the full text adds that it may play an important role in babesiosis pathophysiology and that larger studies are needed (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Serum MDA was higher in infected dogs than in healthy dogs, which fits increased lipid peroxidation, but the design cannot show that oxidative stress drives anaemia or organ injury. MDA did not separate complicated from uncomplicated cases or anaemia grades, so it did not track severity in this sample. Reduced renal clearance could also raise serum MDA; the authors discuss this and judge it unlikely to explain the finding (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only seven complicated cases and one severely anaemic dog (full text); single admission sample with unknown time since infection (full text); mixed breed, age and sex (full text); healthy controls were mongrel dogs (full text); no antioxidant measures; imidocarb given on the day of admission (full text).

  - All 35 infected dogs were also part of a larger study by Beck et al. (2009), in which Babesia was characterised by PCR (full text)
  - All infected dogs received a single subcutaneous dose of imidocarb dipropionate (6 mg/kg) on admission (full text)
  - Among the 28 uncomplicated dogs, 1 was severely, 9 moderately and 18 mildly anaemic; MDA means were 38.93 ± 13.04 μmol/l (moderate) and 34.93 ± 15.75 μmol/l (mild) (full text)
  - Platelet count was 30 ± 30.05 ×10^9/l in infected and 258 ± 59.59 ×10^9/l in healthy dogs; total protein, albumin and glucose also differed from controls (full text)
  - Four of the seven complicated dogs died despite treatment; overall mortality was 11.4 percent (full text)
  - Two dogs met criteria for multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (full text)
  - Groups were compared with the Tukey HSD test, with P < 0.05 taken as significant (full text)
