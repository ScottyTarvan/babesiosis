## Abstract-derived summary

The authors sampled 44 dogs presenting with fever, enlarged lymph nodes and reduced urine output, to describe haematological and biochemical changes in B. gibsoni infection with renal involvement. Blood smears showed piroplasms in 5 dogs, whereas PCR identified 10 infected animals, and these 10 were taken forward for haematology and biochemistry. Anaemia and thrombocytopenia dominated the haematology, and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine were raised, which the authors read as renal insufficiency. At necropsy, the kidney capsule was firmly adherent, and histology showed dilated tubules and fibrosis. The authors argue that in chronic infection, an inflammatory response persists without ongoing parasite replication and can injure several organ systems.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** the authors state that inflammation set off by infection may damage multiple organ systems in the chronic stage, so disease progression and overall condition should be assessed.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive syndrome report linking B. gibsoni PCR positivity with azotaemia and renal fibrosis. The chronic-inflammation explanation is a proposed mechanism, not tested here. Tubular dilatation and capsular adherence with fibrosis are non-specific and could reflect pre-existing chronic kidney disease, so renal damage cannot be attributed to babesiosis from the abstract. It does flag PCR sensitivity exceeding smear.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 10 confirmed cases; no controls; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) and prior renal disease not addressed in the abstract; number necropsied and timing unclear; no urinalysis or staging stated; journal not indexed in DOAJ/MEDLINE, DOI does not resolve, and no peer-review policy could be viewed, so peer review is unverified.
