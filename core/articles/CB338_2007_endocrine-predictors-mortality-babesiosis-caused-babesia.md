## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective, cross-sectional observational study examined whether pituitary-adrenal and pituitary-thyroid hormones measured at hospital admission relate to outcome in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis rossi. Ninety-five dogs were enrolled, with infection confirmed by PCR and reverse line blot; dogs carrying B. canis vogeli or Ehrlichia canis were left out. Blood was taken before treatment and assayed for cortisol, ACTH, total and free thyroxine, and TSH. Dogs fell into three outcome groups: 7 hospitalised dogs that died, 56 hospitalised survivors, and 32 outpatients. Non-survivors had higher cortisol and ACTH and lower total and free thyroxine than both other groups, while TSH did not differ among groups. The authors report that death was associated with raised cortisol and ACTH and with reduced T4 and fT4.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** Mortality was significantly associated with high cortisol and ACTH and with low T4 and free T4 concentrations in dogs with B. canis rossi babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Admission hormone concentrations separated the dogs that died from survivors and outpatients, which is consistent with raised pituitary-adrenal hormones and lower thyroid hormones in the dogs that died. The design is observational, so the findings show association with outcome, not that hormone changes cause death. The thyroid pattern may reflect non-thyroidal illness rather than primary thyroid disease, though the abstract does not test this.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 7 deaths, so estimates for non-survivors are imprecise; single admission sample, with no follow-up measurements described in the abstract; no adjustment for other severity markers is described in the abstract; the abstract names no setting beyond hospital admission; full text not read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
