## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether dogs with tick-borne haemoparasite infections show thyroid hormone disturbance consistent with euthyroid sick syndrome. Twelve dogs were split into a group infected with Ehrlichia canis alone and a group co-infected with E. canis plus Babesia canis vogeli and/or Anaplasma platys, with nested PCR used for diagnosis. Total T3, total T4, free T4 and canine TSH were assayed by chemiluminescence before and after 28 days of doxycycline at 10 mg/kg daily. At baseline every dog had total T3 and/or T4 outside the reference range, with low T4 common and some raised T3. After treatment all were E. canis-negative, but eight stayed infected or were reinfected with other agents. Dogs that cleared all pathogens mostly normalised, whereas the reinfected G1 dogs kept low T4. The authors attribute the changes to euthyroid sick syndrome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that thyroid hormone alterations were due to the hemoparasite infections, represent euthyroid sick syndrome, and may help prognosis and follow-up.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows non-thyroidal-illness-type endocrine changes in sick dogs co-infected with B. vogeli, a systemic MET/SEV observation. It cannot attribute any effect to Babesia: E. canis was present in every dog, no Babesia-only group exists, and the number of B. vogeli-positive dogs is not given. Causality is not shown, because there are no healthy controls and doxycycline and recovery from illness are confounded with pathogen clearance. The prognostic claim is hypothesis-level only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 12, no uninfected controls, unequal and unstated group sizes, co-infection throughout, treatment confounding, no cTSH or FT4 results summarised in the abstract, reference intervals not stated, and internally inconsistent group labelling in the abstract (post-treatment reinfected dogs are assigned to G1, which was defined as E. canis alone).
