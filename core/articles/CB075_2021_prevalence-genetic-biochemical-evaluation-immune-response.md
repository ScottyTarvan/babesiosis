## Abstract-derived summary

The authors screened 242 working police dogs in Egypt for canine piroplasms, using molecular detection of the 18S rRNA gene with sequencing and phylogenetics, and compared infected and healthy animals. Sixty-two dogs (about one quarter) were positive for B. vogeli, with a higher infection rate in males; some had mixed infection with E. canis. Blood count, biochemistry, oxidative stress markers and expression of the cytokines IFN-γ and TNF-α were measured in healthy dogs, B. vogeli-infected dogs and mixed-infection dogs. Oxidative stress markers and both cytokines were reported as higher in infected dogs. The sequence was deposited in GenBank (MT565474.1). The authors concluded that B. vogeli adversely affects police dog health and may be fatal in some animals.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile) with associational immune/oxidative data
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. vogeli infection had wholly adverse effects on police dog health and could cause death in some dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a clinicopathological profile (anaemia, raised liver and renal indices, thrombocytopenia and leukopenia only in mixed infection) plus elevated IFN-γ, TNF-α and SOD in infected dogs, consistent with a pro-inflammatory, oxidative host response. It does not show causality or distinguish B. vogeli effects from E. canis effects, and does not establish a mechanism of anaemia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** E. canis co-infection in 28/62 dogs with undescribed detection method; cytokine/oxidative subgroup sizes unstated and apparently very small (about 4-5 per group, puppy controls); abstract overstates oxidative findings (full text: MDA and CAT not significant, SOD significant); inconsistent group counts and species naming (B. vogeli vs B. canis) between tables and text; no ethics approval number.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
