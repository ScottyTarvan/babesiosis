## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective and prospective case series reviewed the records of 363 dogs with confirmed Babesia infection, seen at veterinary practices in Lusaka, Zambia, between 2000 and 2013. Demographic data, presenting complaints and clinical signs were examined alongside haematology and parasite species. Mongrels were the largest breed group, and males made up about two thirds of the dogs. Anorexia and lethargy were the commonest complaints, while fever, pallor, lymphadenopathy and tick infestation led the examination findings. Anaemia affected nearly every dog, and nucleated red cells were seen in a little over forty percent. A mixed Babesia rossi and Babesia gibsoni infection was the most frequent pattern, with smaller proportions infected by a single species. Treatment relied mainly on tetracyclines and imidocarb and was usually followed by clinical improvement. The authors report the first detection of B. gibsoni in naturally infected dogs in Zambia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study documents the Babesia species present in Zambian dogs and the clinicopathological presentation of canine babesiosis there, and it highlights differences from the presentation described in other countries of the subregion.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The series gives a large descriptive haematological profile of naturally infected dogs, dominated by anaemia, and the authors present it as the first systematic clinicopathological study of the disease in this region (full text). Because most dogs with species data had mixed infections, the findings cannot be assigned to one species, and the comparison of PCV between infection groups was not significant (full text). The data do not allow conclusions about mechanisms or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** convenience sampling from three clinical sites (full text); mixed retrospective and prospective records; no control group; species identified by PCR in only 62 of 363 dogs (full text); diagnosis by blood smear, which may miss infections; primers not specific for B. vogeli (full text); outcomes poorly defined; treatment was not standardised.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Records came from the University of Zambia School of Veterinary Medicine clinic and two private practices in Lusaka; retrospective records covered 2000 to 2009 and the prospective phase ran from March 2009 to December 2013 (full text)
  - Of 412 records positive on blood smear, 363 met the inclusion criteria; dogs with other diagnosed concurrent disease or incomplete records were excluded (full text)
  - Seminested PCR was run on 62 Babesia-positive dogs from the prospective phase: 20 had B. gibsoni (Asian genotype) alone, 5 had B. rossi alone and 37 had a mixed infection (full text). The 59.7%, 32.2% and 8% in the abstract are therefore proportions of these 62 dogs (full text)
  - Overall mean PCV was 18.4% (SD 9.2, range 5.0 to 49.0). Anaemia was severe (PCV below 15%) in 55.1%, moderate in 33%, mild in 8.0% and absent in 3.6% (full text)
  - The anaemia was hyporegenerative, normocytic and normochromic in 65.3% of cases; hypochromasia in 34.7% and thrombocytopenia in 17.1% (full text)
  - Mean PCV was 10.67% (SD 1.53) with B. rossi alone, 16.11% (SD 6.81) with B. gibsoni alone and 20.15% (SD 9.94) with mixed infection; the difference was not significant (ANOVA P = 0.15). Only 5 dogs had B. rossi alone (full text)
  - Mean rectal temperature was 39.6 C; haemoglobinuria was not recorded in any case. A weak positive correlation was found between PCV and rectal temperature (r = 0.231) (full text)
  - Hospitalised dogs (152 of 363, 41.9%) had a lower mean PCV than outpatients (15.36% vs 21.36%); the text calls this not significant although the quoted ANOVA P value is 0.030 (full text)
  - Blood transfusion was given to 7 dogs (1.9%); doxycycline with imidocarb was the most common injectable antiprotozoal treatment (50.96%), and dexamethasone was given to 136 dogs (full text)
  - The authors regard the disease in Zambia as less virulent than in South Africa and possibly uncomplicated; this is an interpretation not tested against a comparison group (full text)
