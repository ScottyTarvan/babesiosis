## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether routine clinical indices can reliably identify canine babesiosis in Nigeria. They enrolled 103 dogs showing compatible signs, such as anorexia, fever, ticks and enlarged lymph nodes or spleen. Thin blood smears were examined, comparing parasite detection in capillary and cephalic blood, and haematology and chemistry were run on automated analysers. Outcome in infected dogs was followed. About 61% of the dogs had Babesia on smears. Breed, sex and age were not significantly related to infection, and rectal temperature did not differ between smear-positive and smear-negative dogs. Heart and pulse rates were significantly higher in positive dogs. Packed cell volume did not differ significantly between groups, and most positive dogs had a normal leukogram, while a minority showed leukocytosis or leukopenia. Most positive dogs received oxytetracycline and recovered. The authors concluded that clinical indices are unreliable diagnostic markers.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Clinical indices are not reliable markers for diagnosing canine babesiosis. In the full-text conclusion, heart and pulse rates are described as sensitive indicators, while temperature, respiratory rate, PCV and WBC count are not (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The comparison is between two groups of clinically suspected dogs, split by a thin-smear result, so it describes how little the clinical picture separates smear-positive from smear-negative dogs, not how infected dogs differ from healthy ones. The smear is an imperfect reference standard, and smear-negative dogs may include infected animals, which could dilute any differences. The anaemia and leukogram data are a descriptive profile of 63 smear-positive dogs, with wide variation in both.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** diagnosis by thin smear only, with no molecular confirmation or species identification; comparison group is clinically suspected smear-negative dogs, not healthy controls; enrolment depended on clinical signs, which shapes the clinical indices compared; recruitment period not stated; internal inconsistencies in the article, listed under *Information beyond the abstract*; treatment not standardised and follow-up outcome incompletely accounted for; many comparisons without adjustment.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

  - Dogs came from three sites in south-western Nigeria: the Veterinary Teaching Hospital of the Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta; the Ministry of Agriculture Veterinary Hospital Complex, Itaeko; and Mokola Veterinary Clinic, Ibadan. There were 48 males and 55 females (full text).
  - Inclusion required tick infestation or history of it, anorexia, and any two of persistent fever, splenomegaly, anaemia, haemoglobinuria and jaundice. Dogs treated in the preceding 3 weeks, with concurrent disease, or with Ehrlichia canis morulae on capillary smears were excluded (full text).
  - Of 103 dogs, 63 were Babesia-positive and 40 were negative on smear. Parasites were seen in 57.4% on cephalic smears, 34.4% on capillary smears and 8.2% at both sites (full text).
  - Mean heart rate was 128 ± 19.7 bpm in positive dogs and 118 ± 17.7 bpm in negative dogs (full text).
  - Mean PCV in positive dogs was 26.4 ± 11.26% (abstract and full text). For negative dogs the abstract gives 31.6 ± 11.9%, and the full-text results give 28.8 ± 10.5% (full text).
  - Among 63 positive dogs, 21 had no anaemia, 14 mild, 8 moderate and 20 severe anaemia by PCV; three had hypoxia and respiratory distress (full text).
  - Leukogram in positive dogs: 39 normal, 14 leukocytosis, 10 leukopenia (full text).
  - Treatment was mostly 5% oxytetracycline at 5 mg/kg for 5 days. Fifty of 63 positive dogs recovered and 11 died; these add to 61, and the text does not account for the other two. Seven deaths occurred among the 44 dogs given oxytetracycline alone (full text).
  - In positive dogs, rectal temperature correlated positively with PCV (r = 0.358) and negatively with WBC count (r about -0.39) (full text).
  - The full text gives 66.5% positivity in males (31/48), but 31/48 is 64.6%; the discussion gives 65%. It gives 56.2% for females (30/55), but 30/55 is 54.5%. Table 3 totals 39 negative dogs, against 40 elsewhere (full text).
