## Abstract-derived summary
This prospective Ghanaian study enrolled patients with severe (n=20) and cerebral (n=13) falciparum malaria to ask whether raised von Willebrand factor (VWF) antigen reflects altered function. VWF collagen-binding activity was raised disproportionately, with medians of 7.6 IU/ml (cerebral) and 7.0 IU/ml (severe) against 1.9 IU/ml in controls (p<0.005), and tracked abnormal ultra-large multimers present in patients but not controls. Activity of the VWF-cleaving enzyme ADAMTS13 stayed persistently low, near 55% of normal (p<0.005). Mixing patient plasma with pooled normal plasma showed time-dependent loss of ADAMTS13 function that IL-6, free haemoglobin, factor VIII and thrombospondin-1 did not explain, implying an unidentified inhibitor.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** small primary human cohort with in-vitro plasma mixing experiments.
- **Authors' conclusion:** severe falciparum malaria involves acute endothelial activation, abnormal circulating ultra-large VWF multimers and reduced ADAMTS13 function, partly via an unidentified inhibitor.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** shared pathway is endothelial activation with microvascular thrombosis. CB004 (B. canis, 30 dogs vs 10 controls) found raised thrombomodulin, VCAM-1, HMGB-1 and suPAR; CB145 showed fibrin microthrombi at necropsy; CB041 (review) proposes capillary sequestration. VWF and ADAMTS13 were not measured in canine studies. Rated **mechanistically plausible**. Differences: P. falciparum sequesters via PfEMP1 binding, whereas B. canis sequestration is only proposed; analytes differ; host splenic status shapes severity (CB021, splenectomised dogs with B. vogeli).
- **Key limitations:** very small groups; control size not stated; unidentified inhibitor; association rather than causation; published 2009.
