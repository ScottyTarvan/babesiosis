## Abstract-derived summary

Canine babesiosis is a tick-borne disease of dogs found around the world, caused by a growing list of Babesia species that molecular methods are helping to separate. Clinical signs are usually linked to destruction of infected red blood cells, but in some animals and with some species an immune-mediated element of the anaemia, or a marked inflammatory reaction, is also present. This complicated form of disease carries substantial illness and death. Many clinical markers have been studied so that clinicians can give more accurate prognoses and adjust treatment, which differs by infecting species. The authors cover how the parasites are classified, how the disease presents, imaging results, markers useful clinically, therapy and prevention.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract gives no separate conclusion. It states that the complicated form of canine babesiosis is associated with high morbidity and mortality and that clinical markers have been investigated to improve prognosis and tailor treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A narrative overview of the syndromes and complications of canine babesiosis and of the markers that have been associated with severity and outcome. It is a secondary source, so the findings it relays are associational and depend on the cited studies. It is most useful as a map of complications (hepatopathy, acute kidney injury, cerebral disease, ARDS, pancreatitis, cardiac dysfunction) and of candidate biomarkers.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** narrative review with no stated search or selection method; secondary reporting of other studies' results; data mainly from B. rossi infections, so findings may not carry over to other species; individual markers rest on single studies in several sections; supportive care for complications is not covered.

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - The authors state that most published data on complications and prognostic markers come from B. rossi infections, so the clinical signs they describe are those of B. rossi unless another species is named (full text)
  - Complicated babesiosis is described as disease in which the changes are not explained by haemolytic anaemia and hypoxia alone, loosely modelled on the WHO classification for malaria (full text)
  - Reported mortality was 29% in dogs with a severe inflammatory reaction and 8% in dogs with severe anaemia, and as high as 45% for complicated cases in one study (full text)
  - Complications described are hepatopathy, acute kidney injury, cerebral babesiosis, ARDS, "red biliary" (relative haemoconcentration), rhabdomyolysis, pancreatitis and cardiac dysfunction (full text)
  - In pancreatitis, mortality of 21% is cited; in the acute virulent form, 87% of complicated cases met SIRS criteria (full text)
  - Central nervous system involvement is cited as carrying a 57 times greater chance of death (full text)
  - Biomarker sections cover blood cell parameters, coagulation profiles, cytokines, acid-base values, metabolic abnormalities, endocrine predictors, renal, liver and cardiovascular markers; MCP-1 is cited at 118 pg/mL in control dogs, 431 pg/mL in infected survivors and 757 pg/mL in non-survivors (full text)
  - B. conradae is described as considered more pathogenic, with mortality of 43% versus 16% cited; the comparison groups for these figures are not named in the review (full text)
  - The treatment and prophylaxis sections are brief and state that supportive management of complications is not covered (full text)
