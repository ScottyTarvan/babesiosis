## Abstract-derived summary

Eighteen dogs with clinical signs of babesiosis attending a veterinary medicine outpatient clinic in Bangalore were screened for Babesia gibsoni by blood smear, with PCR as the confirmatory test. Immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) was identified using a saline agglutination test, smear spherocytosis and the Coombs test. The authors then compared blood counts and serum chemistry in these dogs. White cell counts were significantly higher, whereas red cell count, haemoglobin, packed cell volume and platelets were significantly lower. Serum ALT, ALP, total bilirubin, creatinine and urea nitrogen rose significantly, albumin fell significantly, and total protein and globulin showed only non-significant shifts. The paper presents these as the haemato-biochemical profile of B. gibsoni-associated IMHA.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that IMHA caused by B. gibsoni is marked by leucocytosis, cytopenias (red cells, haemoglobin, PCV, platelets) and raised hepatic and renal markers with hypoalbuminaemia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinical-pathology profile of severe B. gibsoni disease, relevant to ERY with secondary hepatic, renal and platelet signals. Because only Coombs-positive infected dogs were studied, the design cannot show which findings arise from an immune mechanism rather than parasite-driven haemolysis, hypoxia or inflammation. Enzyme and azotaemia explanations (hypoxic liver injury, dehydration, hypotension) are the authors' speculation, not tested. Antibody tests are frequently positive in babesiosis and do not discriminate primary from secondary IMHA.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; control group of 6 with age/breed matching unreported; recruitment period and the number screened before selecting 18 not reported; no Coombs-negative infected comparison; no urinalysis or urine specific gravity, so prerenal versus intrinsic azotaemia unresolved; no coagulation, parasitaemia, treatment or outcome data; co-infections not excluded.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
