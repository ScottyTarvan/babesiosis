## Abstract-derived summary

This Brazilian study described the blood count findings in dogs diagnosed with Babesia spp. by parasitological examination. The authors analysed complete blood count records of 101 household dogs from the municipality of Araçatuba, São Paulo. Dogs of either sex, whether or not they had a defined breed, were grouped as young, adult or elderly, and the results underwent descriptive and inferential statistics to look for associations among the variables of interest. Anaemia was found in 50.5% of the dogs and thrombocytopenia in 84.2%. Occurrence of Babesia spp. was greater in dogs with a defined breed, adults and females. The authors concluded that red cell and platelet counts were reduced in the group studied. The abstract names B. canis and B. gibsoni as the main agents, spread by ixodid ticks, but does not say which species infected these dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Erythrocyte and platelet counts were decreased in the group of dogs studied.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives a descriptive haematological profile of infected dogs, with anaemia in about half and thrombocytopenia in most. These findings are consistent with red cell and platelet involvement in canine babesiosis, but with no control group in the abstract the results cannot show how far the changes are associated with infection rather than other conditions. The breed, age and sex statements describe who was positive in this sample and are not evidence of susceptibility.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; species not identified; diagnostic method described only as parasitological; no control group or reference intervals stated; anaemia and thrombocytopenia definitions not given; no co-infection data reported; single municipality; the denominator for the breed, age and sex comparison is not given.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
