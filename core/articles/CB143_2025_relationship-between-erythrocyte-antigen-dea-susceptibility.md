## Abstract-derived summary

This cross-sectional Nigerian study asked whether the DEA 1 blood group alters a dog's susceptibility to babesiosis. Blood from 200 client-owned dogs was typed with the Alvedia® test kit (DEA 1.1, 1.2, 1.0) and screened for Babesia DNA by PCR. DEA 1-positive dogs made up 63.5% of the sample versus 36.5% negative; most breeds (Boerboel, Rottweiler, Caucasian, local) were predominantly DEA 1 positive, whereas German Shepherds were evenly split, and no sex effect was seen. PCR positivity was 63.0% in DEA 1-positive and 60.3% in DEA 1-negative dogs, a non-significant difference (chi-square, p > 0.05). The authors suggest DEA 1 status may not influence tolerance of, or susceptibility to, Babesia infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (null result)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The DEA 1 blood group may not influence tolerance or susceptibility of dogs to Babesia infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Addresses a host erythrocyte-surface factor in susceptibility, a peripheral pathophysiological question (included as borderline versus epidemiology). A null association in PCR positivity says nothing about severity, haemolysis or outcome, and cannot exclude effects of other blood groups or species-specific effects.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species not identified; agarose-gel PCR only; all dogs were clinically suspected cases, so no healthy comparison and a high-prevalence referral population (62% positive); no severity or outcome data; sampling method and recruitment period not stated; breed confounding (DEA 1 frequency varies by breed); test statistics not reported; no power calculation.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
