## Abstract-derived summary

Eight dogs with clinical signs of babesiosis, including fever, anorexia, anaemia and lethargy, were enrolled at a university teaching hospital in Kerala, India, after piroplasms were seen on blood smears. PCR identified Babesia canis vogeli in every animal. Haematological analysis showed highly significant changes in granulocyte and monocyte counts, MCV, MCH and platelet counts, and significant changes in total red cell count. Two of the eight dogs satisfied the SIRS definition of Matijatko and colleagues (2010). The authors argue that this subspecies, regarded as mild elsewhere, can produce serious complications in this region. The study enrolled only eight dogs, so the findings are preliminary.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. c. vogeli, considered benign in some regions, can cause severe complications in a different geographical setting.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports the idea that B. vogeli can present with thrombocytopenia, anaemia and a SIRS phenotype, but the severe cases may reflect host factors (pregnancy, neonatal age, cerebral signs) rather than subspecies virulence. Haematology only; no coagulation, organ-function or parasitaemia data, so mechanism is not addressed. SIRS here is a phenotype label, not evidence of cause.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=8 selected for severe signs; no concurrent control group (one-sample t-tests against fixed reference values, no multiplicity adjustment); wide age range; recruitment period not stated; co-infections not stated as excluded; sampling time relative to treatment unclear; SIRS thresholds not stated; one death (SIRS-positive) after transfusion and abortion.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
