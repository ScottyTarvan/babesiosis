## Abstract-derived summary

Blood samples from 503 dogs in Ikwuano Local Government Area, Abia State, were examined for haemoparasites. Overall, 359 dogs (71.4%) were positive. Babesia was the most frequently reported genus, ahead of Anaplasma and trypanosomes, and most infected dogs carried Babesia together with Anaplasma. Positivity was higher in females than in males and differed between the six areas sampled, with Oboro highest and Oloko lowest. By month, December, March and November showed the highest positivity and August the lowest of those listed. Packed cell volume and haemoglobin were significantly lower in the infected groups. The abstract gives no species-level identification, no clinical data and no group sizes for the haematological comparison.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (prevalence survey with a PCV and haemoglobin comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Haemoparasites, including Babesia, were common in the sampled dogs. The authors read the lower PCV and haemoglobin of infected animals as anaemia associated with the infections, and recommend continued surveillance (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The article is mainly an occurrence survey, but Table 3 gives PCV and haemoglobin for a Babesia group separate from the mixed Babesia/Anaplasma group, so some Babesia-specific host data can be read (full text). Babesia-positive dogs had lower PCV and haemoglobin than non-infected dogs, and the mixed-infection group had the lowest values (full text). This is an association in a cross-sectional sample; it does not show that Babesia caused the anaemia, and the baseline and group definitions are unclear.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** smear-only detection (full text), with no species identification; unclear whether the Babesia group excludes Anaplasma co-infection (full text); group sizes and the number of non-infected dogs not reported (full text); one shared non-infected mean used for all comparisons (full text); no clinical signs, other haematology or outcome data (full text); no adjustment for sex, age or location (full text); internal numerical inconsistencies in sex counts (full text); no ethics or data availability statement seen (full text).
