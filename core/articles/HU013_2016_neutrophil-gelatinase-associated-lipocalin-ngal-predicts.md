## Abstract-derived summary
Pilot evaluation of whether markers of tubular damage can anticipate kidney injury in imported falciparum malaria. Thirty-nine patients from the Rotterdam Malaria Cohort with serum and urine collected at presentation were included, ten meeting severe malaria criteria. Six (15%) later developed AKI. Serum NGAL, urine NGAL and urine KIM-1 each showed large areas under the ROC curve, and urine NGAL performed best: AUROC 1.00 with positive predictive value 1.00 (95% CI 0.54-1.00) and negative predictive value 1.00 (95% CI 0.89-1.00). Comparison was made against serum creatinine, the conventional marker, which tends to rise only after injury is established. The authors call for larger studies.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** small human diagnostic-accuracy pilot in non-immune travellers.
- **Authors' conclusion:** NGAL and KIM-1 perform well for AKI, urine NGAL excellently; larger studies must show superiority over creatinine.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** rated directly demonstrated in both hosts for the same readouts, urinary NGAL and KIM-1 as tubular injury markers. Canine studies: CB024 (n=42 vs 14, raised urinary KIM-1), CB103 (n=22 vs 8, NGAL rose before azotaemia), CB124 (NGAL and KIM-1 raised in IRIS grade I), CB053 (B. rossi, markers raised while creatinine unchanged), CB001. Differences: the human data are malaria, not babesiosis; malaria sequestration has no exact Babesia equivalent; canine work compared with healthy controls rather than predicting later AKI; AKI definitions differ (KDIGO vs IRIS); assays are species-specific.
- **Key limitations:** 6 AKI events make the perfect AUROC likely optimistic; single-centre; non-endemic travellers.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
