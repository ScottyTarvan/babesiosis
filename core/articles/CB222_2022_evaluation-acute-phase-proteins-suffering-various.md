## Abstract-derived summary

Three acute phase proteins, SAA, Hp and CRP, were quantified in client-owned dogs with babesiosis (n=39), parvoviral enteritis (n=14), pyometra (n=21) or acute pancreatitis (n=14), and compared with 23 healthy dogs. All three markers differed significantly between groups (P<0.001), and average concentrations were higher in diseased dogs than in healthy controls. Parvoviral enteritis and pyometra produced the largest rises. Babesiosis gave the smallest response among the diseases tested, although its mean concentrations were still well above healthy values. The authors conclude that the disorders produced different inflammatory responses, with the magnitude of the response differing by disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Different canine diseases elicit distinguishable acute phase responses, with babesiosis causing a less marked response than parvoviral enteritis or pyometra, so single markers should not be used alone.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful for placing the babesiosis acute phase response in context: SAA and CRP are strongly induced (roughly 120-fold and 23-fold above controls from reported means) while Hp is only modestly raised, which the authors tentatively attribute to haemoglobin binding during intravascular haemolysis (not tested). It describes inflammation magnitude at presentation, not mechanism, severity or outcome, and does not show that babesiosis is "less inflammatory" in any causal sense. Babesiosis, an infection with haemolysis, is compared with unrelated aetiologies of differing stage and age.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time point; disease stage and severity unrecorded; babesiosis dogs not stratified by complications or outcome; species by smear morphology only and co-infections not excluded; groups differ markedly in age (parvoviral enteritis puppies of 2-6 months); small groups; healthy controls were not matched; pyometra n=21 in the abstract and tables but the clinical description cites 22 dogs; no ROC or prognostic analysis.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
