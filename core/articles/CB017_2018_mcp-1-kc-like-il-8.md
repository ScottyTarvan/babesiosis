## Abstract-derived summary

The authors quantified nine serum cytokines and chemokines (GM-CSF, KC-like, IL-2, IL-7, IL-8, IL-10, IL-15, IL-18, MCP-1) in 16 healthy dogs and 31 dogs with B. canis babesiosis, 18 of them complicated, all given Imizol. Sampling was at presentation and six days later; one dog died (3.2%). Day-1 MCP-1 was higher in complicated cases (p=0.00016), and KC-like differed between groups on days 1 and 7 (p about 0.03 to 0.04). In uncomplicated dogs only, IL-8 rose over time and correlated inversely with erythrocyte count and haematocrit (rs -0.58, -0.60). KC-like tracked IL-8 and band neutrophils. MCP-1 and IL-7 discriminated complications (AUC 0.906, 0.896); combinations reached AUC 0.983. They conclude the cytokine pattern differs from B. rossi and may help prognosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (with prognostic modelling)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. canis elicits a cytokine pattern distinct from B. rossi, and some inflammatory mediators may predict complications and suggest therapeutic targets.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a chemokine-associated (MCP-1, KC-like/IL-8) inflammatory signature linked to complicated disease and, for IL-8, to anaemia severity in uncomplicated cases. These are associations; "critical mediators" in the title is not demonstrated causally. Complicated cases were defined by a WHO malaria-style classification and were SIRS-positive (full text), so inflammatory markers partly track the outcome definition.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small single-centre retrospective cohort; recruitment period not stated; apparent (not externally validated) AUCs, especially for multi-marker combinations; samples drawn 6 to 8 h after imidocarb and under supportive therapy including anti-inflammatories (full text); multiple comparisons; overlap with earlier cohorts from the same group not established.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
