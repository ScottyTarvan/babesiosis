## Abstract-derived summary

This narrative review surveys tick-borne babesiosis and theileriosis in Canada, the United States and Mexico, covering pathogenesis, diagnosis and epidemiology across people, livestock, horses, cervids and dogs. The causal apicomplexans share a clinical picture of fever, haemolytic anaemia, jaundice and splenomegaly. Human disease is dominated by B. microti in the north-eastern and upper Midwestern US. Bovine babesiosis (B. bovis, B. bigemina) persists in Mexico and threatens reintroduction into the southern US. Sporadic equine and bovine theileriosis outbreaks have occurred, and T. haneyi is a newly described equine species. For dogs, at least four Babesia species occur in the region, with disease ranging from subclinical to acute severe anaemia. No quantitative data are given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors present babesiosis and theileriosis as established and emerging diseases of human and animal health in North America that warrant continued attention to pathogenesis, diagnosis and epidemiology.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Canine content is a minor section of a broad multi-host review. It supplies a species-by-species clinical and geographic overview (including B. conradae as more pathogenic than B. gibsoni, with intravascular and extravascular haemolysis) rather than a mechanistic synthesis. Useful as a North American epidemiological and syndromic framing source, not as evidence for specific pathophysiological pathways; immune-mediated and organ-dysfunction mechanisms in dogs are not developed.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** narrative, non-systematic methods; canine pathogenesis is brief and largely clinical-level; regional scope; much canine species-distribution data rests on a few surveys (some Mexican identifications unconfirmed).
