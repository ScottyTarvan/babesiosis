## Abstract-derived summary

Five hundred dogs with suspected tick-borne disease were evaluated, with age, breed, sex and clinical signs recorded by questionnaire and EDTA and serum samples collected. The authors related platelet counts, indices and smear morphology to molecularly diagnosed E. canis and B. gibsoni infection. Platelet counts fell markedly in both infections. In babesiosis, mean platelet volume and mean platelet mass were raised and plateletcrit lowered. Platelet distribution width rose in both diseases, while mean platelet component increased with severe thrombocytopenia in ehrlichiosis. Smears from ehrlichiosis showed more small, polychromic, non-activated platelets; B. gibsoni smears showed larger, hypochromic, activated platelets. Anisocytosis and polymorphism occurred in both. Numeric values and statistics are not given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Platelet indices and morphology differ between E. canis and B. gibsoni infection and can support diagnosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Larger, activated platelets with higher MPV in B. gibsoni are compatible with thrombopoietic response to peripheral platelet consumption, but the study cannot distinguish consumption, sequestration or immune destruction. Activation is judged morphologically, with no functional assay. It is a descriptive haematology comparison, not a mechanistic study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** diagnostic framing; unclear how the 500 suspected cases were classified (only 157 infected dogs plus 15 controls accounted for on the publisher page); co-infection status not clear; sampling period ambiguous (a "2021" date appears for controls); single time point; analyser and manual methods vary; no outcome data.
