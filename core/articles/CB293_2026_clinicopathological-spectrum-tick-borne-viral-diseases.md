## Abstract-derived summary

The author compared laboratory findings in dogs with ehrlichiosis, babesiosis or canine parvovirus enteritis seen at three teaching hospitals in Sao Paulo State, Brazil, between January and October 2024, with 30 clinically healthy dogs as a reference group. Of 123 dogs in total, 24 (19.5%) had babesiosis. Diagnosis combined point-of-care serology, smear review and PCR. Babesiosis produced the deepest anaemia (mean PCV 18.3 ± 5.1%), parvovirus the most marked leukopenia (mean WBC 3.1 ± 1.5 × 10³/µL), and 84.2% of ehrlichiosis cases had platelets below 100 × 10³/µL. Raised ALT and BUN and low albumin occurred in every disease group. The author concludes that the three diseases show distinguishable laboratory patterns that could support early diagnosis and targeted treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Each disease yields a recognisable haematological and biochemical pattern, with haemolytic anaemia dominating babesiosis, supporting pattern-based early diagnosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Informs the babesiosis clinicopathological phenotype only (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, mild hepatic and azotaemic changes) and shows no mechanism. Group comparisons are against other infections and healthy dogs, not against severity or outcome, so no biomarker or prognostic inference is possible.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single-author report with an unverified institutional affiliation; babesiosis species rests on one 18S PCR; the stated serological confirmation with SNAP 4Dx Plus is doubtful because that kit has no Babesia antigen; 6.5% co-infections excluded; "regenerative" anaemia asserted without reticulocyte data; cross-sectional, no treatment or outcome data; referral selection bias; internal inconsistencies (acceptance before receipt; a cited review attached to a statistics software statement); publisher AkiNik has been flagged in predatory-publisher discussions (not independently verified here), and an entomology journal is an unusual venue for canine clinical work.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
