## Abstract-derived summary

Brown and colleagues infected beagle puppies with Babesia gibsoni in a case-controlled longitudinal experiment that tracked clinical, blood, cytokine and acute phase protein responses over time. Infected puppies developed a short-lived fever and anaemia, neutropenia that came and went, and a brief but severe fall in platelet count, yet no obvious harm to their clinical condition was noted. C-reactive protein rose markedly in an acute phase response that began late after infection but appeared before parasites were detected in peripheral blood. Several cytokines also increased sharply, again after a delay and following both the first detectable parasitaemia and the acute phase response. The authors propose that the findings improve understanding of immune responses in babesial infection and of the contribution of systemic inflammation to canine babesiosis pathophysiology.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study furthers understanding of the immune response during babesial infection and of the role of systemic inflammation in the pathophysiology of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In an experimental B. gibsoni infection, a rise in C-reactive protein was reported before peripheral parasitaemia was detected, and cytokine increases followed parasitaemia and the acute phase response. The control group and longitudinal sampling allow the order of changes to be described. The thrombocytopenia and anaemia were not accompanied by an apparent adverse clinical effect in these puppies.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; group sizes not stated in the abstract; young experimentally infected beagles may not reflect natural infection in client-owned dogs; specific cytokines and time points not named in the abstract.
