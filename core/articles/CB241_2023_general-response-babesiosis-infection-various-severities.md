## Abstract-derived summary

The study set out to describe metabolic and functional changes in dogs with babesiosis of differing infection intensity, using blood smear microscopy, centrifugation and spectrophotometric biochemistry with statistical comparison. Reported haematology included low red cell counts, hypochromia, thrombocytopenia and reduced haematocrit and thrombocrit. Leukocytopenia with eosinopenia was noted at mild infection, and lymphocytopenia with raised monocyte and neutrophil counts elsewhere. Serum AST, ALT, ALP and GGT activities were elevated, which the authors interpret as toxic injury to heart, liver, skeletal muscle, brain and kidney from parasite products. Low total protein and albumin and azotaemia were read as increased catabolism, especially hepatic. Changes were greatest at high infection intensity. The authors propose these variables as markers of functional and metabolic disturbance across intensities.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (associational across infection-intensity strata)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The measured haematological and biochemical parameters can serve as markers of functional and metabolic change at different infection intensities.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a graded multisystem laboratory phenotype (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatocellular/cholestatic enzyme rise, azotaemia, hypoalbuminaemia). Intensity was apparently defined by parasitaemia; this is not shown to track clinical severity or outcome. Enzyme changes are non-specific, and attribution to myocardium, brain or "babesia waste products" is speculative without organ-specific markers or histology. This is a severity-associated phenotype, not mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n, controls and stratification criteria not reported in abstract; species not molecularly confirmed; co-infections not addressed; single time point; no outcome data; possible cohort overlap with the authors' earlier paper.
