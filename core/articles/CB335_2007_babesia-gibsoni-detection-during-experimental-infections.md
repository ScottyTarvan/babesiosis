## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs were experimentally infected with Babesia gibsoni to evaluate a combined atovaquone and azithromycin regimen and to establish how well nested PCR, indirect fluorescent antibody testing and microscopy detect infection at different stages. The drug combination lowered the number of parasites in the blood but did not clear the infection, and resistance seemed to emerge in one animal. Nested PCR performed best early and during the acute phase, serology was most dependable once infection became chronic, and microscopy was useful only during acute disease. The authors also report, for the first time, parasite detection in tissue samples taken from chronically infected animals, which they suggest may indicate sequestration of the organism in host tissues.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (experimental infection)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Atovaquone with azithromycin reduced parasitaemia without eliminating B. gibsoni, and resistance appeared in one dog; the detection methods performed differently by infection stage. Detection of the parasite in tissue samples during chronic infection suggests possible sequestration.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The main endpoints are treatment response and diagnostic performance, which are outside the review's scope on their own. The pathophysiology-relevant element is the tissue detection in chronic infection, which is a host-tissue distribution observation. It is compatible with sequestration but does not show it. The record was included on adjudication of this single observation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only, full text not read; number of dogs not reported; no host haematology, biochemistry or tissue pathology reported in the abstract; resistance reported in one dog only; tissue detection does not by itself show sequestration or tissue injury.
