## Abstract-derived summary

Acid-base abnormalities are known in severe Babesia rossi disease but had not been examined in B. canis infection. The authors measured acid-base variables, blood gases and electrolytes in 14 dogs with natural B. canis babesiosis and compared them with 10 healthy dogs. Half of the infected dogs had a mixed acid-base disorder and half a simple one; the commonest mixed pattern was metabolic acidosis combined with metabolic alkalosis. Acidosis was attributed to hyperlactataemia and hyperchloraemia, alkalosis to hypochloraemia and hypoalbuminaemia, and respiratory alkalosis to hypoxaemia. The authors conclude that diverse disorders occur and that a strong-ion-difference approach better resolves mixed disturbances.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Varied, often mixed acid-base disorders occur in canine B. canis babesiosis, and the strong-ion approach clarifies them.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Extends the B. rossi acid-base literature to B. canis, pointing to lactate (perfusion/hypoxia), chloride and albumin shifts, and respiratory alkalosis co-occurring with hypoxaemia as components; mechanisms are inferred from the strong-ion framework, not tested. Disease severity grading and outcome links are not reported in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n, species not molecularly confirmed in abstract, single sampling point, severity strata and treatment status unknown, co-infections not described.
