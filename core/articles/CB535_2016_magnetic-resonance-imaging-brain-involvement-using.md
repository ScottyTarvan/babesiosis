## Abstract-derived summary

This experimental imaging study tested a new manganese-based paramagnetic complex, Mn-DCTA (Cyclomang, a 0.5 M manganese(II) solution with a cyclohexane-diamine tetraacetic acid chelator), as a contrast agent for magnetic resonance imaging of the brain in dogs severely affected by Babesia canis. Lesions of the central nervous system were visualised in every case, with strong enhancement where the agent accumulated at the rim of each damaged zone. The amplification index was 1.19 ± 0.11 in the central parts of the foci and 1.47 ± 0.17 at their periphery. The pituitary gland (1.18 ± 0.05) and the vascular plexuses in the lateral ventricles (1.12 ± 0.09) also took up the agent. The authors saw no pathological or physiological reactions after the injection and concluded that it could serve as a contrast agent for imaging brain injury in dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (imaging)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Mn-DCTA allowed contrast visualisation of focal injuries to the central nervous system and could be regarded as a paramagnetic contrast agent for MRI of brain injuries in dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Contrast-enhanced MRI showed focal central nervous system lesions in dogs with severe B. canis infection, which is host-side imaging evidence of cerebral involvement in severe disease. The main aim was to evaluate the contrast agent, so the report says little about mechanism. Without controls or histology in the abstract, the findings do not show that the infection caused the lesions.
