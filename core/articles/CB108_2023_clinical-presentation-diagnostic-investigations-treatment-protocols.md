## Abstract-derived summary

The authors collated dogs given a final diagnosis of a tick-borne disease at seven UK referral hospitals between January 2005 and August 2019. Of 76 dogs, 25 had ehrlichiosis, 23 babesiosis, eight Lyme borreliosis and six anaplasmosis, and 14 carried co-infections with two or three agents. Most dogs, except those with anaplasmosis or Lyme borreliosis, had travelled to or from an endemic country, yet three ehrlichiosis cases and single B. canis and B. vulpes cases had not, indicating local transmission. Signs and laboratory changes were reported as varied and non-specific. Pathogen-directed therapy achieved clinical remission in 64 dogs (84%). The authors advise considering tick-borne disease in compatible cases even without travel and confirming infection with specific tests so that targeted treatment can start promptly.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Tick-borne diseases should be suspected in compatible dogs in non-endemic regions regardless of travel history, with specific testing to enable targeted treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A pooled epidemiological and clinical case series; the abstract gives no babesiosis-specific clinicopathological, severity or mortality data, so its contribution to pathophysiology is limited to confirming that babesiosis presents non-specifically. The 84% remission figure covers all tick-borne cases and is not babesiosis-specific. Value lies mainly in documenting autochthonous B. canis and B. vulpes cases in the UK.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** referral population (selection bias), pooled disease groups with co-infections, babesiosis subset small, species identification and outcome by pathogen not reported in abstract, no controls.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
