## Abstract-derived summary

The review compares how different Babesia species that parasitise dogs differ in disease severity, using published clinical descriptions as its evidence base. It frames red cell invasion as causing both intravascular and extravascular haemolysis, and notes that for some species an overly vigorous host inflammatory reaction adds further injury. Because vaccines are scarce and treatment can be difficult, the authors argue that understanding pathogenesis is a prerequisite for better therapy, and that the spread of ticks and greater dog movement raise the importance of the topic. Differences in virulence among species are presented as a way to probe the molecular basis of disease. The focus is the clinical picture as reported for each species.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. rossi appears the most pathogenic, followed by B. canis, then B. gibsoni, with B. vogeli least pathogenic; B. conradae and B. vulpes look significantly pathogenic but need larger case series.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful synthesis of species-level severity (mortality, anaemia, SIRS/MODS, organ pathology, coagulopathy, glucose/lactate and cortisol/T4 markers). The comparison is indirect, built from heterogeneous case series, so differences in species may be confounded by population, vector, co-infection and treatment. Basic descriptive pathology is identified as sparse outside B. rossi. Proposed comparative parasite genomics is an idea, not a result.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** narrative review with no described search or selection method; uneven evidence between species; mortality figures taken from non-comparable studies; authors' inference of the species ranking is qualitative.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
