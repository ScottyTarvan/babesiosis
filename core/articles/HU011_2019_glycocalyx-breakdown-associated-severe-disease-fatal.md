## Abstract-derived summary
Prospective study of Indonesian adults with severe or moderately severe falciparum malaria, plus healthy controls (129 in all). Urinary glycosaminoglycans, shed fragments of the endothelial glycocalyx, were measured at enrolment by dye binding and by mass spectrometry, alongside vascular nitric oxide bioavailability. Values rose stepwise from controls to moderate to severe disease (dye-assay means 0.22, 1.78 and 3.98; P<0.001). Among severe cases, the three patients who died had higher values than 39 survivors (P=0.03). Breakdown correlated with parasite biomass (r roughly 0.3 to 0.5) and inversely with nitric oxide bioavailability. Severity was defined by WHO criteria, and blood and urine were collected together at enrolment.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** prospective observational human biomarker study.
- **Authors' conclusion:** glycocalyx breakdown is associated with severe disease and death in adult falciparum malaria.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** no canine study measured syndecan-1 or urinary glycosaminoglycans; CB080, CB146, CB111 and CB087 show only non-specific oedema, congestion or endothelial desquamation (glycocalyx link rated speculative). Haemolysis and oxidative stress occur in dogs (CB099, CB006) but nitric oxide was not measured (speculative). Microvascular obstruction (CB041 sequestration hypothesis, CB145 microthrombi, CB010 VESA predictions) and Ang-2/Tie2 activation (CB004 markers) are rated mechanistically plausible. Key differences: PfEMP1-mediated sequestration in P. falciparum, adults in an endemic setting, no validated canine glycocalyx assay.
- **Key limitations:** only 3 deaths; single enrolment sampling gives no temporal sequence; association, not causation.
