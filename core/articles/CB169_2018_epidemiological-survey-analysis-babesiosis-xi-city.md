## Abstract-derived summary

The study characterised canine babesiosis at a university teaching hospital in Xi’an, China, covering seasonality, signalment and blood changes. The authors analysed 194 diagnosed cases from January 2013 to December 2015. Cases clustered in autumn, young (under 3 years) male dogs were the main group affected, and Teddy dogs were the most susceptible breed. Haematology showed lower red cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit, platelets and eosinophils, with higher mean corpuscular volume and white cell count. Biochemistry showed lower calcium and higher alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin, urea nitrogen, phosphorus, ALT and AST. The authors read these as severe regenerative anaemia with inflammation, some liver and kidney injury and possible coagulation dysfunction, and advise hepatic and renal protectants during treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Affected dogs had severe regenerative anaemia with an inflammatory response and impaired liver and kidney function, which may disturb coagulation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A large single-centre clinicopathological profile consistent with haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and leucocytosis in babesiosis. Raised bilirubin, AST and urea nitrogen are non-specific (haemolysis, hypoxia, prerenal azotaemia can all contribute); organ injury and coagulopathy are inferred, not demonstrated, since no coagulation tests or histology are described in the abstract. Macrocytosis is compatible with regeneration but reticulocyte data are not reported. Association only; no mechanism or prognostic value tested.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species and diagnostic method not stated; no control group or numeric values in abstract; co-infections not reported as excluded; referral-hospital bias; no outcome data; 15 days from receipt to acceptance suggests limited peer review, and the publisher (Science Publishing Group) has been listed as potentially predatory (background knowledge, not re-verified).
