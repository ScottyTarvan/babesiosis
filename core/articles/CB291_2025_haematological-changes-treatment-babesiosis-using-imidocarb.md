## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined haematological and renal changes in 112 dogs with laboratory-confirmed babesiosis treated at veterinary clinics in Kyiv, compared them with healthy controls, and contrasted outcomes after imidocarb dipropionate versus diminazene aceturate. Anaemia, thrombocytopenia and acute kidney injury were graded with standard schemes. Thrombocytopenia was almost universal (96.0%), leukopenia occurred in 54.4% and anaemia in 45.5%, with bicytopenia or pancytopenia in 75.0%. Creatinine was raised in 25 dogs (22.3%; mean 292.22 ± 201.49 µmol/L) and hyperazotaemia in 57 (50.9%). Overall mortality was 8.0%: 6.2% with imidocarb and 10.6% with diminazene. The authors conclude that babesiosis causes marked blood-cell changes complicated by acute renal failure, and that imidocarb appears more effective.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile; the treatment comparison is unadjusted)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that babesiosis produces severe haematological changes complicated by acute renal failure, and that lower mortality with imidocarb indicates higher therapeutic efficacy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Eligible as a natural-infection severity/phenotype series: near-universal thrombocytopenia, frequent bi- or pancytopenia, and azotaemia in about half of dogs with 8% mortality. It cannot separate haemolysis, marrow suppression and consumption as causes of the cytopenias. The 6.2% versus 10.6% mortality contrast is a crude small-number comparison and is not evidence of pathophysiology or of drug efficacy.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species and confirmation method not reported; control n, treatment allocation, group sizes and baseline severity not given in the abstract; no statistical test reported for mortality; 96.0% thrombocytopenia is not an integer fraction of 112, so denominators are unclear; regional journal, peer-review process not verified; full text not accessible.
