## Abstract-derived summary

A seven-year-old intact male Australian Shepherd was referred with two days of lethargy and loss of appetite. Haematology and biochemistry showed a marked non-regenerative anaemia, low platelet counts, prerenal and renal azotaemia, and raised bilirubin. Piroplasms seen inside red cells on a blood smear pointed to babesiosis, and PCR confirmed Babesia canis. Treatment with imidocarb and fluid therapy brought an initial improvement. By day 7, however, the dog was judged to have immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia, based on spherocytes, red cell agglutination and a positive direct Coombs test. Corticosteroids produced a rapid recovery. The authors describe this as a classic babesiosis presentation complicated by immune-mediated haemolysis, and they stress the diagnostic value of the blood smear.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The case shows classical babesiosis complicated by immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia and underlines the diagnostic importance of blood smear examination.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates the secondary immune-mediated haemolysis that can follow B. canis infection, here emerging after treatment began; it supports an immune component of anaemia beyond direct parasite-driven erythrocyte destruction. Temporal association only: whether IMHA was triggered by infection, by drug or was coincident cannot be resolved from one dog, and the initial non-regenerative picture may reflect early timing. No mechanistic or quantitative data.
