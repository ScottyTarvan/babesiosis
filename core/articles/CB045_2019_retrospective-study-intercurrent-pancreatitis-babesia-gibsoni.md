## Abstract-derived summary

Because pancreatitis is reported to be common in B. rossi babesiosis, the authors asked whether B. gibsoni infection is similarly associated with pancreatic injury. They retrospectively reviewed clinical signs, haematology and serum biochemistry, including canine pancreatic-specific lipase (cPL), from 20 infected dogs. Only two dogs had cPL above the 400 µg/l threshold used to suspect pancreatitis. cPL concentrations were unrelated to the degree of anaemia or to parasitaemia, but were positively correlated with band neutrophil count, platelet count and blood urea nitrogen. The authors concluded that pancreatitis appears less frequent in B. gibsoni than in B. rossi infection, while noting that the associations they found require further study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive case series)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Pancreatitis prevalence seems lower in B. gibsoni-infected dogs than in B. rossi-infected dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Informs the multi-organ (MET) picture of non-African babesiosis: pancreatic enzyme elevation is infrequent and uncoupled from haemolysis and parasite burden, so these data neither show nor exclude a direct parasite-mediated pancreatic mechanism. The correlations with bands, platelets and BUN are hypothesis-generating signals of systemic inflammation or hypoperfusion, not evidence of mechanism, and are driven by very few high-cPL dogs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=20; no controls; pancreatitis inferred from cPL alone (no imaging or histology); only 2 dogs above cut-off; treatment and comorbidities (e.g. hyperadrenocorticism, glucocorticoids) not reported; multiple correlations without evident adjustment; cross-species comparison with B. rossi is indirect.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
