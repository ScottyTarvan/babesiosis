## Abstract-derived summary

From October 2023 to September 2024, dogs with babesiosis presenting at clinics in two Andhra Pradesh districts were assessed for neurological involvement. Infection was confirmed on Giemsa-stained smears and by PCR giving a 670 bp B. gibsoni-specific product. Eleven of 136 babesiosis cases (8.08%) were classed as cerebral on the basis of nervous signs and parasites reported in erythrocytes within cerebrospinal fluid. Signs included ataxia, paddling, opisthotonus, seizures, head pressing, stupor, rigidity syndromes, nystagmus, blindness and paraplegia. Every dog received diminazene, doxycycline, enrofloxacin and metronidazole with supportive care. The study followed treatment response and post-therapy assessment in these dogs. Only two of the eleven recovered at post-treatment assessment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with neurological signs due to Babesia may respond poorly to treatment, and early detection might improve survival (full text; not stated in the packet abstract).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a phenotype and outcome series for B. gibsoni cerebral disease (full text: 9 of 11 dead by day 28, about 82%), with only mild anaemia (full text: Hb about 10 g/dl) despite severe neurological signs, which argues against anaemia alone explaining the syndrome. It does not establish mechanism, and the title's therapy framing is not testable here (no untreated or alternative-regimen arm).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=11, polypharmacy confounding, no confirmed exclusion of co-infections (only non-babesial neurological diseases excluded), no neuroimaging or necropsy, CSF findings stated in abstract but no CSF data in the results, internal inconsistencies (text directions contradict tables; platelet units implausible), a master's thesis output; peer-review rigour not independently established.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
