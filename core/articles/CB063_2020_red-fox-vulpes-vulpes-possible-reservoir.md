## Abstract-derived summary

Red foxes are known carriers of several vector-borne haemoprotozoa, but whether infection harms them is unclear. The authors screened blood and spleen DNA from 383 necropsied foxes and 25 live-trapped cubs in four Polish regions for Babesia (18S rRNA, cox1) and Hepatozoon (18S rRNA) by PCR, and related prevalence to age, sex, region and local questing Dermacentor reticulatus abundance. Among adults, H. canis was found in 174/381 (45.7%) and B. vulpes in 117/381 (30.7%), while B. canis DNA was detected in 9 individuals (2.4%). B. vulpes infection was associated with significantly heavier spleens and kidneys, particularly in foxes co-infected with H. canis. Fox B. canis prevalence tracked that in questing ticks, which the authors take to mean foxes may help seed new B. canis foci.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Splenic and renal enlargement identified B. vulpes-infected foxes, especially those co-infected with H. canis, and foxes may contribute to establishing B. canis foci in newly invaded tick areas.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Wild-canid data on organ-level host response (splenomegaly as reticuloendothelial/haematopoietic reaction; renal enlargement of unspecified mechanism) to a small piroplasm. It does not show causation, mechanism or clinical disease, and informs domestic dog pathophysiology only by analogy. Primarily a reservoir/ecology study; included for its pathogenic-output component.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Cross-sectional, so infection cannot be linked to onset or outcome; necropsy foxes may be a biased sample (cause of death not reported in abstract); H. canis co-infection confounds organ indices; no histology reported in abstract; B. canis cases are few (n=9).
