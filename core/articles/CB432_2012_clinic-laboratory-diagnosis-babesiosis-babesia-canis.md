## Abstract-derived summary

Blood samples from 358 dogs suspected of babesiosis in Central Europe were examined, and 113 were confirmed as Babesia canis positive by a stained blood smear read with the concentration line technique. All 113 confirmed dogs were thrombocytopenic. Red cell count, packed cell volume and haemoglobin fell below reference intervals in 62.8%, 61.1% and 46.0% of them, and five dogs had a raised reticulocyte count. Low white cell, lymphocyte and neutrophil counts occurred in 54.9%, 47.8% and 30.4% of dogs, while 6.5% had monocytosis. A classification and regression tree built on leucocyte, thrombocyte and reticulocyte counts reached 93.5% accuracy for identifying babesiosis. Cases clustered in two seasons, which fitted climatic conditions and tick activity at the probable time of infection. The authors conclude that a tentative in-clinic diagnosis can rest on typical haematological changes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** A tentative diagnosis of canine babesiosis can be made from typical haematological changes, and the case timing matched the seasonality of the tick vector, so babesiosis should rank higher among differentials.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives a haematological profile of 113 smear-confirmed dogs, with universal thrombocytopenia, frequent anaemia and leukopenia, and a raised reticulocyte count in five dogs. These descriptive findings fit the erythrocyte and platelet categories. The stated diagnostic aim rests on the CART model, which has no external validation reported. The seasonal pattern is an association with climate and sample-submission month, not a measured vector exposure.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; species identified by smear without molecular typing; the suspected dogs not confirmed by smear are not described as controls; denominators for the leukocyte percentages are not given in the abstract; the CART model has no reported external validation; the setting is not stated in the abstract (Central Europe comes from the title).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
