## Abstract-derived summary

This German prospective study followed 23 dogs with acute B. canis infection, confirmed by PCR with sequencing, from diagnosis and first imidocarb injection (T0) to a second-injection visit (T1) and a later control (T2). Blood counts, chemistry, coagulation profiles, Coombs testing and Babesia antibody levels were recorded. Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia and acute-phase responses were the commonest abnormalities. Imidocarb dosing ranged widely (1.7 to 7.0 mg/kg, median 4.2). Every dog was PCR-negative at T1 (median day 15) and T2 (median day 32). Laboratory values improved by T1 with no further gain by T2. Dogs seronegative at T0 seroconverted by T1, and titres mostly declined by T2. All had an uncomplicated course. The authors advise PCR re-testing about two weeks after the first injection and omitting the second dose if negative and improving, to preserve antibody levels.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute B. canis infection in these dogs resolved quickly with an uncomplicated course, and a second imidocarb dose may be unnecessary when PCR is negative and laboratory values have improved.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a clinicopathological profile of uncomplicated B. canis babesiosis (universal thrombocytopenia, frequent anaemia, acute-phase response, mild coagulation changes, Coombs positivity in a minority). It informs the mild end of the severity spectrum and humoral kinetics, not mechanism. Coombs positivity suggests an immune component but, without erythrocyte morphology, does not establish immune-mediated haemolysis; the protective role of antibody is the authors' premise, not tested here.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=23, no control group; outcome confounded by imidocarb treatment and variable dosing; clinical follow-up data available for only 5 dogs at T1 and 6 at T2; no complicated cases to contrast; strains not subtyped; co-infection exclusion described as "as best as possible".

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
