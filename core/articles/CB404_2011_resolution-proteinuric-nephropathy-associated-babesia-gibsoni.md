## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a 4-year-old castrated male Labrador retriever with a brief history of poor appetite, lethargy, small-bowel diarrhoea, increased urination and increased thirst. Laboratory findings pointed to a protein-losing kidney disease with azotaemia of renal origin. After broad infectious disease screening, whole-blood PCR identified Babesia gibsoni. Renal histopathology findings fitted membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis with immune complex deposition. The dog received azithromycin and atovaquone, plus a single dose of a corticosteroid and cyclophosphamide. Three months after treatment finished, the dog was clinically well and the laboratory abnormalities, including the Babesia PCR result, had normalised. The authors suggest that unusual presentations of B. gibsoni infection be considered when proteinuric kidney disease is found.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Atypical presentations of B. gibsoni infection should be considered in dogs with proteinuric nephropathy.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case associates B. gibsoni infection with an immune-complex glomerulonephritis phenotype on histopathology, a mechanistic observation relevant to renal injury in canine babesiosis. Resolution of proteinuria and of the PCR result after antiparasitic and immunosuppressive treatment is consistent with an association between infection and the nephropathy, but a single case cannot show that the infection caused it, and the contribution of the immunosuppressive dose cannot be separated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; no control or comparison dogs; combined antiparasitic and immunosuppressive therapy; follow-up of three months; other causes of immune-complex glomerulonephritis not fully excluded from abstract-level information; full text not read.
