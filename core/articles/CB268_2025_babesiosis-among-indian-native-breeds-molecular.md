## Abstract-derived summary

A kennel in India housing 51 dogs of 19 indigenous breeds from several states was investigated after puppies died suddenly with haemorrhagic enteritis. Virology, bacteriology, clinical pathology and parasitology were undertaken. Dogs were heavily tick-infested, with melena and pale mucous membranes. Enteric viruses and significant bacteria were not detected, and PCR for Trypanosoma evansi, Anaplasma platys and Ehrlichia canis was negative, whereas B. canis, B. vogeli and B. gibsoni were detected; ticks were Rhipicephalus sanguineus s.l. Blood work showed anaemia, leukocytosis and thrombocytopenia, with raised total protein, albumin and creatinine. The authors attribute the outbreak to the three Babesia species and suggest native breeds tolerated infection better than crossbreds, given good survival.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The outbreak was caused by B. canis, B. vogeli and B. gibsoni, and native breeds appear more tolerant than crossbred dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a natural multi-species outbreak profile (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia) and a possible host-resistance signal, not a mechanism. Raised albumin, total protein and creatinine may reflect dehydration or haemoconcentration rather than renal injury. Attribution of puppy deaths from haemorrhagic enteritis to Babesia is not established: melena and enteritis have other causes (e.g. hookworm, parvovirus-negative enteropathies, thrombocytopenic bleeding) that the abstract does not exclude.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no comparator crossbred group reported, so "better tolerance" is inference; per-species and co-infection counts not reported; heavy tick burden and kennel conditions confound; survival not quantified and treatment not described in abstract; breed effect cannot be separated from age, management or treatment; peer-review practice and DOI registration not verified.
