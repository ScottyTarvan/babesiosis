## Abstract-derived summary

Four dogs were experimentally infected with Babesia gibsoni to test a combination of clindamycin, metronidazole and doxycycline, and to judge whether platelet count and plasma C-reactive protein (CRP) could indicate how well treatment was working. After the drug combination, B. gibsoni was no longer detected in the peripheral blood of three of the four dogs, whereas the fourth showed a marked uncontrolled relapse after a temporary recovery. During recovery, CRP concentrations fell as packed cell volume rose, an inverse relationship that led the authors to suggest CRP as an optional clinical marker of treatment response. Although framed as a treatment evaluation, the work also records host measures (packed cell volume, platelet count and CRP) in infected dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (within-dog correlations during recovery; n = 4)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The three-drug combination is proposed as a candidate treatment for B. gibsoni infection, and plasma CRP is proposed as an optional clinical marker for estimating the response to treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is mainly a small treatment evaluation, so its value for the pathophysiology question lies in the host time course it records: thrombocytopenia, falling packed cell volume and raised CRP in the same infected dogs, with CRP falling as packed cell volume recovered. These are within-dog associations during recovery and do not show a mechanism. The authors themselves did not recommend CRP or platelet recovery as prognostic estimates (full text).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only four dogs; no untreated or concurrent control group (full text); splenectomised dogs, so the host response may differ from that of intact dogs (reviewer inference; splenectomy from full text); earlier diminazene treatment in three dogs, so a residual or synergistic drug effect was not excluded (full text); relapse in one dog unexplained (full text); duration of medication set by each dog's clinical status (full text)

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Four clinically healthy dogs, PCR-negative for B. gibsoni with normal blood counts and chemistry before inoculation; dogs 1-3 were splenectomised before and dog 4 after inoculation (full text).
  - Inoculum was whole blood equivalent to 5.0 x 10^7 B. gibsoni particles given intravenously, taken from a dog referred to the Kagoshima University teaching hospital and diagnosed by haematology and PCR (full text).
  - Dogs 1-3 first received diminazene aceturate (3 mg/kg, three doses) once PCV fell below 15%; none cleared the parasite, and relapse was defined as PCV below 20% after diminazene (full text).
  - The combination was clindamycin 25 mg/kg, metronidazole 15 mg/kg and doxycycline 5 mg/kg, each orally twice daily; dog 4 received it without diminazene (full text).
  - Platelet nadirs in dogs 1-3 were 0.9, 0.6 and 0.3 x 10^4/uL, and PCV fell to 10% in each; CRP (reference below 1.0 mg/dL) peaked at 4.3 mg/dL in dog 1 and 2.75 mg/dL in dog 4 (full text).
  - Dog 3 initially responded, then relapsed from day 129 after inoculation (PCV 19%, platelets 1.0 x 10^4/uL, parasitaemia 6.8%), stayed PCR-positive and was euthanised at day 232 (full text).
  - Dog 4 had transient parasitaemia (smear positive by day 4, PCR by day 5) without clinical signs and was negative by day 21; anaemia, a CRP rise and recurrent parasitaemia followed splenectomy at day 126, and it had no signs of babesiosis by day 662 (full text).
  - In the recovery phase, a significant PLT-PCV relationship was found in two dogs but not the others, and a significant inverse CRP-PCV relationship in all four (Pearson correlation; coefficients shown only in figures) (full text).
  - Combination therapy took roughly 50 days to show clinical benefit, and no clindamycin, metronidazole or doxycycline adverse effects were seen (full text).
