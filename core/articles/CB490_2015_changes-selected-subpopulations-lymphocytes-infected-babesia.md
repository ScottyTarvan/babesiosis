## Abstract-derived summary

The authors followed lymphocyte subsets in the blood of dogs with Babesia canis infection and compared them with healthy dogs. Sixteen dogs were studied: six healthy controls and ten infected dogs, each of which received one dose of imidocarb at 5 mg/kg once the diagnosis was made. Flow cytometry was used to count several immune cell phenotypes over a 12-day period. Infected dogs had lower percentages of CD3+, CD4+, CD8+ and CD21+ lymphocytes than controls, and this deficit lasted through the whole observation period. After imidocarb treatment every measured subset rose in the infected dogs but remained below normal values. The authors suggest that clinical recovery may depend on the strength of cellular immunity, particularly the CD4+ T cell profile, and that clearing the parasitaemia may allow the lymphocyte populations to recover.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors propose that resolution of clinical signs may track how strongly cellular immunity is stimulated, particularly the CD4+ T cell profile. They also report that imidocarb cleared the parasitaemia, which they link to recovery of the lymphocyte populations.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Infected dogs had lower circulating T-cell (CD3+, CD4+, CD8+) and B-cell (CD21+) percentages than healthy controls, and the subsets rose after treatment without returning to control values. Because the design compares infected and healthy dogs and tracks them after a single treatment, the findings show association with infection and treatment, not a cause of recovery. The link between CD4+ cells and clinical resolution is a hypothesis from the authors rather than a tested result.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small groups (10 infected, 6 controls); no untreated infected group, so changes after imidocarb cannot be separated from the natural course of disease; percentages reported rather than absolute counts (as far as the abstract shows); diagnostic method, disease severity, breed and setting not stated in the abstract; full text not read.
