## Abstract-derived summary

Two male dogs in Serbia, an 11-year-old Shih Tzu and a 2-year-old Pit Bull Terrier, both with a history of being bitten by another dog, presented with babesiosis signs. Both had regenerative anaemia, thrombocytopenia and monocytosis, and the Shih Tzu also had immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA). PCR confirmed B. gibsoni as the sole infection. Treatment was single-dose imidocarb plus a metronidazole, clindamycin and doxycycline combination (MCD); the Shih Tzu additionally received three weeks of prednisolone. The Pit Bull recovered, though low platelets persisted for nine months. The Shih Tzu deteriorated, and recovered only after prednisolone was stopped and azithromycin with atovaquone was given. Follow-up with PCR showed fewer relapses in the Pit Bull. The authors conclude B. gibsoni is now clinically relevant in Serbia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni has become a clinically significant pathogen in Serbia, and MCD appears useful for acute infection, though parasite elimination remains unproven.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Two contrasting phenotypes of the same species: IMHA with pancytopenia and relapse (Shih Tzu) versus regenerative anaemia with prolonged thrombocytopenia and macroplatelets (Pit Bull). This is syndrome description, not mechanism. Worsening during prednisolone is temporally associated with renewed parasitaemia, but cannot show that immunosuppression caused it.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=2; no controls; old age, prior E. canis exposure and doxycycline pre-treatment confound the Shih Tzu; B. gibsoni resistance genotype not tested; treatment confounding; Shih Tzu not tested for haemoplasmas.
