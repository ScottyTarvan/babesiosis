## Abstract-derived summary

The authors hypothesised that red cell redox balance in dogs with acute B. canis infection stays disturbed even after imidocarb treatment and apparent clinical cure. Eight owned dogs were sampled at presentation and 15 days after treatment. Blood count, serum acute phase proteins (ceruloplasmin, haptoglobin, paraoxonase-1), erythrocyte antioxidant enzymes (catalase, glutathione peroxidase) and oxidative markers (erythrocyte malondialdehyde, serum thiols) were measured. At presentation dogs were anaemic with severe thrombocytopenia, low-to-normal leukocytes, raised ceruloplasmin, haptoglobin within reference limits, reduced paraoxonase-1 and weakened erythrocyte antioxidant defence. After treatment, counts and acute phase proteins largely normalised and antioxidant enzyme activity rose, but malondialdehyde stayed high, suggesting persistent erythrocyte oxidative damage.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (repeated measures, pre/post treatment)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Elevated malondialdehyde after successful treatment indicates lingering oxidative damage to erythrocytes despite clinical recovery.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Links acute B. canis infection to a positive acute phase signature (ceruloplasmin up, paraoxonase-1 down as a negative acute phase protein) and depleted erythrocyte antioxidant defence, compatible with, but not proof of, oxidative injury accompanying haemolysis. Persistent MDA is hypothesis-generating; it does not show causal contribution to anaemia or any clinical sequel, and MDA in erythrocytes may reflect red cell population turnover or treatment effects.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=8; no healthy control group mentioned (reference intervals used); single follow-up at 15 days; species not molecularly confirmed in abstract; co-infections not stated; imidocarb itself a possible confounder; thiol result not reported in abstract.
