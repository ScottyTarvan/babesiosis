## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether circulating proMMP-2 and proMMP-9, gelatinases linked to inflammation, change between admission and recovery in dogs with acute B. canis infection, and whether the change differs with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS). Twenty-four dogs were sampled at presentation and two weeks later; haematology, biochemistry, acute-phase proteins, microscopic parasitaemia and gelatin zymography were applied. Cultured endothelial cells, short-term leukocyte cultures and platelet lysates showed that all three cell types release both proMMPs. Both were lower at presentation than at recovery (p < 0.001). At presentation proMMP-9 correlated negatively with parasitaemia (rho = -0.616) and positively with leukocyte (rho = 0.704) and neutrophil (rho = 0.741) counts. The rise in proMMP-2 was smaller in SIRS dogs (p = 0.038); proMMP-9 did not differ by SIRS status.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Low proMMP-2 and proMMP-9 at presentation reflect thrombocytopenia and leukopenia, and reduced proMMP-2 may be associated with SIRS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a view that gelatinase levels in serum partly track the circulating pool of platelets and leukocytes (cellular sources shown in vitro), so low values may mainly reflect cytopenias rather than indicate a role in pathology. It does not show that MMPs contribute to endothelial injury or microvascular leak in vivo, and gelatinolytic (pro)enzyme levels are not active-enzyme or substrate data. The SIRS association is a weak signal, and SIRS subgroup sizes are not reported.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n (24); no healthy control group mentioned in abstract; SIRS subgroup sizes not reported; treatment between time points is a confounder; B. canis identification method and co-infections not described in abstract; multiple correlations without stated adjustment.
