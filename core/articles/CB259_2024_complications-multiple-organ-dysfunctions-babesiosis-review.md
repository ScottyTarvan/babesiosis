## Abstract-derived summary

The review treats canine babesiosis as a significant tick-borne disease of domestic and wild canids with worldwide spread, caused by several Babesia species. It surveys the literature on geographical distribution, life cycle and transmission of the parasites that infect dogs. The authors say they assembled information from peer-reviewed sources to describe complications and multiple organ dysfunction, together with recent progress in understanding pathophysiology. Highlights state that shifting distribution makes the disease a growing global concern, that multiple organ failure and death can follow, that antiparasitic treatment may not reverse organ lesions, that evidence for treating small-form infections is thin, and that understanding organ pathogenesis could support better therapies. The abstract reports no data of its own.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that the pathogenesis of complications and multiple organ dysfunction needs further study to enable improved treatment and prevention.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A broad organ-by-organ synthesis, useful as a map of the severity literature (uncomplicated versus complicated disease; anaemia, immune-mediated haemolysis, coagulopathy, hepatic, renal, cerebral, pulmonary and haemodynamic complications). It adds no new data. Mechanistic statements such as hypoxia-driven injury and cytokine-mediated SIRS/MODS are drawn from cited primary studies and should be traced to those sources before use.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no stated search or appraisal method; findings from B. canis, B. rossi and B. gibsoni are merged, so species-specific pathophysiology is blurred; some conclusions rest on small or older studies and secondary citation; evidence quality is not graded.
