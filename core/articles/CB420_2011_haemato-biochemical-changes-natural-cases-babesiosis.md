## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe haematological and serum biochemical findings in four dogs naturally infected with Babesia that were presented to a veterinary clinical complex in Ludhiana, Punjab, between August 2008 and April 2009. Measurements covered haemoglobin, red cell indices, packed cell volume, leucocyte, platelet and reticulocyte counts, together with urea nitrogen, creatinine, total protein, albumin, bilirubin and the liver enzymes AST, ALT and ALP. Most dogs showed normocytic normochromic anaemia with thrombocytopenia. White cell counts, total and differential, showed no specific pattern. Biochemical values stayed within normal ranges, although they differed from dog to dog. The authors link the infection to anaemia and thrombocytopenia, with other clinical values unchanged. They also state that severe babesiosis anaemia, which can cause death within a short illness, has become more frequent in Punjab, which they relate to the introduction of exotic breeds.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors state that Babesia infection apparently caused anaemia and thrombocytopenia, while other clinical values did not change.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a very small series that reports anaemia and thrombocytopenia in naturally infected dogs. With no control group and no reported statistics in the abstract, the findings describe co-occurrence in infected dogs and cannot show that infection caused the changes. Biochemical values within normal ranges, varying between dogs, give limited information on organ involvement.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only four dogs; no control group reported; Babesia species not identified in the abstract; no severity or outcome data reported; individual values and statistics not shown in the abstract; abstract only, so methods are unverified.
