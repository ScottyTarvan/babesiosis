## Abstract-derived summary

Following detection of B. conradae and other blood-borne agents in two sighthound kennels with histories of fighting coyotes, the investigators characterised laboratory abnormalities, risk factors and co-infections in 55 Greyhounds and crosses. Dogs underwent haematology, biochemistry, several PCR assays, vector-borne ELISA and Bartonella and tularaemia serology or culture. Twenty-nine dogs were PCR-positive for B. conradae; 16 of these carried other agents, including haemoplasmas, E. canis and a Hepatozoon felis-like organism. Infection was associated with aggressive encounters with coyotes, lower haematocrit, leukocyte count, MCHC, platelets and albumin, and higher MCV, MPV and globulin. The authors suggest considering B. conradae in dogs with cytopenias and altered proteins, and that fighting may transmit it.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathological profile)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. conradae should be suspected in dogs with anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypoalbuminaemia and hyperglobulinaemia, and interaction with other canids may transmit it.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Characterises an anaemia with raised MCV and lowered MCHC (regeneration not assessed in the abstract) with thrombocytopenia and macroplatelets plus hypoalbuminaemia/hyperglobulinaemia, but with a high co-infection burden the effects cannot be attributed to B. conradae alone. Provides syndrome description, not mechanism; no direct evidence of haemolysis type or immune-mediated disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Coinfections (16 of 29 positives per abstract, 55%) and a single breed; unequal testing across dogs (biochemistry n=52); possible multiple-testing error; control selection by PCR status; unpublished screening PCR; kennel counts reported in text (20 and 25) do not sum to 55 as I read them, unresolved.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
