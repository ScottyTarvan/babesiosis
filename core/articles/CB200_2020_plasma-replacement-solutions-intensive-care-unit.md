## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined secondary haemodynamic and haemorheological disturbances in dogs with acute, naturally occurring babesiosis and compared two plasma-substituting infusions for the resulting shock. Affected dogs were described as being in moderate, subcompensated shock, with a contracted circulating volume of both cells and plasma, lowered haematocrit, increased spontaneous platelet and erythrocyte aggregation, hypotension and a shock index roughly twice normal. The estimated circulating-volume deficit was about 30%. Rheopolyglucin (colloid) or rheosorbilact (crystalloid) was given intravenously at 5 ml/kg daily for 3 days. The colloid corrected hypovolaemia and hypotension better, whereas the crystalloid was the stronger anti-aggregant; both restored all measured variables within 72 hours. The authors recommend colloid first, then crystalloid.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with an uncontrolled-comparison therapeutic component)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that acute canine babesiosis produces subcompensated shock and that sequential rheopolyglucin then rheosorbilact corrects it.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant as a haemodynamic and microcirculatory description: hypovolaemia, hypotension and increased cell aggregation fit a shock-like (distributive plus haemolytic) syndrome. The "blood loss" of about 30% reads as a circulating-volume deficit, not haemorrhage. The abstract does not show mechanism (haemolysis versus plasma redistribution versus endothelial leak), and treatment response does not prove the proposed pathophysiology.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Sample size, group allocation, controls and recruitment not reported in abstract; Babesia species and diagnostic confirmation not stated; measurement methods for aggregation and blood volume unknown; no outcome (survival) data; co-infections not addressed; agents are regionally marketed products with unclear international equivalents; journal peer-review policy not verified.
