## Abstract-derived summary

Few clinical descriptions of natural B. gibsoni infection exist, so the authors reviewed clinical and laboratory findings for PCR-confirmed dogs presented to a Taiwanese teaching hospital between January 2014 and December 2015. Of 60 dogs, 20 (33.3%) had a concurrent disease and 40 (66.7%) had B. gibsoni alone; anaemia severity did not differ between these groups. Anaemia was found in 49 dogs (81.7%), about half of them severe to very severe (PCV below 20%), and thrombocytopenia in 37 (61.7%). Biochemical changes included hyperglobulinaemia (28/53), hyperbilirubinaemia (10/28) and raised liver enzymes (7/48). A naturally atovaquone-resistant strain was detected in 2 of the 60 client-owned dogs and 5 of 33 PCR-positive stray dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The data give a useful clinical picture of B. gibsoni infection and show that naturally atovaquone-resistant strains already occur in Taiwan.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports the dominant syndrome of anaemia with thrombocytopenia, hyperglobulinaemia and hepatobiliary changes (hyperbilirubinaemia, raised liver enzymes) in natural infection; the abstract does not say whether the hyperglobulinaemia is polyclonal. The abstract gives no data on haemolysis mechanism (immune-mediated vs direct), regeneration, parasitaemia, outcome or severity predictors, so it supports phenotype description, not mechanism. The atovaquone-resistance finding is treatment-relevant rather than pathophysiological.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No uninfected control group; single hospital referral population (selection toward severe cases); denominators vary per analyte; concurrent diseases not specified in abstract; no outcome data; treatment history of stray versus client-owned dogs not described.
