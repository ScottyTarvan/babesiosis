## Abstract-derived summary

This study asked whether anaemia changes serum levels of three liver enzymes, ALT, AST and ALP, in dogs infected with Babesia canis. Samples from 230 infected dogs were split into an anaemic group (A) and a non-anaemic group (B). Activity of the three enzymes did not differ significantly between the two groups. The authors suggest that mild anaemia on its own does not alter these enzymes in canine babesiosis, while acknowledging that a substantial hepatic effect of more severe anaemia cannot be ruled out. The study therefore addresses whether hepatic enzyme changes in infected dogs can be attributed to anaemia, and it reports no group difference at the mild degree of anaemia studied.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Mild anaemia as the only factor has no influence on ALT, AST or ALP activity in canine babesiosis; a major effect of more severe anaemia on the liver cannot be excluded.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Within the 230 infected dogs, enzyme activities did not differ between anaemic and non-anaemic dogs. This speaks against anaemia alone, at the mild degree seen here, being linked to higher serum ALT, AST or ALP. The comparison does not show whether the infection itself is associated with hepatic enzyme changes, because no uninfected control group is described in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no uninfected controls described; anaemia definition and severity grading not stated in the abstract; Babesia species method not stated; authors note that more severe anaemia was not excluded as a factor.
