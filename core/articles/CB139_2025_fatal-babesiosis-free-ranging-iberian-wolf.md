## Abstract-derived summary

A wild wolf carcass recovered in Cantabria, north-west Spain, in 2022 underwent full necropsy, histology and molecular testing. The authors attributed death to a systemic haemolytic disease due to Babesia canis, with concurrent Hepatozoon canis and Leishmania infantum. They then screened a panel of vector-borne agents in Cantabrian wolves (n = 91). B. canis was found in 6.3% (5/79), H. canis in 96.3% (78/81) and L. infantum in 23.8% (21/88); no wolf carried Ehrlichia, Anaplasma or Rickettsia. Co-infection frequencies were also analysed. The authors present it as the first fatal babesiosis case in a wild wolf and as initial records of B. canis and H. canis in Iberian wolves, with low B. canis but very high H. canis prevalence.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (case report with pathology)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The wolf died of systemic haemolytic disease caused by B. canis, with H. canis and L. infantum co-infection, and the wolf population carries these pathogens at the reported prevalences.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A rare post-mortem description of B. canis disease in a wild canid. Reported lesions (icterus, hepatosplenomegaly, splenic erythrophagocytosis and haemosiderosis, cholestasis, dark urine, diffuse interstitial pneumonia with oedema) fit haemolysis with pulmonary involvement, as in severe canine babesiosis. It cannot separate Babesia effects from co-infection or cachexia. Relevant as a syndrome observation, not a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; no antemortem haematology or biochemistry; intraerythrocytic parasites not clearly identifiable on histology (attribution rests on spleen qPCR/sequencing); cachexia (BCS 1/5) and two co-infections confound; host is a wolf, so extrapolation to dogs is uncertain; survey from banked tissue, sample sizes vary by pathogen.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
