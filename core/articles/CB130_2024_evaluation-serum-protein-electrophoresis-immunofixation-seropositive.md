## Abstract-derived summary

Vector-borne infections of dogs cause nonspecific dysproteinaemia and have occasionally been linked to monoclonal gammopathy, which is normally attributed to immunoglobulin-secreting neoplasia. The authors ran serum protein electrophoresis on 143 serum samples from dogs positive for assorted vector-borne pathogens and applied immunofixation to those with abnormal globulin fractions (n=112). Three-quarters (84/112) showed polyclonal expansion, and only two dogs suggested an M-protein on combined interpretation. Principal component analysis separated groups: Ehrlichia canis/Anaplasma phagocytophilum and B. gibsoni/Candidatus Mycoplasma haematoparvum samples had relatively higher gamma-globulin, and the latter showed stronger IgA and IgM than IgG labelling, whereas Dirofilaria immitis clustered with beta-globulin and IgG4 prominence. The authors conclude that existing criteria rarely flagged M-protein and that patterns differ among pathogens.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (marginal relevance to Babesia pathophysiology)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Prior interpretive criteria suggested an M-protein in very few seropositive dogs, and SPE/immunofixation patterns differ between infectious agents.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Babesia contributes only a five-dog, single-timepoint (day 28) experimental subset that is confounded by Ca. M. haematoparvum co-infection, so the IgA/IgM-skewed polyclonal pattern cannot be attributed to B. gibsoni alone. Included as host humoral-response context (hypoalbuminaemia, gamma expansion), not as evidence on severity or mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=5 Babesia dogs; co-infected inoculum; one timepoint; no healthy control group in statistics (pooled reference serum only); exploratory PCA; abstract wording obscures that the group was experimental.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
