## Abstract-derived summary

Using 320 dogs with naturally acquired Babesia rossi infection seen at the Onderstepoort Veterinary Academic Hospital during 2006-2016, all confirmed as mono-infections by multiplex PCR, the authors sought to refine clinical classification and identify parameters linked to severity and death. Odds of dying were significantly raised with higher band neutrophil counts, collapse, cerebral signs, hypoglycaemia, hyperlactataemia, azotaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, hypercortisolaemia and low thyroxine. Joint component analysis grouped these variables with death, yet multivariate logistic regression could not isolate independent predictors. ROC analysis favoured low total thyroid hormone, high bilirubin, urea and cortisol as the best discriminators. The authors offer easily measured criteria to separate uncomplicated from complicated disease and suggest that more proximal pathogenic mechanisms remain unevaluated.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile) with associational/prognostic analysis
- **Authors' conclusion:** The disease is complex and multisystemic, and likely involves mechanisms more proximal in pathogenesis than those measured.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Large natural-infection series that anchors the multisystem phenotype of B. rossi babesiosis (neurological, metabolic, renal, hepatic, endocrine, haemodynamic correlates of death). The failure of multivariate models to find independent predictors suggests collinear markers of a common upstream insult (e.g. hypoperfusion or systemic inflammation) rather than separate mechanisms; the data show association with mortality, not causation or mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single referral hospital (selection towards severe cases); treatment confounding and data-collection design not described in abstract; no controls; multicollinearity among predictors; ROC cut-offs not externally validated; possible overlap with other Onderstepoort cohorts not established.
