## Abstract-derived summary

This report examines dogs with concurrent Babesia and Dirofilaria infection, proposing that oxidative stress, driven by Babesia canis parasitism, is the main pathogenetic component of a cytolytic syndrome in this mixed infection. Using haematology, serum biochemistry and ultrasonography, the authors report a direct association between oxidative stress and hepatoprivial (liver-insufficiency) syndrome. Affected dogs showed hypochromic anaemia, leukocytosis, raised total protein, hypoglycaemia, disturbed pigment metabolism and higher serum enzyme activities, interpreted as hepatocellular damage and impaired hepatic metabolism. Lipid peroxidation in hepatocytes was linked to enzyme leakage and to weaker antioxidant defence, with a relative fall in blood vitamin A. The authors conclude that co-infection amplifies oxidative injury and hepatic involvement. Group sizes and the oxidative markers measured are not given in the abstract.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia-Dirofilaria co-infection intensifies oxidative stress, which underlies the mixed-infection pathogenesis and increases hepatic involvement (hepatoprivial syndrome).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant to the hepatic and redox component of canine babesiosis, but the design cannot separate Babesia from Dirofilaria effects, and the abstract asserts rather than shows that oxidative stress is the leading cause; correlation between oxidative markers and liver indices does not establish causation. Hypoglycaemia and enzyme changes are syndrome-level observations.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No sample size or Babesia-only/Dirofilaria-only comparators reported; species and infection confirmation methods unreported; confounding by filarial burden and treatment possible; no effect sizes in abstract; publisher is a conference-proceedings series and peer-review depth was not verified.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
