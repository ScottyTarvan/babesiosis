## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective study asked whether dogs with natural B. rossi infection show thromboinflammation and whether it tracks severity. Ninety-seven infected dogs (RLB-PCR confirmed), sampled at presentation before treatment, were compared with 15 healthy controls; 12 infected dogs (12%) died. Thromboelastography, standard coagulation tests, factor activities, fibrinolysis markers, CRP and SAA were measured. Infected dogs had marked thrombocytopenia with platelet activation, preserved clot strength, high fibrinogen, delayed clot initiation and propagation, and prolonged PT and aPTT with low factor activities in non-survivors. Fibrinolysis was minimal, accompanied by raised α2-antiplasmin and fibrin microthrombi in several organs at necropsy. CRP and SAA were markedly increased. The authors conclude B. rossi causes thromboinflammation and speculate that targeting it could improve outcomes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (prognostic comparison, survivors vs non-survivors, with descriptive pathology)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. rossi infection induces thromboinflammation (thrombocytopenia, platelet activation, factor consumption, fibrinolytic shutdown, systemic inflammation) that may drive endothelial injury, microthrombosis and organ failure.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Strong integrated description of a hypercoagulable, hypofibrinolytic phenotype with concurrent acute-phase response. The abstract does not show that thromboinflammation causes death; endothelial injury was not measured, and TEG does not assess endothelium. Severity discrimination rests mainly on factor activities, not on CRP/SAA.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time point at presentation; 12 non-survivors; healthy controls differed in sex; varying disease stage and clinician-driven treatment; limited fibrinolysis assays; overlap with earlier cohorts from the same group not stated.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
