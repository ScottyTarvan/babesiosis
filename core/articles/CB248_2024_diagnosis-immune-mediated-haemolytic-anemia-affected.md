## Abstract-derived summary

This hospital-based Indian study asked how often immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) accompanies Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs. Among 136 dogs clinically suspected of infection, blood-smear microscopy together with PCR screening identified 64 infected animals. These were then assessed for an immune component of haemolysis using three approaches. The antiglobulin (Coombs) test was positive in 28 of 64 dogs (43.8%), a slide saline-agglutination test in 21 (32.8%), and spherocytes were recorded on smears in 18 (28.1%). The abstract reports counts only; it gives no information on anaemia severity, regenerative response, outcome, test overlap, or any uninfected or non-immune-mediated comparison group. Each figure is the number and share of the 64 infected dogs that were positive on one test or showed one finding.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The Coombs test detected IMHA more often than saline agglutination or smear spherocytosis in B. gibsoni-infected dogs (conclusion taken from the full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that a substantial minority to near-half of PCR/smear-positive dogs in one referral population carry markers of antibody- or complement-mediated red cell injury. Positivity is test-defined, so it does not separate primary IMHA from parasite-associated secondary immune haemolysis, and detection rates reflect test sensitivity rather than mechanism. Higher Coombs yield is not evidence of superior diagnostic accuracy without a reference standard.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control groups; no recruitment period; PCR target and co-infection testing (e.g. Ehrlichia) not reported; Coombs protocol used a single reagent with no stated reagent specificity or negative-control dogs; methods text says 18 dogs positive on all three tests were selected whereas results use 64, an internal inconsistency; no outcome data; journal is a biochemistry title with a short turnaround (about 10 weeks to acceptance) and peer-review rigour could not be independently verified.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
