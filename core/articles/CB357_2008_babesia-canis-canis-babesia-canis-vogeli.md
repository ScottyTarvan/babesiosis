## Abstract-derived summary

Italian dogs presenting with signs compatible with tick-borne disease were tested for Babesia by PCR-RFLP, and the clinicopathological findings of the infected animals were described. Of 164 blood samples, 45 were positive: 34 for Babesia canis canis and 11 for B. canis vogeli. The former was found mostly in northern Italy and the latter mostly in central and southern regions. Partial 18S rRNA sequencing of seven positive samples matched GenBank entries for the respective subspecies. Most B. c. canis dogs had thrombocytopenia, hyperfibrinogenaemia, non-regenerative normocytic normochromic anaemia, haemolysis and neutropenia, with haemoglobinuria in 13 of 19 dogs, raised C-reactive protein, and hypoalbuminaemia in 17 of 26 dogs. Dogs with B. c. vogeli showed no uniform pattern; they included young dogs with haemolytic anaemia and older, splenectomised or immunocompromised dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Both B. c. canis and B. c. vogeli occur in sick Italian dogs, and the two subspecies differ in their clinicopathological pattern.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study describes the laboratory profile of PCR-confirmed infections and contrasts the two subspecies. The B. c. canis profile (thrombocytopenia, non-regenerative anaemia, haemoglobinuria, raised C-reactive protein, hypoalbuminaemia) is consistent with intravascular haemolysis and an acute-phase response. The B. c. vogeli group is small and mixed, so its pattern is only weakly characterised.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level appraisal only; no uninfected control group reported in the abstract; dogs were selected because tick-borne disease was suspected; B. c. vogeli group of 11 dogs with heterogeneous predisposing conditions; per-parameter denominators differ (for example 13/19 and 17/26); descriptive design without outcome analysis.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
