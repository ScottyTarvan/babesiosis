## Abstract-derived summary

Serum biochemistry was examined in 55 naturally infected dogs presenting in Lviv between September 2023 and May 2025, described as acute uncomplicated or complicated babesiosis. Frequent findings were hyperbilirubinaemia (63.6%), raised AST (83.6%) and ALP (80.0%), higher ALT in 25.5%, urea above range in 47.3% and creatinine in 20.0%. Glucose was low in 9.1% and high in 30.9%. Electrolyte disturbances included hypokalaemia (40%) and hyperphosphataemia (43.6%). Total protein showed no significant change, though the albumin-globulin ratio was reduced in 40%. A high urea/creatinine ratio in 83.6% was read as prerenal azotaemia from hypovolaemia. Hyperphosphataemia is proposed as a possible early severity marker.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors attribute the biochemical pattern to hepatopathy, acute renal dysfunction, dehydration and starvation, and suggest hyperphosphataemia merits study as a severity indicator.
