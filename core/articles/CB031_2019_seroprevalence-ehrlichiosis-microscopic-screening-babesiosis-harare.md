## Abstract-derived summary

This cross-sectional study of dogs attending selected Harare clinics screened randomly chosen animals for Ehrlichia antibodies by ELISA and for Babesia by blood-smear microscopy. Ehrlichia seroprevalence was 75.2% (88/117; 95% CI 66.2-82.5) and microscopic babesiosis prevalence was 47.9% (56/117; 95% CI 38.6-57.3). Age, breed and sex showed no association with either condition. Of the babesiosis cases, 82.1% (46/56) were also seropositive for Ehrlichia. The commonest laboratory abnormalities were low albumin (53.8%), anaemia (53.0%) and low platelet counts (40.2%). According to the authors, thrombocytopenia and hypoalbuminaemia were more marked in dogs with babesiosis alone, whereas anaemia was greater in dogs with both exposures.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Ehrlichia exposure and microscopic babesiosis are both common in Harare, with anaemia, thrombocytopenia and hypoalbuminaemia the main laboratory findings and differing between single and combined exposure.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly an epidemiological survey; the clinicopathological data give a weak, descriptive picture of anaemia, thrombocytopenia and hypoalbuminaemia in a co-endemic setting. Cannot attribute any abnormality to Babesia versus Ehrlichia, since serology reflects exposure only. Contributes no mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No species identification or PCR; microscopy insensitive at low parasitaemia; serology cannot show active infection or exclude cross-reactivity; only 10 babesiosis-only dogs; no clinical or outcome data; reference ranges from a local laboratory.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
