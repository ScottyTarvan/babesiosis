## Abstract-derived summary

Babesia canis and Dirofilaria immitis overlap geographically, and the authors asked whether an unrecognised, asymptomatic heartworm infection makes acute canine babesiosis worse. They compared naturally infected dogs (13 with B. canis alone, 18 with D. immitis alone, 7 with both) against 15 healthy controls, using blood counts, biochemistry, agarose-gel protein electrophoresis and a serum amyloid A (SAA) ELISA. Most dogs with acute babesiosis, with or without heartworm, were classed as septic. Laboratory changes followed the babesial infection regardless of heartworm status. Co-infection modestly influenced anaemia and aggravated dehydration, raised gamma-globulins, and abolished the leukopenia seen with B. canis alone. SAA rose to a similar degree in both babesiosis groups and was unchanged in heartworm-only dogs versus controls. The authors conclude that subclinical heartworm does not alter the overall acute-phase response.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Asymptomatic D. immitis infection does not influence the overall acute-phase response to acute B. canis infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports an acute-phase/sepsis-like phenotype accompanying the babesial infection, with SAA and protein fractions as inflammation readouts. The modest co-infection effects (anaemia, gamma-globulins, preserved leukocyte count) are group-level shifts and do not establish a mechanism. This is a clinicopathological and acute-phase profile, not evidence of causal immunopathology.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 7 co-infected dogs, so low power to detect differences; no outcome data in the abstract; diagnostic method and Babesia species confirmation not stated in the abstract; sepsis criteria not given in the abstract; cross-sectional single time point; treatment and other comorbidity confounding not described in the abstract.
