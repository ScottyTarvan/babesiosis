## Abstract-derived summary

This Brazilian case report describes a dog with non-regenerative haemolytic anaemia attributed to babesiosis, and discusses how such a case can be diagnosed and treated. The dog did not improve with the initial antibabesial treatment, so a repeat evaluation was performed, and it identified secondary non-regenerative haemolytic anaemia. The animal was given prednisolone and mycophenolate mofetil, responded well, and was checked at regular intervals to track red cell recovery over time. The abstract gives no laboratory values, no species identification, and no details of the haemolysis work-up or the treatment doses. It also does not say which test produced the secondary diagnosis or how long follow-up lasted.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Haemolytic anaemia secondary to babesiosis can be reversible with immunosuppression added to antiparasitic care (full-text aim and discussion).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates the clinical concept of a post-infectious immune-mediated haemolytic component that persists after parasite control, a distinct syndrome from direct parasite-mediated haemolysis. The case does not demonstrate immune mediation: the autoagglutination test was negative, no Coombs test or reticulocyte count is reported, and "non-regenerative" is asserted without regeneration indices. No mechanism is established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; species identified by smear only and keyword/text inconsistent (B. gibsoni vs B. canis); prednisolone and doxycycline given before the second evaluation (treatment confounding); follow-up haematocrit around 35% is only mildly below reference; table values partly garbled in the PDF; peer-review process not described on the journal page.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
