## Abstract-derived summary
Authors reviewed 34 consecutive patients hospitalised across 13 years with severe Babesia infection; the mean delay from symptom onset to diagnosis was 15 days. Against uninfected febrile controls, cases more often reported malaise, joint and muscle pain and breathlessness, and more often had low platelet counts and abnormal liver tests (P<.05). Complications, among them respiratory failure, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), heart failure and kidney failure, arose in 41%. Complicated disease went with severe anaemia (haemoglobin below 10 g/dL, P=.01) and, less convincingly, parasitaemia above 10% (P=.08). Despite combination antimicrobials, parasitaemia lingered a mean 8.5 days (range 3-21). Seven patients had exchange transfusion, which lowered high parasitaemia; three died.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** descriptive human case series with a febrile control comparison; clinical counts only.
- **Authors' conclusion:** earlier recognition and treatment of babesiosis may improve outcomes.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** shared pathway is thrombocytopenia progressing to consumptive coagulopathy and DIC. Canine work measured mechanism: CB145 (B. rossi, 97 dogs vs 15 controls) showed platelet activation, fibrinolytic shutdown and fibrin microthrombi; CB011 (B. canis, n=25) found lower antithrombin and raised thrombin-antithrombin complexes; CB034 (B. rossi) used thromboelastography; CB079 and CB005 report DIC. Rated **mechanistically plausible**: both hosts show thrombocytopenia and DIC clinically, but human data lack coagulation measurements. Differences: Babesia species, hospitalised severe human patients (splenic status not reported in the abstract) versus mixed canine severity, and treatment.
- **Key limitations:** no DIC case count or coagulation tests in the abstract; control size not given; small single-region series; unadjusted comparisons; P=.08 finding is borderline; pre-2016.
