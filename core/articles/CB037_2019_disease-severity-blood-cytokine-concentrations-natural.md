## Abstract-derived summary

The authors set out to relate circulating cytokine levels to clinical severity in natural B. rossi infection. Serum from 17 control dogs and 55 PCR-confirmed infected dogs, the latter divided into 23 critically ill and 32 relatively well animals, was assayed for 11 cytokines alongside biochemical severity markers. IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, MCP-1 and TNF-α differed among groups; more severe disease generally coincided with a more pro-inflammatory profile, whereas IL-8 fell as severity rose. IL-6, MCP-1 and TNF-α were higher in the nine non-survivors than in the 46 survivors, again with the opposite pattern for IL-8. IL-6 correlated inversely with glucose, and several cytokines correlated positively with cortisol, parasite density and band neutrophils and inversely with thyroid hormone. The authors describe a cytokine storm resembling malaria and sepsis, with redundancy among mediators.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (with a prognostic comparison, survivors vs non-survivors)
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. rossi infection produces a pro-inflammatory cytokine storm that tracks with disease severity and adverse outcome, and the number of cytokines involved suggests redundancy once disease is established.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a severity-graded systemic inflammatory phenotype (INF) linked to metabolic and endocrine derangement (glucose, cortisol, thyroid hormone), which is consistent with a sepsis-like host response. The data are cross-sectional serum concentrations, so they show association with severity and death, not that any cytokine drives pathology. The reverse trend for IL-8 is notable and unexplained in the abstract. Not a mechanistic study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time point; small non-survivor group (n = 9); control selection and matching not described in abstract; severity group criteria not given in abstract; multiplicity across 11 cytokines and several correlations not addressed in abstract; treatment and sampling timing not reported in abstract; serum cytokine levels cannot be mapped to cellular source.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
