## Abstract-derived summary

Fifteen dogs of varied breed, age and sex with suspected B. gibsoni infection were enrolled at two university veterinary hospitals in Thrissur district, Kerala, between March and September 2018, and compared with eight apparently healthy uninfected dogs. Giemsa-stained smears confirmed organisms. Blood counts were obtained on an automated analyser and serum chemistry on a semi-automated analyser. Statistical testing found that infected dogs differed from controls in rectal temperature, haemoglobin, red cell count, packed cell volume, platelet count, albumin and alkaline phosphatase. Total protein, ALT, urea nitrogen, creatinine and MCV were not significantly different. The full-text conclusion holds that anaemia and thrombocytopenia with fever can be presumed to indicate B. gibsoni infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathological profile)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with fever, anaemia and thrombocytopenia may be presumed B. gibsoni-infected; TP, ALT, creatinine and BUN were judged unrelated to the disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The data support a phenotype of anaemia and thrombocytopenia with fever and raised ALP, without evidence of azotaemia in this small series. They do not establish mechanism: the authors' suggested immune-mediated destruction, splenic sequestration or DIC were not tested (no Coombs test, coagulation panel, reticulocytes or parasitaemia). A non-significant MCV change leaves regeneration unassessed. ALP could be confounded by age (puppies included). Albumin direction is not stated in the abstract and could not be established from the PDF tables.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; control selection and age matching undescribed; species by smear morphology only; co-infections (Ehrlichia, Anaplasma) not reported as excluded; wide age range; no outcome or treatment data; multiple comparisons uncorrected; table layout in the PDF partly misaligned; journal peer-review standing not verified (not MEDLINE-indexed; about 5 weeks from receipt to acceptance).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
