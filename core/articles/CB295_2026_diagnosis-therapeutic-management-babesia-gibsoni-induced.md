## Abstract-derived summary

Two dogs were referred to a teaching hospital in Akola, India, mainly because they could not walk. Both were febrile and had enlarged lymph nodes, congested mucous membranes, slow capillary refill, tachycardia, tachypnoea, paraplegia and light tick burdens. Bloodwork showed low platelets, neutrophilia, high BUN, creatinine and SGOT, and marginally low protein. Babesia gibsoni infection was diagnosed from blood smears, a rapid antibody test and PCR. Each dog received artesunate with azithromycin plus supportive care, including neurotropic vitamins, methylprednisolone, gastroprotectants and herbal liver and platelet products. Both improved, with clinical and laboratory values returning to normal and no parasites detectable by day 30. The authors conclude that accurate diagnosis followed by this combined regimen produced uneventful recovery.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Timely, targeted artesunate-azithromycin therapy with supportive care cleared B. gibsoni and led to full clinical, haematological, biochemical and neurological recovery.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents paraplegia as a rare neurological presentation of B. gibsoni infection in the same vein as earlier single-case reports, but gives no neurolocalisation, spinal imaging or CSF, so the mechanism (hypoxia, vascular compromise or immune-mediated injury, as the authors speculate) is not examined. Causal attribution rests on temporal association with recovery. The laboratory pattern (thrombocytopenia, BUN and creatinine rise, AST rise without ALT rise; the abstract calls creatinine elevated, while the full-text values of 1.2 and 1.3 mg/dL are given without a reference range) fits a systemic or prerenal picture, not a neural one.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=2; alternative causes of paraplegia (disc disease, trauma, tick paralysis, other tick-borne co-infections) not excluded; recovery cannot be separated from corticosteroid and supportive treatment; one dog had normal Hb and PCV, so anaemia is not part of the syndrome; text and tables disagree on whether Dog II was ICT-positive at presentation; editorial quality concerns (the "How to cite" box names an unrelated article; publisher AkiNik has been flagged in predatory-publisher discussions, not independently verified here).
