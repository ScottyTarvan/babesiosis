## Abstract-derived summary

This study describes eight dogs with Babesia infection confirmed by haematological, biochemical and multiplex PCR testing. Canine babesiosis is a haemoprotozoan infection that affects dogs, and the study's stated aim was to review molecular examination and haemato-biochemical changes in the disease. The most frequent presenting signs were reduced appetite, pale or yellowed mucous membranes, high temperature and dark urine. Blood results showed lower red cell count, haemoglobin, packed cell volume and platelets, alongside a higher white cell count. Serum enzymes, total bilirubin, urea nitrogen and creatinine were also raised. The authors offer an overview of the molecular and haemato-biochemical picture of canine babesiosis. The abstract reports no outcome data, controls or statistical comparisons.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors suggest that haemato-biochemical changes could help determine severity of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a multisystem phenotype (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatic enzyme and bilirubin elevation, azotaemia) compatible with haemolysis, hepatic hypoxia and renal injury, but it does not test any mechanism or show severity prediction. Authors attribute changes to immune-mediated haemolysis and hypoxia from the literature, not from their data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 8, no healthy control comparison, no inferential statistics, no outcome or severity grading; species not determined (only genus-level PCR); co-infection (E. canis was in the multiplex) not reported; recruitment period not stated; selection from 34 dogs unexplained.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
