## Abstract-derived summary

The study profiled circulating lymphocyte subsets in dogs with virulent B. rossi babesiosis and asked whether they track clinical severity. Forty-four naturally infected dogs, classed as complicated or uncomplicated using clinical findings and in-house laboratory tests, and five healthy controls were sampled before treatment and again at 24 h and 48-72 h. Flow cytometry used antibodies against CD3, CD4, CD8 and B cells. Complicated cases had lower proportions of CD3+ cells (versus controls P=0.014; versus uncomplicated P=0.007) and CD4+ cells (P=0.027; P=0.014). CD8+ proportions were lower in both infected groups than in controls (P<0.001; P=0.005). B-cell percentage was higher in complicated dogs at 48-72 h. The authors suggest functional immune suppression, possibly through apoptosis or redistribution of effector lymphocytes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Findings could reflect functional immune suppression from increased apoptosis, lymphocyte redistribution and/or other immune-modulating effects of B. rossi infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that T-cell subset proportions (CD3, CD4, CD8) are depressed in virulent B. rossi infection, and CD3 and CD4 proportions are lowest in complicated disease, linking lymphocyte phenotype to severity class. Percentages alone cannot distinguish apoptosis, sequestration or redistribution from absolute loss, and no functional immune assays are reported in the abstract; the immunosuppression mechanism is hypothesised, not demonstrated.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Only 5 controls; complicated/uncomplicated group sizes not in abstract; proportions rather than absolute counts (not stated); treatment given between time points; species confirmation method not stated; single-centre likely but not confirmed; full text not accessed.
