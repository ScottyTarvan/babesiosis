## Abstract-derived summary

Following reports linking B. canis to acquired male infertility, the authors examined B. gibsoni. Among 100 male dogs suspected of babesiosis, 37 were PCR-positive; co-pathogens were screened by PCR and serology. Semen from six infected American Staffordshire Terriers, sampled before treatment and six months later, was compared with eight healthy dogs. Babesia was detected in semen of one dog initially and two dogs after treatment. Sperm concentration was unchanged, but progressive motility, velocity and viability were markedly worse than in controls (for example progressive motility about 26.5% versus 80.5%; dead cells 53.5% versus 6.5%). The authors conclude that parasites in semen may impair sperm quality and cause lasting sterility.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia in semen may harm sperm quality and cause acquired sterility persisting six months after treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating evidence of reproductive-tract involvement and possible venereal transmission, not a demonstrated mechanism. Causality is unresolved: treatment drugs, concurrent seropositivity or infections, and breed or fighting-related factors are plausible alternative contributors.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=6 infected dogs, all one breed and age band versus medium-sized controls of unstated breed; no pre-infection baseline; two different drug regimens; seropositivity to several vector-borne pathogens and one mycoplasma co-infection; semen parasite DNA not sequenced (possible blood contamination); Kruskal-Wallis on very small groups. Authors acknowledge small n and lack of semen sequencing.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
