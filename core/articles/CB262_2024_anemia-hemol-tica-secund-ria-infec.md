## Abstract-derived summary

Immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia involves red-cell destruction by antibody, complement or the mononuclear phagocyte system, and may be primary or secondary to an underlying cause such as infection. Canine babesiosis, tick-borne and caused by B. canis or B. gibsoni, is presented as one such trigger, diagnosed from haematology, specific tests and signs such as lethargy, pallor and jaundice. The report describes a 5-year-old intact mixed-breed female weighing 2.8 kg diagnosed with babesiosis, whose course later turned into a secondary, non-regenerative immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia. Management comprised blood transfusions, doxycycline, imidocarb dipropionate and combined prednisolone-ciclosporin immunosuppression. The abstract gives no laboratory values, and the case is described as a single dog.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia infection triggered a secondary non-regenerative immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia in this dog.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Illustrates a severe anaemic phenotype with a suspected immune-mediated component after babesiosis, a recognised but mechanistically unproven pathway. The report does not establish immune mediation or the causal role of Babesia, because key confirmatory tests are absent (see limitations). It is hypothesis-generating at best.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; Babesia diagnosed by serology after a negative smear, species unknown, no PCR; concurrent Ehrlichia canis IgG seropositivity offers an alternative or additive cause of non-regenerative anaemia and thrombocytopenia; no Coombs test or autoagglutination reported and bone marrow examination declined, so IMHA remains presumptive and marrow suppression is not excluded; steroid, antiparasitic and transfusion treatments confound the response; lab tables are embedded as images; ethics and data availability not stated; journal peer-review process not documented on the article page.
