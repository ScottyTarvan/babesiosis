## Abstract-derived summary

Ten dogs with confirmed babesiosis treated at a Poltava clinic between March and September 2025 were compared with ten randomly chosen control animals. Infected dogs had about 49% fewer erythrocytes, 47% lower haemoglobin, a haematocrit of 14.2 ± 2.4% and platelets of 43.6 ± 11.4 G/L (all P < 0.001), plus a higher monocyte percentage (18.2 ± 2.6%). Serum total protein fell by 23.5% and globulin was 28.4 ± 3.9 g/L. Urea (+80.1%), creatinine (+52.8%), AST (+42.4%), total bilirubin (+93.2%) and alkaline phosphatase (+66.5%) rose; calcium was slightly raised (2.4 ± 0.1 mmol/L) and sodium lowered (130.7 ± 1.9 mmol/L). The author concludes that the disease is multi-organ, with anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hepatic and renal dysfunction and metabolic disturbance.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (associational case-control comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The author concludes that the blood profile shows systemic metabolic disturbance, intoxication syndrome and hepatic and renal dysfunction, confirming multi-organ lesions in babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A small clinicopathological profile consistent with severe anaemia, marked thrombocytopenia, azotaemia and hepatobiliary enzyme elevation. It supports a multisystem phenotype but not mechanism: "intoxication" is not measured, and the lowered globulin and sodium could reflect dilution, protein or gastrointestinal loss or hepatic dysfunction, which the abstract does not address.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 10 per group; species and diagnostic method not reported; control selection and matching (age, breed, health screening) unclear; timing relative to treatment, co-infections and hydration status not reported; multiple comparisons without stated correction; regional journal, peer-review process not verified; full text not accessible.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
