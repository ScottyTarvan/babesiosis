## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether the serum protein profile of dogs with B. canis could point to faster or more sensitive detection than blood smear. They compared sera from three healthy and three infected dogs (smear and PCR positive) by two-dimensional gel electrophoresis, then identified selected spots by nano-LC ion-trap mass spectrometry. Ten spots were more abundant in infected animals. They mapped to three canine proteins: serotransferrin, a serotransferrin X1 isoform and hemopexin. A network analysis indicated these proteins interact with one another. The authors conclude that higher serotransferrin and hemopexin accompany B. canis infection and could be explored as diagnostic or vaccine candidates.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (exploratory, hypothesis-generating)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Raised serotransferrin and hemopexin are related to B. canis infection and are candidate diagnostic or vaccine molecules.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hemopexin and transferrin are iron/haem-handling proteins, so the finding is compatible with several explanations, including haemolysis-related iron handling, but the study does not establish the direction or mechanism of the association, nor its specificity versus other inflammatory states. Syndrome-level or severity inference is not possible; clinical status beyond infection was not described.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 3 per group; fold changes small (about 1.2-2.2); the full text reports per-spot p-values of about 0.50-0.96, which do not meet its stated p < 0.05 threshold; no orthogonal validation (immunoblot/ELISA); albumin/IgG-depleted sera; co-infections not reported as excluded; clinicopathological data not given; no non-Babesia disease controls.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
