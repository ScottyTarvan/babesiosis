## Abstract-derived summary

Circulating phosphoproteins in dogs are poorly characterised. The authors profiled serum from two groups of ten animals, healthy dogs and dogs affected by Babesia canis babesiosis with no organ dysfunction, using gel-free LC-MS/MS on individual samples after phosphopeptide enrichment, plus tandem-mass-tag quantification of pooled samples. They found roughly 50-55 phosphoproteins and 89 phosphosites not previously reported in dogs, mostly serine (71.9%), then threonine (16.8%) and tyrosine (11.2%). Fetuin A was the most abundant phosphoprotein (50-70% of all phosphoproteins), followed by kininogen-1 (10-20%). Three phosphopeptides differed between groups: serum amyloid A phosphorylated at S101 was higher in infected dogs, whereas phospho-kininogen 1 (T326) and fibrinogen alpha (T20/S22) were lower. The authors propose that altered extracellular phosphorylation may matter in babesiosis and call for further work.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (host-response omics, descriptive)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Phosphoprotein alterations in B. canis babesiosis suggest an underexplored role for extracellular protein phosphorylation in health and disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Higher phospho-SAA fits the acute phase response; lower phospho-fibrinogen alpha and phospho-kininogen touch coagulation and kallikrein-kinin pathways, but whether the changes reflect altered phosphorylation stoichiometry or simply changed parent-protein abundance is not established. It is a hypothesis-generating biomarker/mechanism lead in mild disease, not evidence of causal involvement or of severity prediction.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=10 per group; single time point; TMT performed on pooled samples (described by authors as preliminary); no severity gradient or non-babesial inflammatory disease control; controls received prophylactic imidocarb (full text); infected dogs selected as SIRS-positive without organ dysfunction (full text); apparent inconsistencies in site numbering between abstract, text and figure legend noted on automated reading of full text (needs manual verification).
