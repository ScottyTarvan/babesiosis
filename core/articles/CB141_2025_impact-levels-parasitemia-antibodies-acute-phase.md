## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective German study examined how haematology and biochemistry relate to acute-phase response, pathogen load, antibody titre and travel history in dogs with acute B. canis infection. Dogs PCR-positive for B. canis and negative for A. phagocytophilum between 2018 and 2024 were graded with a haematological severity score (HES) summing grades of anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, pancytopenia and leukocytosis. Of 342 dogs, 59.2% of those with a known history (113/191) had never been abroad. Common findings were raised CRP (87.4%), thrombocytopenia (85.1%), anaemia (78.7%) and hyperbilirubinaemia (74.2%). Dogs without foreign stays had higher HES (median 6 vs 2), CRP (116.2 vs 40.0 mg/l) and parasitaemia, but lower antibody levels than imported dogs (all P < 0.001). Parasitaemia correlated with CRP (rho 0.444) and HES (0.348); antibody level correlated inversely with parasitaemia (-0.666) and HES (-0.652). The authors attribute milder disease in antibody-positive dogs to protective immunity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathology with correlation analysis)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with high antibody levels had milder clinicopathological changes and lower parasitaemia, which the authors explain by protective antibody activity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful quantitative link between parasite burden (ddPCR), acute-phase response (CRP) and a composite haematological severity score, supporting a positive association between parasite burden and severity. It does not demonstrate that antibodies cause milder disease; prior exposure, time since infection onset and strain differences are confounded with antibody status. Syndrome/severity description rather than mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective, variable data completeness per parameter (travel history known for 191/342); no genotyping; other co-infections (e.g. leishmaniosis, ehrlichiosis in imported dogs) not excluded; single sample per dog; outcome not analysed; HES is an unvalidated composite score.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
