## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined oxidative status in ten dogs with naturally acquired Babesia gibsoni infection, confirmed on Giemsa-stained blood smears, and compared them with six apparently healthy dogs presented for check-ups or vaccination. The infected dogs had a history of tick exposure, irregular fever and prolonged illness. Erythrocytic catalase and superoxide dismutase activities and erythrocyte lipid peroxide (malondialdehyde) content were higher in the infected group (P<0.01); for example, lipid peroxide was 6.01 versus 1.89 nmol MDA per mg haemoglobin. Blood iron, zinc and copper concentrations were lower in infected dogs, with iron at 89.87 versus 126.44 microg/g. The authors concluded that infection was accompanied by oxidative damage and suggested that low micronutrient levels may contribute to anaemia and oxidative stress.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study showed oxidative damage in dogs naturally infected with B. gibsoni. Low blood iron, zinc and copper seem to have an additional role in the development of anaemia and oxidative stress.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Infected dogs had higher erythrocytic antioxidant enzyme activity and lipid peroxide content than the control dogs, which is consistent with oxidative stress in the red cells during infection. The lower iron, zinc and copper concentrations come from one comparison, and the abstract reports no repeated sampling, so the design shows an association only and cannot say whether the micronutrient changes preceded or followed the oxidative changes. The proposed link to anaemia is the authors' suggestion; the abstract reports no haematological measures.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small groups (10 infected, 6 controls); controls of mixed age, sex and breed; species identified by blood smear only, without molecular confirmation; no severity grading or outcome data in the abstract; no anaemia indices reported in the abstract; no repeated sampling; setting and recruitment not stated in the abstract.
