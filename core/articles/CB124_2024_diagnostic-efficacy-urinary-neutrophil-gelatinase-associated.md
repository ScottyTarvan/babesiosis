## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (uNGAL) and kidney injury molecule-1 (uKIM-1) can detect acute kidney injury (AKI) early in dogs with leptospirosis or babesiosis, alongside conventional markers. AKI was defined as a serum creatinine rise above 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours and staged by IRIS grade. Serum creatinine, blood urea nitrogen and the two urinary markers were compared across AKI grades and against control dogs. Differences between grades were significant (P<0.01), and both urinary markers were already elevated in IRIS grade I AKI (P<0.001). The authors present the markers as promising early indicators of renal damage, especially for non-azotaemic AKI, and call for larger validation cohorts including other AKI causes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (biomarker comparison)
- **Authors' conclusion:** uNGAL and uKIM-1 are useful early indicators of renal damage, particularly in non-azotaemic AKI, pending validation in larger, more diverse cohorts.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The grade-related rise in urinary markers is consistent with early renal tubular injury in these dogs, but the abstract pools babesiosis with leptospirosis, so babesiosis-specific estimates (and mechanisms such as haemoglobinuria, hypoxia or inflammation) are not established. Retained as a biomarker/renal-injury study rather than a diagnostic-test-only paper because it addresses early renal injury.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes, species, country and diagnostic criteria for babesiosis not reported in abstract; AKI defined by creatinine, which is the same marker used as comparator (circularity); no sensitivity, specificity or AUC given in the abstract; two aetiologies combined.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
