## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether apolipoprotein A-1 (ApoA-1), the main HDL protein, behaves as a negative acute-phase protein in canine babesiosis. Eighty-four dogs formed four groups: 23 with acute PCR-positive Babesia canis, 26 Babesia-seropositive dogs, 17 with haemotropic Mycoplasma infection and 18 healthy dogs. Blood counts, protein fractions, lipids and C-reactive protein (CRP) were compared and correlated with ApoA-1. ApoA-1 did not differ significantly among groups, although the acute B. canis group had the lowest values. CRP and triglycerides were significantly higher in acute babesiosis, indicating an acute-phase response. ApoA-1 correlated moderately and negatively with cholesterol and positively with eosinophils. Seropositive and Mycoplasma-infected dogs showed no acute-phase response. The authors consider ApoA-1 a questionable negative acute-phase marker.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** An acute-phase response with raised CRP and triglycerides occurs in acute B. canis infection, but ApoA-1 is of doubtful value as a negative acute-phase protein.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports CRP and hypertriglyceridaemia as acute-phase/lipid-metabolism readouts of acute babesiosis; a negative ApoA-1 result narrows candidate HDL-related markers. The abstract wording that ApoA-1 was "decreased" in group I conflicts with the full-text medians (70.9 vs 73.8-75.2, P>0.05), so the effect is numerical only. Correlations with cholesterol and eosinophils are not mechanistic.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small groups; time since infection onset unknown; comorbidities not fully excluded; ELISA cross-reactivity possible; single time point; no severity or outcome analysis.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
