## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective necropsy study examined the heart in dogs that died of naturally acquired European babesiosis, a topic the authors describe as poorly characterised. Cases were retrieved from the pathology archive of the Zagreb veterinary faculty; infection was identified by intra-erythrocytic (not intralymphocytic) trophozoites in cytology or histology. Necropsy reports, gross photographs and heart sections were re-reviewed. Haemorrhage and pericardial effusion dominated gross findings, whereas histology most often showed intravascular monocytic leukostasis, haemorrhage, endomysial oedema, myocardial necrosis, congestion and myocarditis. Case numbers and frequencies are not given in the abstract. The authors conclude that cardiac lesions should be anticipated in complicated European babesiosis and that affected patients ought to be assessed for cardiac damage.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cardiac lesions are expected in complicated European babesiosis and warrant clinical evaluation for cardiac damage.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides organ-level pathology for the cardiac dimension of severe disease; monocytic leukostasis, haemorrhage, oedema and necrosis are compatible with microvascular/endothelial injury and hypoxic or inflammatory myocardial damage, but this is inference, not demonstrated mechanism. Fatal cases only, so lesion prevalence in survivors and clinical impact (arrhythmia, troponin, function) are not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** necropsy selection bias towards fatal disease; no controls; retrospective archive review; species not molecularly confirmed; co-morbidities, treatment and agonal changes possible confounders; sample size not available from abstract.
