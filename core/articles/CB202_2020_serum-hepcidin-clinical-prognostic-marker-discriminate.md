## Abstract-derived summary

The study asked whether serum hepcidin predicts survival in canine babesiosis. Infection was confirmed by semi-nested PCR, and oxidative stress, inflammation, systemic iron indices and hepcidin were measured in 18 infected dogs that survived, 14 that died, and 32 healthy controls. Among non-survivors, hepcidin correlated positively with C-reactive protein, serum iron, total and unsaturated iron-binding capacity and lipid peroxidation (TBARS), and negatively with catalase, zinc and low haemoglobin density. A hepcidin threshold of 32.32 ng/mL separated outcomes with 92.86% sensitivity, 100% specificity, AUC about 0.984 and Youden index 0.928. The authors propose hepcidin as an early prognostic marker that could flag dogs needing intensive care.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic (associational)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that hepcidin above 32.32 ng/mL predicts non-survival and could prompt intensive care.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Links an acute-phase, iron-regulating peptide to outcome alongside inflammation (CRP) and oxidative markers, consistent with inflammation-driven iron sequestration, but correlations in non-survivors do not establish that hepcidin mediates death. Mortality was strongly species-dependent in this cohort (full-text counts: 0% B. vogeli, 25% B. gibsoni, about 92% mixed), so hepcidin may partly be a proxy for species or mixed infection and disease severity.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small single-centre sample; cut-off derived and tested in the same cohort without external validation (AUC and perfect specificity probably optimistic); recruitment period, exclusion criteria and co-infection screening (e.g. Ehrlichia) not reported; no defined follow-up window; species not sequence-confirmed; inconsistent P values for TBARS between sections; treatment varied by species and clinician.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
