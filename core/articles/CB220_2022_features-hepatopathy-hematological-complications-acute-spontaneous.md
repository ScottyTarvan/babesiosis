## Abstract-derived summary

The authors set out to link liver injury with haematological disturbances in acute natural canine babesiosis, comparing 20 infected dogs with 20 healthy controls. Disease was described as progressing from an anaemic to an icteric form, with normochromic anaemia, leukocytosis and thrombocytopenia. Erythrocyte and platelet aggregation was increased, and circulating blood volume deficits of 24% (anaemic form) and 34% (icteric form) were used to define shock. Hypofibrinogenaemia, soluble fibrin-monomer complexes and fibrin degradation products including D-dimer indicated DIC, described as consumption coagulopathy in the anaemic form and hypocoagulation in the icteric form. Liver enzymes and bilirubin rose, more so with progression. The authors propose a vicious circle among DIC, shock and hepatopathy and recommend intensive care.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hepatopathy, shock and DIC reinforce each other in acute babesiosis, with haematological complications causing liver injury and vice versa.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful clinicopathological staging of severity (anaemic versus icteric) that integrates haemorheology, coagulation and hepatic markers, supporting microcirculatory and thrombotic involvement. The causal loop is a hypothesis; the infected-versus-healthy comparison described in the abstract cannot demonstrate it.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Species and diagnostic confirmation not stated in abstract; small n; how forms were assigned and group sizes per form unclear; method for blood-volume deficit not described; DIC staging criteria not validated in dogs in abstract; treatment and co-infection confounding not described; no outcome data in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
