## Abstract-derived summary
The authors devised an ex vivo test in which plasma drawn at ICU admission is incubated with isolated neutrophils and the resulting NET formation graded semiquantitatively. Among 341 consecutive adult ICU patients, NET formation was absent in 22.0%, mild in 49.9%, moderate in 14.4% and strong in 13.8%; strong responses clustered in sepsis (P<0.0001). After APACHE II adjustment, NET-forming capacity independently predicted DIC and 28-day death, whereas cell-free DNA, myeloperoxidase and MPO-DNA complexes did not. IL-8 concentration tracked closely with NET formation, IL-8 inhibition reduced NETosis, and MAPK activation emerged as a major route. The assay captures the plasma's capacity to induce NETs, not NETs circulating in the patient.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** primary human prospective cohort plus ex vivo and in-vitro mechanistic work, in a mixed ICU population.
- **Authors' conclusion:** the assay directly measures NET-forming capacity and could guide management and targeted treatment.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** shared pathway is NET-driven immunothrombosis and DIC. No COA canine study measured NETs, citrullinated histones or cell-free DNA; the nearest data are CB145 (B. rossi, 97 dogs; platelet activation, fibrin microthrombi, raised CRP/SAA) and CB004 (B. canis, n=30; raised HMGB-1, a damage signal that can induce NETs, but NETs unmeasured). Rated **speculative**: no babesiosis study in either host measures NETs, so the link rests on sepsis. Differences: sepsis is bacterial and IL-8 dependent, whereas babesiosis is intraerythrocytic with haemolysis; ex vivo capacity is not tissue NET burden.
- **Key limitations:** abstract gives no sepsis subgroup size; adjusted only for APACHE II; ex vivo assay; observational association.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
