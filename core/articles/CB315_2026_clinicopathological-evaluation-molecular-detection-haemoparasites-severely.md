## Abstract-derived summary

Thirteen dogs with packed cell volume below 15% that were referred for emergency blood transfusion were compared with 13 apparently healthy donor dogs. Investigations included blood and buffy coat smears, a Knott-type concentration test, a genus-level PCR panel with species-level identification, haematology, saline agglutination, osmotic fragility and cross-matching. Babesia gibsoni predominated, found in 12 recipients, with Anaplasma platys in one. Molecular detection was more sensitive than microscopy (100% versus 66.67%). Recipients were markedly anaemic and thrombocytopaenic, and the A. platys-infected dog had severe thrombocytopaenia. Saline agglutination was restricted to dogs co-infected with microfilariae and B. gibsoni. Microfilariae were seen more often on peripheral blood smears than on other venous-blood tests. Cross-matching found no incompatibilities.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni predominated in severely anaemic dogs, microscopy was less sensitive than molecular screening, and agglutination in microfilaraemic co-infected dogs warrants further investigation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Describes the haematological phenotype of transfusion-requiring anaemia in predominantly B. gibsoni infection. Reduced osmotic fragility with unchanged indices fits a reticulocyte-rich regenerative response but does not define a membrane mechanism, and no reticulocyte count is among the reported parameters. SAT positivity, confined to microfilaraemic B. gibsoni co-infected dogs, is hypothesis-generating only; no causal role for microfilariae or immune-mediated destruction is shown. No outcome data, so not a severity-biomarker study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=13 (12 B. gibsoni), enrolment restricted to severely anaemic dogs whose owners consented (13 of 40 anaemic dogs presented); controls are healthy donors, so effects mix anaemia severity with infection; mixed co-infections (A. platys, Ancylostoma, unidentified microfilariae); no sequencing confirmation described; smear sensitivity figure (stated 66.67%) is internally inconsistent with the full-text smear detection count (9/13); no ethics-committee statement found; 15 days from receipt to acceptance.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
