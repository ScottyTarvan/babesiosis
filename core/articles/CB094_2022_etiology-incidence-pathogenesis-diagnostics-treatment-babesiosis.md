## Abstract-derived summary

This review summarises current knowledge of Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs, covering etiology, occurrence, clinical signs, pathogenesis, diagnosis and treatment, with attention to clinical and laboratory peculiarities. The authors note that this small piroplasm usually causes inapparent infection, which makes carrier animals hard to identify. It is common in endemic regions, particularly Asia, where ticks transmit it directly. In Europe, where the vector is absent, cases appear only sporadically but are increasingly seen in predisposed fighting breeds, notably the American pit bull terrier. Because treatment lowers parasitaemia and improves signs without clearing the parasite, the authors regard therapy as a frequent challenge deserving further study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Persistent parasitaemia after treatment makes B. gibsoni infection difficult to manage, so the disease merits closer study.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful synthesis of the B. gibsoni phenotype (regenerative haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, chronic carrier state, visceral lesions) for comparison with B. canis/rossi. It is a secondary source: mechanisms such as thrombocytopenia (splenic sequestration versus immune destruction) are stated as incompletely established, and it adds no new data.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** review with no search or appraisal methods stated in the abstract; cited pathology studies are mostly small and unquantified in the text; species comparisons rest on heterogeneous sources; an apparent CRP unit error (mg/mL) in the source text needs checking against the original.
