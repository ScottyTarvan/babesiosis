## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective cohort asked whether basal cortisol, DHEA, DHEAS and their ratios independently forecast death in septic patients. Adrenal hormones were measured during the first day of sepsis; ROC curves and adjusted logistic regression addressed in-hospital, 28-day and 90-day mortality, with lactate, CRP, SOFA and APACHE II as comparators. Cortisol discriminated best, with AUCs of 0.758, 0.759 and 0.705, while for 28-day death the other markers scored 0.618 to 0.647. Cortisol above 17.5 ug/dL carried adjusted odds ratios of 10.13 (in-hospital) and 9.45 (28-day), falling to 4.26 at 90 days. Androgen associations were mostly non-significant, and ratios did not beat cortisol alone, except cortisol/DHEAS for 90-day death (AUC 0.737). Early cortisol was judged the strongest prognostic factor.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Prospective single-cohort prognostic study in ICU sepsis; no external validation reported in the abstract.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Basal cortisol in the first 24 h was the best prognostic factor for in-hospital and 28-day mortality, outperforming SOFA and APACHE II; cortisol/DHEAS independently predicted long-term mortality.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category MET; raised cortisol tracking severity is rated **directly demonstrated in both** (association only). CB040 (*B. rossi*, n=320) found high cortisol discriminated death best though no variable was independently predictive; CB105 (experimental *B. rossi*, n=5) showed cortisol rising with severity and inoculum dose; CB083 (*B. vogeli*) found higher cortisol than uninfected dogs. Differences: the human host is bacterial or viral sepsis, not a haemoparasitic infection; dog cortisol was not an independent predictor; single-timepoint canine sampling, prior supportive care or steroids, assay and binding-protein differences; stress response versus adrenal insufficiency was not tested in dogs.
- **Key limitations:** 139 patients, one cohort; AUC differences versus scores not tested formally in the abstract; wide intervals at 90 days; not a babesiosis or malaria population.
