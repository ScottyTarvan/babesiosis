## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined the activity of protein C, one of the three major anticoagulant pathways, in dogs with babesiosis. Measurements were made before and after therapy. The authors report lower protein C activity and discuss possible explanations: release of inflammatory cytokines, reduced synthesis in the liver, or increased erythropoietin production. Red blood cell counts and haematocrit were also decreased, which the authors attribute to possible mechanical, immune-mediated or toxin-mediated haemolysis. Activities of the liver enzymes aspartate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase, together with total bilirubin, were raised, which the authors read as hepatocellular injury. The abstract presents coagulation disturbance as one of several complications of canine babesiosis. It gives no number of dogs, no diagnostic method, no Babesia species and no numerical results.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Protein C activity was lower in dogs with babesiosis, and the authors propose cytokine release, reduced hepatic synthesis or increased erythropoietin production as possible explanations. Raised liver enzymes and bilirubin point to liver cell damage.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study links a lower protein C activity to canine babesiosis alongside anaemia and liver injury markers, which places it within the coagulation and multisystem picture of the disease. The mechanisms named for the protein C decrease and for the haemolysis are the authors' suggestions, not tested in this design. The before-and-after-therapy comparison cannot show which factor lowered protein C.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; number of dogs not reported; no healthy control group mentioned; Babesia species and diagnostic method not stated; no numerical results in the abstract; mechanisms are proposed, not tested.
