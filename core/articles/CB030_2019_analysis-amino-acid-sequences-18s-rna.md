## Abstract-derived summary

The authors analysed DNA from 60 dogs with babesiosis, split into three groups of 20 by 18S rRNA genotype (A, B or C), defined by the dinucleotide at positions 150-151 (GA, AG or TT). Sequences were translated to amino acids and compared using DNASTAR. Serum from the 60 infected dogs and 10 healthy controls was profiled by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. They report that the substitutions at these positions change the translated sequence, and that the clinical course differed between genotypes, although the abstract supplies no figures for this. All three genotypes were accompanied by a serum protein fraction of roughly 51 kDa, which the authors suggest is a nonspecific marker of infection that cannot separate genotypes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Genotype-defining mutations alter the translated sequence and disease course differs between genotypes, while a 51 kDa serum fraction marks infection irrespective of genotype.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Possible hint of parasite-genotype-linked virulence plus a host serum proteome signal, but the abstract gives no data on how "course" was measured. 18S rRNA is non-coding, so translating it to amino acids is biologically questionable. The 51 kDa peak is unidentified in the abstract, so it is a descriptive host-response signal, not a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Equal-sized groups suggest selection by genotype; unknown clinical metrics, confounders and co-infections; country and recruitment not reported; small control group; protein identity not established; single locus.
