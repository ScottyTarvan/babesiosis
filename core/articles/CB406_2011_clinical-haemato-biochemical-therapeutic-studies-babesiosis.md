## Abstract-derived summary

This case series describes 18 dogs with clinical babesiosis from a college veterinary clinic in the Kangra Valley, Himachal Pradesh, out of 1570 dogs presented (incidence 1.15 per cent). The title indicates that haematological and biochemical findings were studied alongside clinical and therapeutic observations, but the abstract reports none of those laboratory results. The most frequently recorded signs were loss of appetite, lethargy, fever that came back repeatedly, pale mucous membranes and vomiting. Treatment consisted of diminazine aceturate at 5 mg/kg by intramuscular injection, given twice 48 hours apart, together with supportive measures: fluids, haematinics, a liver tonic and multivitamins. The authors report that this regimen was effective in 83 per cent of the cases. The abstract does not name the Babesia species.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report that diminazine aceturate with supportive therapy was effective in 83% of cases; the most common signs were anorexia, lethargy, recurrent fever, pale mucous membranes and emesis.

The abstract supports only clinical signs and treatment response; pale mucous membranes are the only sign pointing to anaemia. Any haematological or biochemical findings that the title promises could not be read, so their contribution to pathophysiology is unconfirmed.
