## Abstract-derived summary

The authors used an optimised Babesia PCR to estimate how often Babesia DNA was found in dogs tested through a North American reference laboratory (9367 samples, June 2015 to June 2018) and to describe B. vulpes cases. Babesia DNA was amplified in 269 dogs (2.9%); B. gibsoni predominated, while B. vulpes occurred in 48 dogs (19 as the sole agent, 29 together with B. gibsoni). Most B. vulpes-positive dogs were pit bull-type animals, and the majority had been linked to dog fighting. Many carried further infections, notably haemotropic Mycoplasma, and some showed serological exposure to other vector-borne agents. Clinicopathology, with or without co-infection, showed anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hyperglobulinaemia, hypoalbuminaemia and proteinuria. The authors propose that B. vulpes often co-occurs with other pathogens and may spread dog-to-dog, as B. gibsoni does.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. vulpes infection in North American dogs is commonly accompanied by co-infections, and dog-to-dog transmission may be frequent.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly a molecular prevalence survey; pathophysiological value is limited to a descriptive clinicopathological profile (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypoalbuminaemia with hyperglobulinaemia, proteinuria) compatible with haemolytic, inflammatory and protein-losing processes. Co-infection prevents attribution of any finding to B. vulpes alone, and no mechanism or outcome is established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** clinicopathology available for only 14 of 48 dogs; no controls; frequent co-infections; convenience sample with selection and geographic bias (New York, fighting-dog population); varying reference ranges; limited follow-up.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
