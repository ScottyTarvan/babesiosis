## Abstract-derived summary

Using PCR and sequencing of a 12S rDNA fragment, the authors estimated the prevalence of the filarial nematode Dirofilaria repens in blood from 147 cats and 257 dogs sampled in Poland in 2013-2015. Among the dogs, 94 clinically healthy animals from the Masovia region were compared with 58 dogs infected with Babesia canis. The parasite was found in one cat, its Wolbachia endosymbiont in two. In dogs it occurred only in Masovia, with a local prevalence of 38% in healthy animals and about 90% in dogs with babesiosis. Sequencing confirmed double infection in 30 dogs with babesiosis and in no healthy dog. Co-infected dogs showed more marked anaemia and thrombocytopenia but smaller rises in alkaline phosphatase and urea than dogs with B. canis alone. The authors suggest that microfilaraemic dogs may be more prone to babesiosis, and that a protective effect against hepatic or renal failure merits further study.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The very high rate of co-infection in dogs treated for babesiosis, with none found in healthy dogs, suggests that microfilaraemic dogs are more prone to symptomatic babesiosis. Biochemical values in co-infected dogs were closer to normal than in dogs with B. canis alone, suggesting milder babesiosis, and the mechanism needs further study.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** D. repens co-infection was associated with lower platelet counts and with lower ALP activity in dogs with babesiosis, but the comparison group of dogs with B. canis alone was very small. The design cannot show whether the nematode influences the course of babesiosis. The pattern of more marked haematological change alongside milder biochemical change is hypothesis-generating only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 5 dogs with B. canis alone against 36 (haematology) or 32 (biochemistry) co-infected dogs (full text); healthy and babesiosis groups drawn from different sources (full text) and not matched; PCR sensitivity not determined (full text); no clinical comparison group of dogs with D. repens alone (full text); severity classes were defined by the authors from the same blood parameters that were then compared (full text); blood data were standard laboratory counts (full text), so timing relative to disease onset and treatment is unknown.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Repository records:** none found in article; raw data (Excel file) available from the corresponding author on request
- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - The 94 healthy dogs from the Warsaw area were a mixed group of pet dogs, sled dogs and shelter dogs; the 58 dogs with babesiosis came from central Poland near Warsaw and included fatal cases (full text)
  - Dog samples were collected between October 2014 and November 2015; babesiosis was first diagnosed by the Lab-Vet laboratory from blood smears and then confirmed by 18S rRNA PCR in all 58 dogs (full text)
  - D. repens DNA was found in 36 of 94 healthy dogs (38.3%) and 52 of 58 dogs with babesiosis (89.7%); the difference was significant by Fisher exact test (P < 0.0001) (full text)
  - One of the 94 healthy dogs (1.06%) was PCR-positive for B. canis and was negative for D. repens (full text)
  - Sequenced Babesia amplicons from 30 co-infected dogs were 99.0% identical to B. canis genotype 2 (full text)
  - Haematology was available for 41 dogs with babesiosis (36 co-infected, 5 with B. canis alone) and biochemistry for 37 (32 co-infected, 5 with B. canis alone) (full text)
  - Co-infected dogs, compared with dogs with B. canis alone, had lower thrombocytes (45.40 vs 91.80 G/l, P = 0.001), lower leukocytes (5.039 vs 7.50 G/l, P = 0.007) and lower ALP (165.48 vs 432.80 U/l, P = 0.007); erythrocytes, haemoglobin, haematocrit, AST, ALT, creatinine and urea did not differ significantly (full text)
  - The abstract's statement of more severe anaemia in co-infected dogs is not supported by the Table 1 comparison, where erythrocytes, haemoglobin and haematocrit did not differ significantly; among the haematological parameters only thrombocytes and leukocytes differed (full text)
  - All 58 dogs with babesiosis were thrombocytopenic; the authors graded severity in three classes (mild n = 10, moderate n = 11, severe n = 20 among the 41 dogs with haematology) from anaemia, hepatic and renal indicators (full text)
  - D. repens prevalence did not differ significantly between severity classes; the only trend was for hepatic dysfunction class (chi-square 3.69, df 2, P = 0.158) (full text)
  - Only two dogs with D. repens alone had blood data, which were mostly within reference ranges (full text)
  - The 105 dogs from northern, southern and western Poland were all D. repens-negative, and the 147 cats were pet cats sampled in 2013-2014 (full text)
