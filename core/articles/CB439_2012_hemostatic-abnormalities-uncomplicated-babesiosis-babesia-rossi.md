## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective, cross-sectional study asked whether dogs with uncomplicated babesiosis keep normal haemostatic capacity despite marked thrombocytopenia. Nineteen client-owned infected dogs were compared with ten healthy controls using thromboelastography, clotting times, fibrinogen, D-dimers and antithrombin. Infected dogs had a lower haematocrit and a much lower platelet count than controls. Most thromboelastography variables did not differ between the groups; only the clotting time K was longer in infected dogs, and the other values, such as maximal amplitude and global clot strength, did not differ at the stricter significance threshold the authors chose. Antithrombin activity was lower and fibrinogen concentration higher in the infected group. The authors suggested that, despite the low platelet counts and the absence of bleeding signs, the tracings could indicate a normocoagulable state.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs with uncomplicated babesiosis had no clinical haemorrhage and normal thromboelastography variables despite severe thrombocytopenia, which could indicate a normocoagulable state.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The findings describe haemostatic variables in uncomplicated B. rossi infection and are consistent with a balance between low platelet numbers and raised fibrinogen, but the cross-sectional design cannot show why clot strength was preserved. Only uncomplicated cases were studied, so the results do not extend to complicated disease or to DIC. The suggested role of platelet activation is the authors' interpretation and was not tested directly.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small sample size; single time point before treatment; uncomplicated cases only; broad haematocrit range in the infected group; control dogs differed in age from infected dogs; no direct measure of platelet activation.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Infection with B. rossi was confirmed, and co-infection with Ehrlichia canis (and other Babesia, Ehrlichia and Anaplasma species) was excluded, by PCR and reverse line blot (full text).
  - Dogs with complications (acute kidney failure, neurological signs, ARDS, haemoconcentration, secondary immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia, icterus) or on drugs affecting haemostasis were excluded (full text).
  - Controls were healthy client-owned dogs admitted for routine surgery or blood donation, matched for age and sex; median age was 6 years in the infected group and 3.25 years in the controls (full text).
  - Blood was collected before any treatment, and thromboelastography was run 30 minutes after sampling; other assays were run in a batch on plasma frozen at -80 C (full text).
  - Three infected dogs had global clot strength above the published hypercoagulable cut-off of 7.2 dyn/cm2 (full text).
  - Within the infected group, platelet count was positively associated with maximal amplitude and global clot strength and negatively with K; haematocrit and fibrinogen were not significantly associated with any thromboelastography variable at P < .01 (full text).
  - aPTT was longer in infected dogs (mean 13.3 versus 11.3 s; P = .001), whereas PT (P = .076) and D-dimers (P = .015) did not differ at the P < .01 threshold (full text).
  - The authors attribute the preserved clot strength tentatively to platelet activation during systemic inflammation, with binding to the high fibrinogen concentration (full text).
  - Authors are affiliated with the University of Pretoria and the University of Copenhagen; the study was approved by the Animal Use and Care Committee of the University of Pretoria (full text).
