## Abstract-derived summary

The study reviewed hospital records of 240 dogs infected with Babesia canis in Vienna, Austria, to establish how often complicated versus uncomplicated disease occurred and whether routine clinical and laboratory variables predicted death. Blood counts, serum chemistry and examination findings were analysed against survival with binary logistic regression. Creatinine above 5 mg/dL and phosphate above 3 mmol/L showed strong associations with death (P ≤ 0.001); albumin below 2.2 g/dL (P = 0.003) and rectal temperature under 38 °C (P ≤ 0.001) were also linked to severity. Mortality reached 33.9% in dogs with renal involvement and 40.0% in dogs with pancreatitis. The authors propose these four measures as dependable predictors of fatal outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic (retrospective associational)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Creatinine, phosphate, albumin and rectal temperature are reliable predictive markers of increased mortality risk in B. canis infection.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a severity phenotype in which azotaemia with hyperphosphataemia (renal dysfunction), hypoalbuminaemia and low body temperature mark non-survivors. Associations indicate prognosis, not mechanism; creatinine and phosphate are closely related renal markers, so their independent contribution is unclear. Reported mortality with renal involvement and pancreatitis highlights organ complications but does not show how they arise.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective single centre; species identification method, recruitment period and definition of "complicated" and "renal involvement" not reported in abstract; odds ratios, survivor numbers and model adjustment not reported; treatment confounding and timing of sampling not described; "reliable" is not supported by any reported validation.
