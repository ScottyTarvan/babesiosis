## Abstract-derived summary

The authors investigated Babesia infection in dogs in Mizoram, northeast India, combining molecular confirmation with clinical, haematological, serum biochemical and oxidative-stress measurements. During 2017-18, 1200 dogs were screened and 53 clinically suspected cases were tested by molecular diagnosis; 15 were positive (28.3% of suspects; 1.25% of all screened). Fever, emaciation, depression, jaundice and enlarged lymph nodes predominated. Infected dogs showed lower packed cell volume, haemoglobin, red cell count, MCHC, total protein and albumin, with higher MCV, white cell count, monocytes and urea nitrogen. Antioxidant defences (superoxide dismutase, catalase, total antioxidant capacity) were reduced while lipid peroxidation was raised. The authors concluded B. gibsoni is the main cause of canine babesiosis in the state and that infection substantially perturbs haematological, biochemical and redox status.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni is the principal agent of canine babesiosis in Mizoram and causes significant haemato-biochemical and oxidant-antioxidant disturbance.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive clinicopathological profile with an oxidative-stress component. The pattern (anaemia, hypoproteinaemia, increased urea nitrogen, reduced antioxidant enzymes, raised lipid peroxidation) is compatible with oxidative injury in haemolytic disease, but an association measured at one time point does not show that oxidative stress drives erythrocyte destruction or organ injury; it may equally reflect anaemia, inflammation or anorexia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 15 confirmed cases; comparator (healthy control) group and its size not described in abstract; species-level results appear to rest on the 15 PCR-positive dogs; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) not mentioned; single time point; no outcome data; treatment/nutritional confounding not addressed in abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
