## Abstract-derived summary

Over one year, 24 of 136 dogs with babesiosis were classed as having cerebral disease. Babesiosis was confirmed by Giemsa blood smears and a B. gibsoni-specific PCR product of roughly 670 bp. Cerebral involvement was supported by neurological signs, parasitised erythrocytes in cerebrospinal fluid, and the same PCR product in CSF. Three dogs were necropsied: brain and meningeal congestion with engorged small vessels, and histologically oedema, neuronal necrosis, microglial proliferation, neutrophil infiltration and small vessels occluded by cells, with piroplasms in cerebral vessels. The authors say such dogs frequently died suddenly and responded poorly to treatment, which they ascribe to thrombosis, congestion and oedema.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors propose microscopy plus PCR of blood and CSF, with histopathology, as a diagnostic route for canine cerebral babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds B. gibsoni cerebral histology (vascular sequestration or occlusion, endothelial injury, oedema, neuronal necrosis) consistent with microvascular pathology described for B. rossi. It documents a syndrome and lesions, not mechanism; the thrombosis explanation for poor response is asserted, not tested.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No control group; only 3 necropsies; mortality and clinical characteristics of the 24 dogs not quantified; PCR sample counts inconsistent (11 dogs in Methods, 24 in Results); CSF erythrocytes may reflect iatrogenic blood contamination; co-infections not systematically excluded; study period and clinic not explicitly stated; peer-review practice of the journal not verified.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
