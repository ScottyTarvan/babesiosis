## Abstract-derived summary

The abstract states only the aim and reports no results, so the figures below come from the full text. The study described 50 client-owned dogs of both sexes, aged 1 to 10 years, with naturally acquired tick-borne infection seen between May and July 2016, mostly around Bareilly, India. Infection was identified on Giemsa-stained blood smears: 41 dogs (82%) had B. gibsoni and 9 (18%) had E. canis. Dogs aged 1 to 5 years made up 60% and Labradors were most often affected. In B. gibsoni dogs the mean packed cell volume was 26.9% and neutrophils 73%; E. canis dogs had fewer neutrophils and more lymphocytes, while other indices did not differ significantly. The authors advise adding antioxidants and corticosteroids to specific therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni predominated (82%) over E. canis (18%), and adding antioxidant and steroid to standard treatment could reduce SIRS and MODS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides a small clinicopathological profile of B. gibsoni dogs (mild anaemia by mean PCV, neutrophil-dominant leukogram with leukocytosis), separable from the E. canis group. It shows nothing about mechanism; the treatment advice is extrapolated from the literature, not tested here. The post-treatment kidney failure noted in 10 recovered B. gibsoni dogs (24%) is an anecdotal observation of possible complicated disease, not a measured outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single-centre n=50; only 9 comparator dogs; smear-only diagnosis (species, co-infection and low-parasitaemia E. canis cases unconfirmed); no controls or reference intervals; dispersion (± values) not defined; clinical signs recorded but not reported by group; no treatment comparison; no peer-review policy visible on the journal page.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
