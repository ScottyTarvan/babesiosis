## Abstract-derived summary

Six healthy male Beagles were used: one to amplify the inoculum, three received a high B. rossi dose and two a low dose. Clinical examination, blood counts and C-reactive protein were recorded daily, and stored plasma was later profiled with a canine Milliplex cytokine bead panel. Treatment was given once predefined experimental or humane endpoints were reached. Parasitaemia was detected on day 1 in the high-dose and day 3 in the low-dose dogs, and rose faster with the higher dose. Heart rate, blood pressure, CRP, neutrophils, monocytes and many cytokines or chemokines (including IFN-gamma, TNF-alpha, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IP-10, MCP-1, KC, GM-CSF, IL-2, IL-7, IL-15, IL-18) differed between groups at several time points. The authors infer that inflammation precedes clinical signs, dose affects its timing, and treatment transiently amplifies it.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Inflammation begins before overt disease, infectious dose shapes its onset, treatment intensifies it shortly afterwards, and a pro-/anti-inflammatory cytokine imbalance may favour parasite replication.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** One of few controlled-timeline datasets of host response in B. rossi, useful for temporal ordering (cytokine and CRP changes versus clinical signs and parasitaemia). Group differences are associations; they do not show that cytokines cause complications or that post-treatment rises cause harm. Syndrome-level relevance to naturally acquired, tick-transmitted disease is uncertain.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small and unequal groups (3 vs 2 infected recipients); no uninfected control group or sham-treated comparison described; males only; inoculum raised in a single donor dog (inoculation route not reported in abstract); treatment timing tied to endpoints, confounding dose and time effects; many analytes tested with minimal power.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
