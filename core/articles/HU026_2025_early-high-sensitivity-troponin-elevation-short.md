## Abstract-derived summary

The authors searched PubMed, Scopus and Embase for studies linking an early high-sensitivity cardiac troponin measurement to short-term death in adults with sepsis defined by Sepsis-3. Seventeen studies covering 6242 patients were pooled with random-effects models. Patients who died had higher troponin than survivors (standardised mean difference 0.87, 95% CI 0.41-1.33). A raised troponin, compared with a normal one, carried higher unadjusted odds of death (OR 1.78, 95% CI 1.41-2.25). When only studies that adjusted for confounders were pooled, the link vanished (aOR 1.06, 95% CI 0.99-1.14). The authors conclude that troponin is higher in non-survivors and associated with mortality, but is not an independent predictor, which fits a marker of general illness severity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational prognostic studies; pooled estimates inherit confounding and design heterogeneity from the included cohorts.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Troponin rises in sepsis non-survivors and tracks mortality crudely, but is not independently associated with death.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category CAR, rated **speculative**. Shared idea: troponin as a severity marker rather than an independent signal in critical illness. Canine studies measured troponin I and CK-MB in 50 *B. canis* dogs, highest in the two that died (CB014), and described cardiomyocyte necrosis and circulatory failure in fatal cases (CB080, CB146). No adjusted prognostic analysis exists in dogs, so the sepsis result is an analogy. Differences: sepsis is a heterogeneous, mostly bacterial syndrome with vasoplegia in adult ICU or ED patients, whereas canine babesiosis is a haemolytic protozoal disease in which anaemia, hypoxia and coagulation disturbance may drive injury; assays and cut-offs also differ across species.
- **Key limitations:** Observational inputs with substantial heterogeneity; the abstract gives no per-study risk of bias; adjusted analyses are fewer and use varying covariates; no babesiosis-specific data.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
