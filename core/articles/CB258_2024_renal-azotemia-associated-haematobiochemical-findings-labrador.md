## Abstract-derived summary

A two-year-old male Labrador was brought to a university veterinary clinic with poor appetite, fever, lethargy, dark yellow urine and heavy tick burden. Examination found fever (103.2 °F), fast heart rate with laboured breathing, pale mucous membranes, black tarry stools and enlarged superficial lymph nodes. Babesia gibsoni was seen on smear and confirmed by PCR. Laboratory work showed anaemia, raised white cell count and elevated liver and kidney markers, while ultrasound found a thickened, hyperechoic renal cortex. The dog had earlier received imidocarb. The clinicians diagnosed renal azotaemia linked to babesiosis, gave diminazene, doxycycline and renal supportive care, and reported clinical recovery with better blood values.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that this was renal azotaemia associated with B. gibsoni babesiosis, with recovery after antiparasitic and renal supportive treatment.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A single case documenting azotaemia with cortical echogenicity changes in small-Babesia disease, so it adds a renal organ-involvement observation (REN) alongside anaemia (ERY) and hepatic markers (MET). It does not show mechanism. The abstract gives no urinalysis, urine specific gravity, proteinuria, blood pressure or SDMA data, so prerenal azotaemia (haemolysis, hypovolaemia, melena-related urea rise) cannot be separated from intrinsic injury. Hyperechoic cortex is non-specific, and prior imidocarb is a possible nephrotoxic confounder. Recovery on combined therapy cannot attribute causality to the parasite.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=1; no numerical laboratory values in abstract; no control or serial renal data; co-infections (for example Ehrlichia) not addressed; timing of imidocarb and follow-up duration not reported; full text not read; journal is a biochemistry title with special-issue publication and no peer-review information accessible.
