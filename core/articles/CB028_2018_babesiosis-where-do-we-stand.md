## Abstract-derived summary

This review surveys canine babesiosis as one of the most widespread and rapidly spreading tick-borne infections of dogs. It outlines which Babesia species infect dogs, their tick vectors and geographic ranges, and the parasite life cycle and routes of transmission. The authors then describe pathogenesis alongside the highly variable clinical presentation, laboratory findings and clinicopathological changes. Diagnostic approaches are covered, from microscopy and serology to molecular methods and recent mass-spectrometry-based assays, followed by treatment and prevention guidance. The authors stress that identifying the infecting species correctly informs treatment choice, monitoring and prognosis. No quantitative results are reported in the abstract, as expected for a review of this kind.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Accurate species identification should guide therapy, monitoring and prognosis in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A broad, comprehensive review whose pathogenesis and clinicopathology coverage is useful as a pre-2018 framing of the field (the abstract names the main pathogenic mechanisms and clinicopathological findings without detail). It is a secondary source and does not itself add primary pathophysiological data; mechanistic claims should be traced to the cited primary studies. Much of the content (vectors, diagnostics, treatment) falls outside pathophysiology.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Review design with no stated search strategy or appraisal method in the abstract; published 2018, so later work in the review window is not covered; breadth over depth on pathophysiology.
