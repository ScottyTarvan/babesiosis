## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined platelet indices in dogs infected with Babesia rossi and asked whether they relate to disease outcome. The authors expected a higher share of large, activated platelets. They enrolled 96 infected dogs, grouped as survivors and nonsurvivors, and 15 controls. Seven indices, including count, mean volume, plateletcrit and mean platelet mass, were measured at presentation, and again after 24 and 48 hours in admitted survivors. Mortality was 13% (12 of 96). Compared with controls, infected dogs of both outcome groups had lower platelet count and plateletcrit and higher mean platelet volume; mean platelet mass was higher only in survivors. No index differed between survivors and nonsurvivors at presentation. In admitted survivors, count and plateletcrit rose by 24 and 48 hours, and mean platelet component concentration rose at 24 hours. The authors suggest that large, activated platelets may help explain why bleeding is absent despite severe thrombocytopenia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Large, activated platelets were significantly increased in infected dogs and may play a role in the lack of a bleeding phenotype despite severe thrombocytopenia in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Infected dogs showed a platelet phenotype with low count and raised mean platelet volume, consistent with a shift towards larger platelets. None of the indices separated survivors from nonsurvivors at presentation, so they do not appear to be useful for early prognosis in this sample. The link to the absence of bleeding is a proposed interpretation, not a tested mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; only 12 nonsurvivors, so power to detect outcome differences may be limited; the sampling schedule at 24 and 48 hours covered admitted survivors only; no functional platelet activation test is described in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
