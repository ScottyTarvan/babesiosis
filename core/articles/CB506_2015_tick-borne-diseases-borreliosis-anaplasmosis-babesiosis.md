## Abstract-derived summary

This review covers three tick-borne infections of dogs in Germany and Austria: Borrelia burgdorferi sensu lato, Anaplasma and Babesia. It names the vectors involved, including Dermacentor reticulatus for Babesia canis and Rhipicephalus sanguineus for Babesia vogeli in the Mediterranean region, and discusses transmission mechanisms, minimum attachment intervals and non-vector routes. For each pathogen it addresses incubation, pathogenicity and clinical findings, with case examples, and it maps prevalence data from symptomatic and asymptomatic dog groups in both countries. Diagnostic approaches are laid out, among them blood smears, species-specific PCR with sequencing and serology, together with haematological, biochemical and urinary abnormalities. New data assess anti-C6 antibody concentration in relation to proteinuria and Lyme nephritis. The review closes with acaricide products, vaccination and imidocarb chemoprophylaxis for large Babesia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review (multi-pathogen narrative review; Babesia is one of three pathogens)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The abstract gives no single conclusion. It stresses that molecular differentiation of Anaplasma species and of large versus small Babesia matters when serological cross-reaction occurs, and that blood smears, PCR and serology are more valuable in combination.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The article is included as a canine review because the abstract states that it covers pathogenicity, clinical findings and laboratory abnormalities (haematology, clinical chemistry, urine) for each pathogen, including babesiosis. Its stated emphasis is distribution, diagnostics and prophylaxis, so the share of Babesia-specific host pathophysiology cannot be judged from the abstract. The new anti-C6 antibody data concern Lyme nephritis, not Babesia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; full text not retrieved; Babesia content is a fraction of a three-pathogen review; no sample sizes or Babesia-specific laboratory values are given in the abstract; species identification method not stated; narrative review with no stated search method.
