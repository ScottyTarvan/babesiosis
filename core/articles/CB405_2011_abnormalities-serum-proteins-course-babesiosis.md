## Abstract-derived summary

This study looked at serum protein electrophoresis in Babesia canis-infected dogs and at how haematocrit relates to the albumin to globulin ratio. Infection was confirmed by PCR with sequencing of the products. Total protein was measured on a clinical chemistry analyser, and albumin plus the alpha, beta and gamma globulin fractions were quantified by densitometry after agarose gel electrophoresis. The commonest abnormalities were reduced alpha globulin fractions, raised beta globulin, a higher albumin to globulin ratio and low total globulin. Alpha-1 globulin was lowered in 54.8% of dogs and alpha-2 in 32.3%. Beta-1 and beta-2 globulins were raised in 38.7% and 51.6%, and the A/G ratio was raised in 38.7%. Hypoglobulinaemia occurred in 32.3%. The authors suggested the changes probably reflect haemolysis, hypertransferrinaemia, complement C3a elevation and dehydration.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors suggest that the serum protein abnormalities in infected dogs probably reflect haemolysis, hypertransferrinaemia, increased complement C3a concentration and dehydration.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study gives a descriptive profile of the electrophoretic serum protein pattern in PCR-confirmed B. canis infection, which bears on the acute-phase and immune response and on haemolysis. The explanations offered for the changes are the authors' suggestions ("probably"); the abstract reports no test of them. The abstract does not report a haematocrit correlation result.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only, so group sizes, controls and statistics are not shown; no healthy control group is mentioned in the abstract; the mechanisms offered are inferred rather than measured; the setting and sampling period are not stated; no severity or outcome analysis is reported.
