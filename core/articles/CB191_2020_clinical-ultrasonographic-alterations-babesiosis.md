## Abstract-derived summary

Over one year, dogs brought to a university veterinary clinic in Mhow, India, with fever, poor appetite and enlarged lymph nodes were screened for blood parasites. Dogs with babesiosis on stained blood smears were then described clinically and by abdominal ultrasound. The most frequent findings were petechiae or epistaxis (75%), tick infestation (72%), inability to stand (69%), dullness (67%), dehydration (64%) and pale mucosae (50%). On ultrasound, the liver was enlarged with altered architecture, and parenchyma was hypoechoic in 7 dogs. Nine dogs had an enlarged spleen with distorted architecture and multifocal lesions, 5 had cystitis, 3 had a distended gallbladder, and 4 had kidneys in which cortex and medulla could not be told apart. The authors present these as typical alterations in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis is accompanied by characteristic clinical signs and by sonographic changes of liver, spleen, bladder, gallbladder and kidney.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that hepatosplenic and renal sonographic abnormalities are common in smear-positive dogs, supporting multisystem involvement (SEV, with MET and REN tags). It does not establish mechanism, timing or prognostic value, and none of the lesions is specific to babesiosis; congestion, inflammation, extramedullary haematopoiesis or concurrent disease are plausible but untested explanations.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group or reference imaging; species not identified and co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) not excluded in the abstract; no per-dog denominators for imaging findings; no haematology, biochemistry or outcome data in the abstract; very short report (2 pages).
