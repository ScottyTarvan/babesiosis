## Abstract-derived summary

In 24 dogs with babesiosis, the authors recorded presenting clinical signs and abdominal ultrasound findings. Lethargy and weakness each affected 91.7% of dogs, as did lymph node enlargement; anorexia was seen in 87.5%, pallor in 83.3%, splenomegaly on examination in 58.3%, and every dog carried ticks. Ultrasound identified an enlarged liver, enlarged spleen, gallbladder abnormalities, altered renal echogenicity and free abdominal fluid, although the abstract gives no frequencies for individual ultrasound lesions. No comparison group is described in the abstract, so the findings describe the clinical and imaging picture within one group of dogs. The authors conclude that combining clinical assessment with ultrasonography improves diagnostic accuracy and clarifies how many organ systems are involved in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Clinical evaluation plus ultrasonography gives a more accurate diagnosis and prognosis and demonstrates multi-organ involvement.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a multisystem imaging phenotype (hepatosplenomegaly, biliary sludge and gallbladder wall oedema, renal cortical echogenicity change, effusion) consistent with haemolysis, splenic clearance and possible hepatic and renal injury. Proposed mechanisms in the discussion (cytokines, ischaemia, haemoglobinuria) are literature-based and untested here; no severity, outcome or biomarker association is shown.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group; no haematology, biochemistry or outcome data to link imaging to severity; Babesia species not reported; co-infections not addressed; ultrasound lesion frequencies not quantified and reader blinding not described; ascites cause (hypoproteinaemia, hepatic dysfunction) not measured; 6 weeks from receipt to acceptance at a publisher (AkiNik) that has been flagged in predatory-publisher discussions (not independently verified here).
