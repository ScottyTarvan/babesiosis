## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective study from Dnipro, Ukraine, examined clinical records of 25 dogs diagnosed with babesiosis, with Babesia canis described as the prevailing species in the country. A full serum biochemical panel was analysed. Dogs given treatment (the title names imidopyran and prednisolone) within the first 24 hours showed favourable shifts in superoxide dismutase, inorganic phosphorus, lactate dehydrogenase and several protein fractions. Urea, creatinine, potassium, albumin, cholesterol, calcium and sodium did not change significantly. The authors interpret this as lowered renal and oxidative burden with improved electrolyte, protein and lipid handling, and suggest further monitoring or therapy may be needed for complete biochemical normalisation. The abstract does not report effect sizes, group allocation, or controls.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Early treatment improved several biochemical markers and stabilised dogs with haemolytic anaemia, with some parameters still needing monitoring.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Offers a clinicopathological profile with oxidative-stress (SOD) and haemolysis/tissue-injury (LDH) markers, but the change is framed against treatment, so natural recovery cannot be separated from drug effect. The claim of reduced renal stress sits uneasily with unchanged urea and creatinine. LDH and phosphorus are non-specific. It is not a mechanistic study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=25; no uninfected controls or untreated comparator described; species and co-infections not confirmed in abstract; imidopyran not defined in abstract; timing and statistics unclear; full text inaccessible; journal peer-review policy not checked.
