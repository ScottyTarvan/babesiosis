## Abstract-derived summary

The report documents presentation, laboratory findings, molecular parasite burden and therapy in three dogs infected with Babesia negevi, a species recognised in dogs only recently. All three had marked lethargy, pallor, anaemia and thrombocytopenia; the adult was febrile and one pup was icteric. Microscopy detected parasites resembling B. negevi and PCR confirmed the species, with Borrelia persica co-detected in two animals. Imidocarb gave clinical improvement and lowered qPCR load (adult 43,451 to 803 parasites/µl; surviving pup 3.29 million to 20,092/µl within two weeks), yet DNA persisted for one to seven months and the icteric pup died. Atovaquone plus azithromycin for ten days produced PCR-negative results in both survivors, so the authors favour that combination.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Imidocarb dipropionate relieves clinical disease but does not clear B. negevi, so atovaquone with azithromycin is recommended.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Relevant as a clinicopathological description of a newly recognised canine Babesia (regenerative status, organ involvement and mechanisms are not reported in the abstract). It shows that very young dogs can reach very high parasitaemia and that disease can occur with anaemia, thrombocytopenia and icterus, and that fatal outcome occurred in one pup with co-infection status unspecified for that animal. It is primarily a treatment-response report and does not establish pathophysiological mechanism or species-specific virulence.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 3, no controls; Borrelia persica co-infection in two dogs confounds clinical interpretation; imidocarb then atovaquone-azithromycin is sequential, uncontrolled treatment; no haematological reference values or organ-function data in the abstract; necropsy findings for the fatal case are not reported in the abstract.
