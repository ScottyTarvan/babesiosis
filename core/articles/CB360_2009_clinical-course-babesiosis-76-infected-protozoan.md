## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers followed 76 dogs with suspected babesiosis presented over three successive tick seasons, using clinical examination plus haematology, biochemistry, blood smears and PCR. Altered urine colour and yellow or pale mucous membranes were the commonest signs, while thrombocytopenia, leucopoenia, anaemia and raised urea and bilirubin were the commonest laboratory changes. All 76 smears showed parasites inside erythrocytes, and PCR detected parasite DNA in 69 samples. The pattern pointed to a haemolytic form of disease. Dogs infected with genetic group B isolates had a mean platelet count of 27.47 thousand/mm3, compared with 61.11 thousand/mm3 for group A. Lower platelet counts correlated with higher body temperature, faster pulse and urine discolouration. Seventy-three of the 76 dogs recovered after imidocarb treatment.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The course of disease and the molecular results suggested haemolytic babesiosis, and the authors report an interrelation between thrombocytopenia and infection with group A or group B parasites. The abstract states no conclusion on PCR for routine diagnosis, although this was one aim.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a clinicopathological series that adds platelet data to the syndrome description and reports an association between parasite genotype group and platelet count. The association does not show that genotype determines thrombocytopenia, because dog-level confounders are not reported in the abstract. Platelet count was also associated with higher body temperature, faster pulse and urine discolouration.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group reported in the abstract; abstract only, so group sizes for genotypes A and B and the statistics for the genotype comparison are not available; single-cohort case series; treatment outcome (imidocarb) is reported without a comparator; PCR was positive in 69 of 76 smear-positive dogs.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
