## Abstract-derived summary

The authors sought the main contributors to disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) in naturally acquired acute canine babesiosis in Ukraine. Fifty smear-positive dogs (parasitaemia about 5-7% of red cells) were compared with fifty healthy controls using haematology, coagulometry, fibrinogen, soluble fibrin-monomer complexes (SFMC), fibrin degradation products (FDP), platelet and erythrocyte aggregation, oscillometric blood pressure and circulating blood volume. Infected dogs had higher SFMC and FDP, lower fibrinogen and platelet counts, more spontaneous cell aggregation, reduced circulating blood volume, haematocrit and mean arterial pressure, and a shock index roughly twice that of controls (all reported as significant). The authors read this as consumptive-stage DIC with grade II subcompensated shock needing emergency admission.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive / associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute spontaneous canine babesiosis is characterised by consumption-coagulopathy DIC and subcompensated (grade II) shock.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Informs the coagulation-haemodynamic syndrome (COA, END) rather than mechanism. Markers (SFMC, FDP, fibrinogen) are compatible with thrombin generation and consumption, but PT and APTT did not differ significantly (full text), and no D-dimer, antithrombin or formal DIC score was used. Platelet decline was moderate (178 vs 288 G/L). The "31% blood loss" is derived from a haematocrit equation and likely reflects haemolytic anaemia and volume shifts, not haemorrhage. The claim that DIC triggers shock is not testable in a single-timepoint design.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Species not typed; co-infections not screened; healthy (not sick) controls; single time point; non-standard in-house haemostasis panel; ANOVA with threshold p-values only; only 3 deaths, no outcome analysis; abstract and full-text n differ in wording; regional journal, though a 10-month review timeline suggests genuine peer review.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
