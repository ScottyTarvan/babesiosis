## Abstract-derived summary

This retrospective analysis covers 342 German dogs with acute B. canis infection diagnosed between January 2018 and December 2024, defined by sequencing-confirmed piroplasmid PCR positivity, negative Anaplasma phagocytophilum PCR, and available haematocrit, platelet and leukocyte data. History, signs, treatment and outcome came from questionnaires. Cases peaked in autumn (63.7%) and mostly arose in northern states (75.7%), with several high-risk regions identified. Lethargy (66.8% of 220) and fever (55.7% of 228) were commonest, and pigmenturia occurred in 26.0% of 204. All 164 dogs with treatment data received imidocarb (median 3.3 mg/kg). The course was uncomplicated in 87.6% of 193 dogs and mortality was 6.7%. The authors conclude that prompt treatment usually gives an uncomplicated course and that thrombocytopenia warrants considering babesiosis year-round.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Acute B. canis infections are usually uncomplicated with prompt treatment and should be a differential for thrombocytopenia throughout the year, even without fever or pigmenturia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Eligible as a large clinical-phenotype and outcome series (borderline: substantial epidemiological content). It shows that most German B. canis cases are mild (thrombocytopenia in all dogs, fever in just over half, pigmenturia in about a quarter), with about 12% complicated courses and 6.7% mortality, but gives no mechanistic or biomarker-outcome analysis. The abstract does not define "complicated".
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Retrospective with questionnaire data missing for many dogs (231/342 responded); outcome known for only 193; diagnostic-submission selection bias; only A. phagocytophilum excluded as co-infection; no genotyping; treatment effect not separable (all treated dogs received imidocarb).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
