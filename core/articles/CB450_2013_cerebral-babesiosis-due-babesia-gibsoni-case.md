## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes a single dog diagnosed with cerebral babesiosis attributed to Babesia gibsoni. The animal showed depression, weakness, ataxia, seizures and anorexia, a combination of signs that points to involvement of the nervous system. According to the authors, the dog responded well to one dose of diminazene aceturate given at 7.5 mg per kg of body weight. The abstract does not say how the parasite species or the cerebral involvement was confirmed, and it reports no haematology, biochemistry, imaging or cerebrospinal fluid findings. The authors use the case to argue that cerebral babesiosis should be considered among the differential diagnoses whenever a dog presents with neurological disease.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that cerebral babesiosis should be considered when a dog has neurological disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report documents a neurological presentation (seizures, ataxia, depression) in a dog with B. gibsoni infection, which is a distinct syndrome relevant to the NEU category. A single case cannot show that the parasite produced the neurological signs; it shows that the signs occurred together with the infection and that the dog was reported to respond well to treatment. Mechanistic information is not available at abstract level.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; abstract only; method of species identification and of confirming cerebral involvement not stated; no clinicopathological, imaging or cerebrospinal fluid data in the abstract; no follow-up period stated; country and setting not stated.
