## Abstract-derived summary

This Polish-language review, with an English abstract, presents canine babesiosis as a tick-borne disease marked by haemolytic anaemia. The authors name Babesia canis as the agent in Poland, spread by Dermacentor ticks, and note that the disease was first described in dogs in the United States in 1934. Jaundice and haemoglobinuria are listed as the usual signs, with occasional vomiting and diarrhoea. Serum biochemistry may show higher AST, ALT, total bilirubin, urea and creatinine, while haematology may show anaemia, leucopenia and thrombocytopenia. Diagnosis rests on history, clinical examination and blood smear microscopy, with immunofluorescence and PCR as aids. No effective vaccine exists, and imidocarb is the most effective drug, although side effects can occur. The authors suggest that genetic differences between subspecies could influence disease severity and treatment response.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
