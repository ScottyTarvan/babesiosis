## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with signs such as anorexia, fever, swollen lymph nodes and tick infestation were screened at a veterinary clinical complex in Puducherry to find B. gibsoni infection and to compare PCR with smear microscopy. Of 85 dogs, six (7.1%) were smear-positive and 28 were PCR-positive, reported as 42.4%. Dogs with B. gibsoni showed tick infestation, anorexia, dullness, fever, pale or congested mucous membranes, rapid breathing and swollen lymph nodes; a few had icterus and haemoglobinuria. Laboratory results were described as showing significantly lower haemoglobin, PCV, red cell and platelet counts and albumin, with higher ALP, globulin, bilirubin, urea nitrogen and creatinine. The abstract gives no effect sizes, comparator group or explicit conclusion.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** No explicit conclusion in the abstract; the design implies that PCR detected more infections than smear examination.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A clinicopathological profile of B. gibsoni infection consistent with haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypoalbuminaemia, hyperglobulinaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia and azotaemia. Azotaemia and raised ALP suggest renal and hepatobiliary involvement or pre-renal effects, but with no mechanistic, histological or severity-stratified data this is syndrome description only, not mechanism or prognosis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** PCR-positive proportion does not match the stated denominator (28/85 = 32.9%; 42.4% implies 66 tested); comparator for "significant" changes not stated; number of dogs in haematology analysis unclear; co-infections (Ehrlichia, Hepatozoon) and hydration status not reported; no outcome data; short article with limited detail; peer-review rigour of the journal not assessed.
