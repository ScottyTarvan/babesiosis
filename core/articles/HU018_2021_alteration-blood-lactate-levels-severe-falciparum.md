## Abstract-derived summary

The authors reasoned that metabolic acidosis in severe malaria is mostly lactic and sought published blood lactate values in severe disease. They searched PubMed, Web of Science and Scopus, retrieving 793 records and including 30 studies, appraised with the Jadad scale and STROBE. Random-effects pooling, meta-regression and subgroup analyses explored heterogeneity. The pooled mean lactate in severe malaria was 5.04 mM (CI 4.44 to 5.64, I2 99.9%). Severe cases exceeded uncomplicated ones by 2.46 mM (CI 0.85 to 4.07, nine studies), and fatal cases exceeded survivors by 2.74 mM (CI 1.74 to 3.75, I2 95.8%, six studies). The authors conclude lactate differs markedly by severity and outcome, and recommend studies of its prognostic value for spotting patients at high risk of severe disease or death.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Systematic review with random-effects meta-analysis of observational studies; very high heterogeneity.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Blood lactate is clearly higher in severe than uncomplicated malaria and in fatal than surviving severe malaria; its prognostic value needs further study.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category MET; hyperlactataemia as a marker of death is rated **directly demonstrated in both** (association only). CB074 (*B. gibsoni*, n=108 plus 30 controls) found lactate, urea and ALT prognostic, with a discriminant score of 79% sensitivity and 83% specificity; CB068 (*B. canis*, n=14) reported mixed acid-base disorders including lactate-related acidosis; CB082 (experimental *B. rossi*, n=5) reproduced dose-dependent acidosis. Differences: this is falciparum malaria, not human babesiosis, where microvascular sequestration is a defining mechanism (CB041 is only a narrative proposal for *B. canis*); canine lactate prognosis comes from *B. gibsoni* while acid-base data come from other species; splenic status, treatment (artesunate vs imidocarb) and lactate methods differ.
- **Key limitations:** I2 of roughly 96-100%; mostly observational inputs; abstract does not state publication-bias results; no human babesiosis lactate data.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
