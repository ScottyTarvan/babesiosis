## Abstract-derived summary

The authors characterised splenic lesions in fatal canine B. rossi babesiosis. Spleens from nine dogs that died naturally with a B. rossi mono-infection were compared with spleens from four healthy dogs euthanised for welfare reasons. One haematoxylin-and-eosin section per dog was assessed, and immunohistochemical markers identified immune cell populations, which were semi-quantified with image analysis software. Infected spleens showed diffuse intermingling of white and red pulp, with the usual boundary between the compartments lost. The proportion of both tissue-resident and bone-marrow-derived macrophages was higher in infected dogs. Apart from a few lymphocytes remaining in periarteriolar sheaths and follicles, most immune cells had moved into the red pulp. The authors state that these changes broadly resemble splenic descriptions from other mammals with lethal malaria or babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (with semi-quantitative associational comparison against controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Splenic architecture in lethal B. rossi infection is disrupted by white/red pulp merging and macrophage expansion, consistent with findings in lethal malaria and babesiosis of non-canid hosts.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides tissue-level evidence of splenic immunopathology (loss of compartmentalisation, lymphocyte redistribution, macrophage expansion) in dogs that died. It is compatible with a role for the spleen in parasite and infected-erythrocyte clearance, but it does not establish mechanism, timing or causal contribution to death, and cannot separate lethal from survivable disease because only fatal cases were examined.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small n (9 vs 4); single H&E section per dog; semi-quantitative analysis; terminal-stage tissue only; controls were euthanised dogs rather than non-lethal infected dogs; parasitaemia, treatment and disease duration not reported in abstract.
