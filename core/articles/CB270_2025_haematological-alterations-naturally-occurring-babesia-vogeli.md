## Abstract-derived summary

During calendar 2023, 200 client-owned dogs with fever, inappetence, pallor and lethargy were seen at two Kerala university hospitals and tested for Babesia vogeli by 18S rRNA PCR and by blood-smear microscopy. PCR detected infection in 31.5% of dogs, compared with 25% by microscopy. Blood profiles of the infected dogs were contrasted with those of 60 healthy dogs. Affected dogs had mild anaemia and moderate thrombocytopenia, with highly significant differences in lymphocyte count, erythrocyte count, haemoglobin, packed cell volume, MCHC, platelet count and plateletcrit. The authors conclude that the anaemia and platelet variables can serve as prognostic indicators of B. vogeli infection in field and hospital settings.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (descriptive clinicopathological profile with healthy controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors propose that anaemia and thrombocytopenia markers can be used as prognostic indicators in B. vogeli babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows that PCR-confirmed B. vogeli infection is associated with mild anaemia and moderate thrombocytopenia versus healthy dogs, i.e. a syndrome-level profile. The prognostic claim is not supported by anything in the abstract: no survival, follow-up or severity-outcome analysis is described, so these remain diagnostic or severity-descriptive markers. Mechanism (haemolysis, immune-mediated, consumption) is not addressed.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No outcome data despite prognostic claim; healthy controls only (no PCR-negative sick dogs); co-infections not reported; means, SDs and p values not in abstract; single-year, two-site hospital sample; severity grading criteria not described.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
