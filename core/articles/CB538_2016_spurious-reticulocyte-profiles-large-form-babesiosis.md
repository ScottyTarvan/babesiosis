## Abstract-derived summary

Automated reticulocyte counts can read falsely high, a phenomenon previously described in dogs with leukaemia. An unusual reticulocyte profile had been seen in one dog with large form babesiosis at the authors' institution. The authors examined all canine blood counts from two haematology analysers and selected the large form babesiosis cases, reviewing their reticulocyte dot plots. They also identified dogs with abnormal profiles but no visible parasites on the smear, and compared fluorescence ratios across groups. Of 92 dogs with babesiosis, 20 had abnormal reticulocyte profiles; eight of them had a separation or continuum of dots between the reticulocyte and mature red cell plots. Among 6980 dogs without Babesia on the smear, 13 had abnormal profiles, three of them with leukaemia. Ratios in the medium-fluorescence range tended to be higher in infected dogs with abnormal plots than in other dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Abnormal reticulocyte dot plots and atypical fluorescence ratios may occur in dogs with babesiosis, and they should prompt clinical pathologists to consider this diagnosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study describes abnormal automated reticulocyte profiles in dogs with large form babesiosis, which may bear on how regeneration is judged in these dogs. Twenty of 92 infected dogs showed an abnormal profile, and 13 of 6980 dogs without Babesia on the smear did too, so the profile is not specific to babesiosis. The abstract does not link the profiles to anaemia severity, outcome or a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; retrospective single-institution design; infection defined by microscopy with species not identified; comparison group is dogs without Babesia on the smear, not matched controls; no outcome or severity data reported in the abstract; two analysers pooled.
