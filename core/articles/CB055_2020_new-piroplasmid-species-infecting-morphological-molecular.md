## Abstract-derived summary

Piroplasms were seen on blood smears from five sick dogs in Israel, and the authors sequenced the parasite's 18S rRNA and cox1 genes. Four dogs also carried Borrelia persica, so soft ticks (O. tholozani) were screened as possible vectors. Dogs showed fever (4/5), anaemia, thrombocytopenia (4/5) and icterus (3/5). Sequences from dogs and ticks were 99-100% identical and formed a new lineage in the western Babesia sensu lato group, close to B. duncani and more distant from B. conradae. Piroplasm stages were larger than other canine small Babesia and smaller than large canine Babesia. Of 212 ticks, 11 (5.2%) carried the parasite's DNA. The authors name the species Babesia negevi and conclude that it warrants clinical attention, while vector status remains unproven.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. negevi n. sp. is a distinct, clinically relevant canine piroplasm, and a fatal outcome despite co-infection in most dogs indicates it needs clinical attention.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Syndrome-level description (haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, icterus) of a newly recognised canine Babesia; it adds to the agent catalogue rather than to mechanism. Because 4/5 dogs had B. persica, attribution of signs to B. negevi is uncertain; the single-infected fatal dog is the most informative observation. Tick PCR positivity does not demonstrate vector competence.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=5, no controls, selection of cases not described, co-infections confounding, no parasitaemia quantification, mixed treatments. The abstract states one fatal dog, whereas full text (Table 1, Results) reports two deaths (dogs 2 and 4, one day after treatment started), an internal inconsistency.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
