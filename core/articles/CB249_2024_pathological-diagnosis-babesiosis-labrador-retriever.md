## Abstract-derived summary

A young male Labrador retriever with a history of tick exposure was brought to a teaching hospital in Hyderabad with poor appetite, dark urine, bloody vomit and diarrhoea. It had relapsing fever, pallor, marked dehydration, enlarged lymph nodes, tachycardia and fast breathing. Laboratory work showed raised creatinine and urea nitrogen, low total protein, albumin and glucose, raised ALT (SGPT), haemolytic anaemia and mild thrombocytopenia. Smears showed large paired pyriform intra-erythrocytic parasites, and PCR identified Babesia gibsoni. At necropsy, bloody froth lined the trachea, lungs were emphysematous and haemorrhagic, and there was hydropericardium, renal congestion with petechiae, hepatic congestion, splenomegaly and haemorrhagic nodes. Histology showed pulmonary oedema and emphysema, focal renal tubular necrosis with interstitial haemorrhage, nodal fibrosis, and splenic lymphoid depletion.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Not stated in the abstract; the title frames necropsy and histology as supporting a pathological diagnosis of babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents a fatal multisystem phenotype in B. gibsoni infection combining haemolysis, azotaemia with tubular injury, hypoglycaemia and hepatocellular enzyme elevation, and pulmonary oedema/haemorrhage. Co-occurrence in one animal cannot assign mechanism; the lung lesions could reflect inflammatory permeability injury, hypoxia or agonal change, and renal tubular necrosis could reflect haemoglobin, hypoperfusion or both. Useful as a lesion catalogue, not as evidence of causation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 1; treatment, time to death and terminal events not described in the abstract; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) and other causes of azotaemia or pulmonary change not reported as excluded; PCR method unspecified; full text not accessible, so methods and discussion could not be assessed.
