## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared peripheral whole-blood transcriptomes of Malawian children with cerebral malaria and malarial retinopathy (Ret+CM, a marker of cerebral sequestration of infected red cells) against children with cerebral malaria but no retinopathy (Ret-CM). Ret+CM showed upregulation of 103 gene-set pathways, including cytokine, coagulation and extracellular-matrix pathways, and neutrophil transcripts led the individual genes. Plasma elastase, myeloperoxidase and proteinase 3 were raised, lactoferrin and lipocalin were not, and neutrophil chemotaxis was impaired, possibly through plasma haem. Neutrophils were scarce in autopsy brain vessels, with no extracellular traps, pointing to soluble products. Ret-CM showed less inflammation, more interferon-alpha and Toll-like receptor signalling.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** human cohort comparing two severe phenotypes; no uncomplicated-malaria or healthy comparison in the abstract.
- **Authors' conclusion:** Activated neutrophils are newly linked to cerebral sequestration; their soluble products may alter endothelium and coagulation.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** INF neutrophil pathway rated mechanistically plausible. Canine: CB050 (peroxidase index higher in non-survivors), CB073, CB082, CB054, CB111; no canine elastase, MPO protein, degranulation or NET measure. The interferon axis is speculative (CB109, CB082, CB075, CB008). Differences: signal marks sequestration vasculopathy, not death; sequestration is P. falciparum-specific; canine MPXI is an analyser readout.
- **Key limitations:** abstract omits n; cross-sectional; severe-versus-severe design; autopsy data scarce.
