## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers screened 150 dogs in Mashhad, north-eastern Iran (47 owned, 66 stray, 37 shelter) for Babesia by microscopy and PCR, and compared clinical, haematological and biochemical findings between infected and uninfected animals. PCR identified infection in 23 dogs (15.3%) whereas microscopy found only 3 (2%); sequencing assigned the two large-form cases to B. vogeli. Housing status and age, but not sex, were associated with infection. Infected dogs had significantly lower haematocrit and red cell counts. All three clinically infected dogs showed severe non-regenerative anaemia, and one B. vogeli-infected dog additionally had pancytopenia, azotaemia and several metabolic and electrolyte abnormalities, including hypoglycaemia and hyponatraemia. The authors highlighted that many infections were asymptomatic and that shelters had high prevalence.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (clinicopathological profile; associational for Hct/RBC)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis is more prevalent than expected in northeast Iran, often asymptomatic, with carriers and shelters warranting control attention.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Offers a clinicopathological snapshot of Babesia-associated disease at the mild end (lower Hct/RBC in infected dogs; species of most infections unreported) and a single severe multisystem case (pancytopenia, azotaemia, hypoglycaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia) consistent with a severe-babesiosis phenotype. Non-regenerative anaemia in all three clinical cases is notable but cannot be attributed to a mechanism (e.g. marrow suppression versus chronic disease or co-infection). The multisystem findings come from one dog and are anecdotal.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very few clinical cases (n = 3, multisystem data n = 1); co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) not stated as excluded; species of most PCR-positive dogs unreported in abstract; single time point; diverse population with differing housing and age; no outcome data.
