## Abstract-derived summary

A dog from Estonia that had earlier had its spleen removed was presented with a sudden loss of appetite and altered urine colour. Its condition deteriorated sharply within a single day despite supportive care. Blood smears showed high numbers of protozoan hemoparasites, together with severe thrombocytopenia, and the haematocrit fell from 46 to 33 percent. The dog was euthanized before any specific antibabesial treatment could begin. Molecular tests of blood from this dog and from two housemates were positive for Babesia. Partial 18S rRNA gene sequences identified the parasite as Babesia canis canis. The authors conclude that dogs without a spleen may develop severe, rapidly progressing babesiosis, a risk that deserves awareness.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs without a spleen may develop severe, rapidly progressing babesiosis, a risk that merits awareness; the full text also advises treating splenectomized dogs with suspected babesiosis as emergencies (full text).
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case documents a very fast clinical decline with high parasitaemia and severe thrombocytopenia in an asplenic dog, which fits a protective role of the spleen. A single animal without a comparison group cannot show that the missing spleen was responsible for the course. The two PCR-positive housemates had few or no parasites on smear (full text), but their spleen status is not reported, so they cannot serve as comparators.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; no necropsy or histopathology; no comparison with spleen-intact dogs; the dog was euthanized before specific treatment, so the natural course is uncertain; limited laboratory panel; the mode and place of infection are uncertain.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
