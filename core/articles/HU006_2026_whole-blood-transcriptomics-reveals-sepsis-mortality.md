## Abstract-derived summary
Investigators followed 161 critically ill adults with sepsis and took blood for RNA profiling by microarray within a day of intensive care admission. Gene activity in patients who died within 30 days was contrasted with survivors, correcting for age, sex and neutrophil numbers. Thirty-day mortality was 37% (60 of 161). Some 1106 genes differed in non-survivors, among them neutrophil granule genes (CEACAM8, ELANE, PRTN3, MPO, CEACAM6, DEFA4, MS4A3) running above 1.8-fold higher even after the neutrophil-count correction. Pathway analysis flagged neutrophil degranulation in this cohort and in a separate 479-patient cohort, and in four of six co-expression modules tied to death. The authors suggest it may be a modifiable target.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** primary human prospective cohort; transcript-level data only (no protein or functional neutrophil assay in the abstract).
- **Authors' conclusion:** neutrophil degranulation genes are involved in sepsis mortality and merit study as a modifiable target.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** shared pathway is neutrophil activation linked to death. CB050 (B. rossi, 140 infected vs 20 controls) found a higher neutrophil peroxidase index in infected dogs and highest in non-survivors; CB073 (B. canis, n=54) tied leukopenia to SIRS; CB082 (experimental B. rossi) showed innate pathway activation by whole-blood RNA-seq. Rated **mechanistically plausible**: no canine or human babesiosis study measured granule proteins, degranulation transcripts or NETs. Differences: readouts are non-equivalent (analyser index vs transcripts), Giannini adjusted for neutrophil count whereas CB050 did not, and ICU sepsis involves organ support and mostly bacterial infection.
- **Key limitations:** observational association; whole-blood mixture; not a parasitic disease; validation cohort used a different mortality window (28 days).
