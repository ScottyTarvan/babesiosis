## Abstract-derived summary

This case report describes what the authors present as the first cerebral canine babesiosis case in Poland. A five-year-old American Staffordshire Terrier showed acute babesiosis with an unusual presentation, combining neurological signs and pancytopenia, and died despite treatment. Piroplasms were seen in red blood cells on Giemsa-stained blood smears. After death, the brain was examined by histopathology and PCR, and a segment of the parasite 18S rRNA gene was sequenced. The sequence matched genotype 18S RNA-B (EU622793), one of two B. canis strains occurring in Poland, which the authors describe as more virulent than the 18S RNA-A strain (EU622792). They suggest that cerebral babesiosis should be considered in dogs with neurological signs, particularly during the tick season.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cerebral babesiosis should be part of the differential diagnosis in dogs with neurological signs, especially in the tick activity season. The 18S RNA-B strain found is described as more virulent than the 18S RNA-A strain.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The report documents a distinct cerebral syndrome, with parasite DNA and brain histopathology, which is relevant to the neurological category. The greater virulence of the 18S RNA-B strain is stated in the abstract, which gives no comparative data, and a single dog cannot show that this strain caused the cerebral disease. The title calls the case presumptive.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; no controls; abstract-level information only; genotype-virulence link not tested in this report; treatment details and histopathological findings not available in the abstract
