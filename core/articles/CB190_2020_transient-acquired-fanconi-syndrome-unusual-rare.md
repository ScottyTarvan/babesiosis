## Abstract-derived summary

Two dogs with marked polyuria, polydipsia and lethargy were found to have glucosuria despite normal blood glucose, together with heavy urinary amino acid loss, consistent with Fanconi syndrome (proximal tubular reabsorption failure). One dog was infected with Babesia gibsoni; the other had eaten cosmetics. The Babesia case received antiparasitic therapy, while the second dog received intensive correction of acidosis, electrolyte disturbance and other deficits. In both, the tubular defect resolved once the suspected underlying cause was managed. The authors state these are the first reports linking either Babesia infection or cosmetics ingestion to acquired Fanconi syndrome in dogs, and suggest considering both as possible causes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesia infection and cosmetics ingestion should be considered possible causes of transient acquired Fanconi syndrome in dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A hypothesis-generating report of proximal tubular dysfunction (incomplete type, no proteinuria) temporally associated with B. gibsoni and resolving with anti-Babesia therapy. The authors speculate about immune-complex tubular injury, but nothing tests it. Causality is not established.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** one Babesia dog; no renal histology (owner declined); prior treatment at another hospital not detailed; other tick-borne co-infections not reported (leptospirosis serology negative); recovery coincided with atovaquone/azithromycin and supportive care, so confounded; initial Babesia diagnostic method not reported; mild chronic kidney changes on ultrasound.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
