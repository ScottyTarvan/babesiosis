## Abstract-derived summary

Investigators sampled 157 blood samples from dogs in Egypt to characterise circulating Babesia and Ehrlichia, relate infection to risk factors, and describe haematological and biochemical change. Sequencing and phylogenetics identified Babesia canis subsp. vogeli in 17.83% and Ehrlichia canis in 12.73% of samples, both exceeding microscopy detection; partial sequences were deposited in GenBank. Blood transcript levels of TNF-α and IFN-γ, measured by qPCR, were significantly higher in infected than in healthy dogs. Stray status and tick infestation were the strongest risk associations. The commonest laboratory abnormalities were haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, lymphocytosis and hyperbilirubinaemia. The authors conclude these two agents predominate locally and recommend control measures, particularly for stray dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. vogeli and E. canis are the most common circulating agents, accompanied by IFN-γ and TNF-α over-expression, haemolytic anaemia and ectoparasite infestation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mainly an epidemiological and descriptive study; the pathophysiological value is the cytokine-transcript signal, consistent with a Th1-type inflammatory response, plus a standard haemolysis, thrombocytopenia and icterus profile. B. vogeli is generally a lower-virulence species, so findings may not extend to severe B. rossi or B. canis disease. Cytokine up-regulation is an association and does not show that it drives the anaemia or thrombocytopenia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Co-infection with E. canis (and possibly mixed infections) confounds both the haematology and the cytokine data; number and selection of controls not reported in the abstract; mRNA rather than protein measured; cross-sectional single sampling; stray and owned dogs may differ in other infections and nutrition; no outcome or severity data; effect sizes not given in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
