## Abstract-derived summary

Babesiosis normally causes non-immune haemolytic anaemia that settles once antimicrobials clear parasitaemia. After noticing occasional warm-antibody autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (WAHA) following treatment, the authors reviewed every babesiosis patient seen at their centre from January 2009 to June 2016, tracking haematological complications for three months. Of 86 patients, 18 were asplenic. Six developed WAHA two to four weeks after diagnosis, when all had responded clinically and biochemically and had cleared B. microti parasitaemia. All six were asplenic (P<0.001), had direct antiglobulin tests positive for IgG and complement C3, and carried warm autoantibodies; no other cause of haemolysis emerged. Four required immunosuppression. None had prior autoimmunity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** retrospective single-centre human cohort; B. microti.
- **Authors' conclusion:** WAHA can follow babesiosis in people without prior autoimmune disease, and asplenic patients seem especially vulnerable.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Category ERY (antibody-driven red cell destruction on top of parasite haemolysis); rated mechanistically plausible. Canine data: Coombs-positive haemolysis (CB096, n=1), IMHA/Evans syndrome responding to immunosuppression (CB047, n=1), Babesia as a co-factor in IMHA cohorts (CB153, CB067), severe anaemia after splenectomy (CB021, CB132) and a review favouring host-immune anaemia (CB107). Differences: post-clearance timing, C3 coating and autoantibody specificity are undemonstrated in dogs; canine evidence is mostly single cases; autoantibody versus parasite-antigen adsorption is unresolved in both hosts.
- **Key limitations:** only 6 events; retrospective, single centre; asplenic subgroup small; confounding not excluded.
