## Abstract-derived summary

Venous blood from five healthy dogs and five dogs infected with B. canis was processed without first removing parasitised cells. Erythrocytes were washed and lysed, the haem group was stripped off, and the globin chains were denatured by disrupting their disulphide bonds. The lysates were then run on polyacrylamide gels containing acid, urea and Triton X-100. All five infected dogs showed an extra protein band running above the beta globin chain, and none of the five healthy dogs did. The authors suggest the band is a globin fragment left behind after proteolysis by the parasite, but note that further work is needed to rule out a band that originates from the parasite itself.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** Small comparative laboratory study of haemoglobin chain separation (qualitative gel finding).
- **Relevance:** Cited under Q1 (haemolysis, free haem, endothelial activation and tubular injury) as the only canine globin-level observation retrieved by the haptoglobin/haem prior-work search (search 1). It hints that the haemoglobin molecule is altered in canine babesiosis, but it did not measure cell-free haemoglobin, haem scavengers (haptoglobin, haemopexin), endothelial markers or kidney injury markers, so it does not close the gap Q1 describes.
- **Key limitations:** Only 5 dogs per group; qualitative band detection with no quantification; band not identified, and parasite origin not excluded because infected cells were left in the samples; no clinical severity, outcome or renal data; species identification and diagnostic method not described in the abstract; 1995 methods and species nomenclature.
