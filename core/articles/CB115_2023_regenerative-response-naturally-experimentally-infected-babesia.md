## Abstract-derived summary

Retrospective analysis of ADVIA 2120 haematology records compared admission ARC values and reticulocyte indices across 103 naturally infected B. rossi dogs, 10 dogs with immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) and 14 healthy controls; five experimentally infected dogs were also followed. Haematocrit was similarly low in the B. rossi and IMHA groups relative to controls (p < .001). ARC exceeded control values in both disease groups (p = .006 and .019) but was lower in babesiosis than in IMHA (p = .041). In experimental dogs, ARC dropped abruptly about 48 h after parasitaemia was detected and recovered after treatment, while reticulocytes shrank as haemoglobin concentration fell. Natural cases had larger reticulocytes with greater volume variation. The authors judge the response inadequate for the anaemia severity.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The regenerative response in natural B. rossi infection is inadequate for the degree of anaemia, possibly from direct parasite or host-response suppression of the bone marrow.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Adds a marrow-response dimension to B. rossi anaemia: ARC is raised against controls yet judged inadequate for the degree of anaemia, and the early ARC fall after parasitaemia in experimental dogs is temporally compatible with suppression. The mechanism (parasite vs cytokine vs other) is not established, and a time-course in only five dogs cannot separate it.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective data; small IMHA group (n = 10) and experimental group; admission-only sampling in natural cases; timing of presentation within disease course and treatment effects not reported in abstract; species confirmation method not stated.
