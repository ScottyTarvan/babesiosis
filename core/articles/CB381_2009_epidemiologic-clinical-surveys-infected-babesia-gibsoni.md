## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers in South Korea examined 117 dogs submitted with a clinical suspicion of Babesia gibsoni infection and confirmed infection by PCR and 18S rRNA gene sequencing in 29 of them. The breeds of infected dogs varied, although pit bull terriers were most often affected. Infection was commoner in male dogs, in dogs under three years of age and in densely populated regions; only six of the 29 had a recorded history of tick exposure. Physical examination findings fitted canine babesiosis and often suggested anaemia. Haematology showed severe haemolytic anaemia together with thrombocytopenia, while blood smears showed very low parasitaemia. The local gene sequences closely matched B. gibsoni isolates reported from Spain, Japan and the United States.

Severe haemolytic anaemia and thrombocytopenia were observed together with very low parasitaemia on smears. The risk-factor findings (sex, age, region, breed) describe who was submitted and infected and do not show cause.
