## Abstract-derived summary

Seven dogs aged 3-4 years (five male, two female) with anorexia, lethargy, weakness, tick burden and haemoglobinuria were seen at a veterinary college hospital and a private clinic in Chhattisgarh, India. Blood smears showed pyriform forms taken as B. canis and oval or signet-ring forms taken as B. gibsoni. Against healthy controls, affected dogs had significantly lower haemoglobin, PCV, red cell count and platelets, and significantly higher BUN, creatinine, ALT and globulin, with lower total protein and albumin. One deep intramuscular imidocarb dose (5 mg/kg) with supportive care was followed by values within normal limits at day 15 and negative smears 48 hours after dosing. The authors treat these as evidence of a successful therapeutic response.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Anaemia and thrombocytopenia are the most significant abnormalities, and single-dose imidocarb with supportive therapy was effective.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Clinicopathological profile consistent with haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia and raised ALT (possible renal and hepatic involvement or hypoxic injury). It cannot separate mechanisms, and the treatment claim is not testable without an untreated comparator.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 7; control n, age and source unstated; microscopy-only species call with no per-dog assignment and no co-infection testing; no severity or outcome stratification; dispersion measure not defined; treatment confounded by supportive care; journal peer-review rigour could not be verified (no article-level statement; accepted about one month before online release).
