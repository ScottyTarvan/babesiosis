## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared two treatment regimens in eight mixed-breed dogs naturally infected with Babesia gibsoni. Four dogs received a combination of atovaquone/proguanil and azithromycin, and four comparison dogs received diminazene aceturate with tetracycline and clindamycin. Before treatment, all eight dogs showed anaemia of mild to severe degree together with a low platelet count. Parasites could no longer be seen on blood smears once treatment had started. Blood PCR stayed negative in the group given the new combination throughout follow-up, whereas more than one comparison dog tested PCR-positive at later checks. The authors conclude that the combination of atovaquone/proguanil and azithromycin can lower parasitaemia and could become a new protocol for treating B. gibsoni infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors propose the combination as a new protocol for treating B. gibsoni infection in dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is mainly a treatment-efficacy study with parasite clearance as its endpoint. Its host-side content is limited to clinical signs and haematocrit and platelet counts, which show anaemia and thrombocytopenia at presentation and improvement after treatment in both arms. These data fit the erythrocyte and platelet categories descriptively and say little about mechanism. Differences in PCR results between arms are associated with the regimen but rest on four dogs per arm.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 8 dogs, 4 per arm; no healthy controls; the comparator is a multi-drug regimen, so individual drug effects cannot be separated; abstract uses "significantly" but no statistical test is described in the text read; follow-up 30 days; liver and kidney findings reported as normal but "data not shown"; recruitment period and setting not stated; the Discussion says treated dogs were PCR-negative from day 3, whereas the Results report treated-dog PCR results only for days 10, 20 and 30.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - The dogs were mixed-breed puppies younger than 1 year, vaccinated and dewormed, with no earlier antiprotozoal treatment; all had anorexia, anaemia, pale mucous membranes, depression or haemoglobinuria (full text)
  - The 8 dogs were randomly split into two groups of 4. The treated group received atovaquone 15 mg/kg with proguanil 6 mg/kg every 8 hours plus azithromycin 10 mg/kg once daily, all for 10 days. The comparison group received diminazene aceturate 5 mg/kg intramuscularly once a week for 3 weeks plus tetracycline 5 mg/kg and clindamycin 10 mg/kg every 12 hours for 14 days (full text)
  - Blood was sampled before treatment and on days 3, 10, 20 and 30, with a complete blood count, Giemsa smear parasitaemia per 1,000 erythrocytes and 18S rRNA PCR; PstI RFLP showed B. gibsoni only, without Hepatozoon canis (full text)
  - Parasites were no longer seen on smears by day 10 in the treated group and by day 20 in the comparison group (full text)
  - Clinical signs including anaemia, pale mucous membranes and haemoglobinuria resolved from day 10 in all dogs (full text)
  - Haematocrit and platelet counts rose after treatment in both groups; one comparison dog (no. 7) showed a renewed fall in both at day 20 (full text)
  - In the comparison group PCR was positive for dogs 6 and 8 at day 10, dogs 5 and 7 at day 20 and dog 7 at day 30 (full text)
  - No adverse effects were observed, and serum tests showed no liver or kidney abnormality over 30 days, but these data are not shown (full text)
  - Animal ethics approval number 2014-0008 (Jeju National University); funded by a 2015 Jeju National University research grant (full text)
  - Table 1 (per-dog age, sex, haematocrit, platelets, parasitaemia) was garbled in the PDF conversion, so per-dog baseline values are not quoted here (full text)
  - The Conclusion section calls the combination effective and stable, says it acted faster early in treatment than the diminazene-based regimen, and says the mechanism and each drug's contribution need further study (full text)
