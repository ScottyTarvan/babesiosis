## Abstract-derived summary

Dogs with naturally acquired babesiosis were studied at the small animal clinics of a teaching veterinary hospital in the Cauvery delta region, Tamil Nadu; the abstract reports 50 cases. Clinical history, physical findings and blood results were recorded, with blood drawn from the cephalic vein. Cases were identified from clinical signs and Giemsa-stained thin smears, then PCR-confirmed. Affected dogs had significantly reduced haemoglobin, erythrocyte count and packed cell volume, together with notable rises in serum bilirubin, ALT, ALP, blood urea nitrogen and creatinine. The authors present these changes as indicating the severity of disease and as informing pathophysiology, diagnosis and prognosis. The abstract gives no numerical results, effect sizes, control group description or outcome data.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Haemato-biochemical alterations are useful for diagnosis and prognosis and for understanding the pathophysiology of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Consistent with haemolytic anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia and azotaemia/hepatocellular enzyme release in B. gibsoni infection, but it is a clinicopathological profile only. Mechanisms (renal hypoxia, hepatocellular damage) are asserted from literature, partly cattle studies, not measured. No outcome data support the prognostic claim, and thrombocytopenia/coagulation was not assessed, so COA is dropped as a tag.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Abstract states 50 dogs but only 10 PCR-confirmed cases analysed (n=10 vs 10); controls not mentioned in the abstract; Table 2 values partly unlabelled; SD vs SE inconsistency; no platelets, glucose or acid-base data reported; albumin difference not marked significant in table yet called significant in text; no survival data.
