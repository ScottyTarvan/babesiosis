## Abstract-derived summary

The authors compared complete blood counts from 13 dogs with suspected Babesia canis infection, with parasitaemia confirmed on blood smear, seen at a Dnipro clinic between February and April 2024, with healthy control dogs, using an automated analyser. Infected dogs had markedly lower red cell count (3.59 vs 6.36 ×10^6/µL), haemoglobin (83.4 vs 158.6 g/L) and haematocrit (22.5 vs 43.5 %; P<0.0001). Thrombocytopenia (mean 38.2 ×10^3/µL), leukopenia (7.08 ×10^9/L), a shift toward neutrophils and lymphopenia were also found. Mean cell volume was reduced (63.5 fL), whereas MCHC, RDW and total protein did not differ. The authors suggest red cell, haemoglobin, haematocrit and platelet changes could act as early diagnostic markers.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Red cell count, haemoglobin, haematocrit and platelet changes may serve as early diagnostic markers of canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Documents the expected haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and leukopenia/lymphopenia pattern in smear-positive dogs, compatible with erythrocyte injury and a consumptive or sequestration-type platelet process, but not distinguishing between mechanisms. "Early stage" is not defined in the abstract, and a naturally infected dog's infection age is unknown, so "early markers" is an inference; no sensitivity/specificity or severity/outcome analysis is reported. The reduced MCV is unusual for acute regenerative haemolysis and is not explained in the abstract (analyser or other causes possible).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n=13; control number, age/breed matching and selection not reported; species not molecularly confirmed and co-infections not shown to be excluded; single time point; point-of-care analyser; no data availability statement on journal page.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
