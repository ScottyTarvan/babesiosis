## Abstract-derived summary

The authors weakened the Oita isolate of Babesia gibsoni by repeated in vitro culture with the microaerophilus stationary phase technique, reaching 400 serial passages. Two dogs given the passaged isolate intravenously at 10^9 showed an attenuated response and made specific antibodies, with an IFAT titre of 1:20,480. The authors read this as reduced virulence with antigenicity retained. Those two dogs and two naive dogs were then challenged intravenously with 2x10^8 erythrocytes infected with the virulent Oita isolate. The previously exposed dogs had lower parasitaemia that fell quickly to undetectable levels, with a small PCV dip that recovered. The naive dogs developed typical acute disease, including severe anaemia and fever. The authors proposed the attenuated isolate as a live vaccine candidate.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serial in vitro passage reduced the virulence of B. gibsoni while keeping its antigenicity. Prior exposure to the attenuated isolate was followed by milder disease on challenge, so the isolate is a candidate for a live vaccine.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a host-response experiment on virulence and acquired protection, relevant to the host-parasite interaction category. Prior exposure was associated with lower parasitaemia and a smaller PCV change after challenge than in the naive dogs. With two dogs per group and no reported statistics, the findings are descriptive, and the contribution to pathophysiology lies mainly in the contrast in anaemia and fever between groups.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** two dogs per group; no statistical analysis reported in the abstract; PCV, parasitaemia and temperature values not given in the abstract; single isolate; focus on vaccine potential rather than mechanisms of tissue injury; no uninfected control group described; full text not read.
