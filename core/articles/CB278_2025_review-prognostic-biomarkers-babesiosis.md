## Abstract-derived summary

This review presents canine babesiosis as a tick-borne haemoprotozoal disease in which anaemia is the dominant syndrome. In complicated disease, the authors attribute multi-organ failure and coagulopathy to leukocyte activation, vascular endothelial injury and mediator release. They propose that markers of these processes can indicate severity and outcome in dogs with multiple organ dysfunction. The abstract also addresses treatment, which it describes as difficult: no drug has been shown to clear Asian-genotype B. gibsoni, but a clindamycin, metronidazole and doxycycline combination is described as promising. The full text additionally covers organ-by-organ pathogenesis, haemato-biochemical, oxidative-stress and coagulation parameters, ultrasonography, diagnosis and therapy.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors conclude that biomarkers of leukocyte activation, endothelial injury and mediator release can indicate severity and outcome in babesial multiple organ dysfunction.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a B. gibsoni-weighted map of proposed mechanisms (haemolysis, immune-mediated anaemia, SIRS, sludging and ischaemia, consumptive coagulopathy), but the "prognostic" claim is largely carried by cited associations, not by pooled prognostic data. Treatment claims depend heavily on the authors' own earlier study (Narayani et al., 2021).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** No stated search strategy or inclusion criteria; narrative, not systematic. Peer-review policy not stated on the journal page. Apparent factual slips (e.g. Babes' report dated 1988, B. annae described as a B. gibsoni subspecies, triple therapy labelled the Marshall Protocol). Several large sections concern diagnosis and treatment, not prognostic markers. Publication-date inconsistencies noted above.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
