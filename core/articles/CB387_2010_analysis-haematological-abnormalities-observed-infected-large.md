## Abstract-derived summary

This analysis examined 350 haematology results from dogs whose blood smears, stained by the May-Grunwald-Giemsa method, were positive for Babesia. The authors set out to describe the blood abnormalities of canine babesiosis and to compare them by age and breed. Red cell, white cell and platelet values came from an automated analyser, and the parameters that were abnormal were then compared statistically across age and breed groups. Low platelet counts were the dominant abnormality. Anaemia and reduced neutrophil and lymphocyte counts were less marked, and a fall in two or three cell lines at once was recorded less often. Packed cell volume differed between young dogs and both adult and old dogs, and white cell counts differed between German shepherds and mongrels.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Thrombocytopenia is the most significant haematological abnormality in canine babesiosis, with anaemia, neutropenia and lymphopenia less severe, and bi- or pancytopenia seen less often.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study is a clinicopathological profile of smear-positive dogs and adds descriptive evidence on the cytopenias that accompany infection. The age and breed differences are associations only. Without controls, a comparison with healthy dogs is not possible, and the findings do not show why the breed or age groups differ.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; no healthy control group mentioned; diagnosis by blood smear only with no species identification; 350 results rather than a stated number of dogs; setting and sampling period not stated; severity and outcome not reported.
