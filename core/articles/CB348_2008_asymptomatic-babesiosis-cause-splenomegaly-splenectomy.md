## Abstract-derived summary

This case report from Poland describes a two-year-old dog that needed a splenectomy after its spleen ruptured while the animal was playing with other dogs. Ultrasonography and surgery both showed an enlarged spleen, which the authors regarded as the probable reason for the rupture. Clinical signs of babesiosis, including haemoglobinuria and fever, together with typical haematological and biochemical alterations, appeared only after the operation. Because the signs followed the surgery so quickly, and splenomegaly had been present beforehand, the authors proposed that an unrecognised, symptom-free Babesia canis infection had indirectly contributed to the splenic rupture. The report is set against the background that B. canis is among the most serious canine parasitic diseases in Poland, and that acute disease usually presents with fever, lethargy, anorexia, haemoglobinuria and vomiting.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The short interval between surgery and the appearance of babesiosis signs, together with the splenomegaly, suggested that an asymptomatic B. canis infection was an indirect cause of the splenic rupture.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case describes a splenic complication (splenomegaly with rupture) in a dog whose babesiosis became clinical only after splenectomy. This is consistent with an association between chronic, unrecognised infection and splenic enlargement, and with a relapse or flare of clinical disease after loss of the spleen. A single case cannot show that the infection caused the splenomegaly or the rupture.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single animal; presumed rather than documented chronic infection before surgery; no molecular species identification seen in the summary; no abstract numbers for laboratory findings; full text not read
