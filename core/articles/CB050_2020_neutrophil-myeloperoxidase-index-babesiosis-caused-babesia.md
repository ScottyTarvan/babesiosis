## Abstract-derived summary

In B. rossi babesiosis, neutrophils are a key innate effector, and the myeloperoxidase index (MPXI) from the ADVIA analyser estimates cellular MPO content. This retrospective study used data from 140 naturally infected dogs and 20 healthy controls to compare MPXI between infected dogs and controls, between non-survivors, survivors and controls, and to correlate MPXI with band and segmented neutrophil counts and a canine multiplex panel of seven cytokines/chemokines. Mortality was 11% (15/140). MPXI was higher in infected dogs overall (P = 0.033) and in non-survivors (P = 0.011) than in controls. Across all infected dogs MPXI correlated positively with IL-10 (r = 0.211) and negatively with IL-8 (r = -0.350). In non-survivors it correlated positively with IL-2, IL-6, IL-18, GM-CSF and MCP-1 (r = 0.613 to 0.713). The authors suggest MPXI merits study as an inflammation and prognostic marker.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (prognostic hypothesis-generating)
- **Authors' conclusion:** MPXI tracked pro-inflammatory cytokines in dogs that died, and its value as a marker of inflammation and prognosis warrants further investigation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Shows higher neutrophil MPO content in infected dogs and non-survivors versus controls, consistent with neutrophil activation or granulopoiesis in the systemic inflammatory response (OXO relevance via MPO-derived oxidants is inferential). The abstract reports no direct survivor versus non-survivor difference, so prognostic discrimination is not shown. Correlations are modest overall and do not establish causality.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** retrospective, single time point, small non-survivor subgroup (n = 15) for within-group correlations, multiple correlations without reported adjustment in the abstract, human reference algorithm on the analyser, cohorts combined across years.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
