## Abstract-derived summary

Review of work on oxidative stress induced by piroplasms (Babesia, Theileria, Cytauxzoon) in domestic animals. The authors argue that erythrocytes, with their high oxygen and haemoglobin content, are early targets of oxidant injury: haemoglobin oxidation and membrane lipid peroxidation are said to destabilise and deform the cells, promoting haemolysis and anaemia. Surveying published studies, they conclude that infected hosts experience oxidative stress and that the resulting injury contributes substantially to pathogenicity. They call for work clarifying the specific role of oxidative stress, more sensitive biomarkers, and testing of antioxidant therapy. The abstract gives no species-specific or numerical findings, and does not state how many studies were reviewed or whether dogs were covered.
