## Abstract-derived summary

Canine babesiosis is a significant tick-borne illness that mainly affects red blood cells but can also have effects across several body systems. The study aimed to identify candidate metabolites and pathways in naturally infected dogs. The authors profiled the serum metabolome of 12 dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis against 12 healthy dogs, using three platforms: untargeted UHPLC-MS/MS, targeted UHPLC-MS/MS and GC-MS. Together these detected 295 metabolites (64 untargeted, 205 targeted, 26 by GC-MS). Differentially abundant metabolites mapped to glutathione metabolism, alanine/aspartate/glutamate metabolism, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, and aromatic amino acid biosynthesis. The authors conclude that host-pathogen interactions can be studied by metabolomics and that infected and healthy dogs are distinguishable by LC-MS and GC-MS, offering new insight into the pathophysiology of B. canis infection.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum metabolomics distinguishes infected from healthy dogs and implicates several metabolic pathways in B. canis pathophysiology.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A hypothesis-generating host-response omics study. Pathway enrichment (notably glutathione and sulphur amino acid metabolism) is compatible with oxidative stress but does not show it; the discussion linking changes to mechanisms such as platelet function is speculative and untested. Observed differences may reflect infection and its sequelae (such as anaemia and fever); this case-control comparison cannot separate parasite-specific mechanisms from these host responses.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n = 12 per group; severity not graded; samples taken before imidocarb but recruitment period, co-infection screening and control matching not stated; age ranges differ between groups; no validation cohort; no dedicated limitations section.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
