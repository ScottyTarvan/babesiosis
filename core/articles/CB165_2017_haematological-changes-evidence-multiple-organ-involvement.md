## Abstract-derived summary

The authors describe blood findings and organ damage in Nigerian dogs with natural babesiosis. Seventeen smear-positive dogs and 17 apparently healthy large-breed dogs were enrolled at a university teaching hospital; infected dogs were split into uncomplicated and complicated groups by clinical presentation, and blood counts and red cell morphology were assessed. Five dogs that died underwent histopathology of brain, heart, lung, kidney, spleen and liver. Healthy dogs had lower neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios than infected dogs, and complicated cases had higher ratios. Anisocytosis was the most frequent red cell abnormality. Every necropsied dog had multi-organ lesions: liver 5/5, lung 3/5, kidney 2/5, heart 2/5, spleen 2/5, brain 1/5. The authors propose the ratio as an index of complications and multi-organ dysfunction as central to pathophysiology.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (haematology associational)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio is a good diagnostic index for complicated babesiosis, and multiple organ dysfunction is a major feature of the disease in Nigerian dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a multisystem, inflammation-associated severe phenotype, with hepatic, pulmonary, renal and cardiac involvement at necropsy. It does not identify mechanisms, lesion types are not described in the abstract, and no accuracy statistics (e.g. AUC, cut-off) are reported to justify "good diagnostic index". The ratio is a severity-associated marker, not evidence of causation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small n; histopathology in only 5 fatal cases (selection toward terminal disease, treatment effects unknown); species not confirmed; co-infections (e.g. Ehrlichia) not excluded in the abstract; controls not shown to be matched; group sizes and recruitment dates not reported; full text not read; journal peer-review practice not assessed.
