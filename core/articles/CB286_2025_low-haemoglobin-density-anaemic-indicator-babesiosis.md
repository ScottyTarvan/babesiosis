## Abstract-derived summary

The authors evaluated low haemoglobin density (LHD%), an index related to functional iron availability and haemoglobin content, as a marker of anaemia in canine babesiosis. They enrolled 108 symptomatic dogs, screened by PCR, and measured blood indices, serum iron, TIBC, UIBC and LHD. Dogs were divided into anaemic and non-anaemic groups using haemoglobin, PCV and red cell count. LHD% was higher in anaemic dogs (P<0.001) and correlated inversely with haemoglobin, PCV, red cell count, MCHC and serum iron. ROC analysis at a cut-off of 1.83% gave 100% specificity and 80.43% sensitivity for functional iron-deficient anaemia. They conclude LHD is an inexpensive indicator of this anaemia type.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** LHD is a cost-effective parameter for identifying functional iron-deficient anaemia in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A haematological descriptor of hypochromia in anaemic infected dogs, not a mechanism. LHD is a sigmoid transformation of MCHC, so its correlation with Hb, PCV and MCHC is partly built in. The ROC reference standard was anaemia status, not an independent iron-deficiency test, so "functional iron deficiency" is inferred. Low serum iron in acute haemolytic or inflammatory disease may reflect hypoferraemia of inflammation or regenerative hypochromia rather than iron deficiency; this was not tested.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no uninfected controls; species and PCR target not reported; single time point; no ferritin, hepcidin or reticulocyte data in this study; recruitment period and ethics not stated; journal peer-review policy not stated on pages checked.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
