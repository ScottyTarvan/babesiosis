## Abstract-derived summary

Acute lung injury and ARDS rank among the deadliest complications of complicated B. rossi infection in dogs, and most fatalities occur within a day of presentation, yet the associated lung pathology had never been characterised. The authors examined lungs from dogs that died of natural infection, combining gross examination, routine histology and immunohistochemistry, and compared immunolabelling against control dogs. Alveolar oedema accompanied every death. Histology indicated acute interstitial pneumonia with haemorrhage and an influx of mononuclear cells into alveolar walls and airspaces; fibrin aggregates lay within alveoli in slightly more than half of cases. Labelling for MAC387 and CD204 (monocyte-macrophages) and CD3 (T cells) was increased relative to controls. The pattern resembles, but only partly matches, the exudative phase of diffuse alveolar damage described in ALI/ARDS.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The lesions overlap incompletely with the exudative stage of diffuse alveolar damage typical of ALI/ARDS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Provides tissue-level support for a pulmonary syndrome in fatal B. rossi infection, with monocyte-macrophage and T-cell involvement and intra-alveolar fibrin pointing to inflammatory and coagulation-linked injury. It describes lesions at death; it does not establish mechanism, timing, or whether parasite-specific factors versus systemic host response drive the injury.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Sample size and control composition not given in abstract; fatal cases only (no survivors or time course); species confirmation, co-infections and treatment effects not described in abstract; single-centre generalisability unknown.
