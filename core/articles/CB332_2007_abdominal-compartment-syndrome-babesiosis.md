## Abstract-derived summary

A nine-year-old female Bull Terrier was presented with complicated babesiosis. During the illness she developed respiratory distress, a distended abdomen and reduced urine production. Measured intra-abdominal pressure was raised, at 23.5 to 25 cmH2O, and these findings led to an additional diagnosis of abdominal compartment syndrome. The report follows the clinical course of both conditions until resolution. The authors point out that this syndrome is poorly documented in veterinary medicine and that clinicians caring for critically ill patients should be alert to it as a possible complication. They also review the pathophysiology of the syndrome and the relevant veterinary literature. The report concerns a single animal and is descriptive only.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report abdominal compartment syndrome in a dog with complicated babesiosis and say that clinicians caring for critically ill patients should be aware of it as a potential complication.
