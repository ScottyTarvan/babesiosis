## Abstract-derived summary

Sixty dogs in Serbia that showed signs consistent with babesiosis were examined clinically and sampled for blood smear microscopy, a complete blood count, a canine SNAP 4Dx test and DNA analysis. The main clinical signs were apathy, anorexia, fever, dark urine, pale mucous membranes, jaundice, enlarged spleen and vomiting. Blood counts showed thrombocytopenia ranging from slight to severe and normocytic normochromic anaemia ranging from mild to very severe. Microscopy was positive in 58 samples, with large piroplasms in 57 and small ones in one. The SNAP test found no co-infections. PCR-RFLP identified B. canis in 58 dogs and B. gibsoni in two; sequencing of the B. gibsoni products and six randomly chosen B. canis products supported these identifications. All dogs received imidocarb twice, two weeks apart. The authors describe this as the first sequence-confirmed report of both species in Serbia.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study provides the first DNA-sequence-confirmed evidence of B. canis and B. gibsoni infection in sick dogs in Serbia, together with the clinical and haematological picture seen in these infections.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The haematological findings (thrombocytopenia and normocytic normochromic anaemia of variable severity) are reported in this natural-infection series. Their link to the pathophysiology categories is descriptive only. The abstract gives no per-species or severity-stratified results and no outcome data, so it cannot support mechanistic or prognostic inferences.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract-level information only; no healthy control group mentioned; numeric haematology values and outcomes not given in the abstract; only 2 B. gibsoni cases, so no species comparison is possible; selection of symptomatic dogs only; co-infection screening limited to the SNAP test.
