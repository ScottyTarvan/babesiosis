## Abstract-derived summary

Researchers examined 144 dogs brought to a teaching veterinary hospital in Mannuthy, Kerala, between April 2018 and October 2019, to document clinical and blood-count changes linked to blood-borne parasites and to compare microscopy with PCR for detection. Anorexia and lethargy were present in every dog. Anaemia and low platelet counts dominated the haematology, and were more pronounced in babesiosis than in the other infections. Microscopy found organisms in 33 dogs (22.9%), whereas PCR identified 113 babesiosis cases, 13 ehrlichiosis, 4 trypanosomosis and 31 mixed infections. PCR was therefore the more sensitive method, and the authors advocate combining clinical-haematological assessment, microscopy and PCR for routine laboratory work-up.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** PCR was more sensitive than microscopy, and combining clinico-haematological work-up, microscopy and PCR is proposed as routine practice.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Pathophysiological content is limited to a qualitative statement that anaemia and thrombocytopenia (erythrocyte destruction/consumption and platelet loss; mechanism not examined) were larger in babesiosis than in ehrlichiosis or trypanosomosis. Largely a diagnostic-comparison study; it does not resolve B. gibsoni versus B. vogeli phenotype, severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no haematological values in the abstract, no species-level split, no healthy controls reported, mixed infections (31) confound attribution, case counts (113 + 13 + 4 + 31 = 161) exceed 144 dogs so categories evidently overlap or are inconsistent, referral hospital selection, outcome and treatment not reported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
