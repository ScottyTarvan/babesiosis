## Abstract-derived summary

Activation of clotting by the host's inflammatory response can progress to consumptive coagulopathy, which is a recognised risk for a poor outcome. This prospective observational study tested whether such coagulopathy is related to death in dogs infected with Babesia rossi. Seventy-two client-owned dogs with PCR and reverse line blot confirmation were enrolled, and dogs co-infected with B. vogeli or Ehrlichia canis were left out. At admission, clotting factor, antithrombin and protein C activities, PT, aPTT, fibrinogen and D-dimer were measured. Thirteen dogs (18%) died. Non-survivors had lower activity of all coagulation factors and of protein C, longer PT and aPTT, and higher D-dimer; antithrombin did not differ significantly. The authors concluded that fatal cases showed a more severe consumptive coagulopathy with procoagulant activation, inhibitor consumption and increased fibrinolysis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs that died from B. rossi infection had a more severe consumptive coagulopathy than survivors, marked by procoagulant activation, inhibitor consumption and increased fibrinolytic activity.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Admission coagulation variables (factor activities, PT, aPTT, protein C and D-dimer) were associated with death in B. rossi infection, which fits a consumptive coagulopathy or DIC-like process in fatal cases. The design is observational with a single admission sample, so the findings show association rather than the sequence of events or a causal route to death. Antithrombin did not separate the groups, which may mean that the inhibitors were not consumed to the same degree; this study does not test that.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** association only; single admission sample; only 13 non-survivors; no inflammatory mediators reported in the abstract; co-infected dogs excluded, which limits generalisability; treatment and case-mix effects on survival not described in the abstract; full text not read.
