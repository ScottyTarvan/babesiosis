## Abstract-derived summary

This small clinicopathological study examined blood counts and differential leukocyte counts in 18 animals with babesiosis, 15 of them dogs and three horses. Results were tabulated and compared statistically with Fisher's exact test in SPSS 18. The authors report that erythrocyte count, haemoglobin and haematocrit were inversely related to parasitaemia, and they describe several anaemia patterns: normochromic normocytic in nine animals, microcytic in two, macrocytic in three, hypochromic in two and hyperchromic in four. Thrombocytopenia is described as another haematological change linked to babesiosis, which may reflect altered platelet production, consumption, sequestration or responsiveness. Very significant associations were reported between leukocytosis and monocytosis (p<0.001), and between thrombocytopenia and lymphopenia; thrombocytopenia was also linked to leukocytosis with monocytosis. Dogs and horses are pooled, and the parasite species and identification method are not described.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors report that anaemia is the most commonly diagnosed disorder in babesiosis, that erythrocyte indices were inversely related to parasitaemia, and that thrombocytopenia with lymphopenia and leukocytosis with monocytosis were significantly associated.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The study is a small descriptive haematology series with associations among blood-count findings, and with parasitaemia for the erythrocyte indices. Because dogs and horses are pooled, the canine-specific contribution cannot be separated from the abstract. The mechanistic statements about toxins, autoimmune destruction and platelet kinetics read as background, not as findings tested in the study.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small sample; dogs and horses pooled; Babesia species and identification method not stated; study setting and case definition not stated; anaemia-type counts in the abstract sum to more than 18, so categories probably overlap; no multivariable or severity analysis; abstract only.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
