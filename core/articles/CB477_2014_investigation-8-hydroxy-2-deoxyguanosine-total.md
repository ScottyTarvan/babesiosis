## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined oxidative stress in dogs naturally infected with Babesia vogeli. Oxidative stress was framed as an imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants that favours the oxidants. Serum 8-hydroxy-2'-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), a marker of oxidative DNA damage, was measured by ELISA alongside total antioxidant capacity (TAC) and nitric oxide (NO) in infected dogs and in a control group. Serum 8-OHdG was higher in the infected dogs than in the controls (P<0.05). NO tended to be higher and TAC tended to be lower in the infected dogs, but neither difference reached statistical significance (P>0.05). The abstract does not give group sizes, the way infection was confirmed, or effect sizes.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors aimed to define oxidative stress in naturally infected dogs. They report higher serum 8-OHdG in infected dogs, with NO and TAC changes that were not statistically significant.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Higher serum 8-OHdG in infected dogs is consistent with oxidative DNA damage accompanying B. vogeli infection, but a comparison of infected and control dogs cannot show the direction of effect. The non-significant NO and TAC changes point the same way as an oxidant-antioxidant imbalance and may reflect low statistical power, which cannot be judged without group sizes.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes not reported in abstract; method of infection confirmation not stated in abstract; clinical status, severity and comorbidities of the dogs not reported; no effect sizes or confidence intervals in abstract; full text not retrieved
