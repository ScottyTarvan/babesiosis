## Abstract-derived summary

The authors examined red cell shape changes in dogs with naturally occurring babesiosis and related them to disease course. In 2019, cases clustered in two seasonal waves, a spring-summer peak in June and an autumn peak in October. Parasitaemia followed infestation intensity early in the year but later decoupled from case numbers. Spring-summer cases were mostly acute to subacute with classic signs, whereas autumn cases were subacute and atypical, with liver and kidney dysfunction, cardiac involvement, neurological signs and shock. Poikilocytosis occurred in 92.3% of affected dogs. Acanthocytes and vacuolated cells were described as irreversible forms, echinocytes as early reversible forms, stomatocytes as accompanying haemolytic anaemia and inflammation, and schizocytes as a sign of disseminated intravascular coagulation. The authors propose grading severity from erythrocyte morphology.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (associational claims)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Erythrocyte shape changes are sensitive markers of disease state, and schizocytes signal DIC requiring intensive care.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a hypothesis-generating morphology-severity link (ERY with COA, REN, MET) and as a report of an atypical, organ-dysfunction-heavy autumn phenotype. The abstract does not show that particular poikilocytes cause or predict specific organ lesions; statements that echinocytes "determine" renal or hepatic disease, or that schizocytes are pathognomonic for DIC, go beyond association (schizocytes occur in other microangiopathies, and echinocytes arise as storage or artefact changes).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** n, diagnostic criteria, controls and statistics not reported in abstract; Babesia species not identified; co-infections and treatment effects not described; coagulation testing not mentioned to support DIC; peer-review practice not verified.
