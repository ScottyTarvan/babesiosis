## Abstract-derived summary

This small clinical series looked at kidney function in twenty dogs with babesiosis seen at a veterinary faculty clinic in Bucharest. The investigators measured blood urea nitrogen, creatinine, total protein, sodium, potassium and urine specific gravity, and related these results to alkaline phosphatase and alanine aminotransferase activities. Urea nitrogen was raised in every dog, ranging from 38 mg/dl to above 130 mg/dl. Creatinine was raised in only twelve dogs (60%), with values from 1.6 mg/dl to above 13 mg/dl. Two dogs (10%) had marked low potassium and three (15%) had mild low sodium. All dogs passed concentrated urine, with specific gravity above 1.030. The authors concluded that electrolytes and urine specific gravity, together with the usual renal panel, help to assess renal function in canine babesiosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Blood biochemistry including electrolytes and urine specific gravity, together with the renal panel, is a useful tool for assessing renal function integrity in canine babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Every dog had raised urea nitrogen, whereas creatinine was raised in only 60%, so the two markers were not uniformly raised; the abstract reports no analysis of how they relate. All dogs had concentrated urine, which the abstract does not discuss in terms of prerenal or intrinsic renal mechanisms. The abstract gives no result for the stated correlation with alkaline phosphatase and alanine aminotransferase.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no control group; 20 dogs from one clinic; species identified only as B. canis with no stated diagnostic method; no severity or outcome data in the abstract; results for the liver enzyme correlation not reported in the abstract; abstract only, full text not retrieved.
