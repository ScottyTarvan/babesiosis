## Abstract-derived summary

The authors argue that routine red cell indices have been studied in canine babesiosis but erythrocyte age structure, acid resistance and iron-transferrin status have not. They examined service-breed dogs with babesiosis in two age groups (6-18 months, n = 10; 2-8 years, n = 15). Infected dogs showed low red cell counts with anisocytosis and poikilocytosis, hypochromia, lower haematocrit and macrocytosis. The red cell population shifted toward older cells, which are cleared quickly, with fewer young cells and shorter haemolysis time in acid challenge. Iron studies showed higher unsaturated iron-binding capacity and lower transferrin saturation in both groups, which the authors say prevents haemoglobin assembly in marrow.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Babesiosis alters erythrocyte population structure and fragility and disturbs iron-transferrin status, impairing haemoglobin formation.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Descriptive erythrocyte-level data are useful for the anaemia mechanism, but the marrow-failure inference was not measured. UIBC is unsaturated binding capacity, not "free iron". Low transferrin saturation can also reflect inflammation-related iron restriction, and a falling young-cell fraction sits oddly with the regenerative response the authors cite. No causality is shown.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small unequal groups; diagnosis and species confirmation not described; no co-infection testing, severity grading, reticulocyte data or follow-up; single time point; study site and dates unreported; reporting inconsistencies in the full text.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
