## Abstract-derived summary

The authors set out to characterise renal injury in dogs with B. canis babesiosis using serum and urinary markers of glomerular and tubular dysfunction, first validating several ELISA-based assays for canine samples and checking interference from haemoglobin, lipid and bilirubin. Forty-two naturally infected dogs were stratified by urine protein:creatinine ratio (UPC) and azotemia into three groups (9, 27 and 6 dogs) and compared with 14 healthy dogs. Urinary IgG, albumin, retinol-binding protein, KIM-1, NAG and uromodulin were creatinine-normalised, and serum SDMA was measured. Every marker except uromodulin differed from controls and correlated strongly with UPC. Albumin and IgG rises pointed to glomerular injury, whereas KIM-1, RBP and NAG rises pointed to proximal tubular injury. The authors concluded these markers detect renal damage earlier than conventional variables.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Urinary and serum markers revealed glomerular and proximal tubular damage in B. canis infection and showed advantages over conventional renal parameters.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports a mixed glomerular and tubular renal lesion pattern in babesiosis, including in non-azotemic dogs. Because groups were defined by UPC, strong marker correlations with UPC are partly circular. Marker location is inferred from known biology; no histopathology or mechanism is shown. "Earlier detection" is not tested longitudinally or against outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small group sizes (n=6 azotemic); single time point; no species-confirmation method or co-infection exclusion stated in abstract; haemoglobinuria/haemolysis interference acknowledged; no outcome data in abstract.
