## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined heart lesions in 20 dogs from Croatia that died or were euthanised because of complicated Babesia canis infection, confirmed by PCR and sequencing. Necropsy and histology were used to classify macroscopic and microscopic changes. Pericardial effusion occurred in 6 of 20 dogs, and epicardial, endocardial or myocardial haemorrhage in 12 of 20. The commonest microscopic findings were oedema between myocardial fibres (16 of 20), haemorrhage (13 of 20) and leukocytes within vessels (12 of 20). Focal cardiomyocyte necrosis was seen in 6 of 20 dogs, and neutrophilic and histiocytic myocarditis in 4 of 20. Clustering of parasitised erythrocytes in myocardial capillaries was proposed as a possible contributor to haemorrhage in 4 of 20 dogs. The authors concluded that B. canis seems about as pathogenic as B. rossi and that cardiac lesions should be expected in complicated cases.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** All 20 dogs had myocardial lesions. The authors suggest that the most extensive necrosis relates to coagulopathy and that the other lesions reflect hypoxia, and they judge B. canis to be about as pathogenic as B. rossi.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This is a descriptive necropsy series of fatal cases that links cardiac histological changes with proposed mechanisms (sludging of parasitised erythrocytes, thrombosis, plasma dilution and oedema). The mechanisms are inferred from lesion patterns and are not tested. Because only dogs that died or were euthanised were examined, the findings describe severe disease and cannot show how common cardiac lesions are in milder cases.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only abstract available; no control or survivor group; selected fatal and euthanised cases only; wide age range (one month to 12 years); no clinical cardiac data (for example troponin or ECG) in the abstract; publication venue and peer-review status not confirmed.
