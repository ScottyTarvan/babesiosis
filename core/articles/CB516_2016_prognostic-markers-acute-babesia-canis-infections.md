## Abstract-derived summary

This study examined whether routine and rapid in-clinic laboratory results could indicate a poor outcome in acute Babesia canis infection. Fifteen naturally infected dogs, sampled at first admission before treatment, were split into seven survivors and eight nonsurvivors and compared on routine laboratory profiles, hand-held lactate and glucose readings, and acute phase response measures. Three further dogs were inoculated experimentally and followed over time. Nonsurvivors had higher lactate, triglyceride and phosphate concentrations, and lower haematocrit, leukocyte counts, total serum protein and thrombocyte counts. Every nonsurvivor was hyperlactataemic, whereas six of seven survivors had lactate within the reference range, and survivors' leukocyte counts were normal while nonsurvivors were leukopenic. In the experimental dogs, lactate, triglyceride, phosphate and thrombocyte values passed the prognostic thresholds only during the acute crisis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors read changes in the admission laboratory profile as a sign of poor outcome, and suggest intensive care for dogs with anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperlactataemia, raised phosphate and triglycerides, and low total protein, at the levels the abstract lists.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Admission lactate and leukocyte count separated the outcome groups most clearly in this small Swiss series, and all eight nonsurvivors died or were euthanised within 48 hours (full text). In the three experimental dogs, lactate, triglyceride, phosphate and thrombocyte counts exceeded their thresholds only at the acute crisis, whereas the leukocyte count fell below its cut-off before any clinical signs (full text). The data describe association with outcome, not the mechanism of death. Parasitaemia did not differ between outcome groups (full text), which is consistent with the abstract's point that prognosis does not always track parasite level.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small sample (7 and 8 dogs; 6 and 6 for haematology and biochemistry, full text); cut-offs derived by ROC analysis in the same cohort, with no independent validation cohort (full text); no data on disease duration or time of infection before admission (full text); no postmortem examination (full text); only 3 experimental dogs, stopped at the first signs of crisis (full text); no uninfected control group described (full text).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Twelve naturally infected dogs were presented to the Clinic for Small Animal Internal Medicine, Vetsuisse Faculty, University of Zurich, and 3 to private practices in Switzerland, in 2011 to 2013; every dog was positive for B. canis by smear, and by PCR with direct sequencing (full text)
  - Six nonsurvivors died spontaneously within 24 hours of admission and 2 were euthanised within 48 hours; all dogs received imidocarb dipropionate, with or without doxycycline (full text)
  - Haematology and biochemistry comparisons excluded the 3 private-practice dogs because other analysers were used (6 survivors, 6 nonsurvivors); lactate, glucose, parasitaemia and acute phase variables used all 15 dogs (full text)
  - Parasitaemia ranged from 0.5 to 3.1% (median 1.2%) with no difference between outcome groups; age, sex and clinical signs also did not differ (full text)
  - ROC cut-offs: lactate 3.95 mmol/L (sensitivity and specificity 100%, AUC 1.00); WBC 4.7 x10^3/uL (100% and 100%, set at the reference border); thrombocytes 27.5 x10^3/uL (83.3% and 83.3%, AUC 0.89) (full text)
  - CRP (P = 0.189) and SAA (P = 0.536) did not differ between survivors and nonsurvivors; SAA stayed below the diagnostic limit in the experimental dogs (full text)
  - Three beagles were inoculated IV with about 1 x 10^6 parasitised erythrocytes from an isolate of a Bernese mountain dog that had travelled to Hungary; signs appeared at 105, 120 and 119 hours, maximum parasitaemia was 1.75%, and end-of-experiment fibrinogen was 2.6, 3.8 and 3.2 g/L with D-dimer 0.26, 0.44 and 0.92 mg/L (full text)
  - Nine of the 15 natural cases had a travel history; the authors list missing data on the course before admission and the small sample size as limitations (full text)
