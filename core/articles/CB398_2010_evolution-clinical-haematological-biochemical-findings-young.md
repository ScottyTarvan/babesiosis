## Abstract-derived summary

Young dogs naturally infected with vector-borne pathogens, identified by cytology or PCR, were followed for one year with serial clinical examinations and blood sampling at seven time points. The dogs were allocated to two groups of 17, one given an imidacloprid-permethrin spot-on and one left untreated, with ten young beagles per group serving as sentinels. Anaplasma platys was detected most often at baseline, followed by Babesia vogeli, Bartonella, Ehrlichia canis and Hepatozoon canis, and several co-infections occurred. Abnormal clinical signs appeared only in untreated dogs. Low platelet counts were the commonest blood abnormality in dogs infected with A. platys or B. vogeli, alone or together, or with A. platys and Bartonella, and raised lymphocyte counts were frequent with B. vogeli. Tick infestation was associated with these infections. The authors stress that clinical or haematological changes cannot easily be attributed to one specific pathogen.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** The study shows how hard it is to link a particular clinical sign or haematological abnormality to one canine vector-borne disease.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** In B. vogeli-infected young dogs, thrombocytopenia and lymphocytosis were frequent findings, including in co-infected dogs. The abstract reports no numbers for the B. vogeli groups, so the size of these changes cannot be judged. Because co-infections were common, the findings are associations with infection status and cannot be assigned to B. vogeli alone.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** abstract only; numbers per pathogen group and platelet or lymphocyte values not reported in the abstract; frequent co-infections; spot-on treatment given to half of the dogs; sentinel beagles differ from the naturally infected dogs; no biochemical results given in the abstract; no severity or outcome analysis.
