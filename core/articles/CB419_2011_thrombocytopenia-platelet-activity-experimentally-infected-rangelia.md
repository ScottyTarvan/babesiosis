## Abstract-derived summary

The study examined platelet number, clotting time and platelet function in young female dogs given Rangelia vitalii-infected blood, focusing on the acute phase. Seven dogs received infected blood and five uninfected dogs served as controls. Parasites were visible inside red cells on blood smears from the fifth day after inoculation. Blood was sampled on days 0, 10, 20 and 30, and bone marrow was collected on days 10 and 20. Infected dogs had fewer platelets than controls on days 10 and 20, and enlarged platelets appeared only in the infected group. Prothrombin and activated partial thromboplastin times were similar between groups. Megakaryocyte counts were higher and platelet aggregation was lower in infected dogs on days 10 and 20. The authors considered splenic pooling or immune-mediated destruction the probable cause of the platelet loss.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** mechanistic (experimental)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Rangeliosis in dogs causes a severe thrombocytopenia during the acute phase of infection. The platelet reduction probably arose from splenic sequestration and/or immune-mediated thrombocytopenia.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The experimental design supports an association between inoculation with R. vitalii and lower platelet counts and reduced platelet aggregation, with normal PT and aPTT. The higher megakaryocyte counts and macro-platelets point to a marrow response, but the proposed mechanisms (splenic sequestration, immune-mediated destruction) were not tested directly in the abstract. The piroplasm sits at the edge of the canine Babesia scope, so findings may not transfer to other canine Babesia species.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** small groups (7 infected, 5 controls); young female dogs only; acute phase only (to day 30); species identified by blood smear in the abstract; mechanism inferred rather than tested; taxonomic scope borderline; full text not read.
