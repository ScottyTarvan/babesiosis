## Abstract-derived summary
Cardiac effects of human babesiosis, an emerging tick-borne infection of red cells, are poorly defined. The investigators reviewed records of every adult admitted with confirmed babesiosis to one US hospital from January 2011 to October 2021. Of 163 patients, 32 (19.6%) had at least one cardiac complication during admission; atrial fibrillation (9.4%), heart failure (8.6%), QTc prolongation (8.0%) and ischaemia (6.8%) were most common. Neither cardiovascular risk factors nor prior heart disease were significantly associated with complications. Affected patients more often had parasitaemia above 10% and had longer hospital and ICU stays and higher mortality. The authors conclude cardiac complications are common and that the severity-outcome relationship needs study.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** descriptive retrospective cohort; associational, clinically coded outcomes
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cardiac complications are common in acute babesiosis, affecting about one-fifth of this inpatient sample, and the relationship between severity and cardiac outcomes needs further investigation.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Shared pathway is cardiac involvement tracking parasite burden, ICU-level illness and death. Canine: CB014 (troponin I and CK-MB raised, highest in two non-survivors), CB146 and CB080 (necropsy myocarditis, necrosis, congestion), CB032 (myocardial haemorrhage). Rated mechanistically plausible for this link, and speculative for the sepsis-based troponin-as-severity-marker analogy, since canine data lack any adjusted prognostic analysis. Differences: older comorbid human patients with clinical diagnoses and no troponin in the abstract, against mostly young dogs assessed by biomarker and necropsy.
- **Key limitations:** Retrospective, single-centre; outcomes are clinical codes rather than measured myocardial injury; no mechanism; same cohort as HU023; severity confounds associations.
