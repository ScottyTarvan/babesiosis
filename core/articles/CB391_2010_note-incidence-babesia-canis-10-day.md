## Abstract-derived summary

The carcass of a 10-day-old female Great Dane pup was submitted after a history of dullness, depression and sudden death. External examination showed pale conjunctival mucous membranes and mild enlargement of the prescapular lymph nodes. At necropsy the authors describe petechial haemorrhages beneath the epicardium, congested lungs, intestine and meningeal vessels, a swollen pale liver, a mildly enlarged dark red kidney, and swollen, haemorrhagic mesenteric lymph nodes. Blood smears from peripheral blood and from the heart, stained with Leishman and Giemsa, contained large, pleomorphic, pear-shaped organisms inside erythrocytes, seen singly, in pairs and in groups, which the authors identified as Babesia canis. The report is a single-animal postmortem description from Chennai, Tamil Nadu, India, and the abstract gives no laboratory values beyond the smear findings.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (single case report)
- **Authors' conclusion:** The pup's sudden death and the necropsy lesions are reported together with large intraerythrocytic Babesia canis forms on blood smears, and the case is presented as an incidence of B. canis in a 10-day-old pup.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The case documents gross pathology in a neonatal dog with smear-positive large Babesia, a presentation that is unusual because of the pup's age. The haemorrhagic, congestive and pallor findings are consistent with a multisystem disease course, but a single carcass without histopathology, haematology or molecular confirmation cannot show that the parasite produced the lesions or that the route of infection was congenital.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single animal; species identified by smear morphology only; no histopathology, haematology, biochemistry or PCR reported in the abstract; no exclusion of concurrent disease or other causes of neonatal death; route of infection not established; abstract only, full text not retrieved.
