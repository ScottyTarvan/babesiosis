## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked which haematological changes accompany blood-parasite infection in dogs presented to a Thai university hospital. The 474 blood samples, collected from dogs in 2016-2019, underwent automated blood counts, and stained blood films were screened for parasites; odds ratios were calculated for abnormalities by parasite. Ehrlichia canis was most frequent (n=127), followed by Hepatozoon canis (n=100) and Babesia canis (n=24). Dogs with B. canis showed associations with anaemia, thrombocytopenia, eosinopenia and lymphopenia (OR 10.09, 33.00, 20.02 and 66.47; p<0.01). The authors conclude that haematological abnormalities are characteristic of tick-borne infection and that the values can guide clinical diagnosis of canine blood parasite infections in the region.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Haematological abnormalities are a hallmark of tick-borne infections, and the reported values may guide diagnosis in Southern Thailand.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A descriptive clinicopathological profile: Babesia-associated anaemia and thrombocytopenia are compatible with haemolysis and platelet consumption or sequestration, and lymphopenia/eosinopenia with a stress or inflammatory leukogram. These are hypotheses only: the study shows association, with no mechanism or severity/outcome data. The abnormalities are not specific to Babesia (Ehrlichia and Hepatozoon showed overlapping patterns).
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small B. canis group (n=24) with very wide confidence intervals; species by microscopy only; textbook reference ranges; no severity, treatment or outcome data; no multiple-testing correction; full-text numbers are internally inconsistent in places (for example MCV and RDW statements versus table odds ratios).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
