## Abstract-derived summary

Working dogs from organised kennels across several Indian agro-climatic zones were studied over 2012 to 2014. Of 330 dogs suspected of babesiosis, microscopy, simple PCR and nested 18S rRNA PCR detected infection in 17, 30 and 51 animals respectively (5.15%, 9.09%, 15.45%), and the authors favour nested PCR for screening. Sequencing identified B. gibsoni in 47 positives and B. vogeli in four. The 51 positive dogs were allocated to three groups of 17 (apparently healthy, uncomplicated, complicated disease) and their haematological and biochemical profiles were compared. Recurrence of illness was common, and typical antiprotozoal drugs did not achieve full sterilisation of B. gibsoni infections. The authors argue that the smaller parasite is displacing B. vogeli in India, which is worrying given its poorer prognosis.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** B. gibsoni appears positively selected over B. vogeli in India, is hard to clear with usual drugs, and nested PCR should be relied on for screening and epidemiology.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Mostly a diagnostic and molecular epidemiology paper, but it adds a species-resolved clinicopathological profile of three severity tiers, including fatal complicated cases. Relevant to the SEV axis (subclinical to complicated phenotypes, cerebral signs, multi-organ dysfunction, shock/DIC) and to haemolytic anaemia. It does not test mechanisms, and species-specific virulence cannot be inferred because B. vogeli is represented by only four dogs.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no uninfected control group reported in the abstract; B. vogeli n = 4; kennel dogs only (strays excluded, per authors); severity criteria not explicit; prior treatment not controlled; short 18S fragment for phylogeny; laboratory abnormalities reported as counts across all 51 dogs rather than group-wise comparisons in the text read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
