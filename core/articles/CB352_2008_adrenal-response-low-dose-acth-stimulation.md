## Abstract-derived summary

This prospective, case-controlled study examined whether adrenocortical function relates to death in naturally infected dogs with Babesia rossi. Sixty-eight infected dogs and fifteen healthy controls had plasma ACTH and serum cortisol measured at admission before treatment, followed by a low-dose tetracosactrin challenge with a second cortisol sample one hour later. Infection was confirmed by PCR and reverse line blot. Outcomes were death after hospitalisation (four dogs), recovery after hospitalisation (48) and outpatient treatment (16). Basal cortisol was higher in infected dogs than controls, whereas stimulated cortisol was not. Both basal and stimulated cortisol were higher in the dogs that died than in survivors and outpatients. Delta cortisol and cortisol-to-ACTH ratio did not differ among outcome groups, although dogs with delta cortisol below 83 nmol/l had higher ratios. The authors read the findings as showing no adrenal insufficiency, as in human malaria.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Raised basal and ACTH-stimulated cortisol and raised cortisol-to-ACTH ratios are taken to confirm the absence of adrenal insufficiency, a pattern the authors say concurs with human malaria.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Higher basal and stimulated cortisol were associated with death in B. rossi babesiosis, but the design does not show that cortisol contributes to mortality. Delta cortisol and the cortisol-to-ACTH ratio did not separate outcome groups. The finding is relevant to the endocrine stress response and severity rather than to a treatable adrenal deficit.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only four dogs died, so the outcome comparison has little power; outcome groups may differ in illness severity at presentation (not reported in the abstract); single time point before treatment; delta cortisol cut-off of 83 nmol/l is described only in the abstract; no adjustment for other factors is described in the abstract; full text not read.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
