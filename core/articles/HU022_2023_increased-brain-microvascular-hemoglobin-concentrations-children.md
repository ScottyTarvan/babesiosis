## Abstract-derived summary
Brain swelling predicts death in cerebral malaria, yet it is unknown whether swelling reflects tissue oedema or blood-vessel congestion, which alter tissue haemoglobin in different ways. Using near-infrared spectroscopy, the investigators measured cerebral microvascular haemoglobin in 46 children with cerebral malaria (smear-positive, Blantyre coma score 2 or less, retinopathy), 33 with uncomplicated malaria and 29 healthy children. Median concentration was 145.2 micromolar in cerebral malaria against 82.9 in uncomplicated malaria (P = 0.008) and correlated with MRI swelling score (r = 0.37). The haemoglobin signal also showed lower self-similarity over 30 minutes (0.63 versus 0.91), suggesting disordered blood-flow regulation. The authors infer that excess blood volume may contribute to swelling.

## Critical interpretation
- **Evidence type:** primary human physiological cohort with comparator groups
- **Authors' conclusion:** Raised tissue haemoglobin and its link to swelling suggest excess blood volume, possibly from vascular congestion, may contribute to brain swelling in cerebral malaria.
- **Relevance to canine babesiosis:** Shared pathway is cerebral microvascular congestion as a driver of swelling. Canine data do not measure brain swelling or perfusion; CB041 proposes capillary sequestration, CB080 found multi-organ congestion at necropsy, CB116 reported brain lesions in experimental infection and CB069 described clinical 'cerebral babesiosis'. Rated mechanistically plausible. Differences include P. falciparum cytoadherence, a paediatric African population, and the absence of any canine imaging or spectroscopy.
- **Key limitations:** Modest correlation (r = 0.37); cross-sectional; congestion is inferred, not directly shown; the comparison with uncomplicated malaria rests on a small cohort; no Babesia data.
