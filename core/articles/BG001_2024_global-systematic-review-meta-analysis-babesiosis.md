## Abstract-derived summary

The authors pooled published prevalence estimates of canine Babesia infection worldwide. They searched five bibliographic databases for studies from 2000 to 2022, screened 23,864 records and analysed 229 eligible studies with a meta-analysis in R, stratifying by region, parasite species and season. The overall pooled prevalence was about 12% (95% CI 9.7-14.6%). Europe gave the highest regional estimate (about 21%), and B. canis was the most commonly detected species (about 22%, with a very wide interval of roughly 6-44%). Summer sampling yielded the highest seasonal estimate (about 10%). The authors recommend routine screening and control measures to limit tick-borne transmission among dogs.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** The authors recommend regular screening and appropriate control strategies to prevent transmission of tick-borne disease among dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Included here only as a background seed reference for the scope and geographic distribution of canine babesiosis. It contains no pathophysiological, host-response or outcome data; the clinical-sign paragraph in the full text is generic. Pooled prevalence mixes serological and direct-detection studies, so it estimates exposure and infection loosely and should not be read as clinical disease burden.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Very high between-study heterogeneity (subgroup I2 reported from 89% to 99%, full text); significant publication bias (asymmetric Doi plot); heterogeneous diagnostic methods; several country-level estimates rest on a single study; subgroup study counts (27 stray, 76 owned; full text) do not reconcile with 229 and the text has minor internal inconsistencies (confidence-level labelling, climate conclusion).

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
