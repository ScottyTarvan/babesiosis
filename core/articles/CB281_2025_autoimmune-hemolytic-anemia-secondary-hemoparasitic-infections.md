## Abstract-derived summary

This case series from a university emergency veterinary hospital (2021-2024) covers 55 dogs with haemolytic anaemia that the authors regard as immune-mediated and secondary to blood-borne parasitic infection. The agents recorded were Mycoplasma haemocanis, Hepatozoon canis, Borrelia burgdorferi, Anaplasma spp., Ehrlichia canis, Dirofilaria immitis and Babesia canis. Infection was established by clinical and haematological assessment, a point-of-care serological kit (SNAP 4Dx Plus) and PCR. The abstract reports no per-pathogen case numbers, no haematological values and no outcome data. The authors conclude that prompt diagnosis and individually tailored treatment matter, particularly with co-infections, and that tick and other vector control should be intensified.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Early diagnosis and tailored therapy are important in haemoparasite-associated anaemia, and vector prevention should be strengthened.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** B. canis is one of seven pathogens and no B. canis-specific data are given in the abstract or full text, so the paper adds little Babesia-specific pathophysiology. The immune-mediated mechanism (antibody/complement-mediated erythrophagocytosis, oxidative injury, systemic inflammation) is presented narratively and was not tested; the text does not document direct antiglobulin testing, spherocytosis or autoagglutination, so immune-mediated haemolysis cannot be separated from direct parasite-driven haemolysis. Useful mainly as descriptive haematology and transfusion/supportive-care practice.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** pooled pathogens; no Babesia-specific n; IMHA criteria not stated; group structure does not match the "55 cases of AIHA" framing (controls had mild, self-resolving anaemia without autoimmune complications); no statistics or survival data; PCR targets unspecified; very short receipt-to-acceptance interval (4 days) and no peer-review statement raise doubt about genuine peer review.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
