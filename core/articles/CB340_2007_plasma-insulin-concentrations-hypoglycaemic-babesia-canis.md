## Abstract-derived summary

Hypoglycaemia is a life-threatening complication of Babesia canis rossi babesiosis, seen in nearly one in five severely ill dogs and tied to mortality. This prospective, cross-sectional study of 94 dogs with naturally occurring virulent disease looked for inappropriate insulin secretion in hypoglycaemic cases. Glucose and insulin were measured on the same pre-treatment jugular sample. Dogs were later sorted by glucose into hypoglycaemic (16), normoglycaemic (62) and hyperglycaemic (16) groups. Median insulin was 10.7 pmol/L in hypoglycaemic dogs, 10.7 in normoglycaemic dogs and 21.7 in hyperglycaemic dogs, with no significant difference between groups. In hypoglycaemic and normoglycaemic dogs, median insulin was below the assay's detection limit, suggesting appropriately low secretion. Only two dogs had insulin that was inappropriately high, one of them hypoglycaemic. The authors concluded that hyperinsulinaemia rarely accounts for hypoglycaemia and speculated that glucose use, glycogen stores and liver function play larger roles.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Hyperinsulinaemia is an infrequent cause of hypoglycaemia in virulent canine babesiosis. Increased glucose consumption, depleted hepatic glycogen and impaired gluconeogenesis are speculated to be more important.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Insulin concentrations did not differ significantly between glycaemic groups. In the hypoglycaemic and normoglycaemic groups the median was below the assay detection limit, which argues against inappropriate insulin secretion as a frequent cause of hypoglycaemia. The alternative mechanisms were not measured in this study and remain hypotheses. The study is cross-sectional with a single pre-treatment sample, so it cannot show how glucose and insulin change over the course of disease.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single time-point sampling; groups defined retrospectively by blood glucose; median insulin below the assay detection limit in two of three groups, which limits resolution at the low end; small hypoglycaemic and hyperglycaemic groups (16 each); no healthy control group reported in the abstract; species identification method not stated in the abstract; alternative mechanisms not tested.
