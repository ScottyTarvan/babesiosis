## Abstract-derived summary

The report describes a ten-year-old female Pomeranian that developed progressive ataxia, lethargy and prolonged loss of appetite without an overt haematological crisis. The authors regard involvement of the central nervous system as rare yet frequently missed in canine babesiosis, especially in older animals whose signs may be vague. Smear examination showed piroplasms inside red blood cells, which were judged consistent with Babesia gibsoni. Treatment combined atovaquone and azithromycin with liver-protective and metabolic support, and the animal improved markedly. Haematology values, neurological examination findings and imaging are not described in the abstract. Only a single case is described, and the abstract reports no molecular confirmation of the species.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Cerebral involvement should be considered in geriatric dogs with B. gibsoni infection presenting with ataxia and non-specific illness.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Hypothesis-generating clinical anecdote. Ataxia in an elderly dog has many causes, and the abstract and publisher page show no neurological localisation, CSF, imaging, PCR or histopathology, so a causal link to cerebral babesiosis is not established. Response to antiprotozoal therapy is suggestive only. It provides no mechanistic data on cerebral microvascular sequestration or hypoxia.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** single case; smear-only species attribution; no neurological work-up or comorbidity exclusion described; no haematology; treatment confounded by supportive drugs; no follow-up detail; journal peer-review rigour not verified.
