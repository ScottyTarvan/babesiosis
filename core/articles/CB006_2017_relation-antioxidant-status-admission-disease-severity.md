## Abstract-derived summary

The authors asked whether oxidative imbalance tracks with severity and death in canine babesiosis. They measured four antioxidant markers (superoxide dismutase, glutathione peroxidase, catalase, total antioxidant status) plus malondialdehyde (MDA), a lipid-peroxidation marker, in 40 infected dogs and 30 healthy controls. Infected dogs were grouped as uncomplicated, one complication, or multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS), and also by anaemia grade. Infected dogs had lower SOD, catalase and TAS than controls, while GPx did not differ. MODS dogs had lower SOD and GPx and higher MDA than both other infected groups. SOD, catalase and GPx correlated inversely, and MDA directly, with fatal outcome. Antioxidant decline was larger in moderately than mildly anaemic dogs. The authors propose these markers as indicators of severity and outcome.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** prognostic (associational)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Antioxidant biomarkers change in B. canis canis infection and could serve as indicators of disease severity and outcome.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Supports depletion of enzymatic antioxidant defences alongside raised lipid peroxidation in the most severe (MODS, fatal) cases, consistent with oxidative stress accompanying multisystem injury and haemolysis. It shows association at admission, not that oxidative stress causes organ dysfunction; markers may reflect anaemia, haemolysis or tissue damage rather than drive them. A severity or outcome biomarker, not a mechanism.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Small groups (full text: 6 with one complication, 5 with MODS, 4 deaths, 1 severely anaemic dog); single admission time point; heterogeneous supportive treatment (authors' caveat); control dog source not described; possible overlap with the group's earlier MDA cohort not stated; no adjustment for confounders reported in the abstract.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
