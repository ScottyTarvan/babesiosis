## Abstract-derived summary

The study measured serum nitric oxide metabolites (NOx), advanced oxidation protein products (AOPP) and total antioxidant capacity (FRAP) in naturally infected dogs from Campo Grande, Brazil. Infections with Leishmania infantum, Ehrlichia canis and Babesia vogeli were identified by PCR. Serum came from 54 dogs with single or mixed infections (L. infantum 19, E. canis 8, B. vogeli 5, L. infantum plus E. canis 12, L. infantum plus B. vogeli 4, E. canis plus B. vogeli 6) and 17 uninfected controls. Overall, infected dogs had higher NOx, AOPP and FRAP than controls (P < 0.01). The abstract reports that nitric oxide in dogs naturally infected with B. vogeli, and AOPP in dogs with E. canis, did not differ from controls. The highest NOx values occurred in the two groups co-infected with L. infantum. The authors concluded that the infected dogs showed redox imbalance.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dogs naturally infected with L. infantum, E. canis and B. vogeli developed a state of redox imbalance, and the authors suggest that the infections may contribute to disease pathology through oxidative stress.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** The abstract reports that nitric oxide in dogs infected with B. vogeli did not differ from controls, but it does not say whether this covers the single-infection group (n = 5) only, and it gives no group-level values. Dogs in the L. infantum plus B. vogeli co-infection group (n = 4) were among those with the highest NOx, so the Babesia-specific signal is mixed. Most Babesia-positive dogs were co-infected with L. infantum or E. canis, so oxidative changes cannot be assigned to B. vogeli alone. The design shows associations between infection status and serum markers only.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small B. vogeli single-infection group; co-infections in most Babesia-positive dogs; no clinical severity or outcome data in the abstract; no group-level values or effect sizes in the abstract; cross-sectional sampling; full text not read.
