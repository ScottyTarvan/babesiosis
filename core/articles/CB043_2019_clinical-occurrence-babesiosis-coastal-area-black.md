## Abstract-derived summary

This clinic-based survey from the Black Sea coast of southeastern Romania examined 306 owned dogs seen between 2016 and 2018 with clinical suspicion of babesiosis. Giemsa-stained blood smears showed large intra-erythrocyte piroplasms in 85 dogs (27.8%). Cases clustered in spring, with 76 of 85 between March and June, and ranged from 3 months to 13 years of age, with Maltese Bichons and crossbreeds most represented. Disease was mild in 38.8%, moderate in 29.4% and severe in 29.4%. On clinicopathological grounds, 38 dogs had uncomplicated disease, 34 had complicated disease with one dysfunctional organ, and 13 had multiple organ dysfunction syndrome; recovery fell stepwise across these groups (81.6%, 70.1%, 38.4%). Older dogs were over-represented among severe cases and MODS. The authors conclude that babesiosis is common in the region and that control measures are needed.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Canine babesiosis occurs commonly in Dobrogea, and appropriate control measures should be adopted.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a syndrome and severity stratification (uncomplicated, single-organ, MODS) with graded recovery, supporting organ dysfunction burden as an outcome gradient. Which organs failed, the criteria used and any mechanism are not stated in the abstract. The age association is crude and unadjusted; it does not show causation.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** species not molecularly confirmed; smear-based detection in clinically suspected dogs; no control group; treatment and co-infection confounding not described; unadjusted age analysis; single clinic.
