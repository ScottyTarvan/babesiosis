## Abstract-derived summary

This study followed serum concentrations of three eicosanoids (prostaglandin E2, thromboxane B2 and leukotriene B4) and a lipid panel (triglycerides, total, HDL and LDL cholesterol) in dogs naturally infected with Babesia canis, and compared them with healthy dogs. Samples were taken on admission and on days 1, 2 and 7 of disease. The infected dogs had SIRS and MODS. Leukotriene B4 stayed higher than in controls throughout, prostaglandin E2 was higher on the second, third and seventh days, and thromboxane B2 began lower than in controls but exceeded them by day 7. Dogs with MODS kept high prostaglandin E2 after therapy, unlike dogs with SIRS, and leukotriene B4 followed a similar tendency. Thromboxane B2 rose significantly only in the SIRS group. The lipid profile of infected dogs also differed from that of healthy dogs. The authors call for further work on the prognostic value of these mediators.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** associational (serial measurements, infected vs healthy dogs and SIRS vs MODS subgroups)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Serum eicosanoids and the lipid profile changed significantly in dogs naturally infected with B. canis. Further studies are needed on the prognostic value of these lipid mediators and on whether they predict progression of SIRS and MODS.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Leukotriene B4, prostaglandin E2 and thromboxane B2 were associated with time course and with the SIRS or MODS phenotype in infected dogs, which points to an association between lipid-mediator signalling and severity in this syndrome. The different trajectories of prostaglandin E2 and thromboxane B2 in the two subgroups suggest that the eicosanoid response varies with severity. The design cannot show that these mediators drive organ dysfunction, and no outcome or prognostic analysis is reported in the abstract.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** group sizes not reported in the abstract; abstract only, so methods and statistics were not appraised; Babesia species identification method not stated; comparison is with healthy dogs rather than dogs with other illnesses; treatment given during follow-up may have influenced later values; no prognostic or survival analysis reported.

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._
