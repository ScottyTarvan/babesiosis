## Abstract-derived summary

A review centred on dogs, with comparative material from horses and wildlife, on how Babesia infection leads to anaemia and wider organ disease. It covers life cycle, epidemiology and differences in pathogenicity between B. canis, B. vogeli, B. gibsoni and B. rossi. The proposed pathogenesis combines extensive red cell destruction, systemic inflammation, liver and kidney dysfunction, metabolic disturbance and oxidative stress, ending in anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukogram changes and organ damage. Laboratory correlates cited include raised liver enzymes, bilirubin, urea and creatinine, higher malondialdehyde and lactate dehydrogenase, and lower superoxide dismutase, catalase and paraoxonase-1. Serum amyloid A, C-reactive protein and antibody titres are said to track severity and prognosis. Diagnostic and treatment sections are also included. The authors conclude that interpreting blood counts, chemistry and immune markers together should allow earlier diagnosis and better monitoring and prognosis, particularly where tick-borne transmission is a risk.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Combined haematological, biochemical and immunological marker panels improve early diagnosis, monitoring and prognostic assessment of babesiosis.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Useful as a map of the erythrocyte, oxidative-stress and acute-phase literature, but the abstract pools several host species and several Babesia species, so canine-specific and species-specific statements cannot be separated at abstract level. Marker-severity links (SAA, CRP, titres) are associational, not mechanistic. LDH is listed as an oxidative-stress marker although it mainly reflects haemolysis and tissue injury. Treatment and diagnostic content is outside pathophysiology scope.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** no search strategy, inclusion criteria or appraisal method stated in the abstract; mixed hosts; full text not read; likely non-systematic selection of sources; no quantitative synthesis.
