## Abstract-derived summary

Anaemia in Babesia rossi babesiosis is often accompanied by a weaker regenerative response than expected, and the marrow contribution had not been well characterised. The authors collected bone marrow smears and histology within 24 hours of death from six naturally infected dogs and five healthy controls, interpreting them alongside the haemogram, an iron stain and immunohistochemistry (CD3, CD20, MUM-1, MAC387, CD204). Infected dogs showed an inflammatory leukogram and inadequately regenerative anaemia. Their marrow was hypercellular from erythroid hyperplasia with dyserythropoietic changes in metarubricytes, together with myeloid hyperplasia with left shift, more CD204-positive resident macrophages, left-shifted megakaryocytes and higher iron content. Whether iron-restricted erythropoiesis contributes could not be determined. The authors conclude that dyserythropoiesis partly explains the poor erythroid response, resembling findings in falciparum malaria.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive (post-mortem pathology with controls)
- **Authors' conclusion:** Dyserythropoiesis is partly responsible for the inappropriate erythroid response in canine babesiosis, as in human falciparum malaria.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** Directly relevant to the anaemia mechanism: marrow findings suggest ineffective erythropoiesis (not simply absent production) alongside macrophage expansion and raised stored iron, consistent with a possible marrow contribution to the anaemia alongside haemolysis. The data show association at death, not causation or time course, and do not resolve iron-restricted erythropoiesis.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** very small n (6 vs 5); fatal cases only, so severity and survivor bias; post-mortem sampling and autolysis; treatment and transfusion history not reported in abstract; species confirmation and co-infection exclusion not reported in abstract.
