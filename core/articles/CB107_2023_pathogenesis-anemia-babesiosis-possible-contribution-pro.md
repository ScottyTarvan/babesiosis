## Abstract-derived summary

This review argues that anaemia in canine babesiosis stems chiefly from the host's immune response, with the parasite's own developmental stages playing only a minor part inside red cells. The authors gather evidence on several immune-mediated routes, including antibody formation, removal of erythrocytes by phagocytes, oxidative harm to cells, activation of the complement cascade and antibody-dependent killing of cells. Infected and uninfected erythrocytes alike are retained in the spleen and caught in small blood vessels. These processes are presented as driven by pro-inflammatory mediators, chiefly IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-8, with an imbalance against anti-inflammatory signalling also contributing. Evidence is also drawn from related disorders, including bovine and murine babesiosis and human and murine malaria, so canine findings are discussed alongside cross-species data.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** review
- **Authors' conclusion:** Immune-mediated mechanisms, driven by pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, rather than direct parasite destruction, are the principal cause of anaemia in infected dogs.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** A useful organising framework for ERY pathophysiology, linking haemolysis to INF, oxidative stress and microcirculatory sequestration. Much of the mechanistic support is extrapolated from malaria and murine or bovine models; the authors themselves note that complement, ADCC, NETosis and several cytokines (IL-4, IL-12, CXCL4) have not been studied in infected dogs. Cytokine-anaemia links are therefore hypotheses, not demonstrated causal pathways.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** Narrative, no stated search method or quality appraisal; small canine studies, some with conflicting results; species heterogeneity; cross-species extrapolation.
