## Abstract-derived summary

The study aimed to determine clinical signs and haematological findings and to compare microscopic detection with PCR. Thirteen dogs with PCR-confirmed Babesia infection were examined. Seminested PCR identified Babesia canis vogeli as the only subspecies detected. Fever and a pale mucous membrane were the most consistent clinical signs. Thrombocytopenia, monocytosis and lymphocytosis were the commonest haematological findings, and red cell parameters were significantly reduced. Microscopy revealed typical large parasites in 76.92% of dogs. The authors suggest that a presumptive diagnosis should rest on fever and anaemia, that thrombocytopenia is the hallmark finding, and that microscopy, although the fastest confirmatory method, can miss low parasitaemia. They also judge seminested PCR a sensitive and accurate diagnostic method.

## Critical interpretation

- **Evidence type:** descriptive
- **Authors' conclusion:** Presumptive diagnosis should rest on fever and anaemia, with thrombocytopenia regarded as the hallmark of the disease. Microscopy is the fastest confirmation but can miss low parasitaemia, and seminested PCR is sensitive and accurate for diagnosis and subspecies differentiation, detecting only B. canis vogeli.
- **Reviewer interpretation:** This small clinicopathological series gives a haematological profile of PCR-confirmed B. canis vogeli infection in Egyptian dogs: red cell, haemoglobin, PCV and platelet values are lower than in controls, and every infected dog is thrombocytopenic (full text). The mechanisms mentioned in the discussion (immune-mediated and direct red cell damage, platelet destruction or sequestration) are drawn from the literature and were not tested here. The findings describe associations with infection at presentation and say nothing about severity or outcome.
- **Key limitations (from available information):** only 13 infected dogs from one referral hospital; control group size not stated; control and infected dogs not matched in any reported way beyond breed, age, sex and locality categories; no severity, outcome or follow-up data; no testing for co-infections described for infected dogs; subspecies assigned by seminested PCR without sequencing described; mean lymphocyte count did not differ from controls although individual lymphocytosis is reported; mechanistic statements are speculative

_Abstract-level appraisal; not a formal risk-of-bias assessment._

- **Information beyond the abstract:**
  - Dogs were referred to the small animal medicine teaching hospital, Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Cairo University; the survey ran from March 2011 to March 2012; breeds were German shepherd, Great Dane, Labrador and Malino; locality was Giza (full text)
  - Control dogs had no tick exposure or clinical signs, were negative on two seminested PCRs one month apart and negative for IgG by ELISA, and were free of internal parasites and vaccinated; the number of controls is not stated (full text)
  - Infection was most frequent in dogs aged 3-5 years, in German shepherds and in males (full text)
  - Ticks were identified morphologically as Rhipicephalus sanguineus, present on 10/13 dogs (full text)
  - Temperature above 39.6 C in 12/13 dogs, anorexia 12/13, splenomegaly 12/13, lymph node enlargement 10/13, pale mucous membranes 8/13, haematuria 2/13 and icterus 2/13 (full text)
  - Infected versus control means (Student's t-test, P < 0.05): RBC 4.05 vs 6.41 x10^6/uL, haemoglobin 11.375 vs 14.74 g/dL, PCV 32.33 vs 43.14 %, WBC 9.650 vs 12.850 x10^3/uL, neutrophils 3.787 vs 6.728 x10^3/uL, monocytes 1.062 vs 0.771 x10^3/uL, platelets 93.667 vs 268.93 x10^3/uL; MCV, MCH, MCHC and lymphocyte means did not differ significantly (full text)
  - Individual-dog findings: RBC below reference in 61.5%, PCV and haemoglobin below reference in 53.8%, neutropenia in 84.6%, lymphocytosis and monocytosis each in 69.2%, thrombocytopenia in 100% (full text)
  - Smears were positive in 10/13 dogs (75.9% in the results; 76.92% in the abstract); PCR was positive in all 13 (full text)
  - The outer-primer PCR gave a 339 bp product in all 13 samples; the second-round seminested reaction showed only the B. canis vogeli product (full text)
