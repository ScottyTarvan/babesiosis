### PMID:42704380
Title: A cost-effective and rapid TE-based DNA extraction for near point-of-care detection of Babesia spp. by RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a.
Journal: Molecular biology reports | First pub: 2026-09-07 | Types: Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: BACKGROUND: Babesia spp. infects a wide range of mammals, including domestic animals, wildlife, and humans, highlighting the need for rapid diagnostics. This study developed a near point-of-care method combining rapid DNA extraction, recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA), and CRISPR-Cas12a for Babesia detection.
METHODS AND RESULTS: The workflow includes three steps: DNA extraction, RPA amplification, and CRISPR-Cas12a signal detection. Five blood DNA extraction methods were evaluated: DNeasy kit, boiling, Tris-EDTA (TE) buffer, methanol-based, and Chelex®100. The commercial kit, boiling method, and TE buffer extraction yielded positive results using RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a based on the cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (COI) gene. The TE buffer extraction and boiling methods were further optimized by RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a assay using Babesia-specific primers based on the 18S rRNA gene. All these extraction methods produced results comparable to the commercial kit. The TE buffer extraction was selected due to its simplicity and speed. The RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a assay specifically detected Babesia spp., including B. vogeli, B. gibsoni, and B. ovata. The limit of detection using TE-extracted DNA was 104 copies per reaction, lower than that achieved with the commercial kit (103 copies per reaction). Evaluation of rapid DNA extraction for Babesia spp. detection by the RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a assay using seven bovine and eight canine blood samples showed complete concordance between the TE buffer extraction and the commercial kit, with six positive samples detected.
CONCLUSIONS: The TE buffer extraction offered a low-cost, simple, and equipment-minimal approach suitable for combination with RPA/CRISPR-Cas12a diagnostics, enabling rapid detection of Babesia spp. in resource-limited or field settings.

### PMID:42797981
Title: Mesenteric Abscess Complicated by Septic Peritonitis in a Dog: Multimodal Diagnosis, Surgical Management, and Serial Quantitative Bacterial Load Monitoring.
Journal: Veterinary sciences | First pub: 2026-09-11 | Types: Case Reports, Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: Mesenteric abscesses are an uncommon cause of septic peritonitis in dogs, and serial quantitative monitoring of bacterial load in peritoneal exudate has rarely been reported. This case report describes the diagnostic work-up and treatment response of a 14-month-old, 33 kg intact male Cane Corso with a mesenteric abscess complicated by septic peritonitis, two weeks after treatment for babesiosis. The dog presented with a 72 h history of anorexia, vomiting, and abdominal pain. Abdominal radiography revealed decreased peritoneal serosal contrast and findings suggestive of foreign material ingestion. Serial ultrasonography identified a mesenteric abscess with peritoneal effusion. Exploratory laparotomy confirmed the diagnosis; the abscess was excised, and the abdominal lavage was performed. Histopathology confirmed a mesenteric abscess with marked neutrophilic infiltration. Bacterial culture isolated Escherichia coli and Enterococcus faecium, with antimicrobial therapy guided by susceptibility testing. Serial quantitative bacterial counts of the peritoneal exudate on three postoperative occasions showed a progressive decline, paralleling clinical and hematological improvement. The dog fully recovered, with complete clinical and laboratory normalization confirmed at the 60-day reassessment. This case suggests that a multimodal diagnostic approach may assist in guiding management and confirming treatment response in canine mesenteric abscess with septic peritonitis, although the role of serial quantitative monitoring requires further evaluation.

### PMID:42882950
Title: Host-Specific Circulation and Molecular Diversity of Babesia, Hepatozoon and Bartonella Species Among Wild Carnivores in Northwestern Italy.
Journal: Animals : an open access journal from MDPI | First pub: 2026-09-22 | Types: Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: Vector-borne diseases (VBDs) represent a concern for animal and public health, and wildlife surveillance is valuable for their monitoring. The occurrence, molecular diversity and host distribution of tick-borne protozoa (TBP: Babesia spp., Theileria spp. and Hepatozoon spp.) and Bartonella spp. were investigated in wild carnivores from three regions of northwestern Italy. Spleen samples from 690 wild carnivores were screened for TBP and/or Bartonella spp. by PCR followed by sequencing. Among the 503 carnivores screened for TBP, 197 animals (39.2%) tested positive for at least one targeted protozoan species, including 87 positives for Babesia spp. (17.3%) and 120 for Hepatozoon spp. (23.9%). Bartonella spp. DNA was additionally detected in only one of the 363 animals tested (0.3%). Sequencing identified Babesia sp. badger types A and C in badgers, B. vulpes in foxes and B. canis in wolves, Hepatozoon canis in wolves and foxes, H. martis in stone martens and Hepatozoon sp. in stone and pine martens. Phylogenetic analyses supported the host-associated clustering of the detected lineages, confirming the molecular identification. These findings indicate the frequent occurrence of different TBP in wild carnivores from northwestern Italy, with marked host associated patterns, contributing to our understanding of VBD epidemiology with a One Health approach.

### PMID:42868802
Title: Tick-borne pathogens in Finnish wildlife: The role of non-native raccoon dog and European hare.
Journal: Ticks and tick-borne diseases | First pub: 2026-10-13 | Types: Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: The role of different host species in the circulation dynamics of tick-borne pathogens (TBPs) in northern ecosystems remains insufficiently characterized, despite the widespread distribution of these pathogens in European wildlife. In particular, the contribution of medium-sized mammals, including non-native species, to TBP circulation is poorly understood. We screened 446 skin samples from nine wildlife species in Finland for multiple zoonotic TBPs to assess pathogen occurrence across host species with particular emphasis on the invasive raccoon dog (Nyctereutes procyonoides) and non-native European hare (Lepus europaeus). Borrelia burgdorferi s.l., Anaplasma phagocytophilum, and Rickettsia helvetica were the most frequently detected pathogens, with prevalence varying among host species. Babesia spp. was predominantly detected in ungulates, whereas Spiroplasma ixodetis and Borrelia miyamotoi were rare. Raccoon dogs and European hares showed generally low pathogen prevalence, while the highest TBP prevalences were observed in squirrels, hedgehogs, and ungulates. We also screened for two Ixodes-associated endosymbionts (Rickettsiella spp. and Midichloria mitochondrii) as potential indicators of tick exposure. Although Rickettsiella spp. was associated with TBP infections, the low prevalence of both endosymbionts suggests their limited utility as tick bite markers. These findings provide a cross-sectional overview of TBP occurrence in Finnish wildlife and demonstrate that host-pathogen associations vary among species. The relatively low pathogen prevalence observed in the raccoon dog and European hare suggests that these species are unlikely to play a major role in maintaining TBP transmission in northern ecosystems, although they may contribute to tick abundance and dispersal.

### PMID:42873390
Title: Comprehensive Assessment of Babesia gibsoni Infection in Dogs: Clinicopathological, Imaging, and Oxidative Stress Perspectives.
Journal: Acta parasitologica | First pub: 2026-10-14 | Types: Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: PURPOSE: The present study investigated clinico-pathological alterations associated with naturally occurring Babesia gibsoni infection in dogs from Mizoram, India.
METHODS: Diagnosis was established using rapid antibody detection, microscopic blood smear examination, and polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Blood samples were collected to study the haemogram, and serum samples were used for the evaluation of serum biochemical profile and oxidant-antioxidant indices. Abdominal ultrasonography was performed in the infected dogs.
RESULTS: Of 138 samples screened, 43 (31.15%), 70 (50.72%), and 85 (61.59%) were positive by rapid antibody test, blood smear examination, and PCR, respectively. The most consistent clinical signs included depression/lethargy, inappetence/anorexia, and pyrexia. Splenomegaly and splenic lesions were the most consistent ultrasonographic findings noticed. Major haemato-biochemical alterations comprised anaemia, leukocytosis with neutrophilia and eosinophilia, thrombocytopaenia, hypoproteinaemia, hypoalbuminaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, azotaemia, and increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels. Dogs with B. gibsoni infection exhibited significantly (P < 0.001) higher lipid peroxide (LPO) levels and significantly (P < 0.001) lower total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and reduced glutathione (GSH). Red blood cell (RBC) count showed a negative correlation with LPO (r = - 0.741; P < 0.01) and a positive correlation with GSH (r = 0.714; P < 0.01) and T-AOC (r = 0.828; P < 0.01), indicating a role of oxidant-antioxidant imbalance in the severity of anaemia.
CONCLUSION: These findings underscore the multiorgan involvement of the disease and suggest a potential role of oxidative stress in the pathogenesis of B. gibsoni-induced anaemia in dogs.

### PMID:42906313
Title: Development and validation of a recombinant chimeric antigen based on Leishmania infantum B-cell epitopes for canine serology.
Journal: Veterinary parasitology | First pub: 2026-10-18 | Types: Journal Article | Lang: eng | Src: MED
Abstract: This study describes the development and validation of a novel multi-epitope chimeric protein (rChi) for the serodiagnosis of canine leishmaniasis caused by Leishmania infantum (CanL). The rChi construct was engineered based on B-cell epitopes from hypothetical proteins previously identified through immunoproteomic profiling. To evaluate its diagnostic performance, an indirect ELISA was conducted using a comprehensive panel of canine serum samples (n = 291), including clinically affected, subclinically infected, and non-infected dogs, as well as Leish-Tec®-vaccinated animals and those infected with potentially cross-reactive pathogens (Babesia vogeli, Ehrlichia canis, and Trypanosoma cruzi). The rChi-ELISA demonstrated superior diagnostic parameters compared to L. infantum soluble Leishmania antigen (SLA), achieving 92.2% sensitivity and 76.2% specificity (AUC = 0.85; Accuracy = 80.8%), whereas SLA showed only 50.0% sensitivity and 68.3% accuracy. These findings indicate that the rChi protein significantly enhances CanL detection and represents a promising tool for more accurate veterinary diagnostics and surveillance.
