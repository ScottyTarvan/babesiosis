### CAR — Cardiac injury and haemodynamics

**Question this direction asks**

Does canine babesiosis injure the myocardium or impair cardiac function, and how often? Do the natriuretic peptide, troponin, ECG and echocardiography findings add prognostic information beyond anaemia and other organ dysfunction? Is low arterial pressure in complicated disease a cardiac or a circulatory problem?

**Evidence base**

- 18 primary articles, all of natural infection or necropsy material. None is an experiment, and none uses multivariable or survival analysis. Search and screening are described in the [search log](search-log.md).
- By design: 3 case-control biomarker or clinical studies ([CB434](articles/CB434_2012_nt-probnp-cardiac-troponin-i-virulent.md), [CB014](articles/CB014_2017_cardiac-disorders-infected-babesia-canis.md), [CB261](articles/CB261_2024_babesiosis-li-k-peklerde-kardiyak-troponin.md)); 1 correlation study ([CB483](articles/CB483_2014_increased-serum-urea-creatinine-ratio-negative.md)); 2 necropsy series ([CB497](articles/CB497_2015_cardiac-lesions-babesiosis.md), [CB146](articles/CB146_2026_cardiac-involvement-european-babesiosis-pathological-perspective.md)); 4 descriptive ECG or echocardiography series ([CB159](articles/CB159_2017_electrocardiographic-evaluation-anemic-blood-parasitosis.md), [CB244](articles/CB244_2024_electrocardiographic-changes-babesiosis-ehrlichiosis-affected.md), [CB263](articles/CB263_2024_transthoracic-echocardiographic-assessmentof-cardiac-dysfunction-babesiosis.md), [CB289](articles/CB289_2025_prevalence-different-types-arrhythmias-babesiosis-ehrlichiosis.md)); 2 other descriptive series where the heart is secondary ([CB219](articles/CB219_2022_effect-external-blood-parasites-fertility-german.md), [CB311](articles/CB311_2026_infectious-aetiopathology-peripheral-oedema-clinico-pathological.md)); 4 single case reports ([CB526](articles/CB526_2016_unique-case-babesia-gibsoni-infected-paraplegia.md), [CB284](articles/CB284_2025_myocardial-dysfunction-due-babesiosis.md), [CB216](articles/CB216_2022_clinical-management-babesiosis-mongeral-case-report.md), [CB266](articles/CB266_2025_diagnosis-treatment-associated-complications-labrador-retriever.md)); 2 narrative reviews ([CB259](articles/CB259_2024_complications-multiple-organ-dysfunctions-babesiosis-review.md), [CB318](articles/CB318_2026_review-clinical-utility-haemato-biochemical-biomarkers.md)).
- By species: B. canis 5; B. rossi 1 (CB434, suspected); B. gibsoni 6 (CB526, CB284, CB266, CB311, the survey CB289 which is mostly B. gibsoni, and the review CB318); Babesia unspecified 5; multi-species review 1.
- Round 4 (2006-2016): 4 articles (CB434, CB483, CB497, CB526). Three are on topic, and they supply the only B. rossi cardiac biomarker study and the largest B. canis necropsy series.
- Abstract-only: 13 of 18. Full text was read for CB284, CB289, CB311, CB259 and CB318.
- Independent sources: 17. CB263 and CB284 share cohort CH18 (the same Chennai critical care unit, probably the same dogs). CB261 (CH15) and CB497 (CH38) each share a cohort with an article outside this category.
- Only CB434, CB014 and CB261 have healthy controls. CB159, CB244 and CB289 pool Babesia with Ehrlichia.
- The category assignment is automatic (NLP). CB526 (paraplegia), CB219 (sex hormones) and CB216 (a case of haemolysis with no cardiac result) look off-topic. CB311 (peripheral oedema, 4 of 30 dogs B. gibsoni) and the two low-confidence reviews are only partly on topic. They are not reclassified here.
- The secondary digest adds 23 articles. Only a few carry cardiac data (CB368, CB518, CB234, CB080), and the rest are reviews or general clinicopathology.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- Myocardial stress and injury markers: in 45 dogs in three severity groups of 15, median NT-proBNP was 246, 650 and 638 pmol/l against 106 pmol/l in 15 controls. Median cTnI was 0.39, 0.4 and 1.45 ng/ml, and only the complicated group differed from controls ([CB434](articles/CB434_2012_nt-probnp-cardiac-troponin-i-virulent.md); abstract only). In 50 B. canis dogs, troponin I was raised in 19 and CK-MB in all (23.17-369.62 U/L); CK-MB is not cardiac-specific in dogs (CB014). hs-cTnI and hs-cTnT were higher in 15 PCR-confirmed B. canis dogs than in 10 controls (p<0.01 and p<0.05; no concentrations given) (CB261; CH15). A B. gibsoni case had raised cTnI and a reported valvular lesion, with competing explanations (CB266).
- Echocardiography: 31 of 92 critical care dogs had dysfunction (24 diastolic, 2 systolic, 5 both) (CB263; CH18). One fatal B. gibsoni case had systolic and diastolic dysfunction with myocardial cell death and haemorrhage at histology (CB284; CH18).
- ECG: sinus tachycardia in 20 of 23 anaemic dogs and ST deviation in 5 (CB159, pooled with E. canis). Low QRS voltage and tall T waves in 6 of 15 dogs each (CB244, pooled). In a survey of 465 smear-positive dogs, 108 (23.2%) had ECG abnormalities, mainly tachycardia (44.4%), low-voltage QRS (20.3%) and atrial standstill (16.6%); its subgroup counts do not add up (58+35+13=106) (CB289). Heart rate was higher in 63 smear-positive than in 40 smear-negative Nigerian dogs ([CB518](articles/CB518_2016_reliability-clinical-monitoring-diagnosis-babesiosis-nigeria.md)). Tachycardia was universal in 16 cases ([CB234](articles/CB234_2023_physiological-clinico-hemato-biochemical-changes-babesiosis.md)). Anaemic B. rossi dogs were tachycardic at presentation ([CB368](articles/CB368_2009_prospective-randomized-comparison-oxyglobin-hb-200.md); 12 dogs).
- Necropsy: of 20 Croatian B. canis dogs, 6 had pericardial effusion, 12 had epicardial, endocardial or myocardial haemorrhage, 16 had interfibre oedema, 6 had focal cardiomyocyte necrosis and 4 had myocarditis (CB497; CH38). Zagreb archive cases showed haemorrhage, effusion, intravascular monocytic leukostasis and necrosis (CB146; n not given). Six Polish B. canis dogs had cardiomyocyte necrosis with multi-organ congestion and effusions ([CB080](articles/CB080_2021_disorders-blood-circulation-probable-cause-death.md); CH27). All of these describe fatal disease.
- Arterial pressure: in 48 B. canis dogs, urea:creatinine ratio correlated negatively with systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure (r -0.3182 to -0.3909). There was no significant correlation in the 23 azotaemic dogs alone (CB483). Blood pressure changes after Oxyglobin and red cell transfusion were similar over 72 hours (CB368).

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Clustering of parasitised erythrocytes in myocardial capillaries was seen in 4 of 20 dogs and offered as a cause of haemorrhage (CB497). CB146 infers microvascular injury from leukostasis. CB080 proposes circulatory failure as the probable cause of death. CB289 attributes ECG changes to anaemic hypoxia, inflammatory injury and metabolic disturbance. CB483 suggests hypotension contributes only in part to the raised urea:creatinine ratio. CB311 raises vasculitis from Doppler findings in 6 dogs without histology, and it is not linked to Babesia. None was tested against a cardiac endpoint.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- NT-proBNP was higher in the two more severe groups than in the mild group (CB434). In CB014, the two deaths had the highest CK-MB (367.33 and 369.62 U/L) and markedly high troponin I. In CB263, 8 of 31 dogs with dysfunction survived (26%), but survival without dysfunction is not given. Reviews list cardiac dysfunction among complications linked to poorer outcome ([CB389](articles/CB389_2010_clinical-management-babesiosis.md), [CB493](articles/CB493_2015_babesiosis-perspective-clinical-complications-biomarkers-treatment.md)). CB318 quotes a troponin accuracy figure taken from human myocardial infarction work.

**Convergence and conflict**

- *B. rossi* cardiac evidence dates from 2012: CB434 (South Africa) shows NT-proBNP rising with severity in suspected *B. rossi* disease. No *B. rossi* necropsy or echocardiography study was found, and *B. vogeli* is uncharacterised.
- Troponin is raised in B. canis (CB014; CB261, independent groups) and in the most severe B. rossi group (CB434). The proportion differs: 19 of 50 dogs in CB014, but only the complicated group differed from controls in CB434. Assays and severity grading differ, so the two are not directly comparable.
- Four necropsy sources agree that fatal cases show myocardial haemorrhage, oedema or necrosis (CB497, CB146, CB080, CB284). CB497 and CB146 are both Croatian and overlap is not stated. Samples are fatal cases only, so lesion frequency in survivors is unknown.
- ECG series agree only on non-specific changes (tachycardia, low voltage, T-wave and ST change), which anaemia, fever and hypoxia can explain. Sinus arrhythmia can be normal in dogs. Three series pool Ehrlichia.
- Interpretation conflicts: CB014 judges cardiac changes of limited clinical importance. CB263 reports 26% survival with dysfunction, but calls dysfunction a "good" prognostic indicator, probably a wording error. CB497 concludes that B. canis is about as pathogenic as B. rossi; no study in this category tests that.
- The only haemodynamic data in this category are CB483 and CB368.

**Unresolved questions**

- Do admission NT-proBNP and cTnI predict death after adjusting for PCV, creatinine and lactate, in a prospective PCR-speciated cohort? CB434 gives only group differences in 15 dogs per group.
- Does NT-proBNP rise before, or independently of, cTnI? Serial samples with renal markers would test this.
- Is the diastolic-predominant dysfunction in CB263 intrinsic or a loading effect of anaemia and fluids? Serial tissue-Doppler echocardiography before and after transfusion would show it.
- Is cardiac biomarker elevation species-dependent (B. rossi, B. canis, B. gibsoni, B. vogeli) when measured with one assay?
- Do ECG changes in PCR-confirmed Babesia-only dogs differ from matched controls after adjusting for PCV and heart rate?
- Does low arterial pressure reflect hypovolaemia, vasodilation or pump failure? Paired MAP, lactate and echocardiography, with troponin in non-azotaemic dogs, would partition it (extending CB483).
- Does myocardial injury resolve in survivors? Troponin kinetics through treatment are not reported.
