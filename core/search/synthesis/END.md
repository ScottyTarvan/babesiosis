### END — Endothelium, microcirculation, perfusion and tissue hypoxia

**Question this direction asks**

Is organ dysfunction in canine babesiosis driven by microvascular events (sequestration, endothelial activation or injury, vascular leak, hypovolaemic or distributive shock, tissue hypoxia) rather than by parasite burden or anaemia alone? Can hypoperfusion be measured ante-mortem and linked to outcome?

**Evidence base**

The category assignment is automatic (NLP classifier, no human review). The base is 14 primary articles (2009-2025; 4 from round 4, 2006-2016) and 35 secondary-tagged articles (15 from round 4). Search methods are in the [search log](search-log.md).

Primary articles by design:
- 6 case reports or very small series (n=1 to 4): [CB454](articles/CB454_2013_generalized-alopecia-vasculitis-like-changes-babesiosis.md), [CB182](articles/CB182_2019_histopathologial-alterations-babesia-gibsoni-infectiona-case.md), [CB066](articles/CB066_2020_babesiosis-disease-rarely-considered-context-male.md), [CB140](articles/CB140_2025_fatal-coinfection-hepatozoon-canis-babesia-vogeli.md), [CB271](articles/CB271_2025_diagnosis-management-splenic-torsion-cane-corso.md), [CB283](articles/CB283_2025_vasculitis-associated-ehrlichia-canis-babesia-canis.md).
- 2 histopathology series: [CB087](articles/CB087_2021_morphofunctional-changes-spleen-components-pathogenetic-substantiation.md) and [CB208](articles/CB208_2021_role-significance-morphological-changes-intraorgan-vessels.md) (CH26).
- 1 each: retrospective case series ([CB372](articles/CB372_2009_septic-shock-babesiosis.md), CH33); blood-pressure case-control ([CB487](articles/CB487_2014_arterial-blood-pressure-values-naturally-infected.md)); clinicopathological case-control ([CB022](articles/CB022_2018_haemato-biochemical-changes-infected-babesia-sp.md)); infusion comparison ([CB200](articles/CB200_2020_plasma-replacement-solutions-intensive-care-unit.md), CH13); proteomic case-control ([CB122](articles/CB122_2024_characterization-lc-ms-ms-based-proteomic.md)); experimental method study in 2 beagles ([CB397](articles/CB397_2010_babesia-gibsoni-detection-blood-smears-formalin.md)).

By evidence type, 12 are descriptive and 2 associational (CB487, CB122). None is mechanistic or prognostic.

By species: 4 B. canis (CB487, CB066, CB122, CB283), 4 B. gibsoni (CB397, CB454, CB182; CB022 suspected), 1 B. vogeli inside a four-pathogen co-infection (CB140), and 5 unspecified (CB372, CB200, CB087, CB208, CB271). CB208 pools dogs with wild rodents. No primary article is on B. rossi.

Ten are abstract-only (CB372, CB487, CB182, CB200, CB087, CB208, CB122, CB140, CB271, CB283); four have full text. Peer review of CB200 and CB208 was not verified.

There are 13 independent sources. CB087 and CB208 are one source (CH26). CB200 (CH13) and CB372 (CH33) have cohort partners outside this category, so none of these is replication.

Five primary articles have low NLP confidence (CB372, CB397, CB087, CB208, CB271). Five look off-topic for END, because their main finding does not concern vessels or perfusion: CB022 (haematology), CB066 (infertility), CB140 (co-infection), CB271 (splenic torsion) and CB397 (probe method).

No primary article measured endothelial function, microcirculatory flow or tissue oxygenation. The secondary set adds arterial pressure, lactate and central venous PO2.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- **Shock and hypotension:** in CB372 (Zagreb, 2007), 10 of 86 babesiosis admissions met septic-shock criteria, which included hypotension unresponsive to treatment. Kidney was involved in 9, and all 10 died within 1 to 4 days, against 2.6% mortality in the other cases. In CB487 (B. canis; 12 complicated, 18 uncomplicated, 20 healthy) mean arterial pressure was 60 in complicated cases, 116 in uncomplicated and 106 in healthy dogs. [CB463](articles/CB463_2014_association-between-decreased-blood-pressure-azotaemia.md) found MAP below 80 mmHg in 7 of 48 infected dogs. CB200 (CH13, species unspecified) reports a circulating-volume deficit of about 30%, a shock index about twice normal and more erythrocyte and platelet aggregation. [CB220](articles/CB220_2022_features-hepatopathy-hematological-complications-acute-spontaneous.md) reports deficits of 24% (anaemic form) and 34% (icteric form).
- **Oxygen delivery:** in [CB368](articles/CB368_2009_prospective-randomized-comparison-oxyglobin-hb-200.md) (B. rossi, 12 anaemic dogs) tachycardia and low central venous PO2 were present on admission. Oxyglobin and red-cell transfusion gave broadly similar results over 72 hours.
- **Post-mortem and histology:**
  - [CB341](articles/CB341_2007_histological-ultrastructural-studies-renal-lesions-babesia.md) (B. canis, 8 fatal cases): tubular necrosis and degeneration of glomerular and interstitial capillary endothelium.
  - [CB497](articles/CB497_2015_cardiac-lesions-babesiosis.md) and [CB522](articles/CB522_2016_septic-shock-similarities-differences-postmortem-findings.md) (CH38, B. canis): haemorrhage in 12 of 20, pericardial effusion in 6 of 20 and endomysial oedema in 16 of 20; in 5 dogs, leukostasis and oedema.
  - CB182 (B. gibsoni, n=1): congestion, oedema and tubular necrosis.
  - [CB189](articles/CB189_2020_encephalic-babesiosis-clinical-epidemiological-pathological-aspects.md) (n=7): cerebral endothelial hypertrophy, leukostasis, thrombosis and vasculitis.
  - [CB279](articles/CB279_2025_molecular-diagnosis-histopathological-analysis-cerebral-babesiosis.md) (B. gibsoni, CH16): vessel occlusion with piroplasms in 3 necropsies.
  - CH26: splenic endothelial shedding and microvessel wall defects with diapedesis.
- **Skin vasculitis:** CB454 (B. gibsoni) showed vasculitis-like or ischaemic skin lesions that improved with antiprotozoal treatment and relapsed off it, after prior prednisolone. CB283 describes generalised vasculitis with E. canis co-infection.
- **Sequelae:** four infertile male dogs after B. canis (CB066). [CB265](articles/CB265_2025_male-fertility-impairment-associated-babesia-gibsoni.md) found Babesia DNA in semen of 6 B. gibsoni dogs and lower sperm motility than in 8 controls, six months after treatment.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Sequestration: [CB041](articles/CB041_2019_mechanisms-involved-persistence-babesia-canis-infection.md) (review) proposes endothelial attachment with coagulation-mediated trapping. [CB335](articles/CB335_2007_babesia-gibsoni-detection-during-experimental-infections.md) detected B. gibsoni in tissue in chronic infection, which the authors say may indicate sequestration. CB397 labelled infected red cells in splenic and renal vessels but tested no adhesion. [CB010](articles/CB010_2017_genome-wide-analysis-gene-expression-protein.md) predicts 103 candidate variant surface antigen models in B. canis (CH21).
- Hypoxic injury: CB341 attributes tubular necrosis to probable hypoxia; CB522 attributes lesions to septic shock, hypoxic necrosis and DIC.
- Neurohumoral and cytokine drivers: serum TNF-alpha correlated inversely with arterial pressures in 11 dogs ([CB473](articles/CB473_2014_increased-concentration-serum-tnf-alpha-correlations.md), CH37). Aldosterone correlated negatively with pressures in [CB485](articles/CB485_2014_hyperaldosteronism-association-hypotension-azotaemia-babesiosis.md), though only 4 of 14 dogs were above the reference interval.
- Testicular microcirculatory disturbance (CB066), not tested. CB265 suggests parasite DNA in semen as an alternative.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- Pressure: lower pressures went with complicated disease (CB487, no outcome data in the abstract) and with higher creatinine and urea (CB463). MAP correlated only modestly with tonicity ([CB044](articles/CB044_2019_hyponatraemia-syndrome-inappropriate-antidiuretic-hormone-secretion.md)).
- Lactate: in [CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md) (B. canis, 7 survivors, 8 non-survivors), non-survivors had higher admission lactate and lower haematocrit. Lactate and leukocyte count separated the groups with 100% sensitivity and specificity in this small cohort (full text).
- Vesicle proteins: CB122 found 73 of 147 vesicle-associated proteins only in infected dogs, enriched for immune, integrin and VEGF annotations. Vesicle size and number did not differ.

**Convergence and conflict**

Independent sources agree on tissue congestion, oedema, effusion and haemorrhage in fatal cases: B. canis (CB341, CH38), B. gibsoni (CB182, [CB188](articles/CB188_2020_pathomorphological-parasitological-molecular-diagnosis-babesia-gibsoni.md)), and unspecified species (CH26, CB189). These lesions are not specific. CB522 found most organ lesions in 5 babesiosis dogs resembled those in 5 leptospirosis dogs.

Hypotension in severe B. canis disease is reported by several sources: CB372, CB487, CB463, CB485, CB473. Overlap among CB463, CB485 and CB473 is not assessed (only CB473 has a cohort). Most are small and not outcome-linked. Hypotension is not universal. Uncomplicated cases in CB487 had higher pressure than healthy dogs, CB463 found MAP below 80 in only 7 of 48, and CB200 describes hypotension in moderate shock.

Mortality in CB372 follows partly from its definition, which includes treatment-resistant hypotension. Coagulation claims in CB208 (DIC, thrombosis) rest on histology without coagulation tests.

What the 2006–2016 evidence adds:
- Shock evidence is not limited to one cohort. Several teams report B. canis hypotension and septic shock (CB372, CB487, CB463, CB485, CB473), though none measured perfusion directly.
- The TNF-alpha, hypotension and renal ischaemia proposal now has a primary association (CB473, 11 dogs), not only a review.
- Lactate has been evaluated: CB516, in *B. canis*. Central venous PO2 appears in CB368 (B. rossi). Both are systemic markers, not tissue measures.
- *B. rossi* tissue and coagulation findings (for example CB080, CB145, CB111, CB147) sit in other categories under the automatic assignment; see INF, COA and PUL.
- B. vogeli still has no data on this axis, except as a co-infection in CB140.

**Unresolved questions**

- Does admission MAP below 80 mmHg (the threshold in CB463) predict death in B. canis, in a cohort with recorded outcome? CB487 reports none in its abstract.
- Does the CB516 lactate cut-off hold in a new B. canis cohort, and in B. rossi, after adjustment for haematocrit?
- Is the hypotension hypovolaemic, distributive or cardiac? Measure blood volume by indicator dilution with echocardiography. The CB200 and CB220 volume-deficit methods are not stated in the abstracts, and CB497 found myocardial lesions in all 20 dogs.
- Do TNF-alpha and aldosterone predict pressure independently of azotaemia, given that CB463 links pressure to creatinine and urea?
- Is sequestration demonstrable? Compare tissue parasite load (qPCR or in situ hybridisation with proper controls) with peripheral parasitaemia in matched organs.
- Are the endothelial lesions of CB087, CB208 and CB341 specific to Babesia? Use blinded semi-quantitative scoring in dogs only, with non-babesial controls.
- Is the vasculitis in CB454 and CB283 due to Babesia, co-infection or immune-mediated disease? Take skin biopsies at first presentation with PCR for co-infections.
