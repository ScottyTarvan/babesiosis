### NEU — Neurological and cerebral disease

**Question this direction asks**

How often do infected dogs develop neurological dysfunction, what lesions underlie it, and what drives it (sequestration, endothelial or inflammatory injury, metabolic or toxic factors)? Is it a distinct, high-mortality phenotype that could be recognised earlier?

**Evidence base**

Eight primary articles (2006-2026). Four are round 4 papers (2006-2016): CB437, CB450, CB507 and CB535. The category assignment is automatic (NLP), with no human review, and was not changed here. Searches are described in the [search log](search-log.md).

- By design:

  - Three clinical case series, all from one cohort: [CB279](articles/CB279_2025_molecular-diagnosis-histopathological-analysis-cerebral-babesiosis.md), [CB300](articles/CB300_2026_therapeutic-studies-post-therapeutic-follow-up.md), [CB313](articles/CB313_2026_neuroclinical-features-epidemiology-diagnosis-cerebral-babesiosis.md).
  - One descriptive imaging study, [CB535](articles/CB535_2016_magnetic-resonance-imaging-brain-involvement-using.md).
  - One narrative review, [CB507](articles/CB507_2015_babesiosis.md).
  - No primary article is a controlled or mechanistic study.
- By species: *B. gibsoni* 5 (four from India, one with no country given), *B. canis* 2 (Poland; CB535 country not stated), and one multi-species review.
- Text read: four are abstract-only (CB437, CB450, CB535, CB274); four had full text.
- Independent sources: five original-data sources. CB437, CB450, CB535 and CB274 are separate; CB279, CB300 and CB313 are one source, cohort CH16 (Proddatur, Andhra Pradesh; 620 screened, 136 smear-positive). CB507 adds no original data. They are not replication.

The others mention neurological terms in passing.
- No *B. rossi* primary article is in NEU. *B. rossi* neurological evidence here is secondary and review-level only.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- CH16 (*B. gibsoni*, India): 11 of 136 smear-positive dogs (8.1%) were classed as cerebral, defined by parasites in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) erythrocytes (CB313, CB300). CB313 notes that dogs were selected for signs, so the percentages are not prevalences.
  - Signs: ataxia, seizures, paddling, opisthotonus, head pressing, stupor, nystagmus, blindness, paraplegia.
  - Only 2 of the 11 had recovered at post-treatment assessment; full text gives 9 of 11 dead by day 28 (about 82%) on diminazene-based polytherapy (CB300).
  - Anaemia was mild (Hb about 10 g/dl in the full text) despite severe signs (CB300).
- CB279 (same cohort) counts 24 cerebral cases among the same 136 dogs, with the *B. gibsoni* PCR product also found in CSF. CB300 and CB313 count 11. The reason is not given.
- Single cases:
  - *B. canis*, Poland: neurological signs with pancytopenia; died despite treatment. 18S rRNA sequence matched genotype 18S RNA-B, which the authors call the more virulent Polish strain (abstract only; no comparative data) (CB437).
  - *B. gibsoni*: depression, ataxia and seizures; reported good response to one dose of diminazene at 7.5 mg/kg. Species and cerebral involvement not confirmed in the abstract (CB450).
  - *B. gibsoni* (smear only), India: a ten-year-old dog with ataxia and lethargy and no haematological crisis improved on atovaquone and azithromycin. No neurological work-up is described (CB274).
- Reviews: cerebral babesiosis is listed among complicated forms (CB493), and neurological dysfunction is said to go with a poorer outcome (CB389). The digest does not give the source studies.

*Lesions and imaging.*
- CH16, 3 necropsies (CB279): brain and meningeal congestion, then oedema, neuronal necrosis, microglial proliferation, neutrophil infiltration, and small vessels occluded by cells with piroplasms in cerebral vessels.
- Retrospective necropsy series, 7 hyperacute fatal cases, species unspecified (CB189): endothelial hypertrophy, monocytic leukostasis, meningitis, thrombosis, haemorrhage and vasculitis. Parasite load poorly explained death.
- Contrast MRI in severe *B. canis* (CB535): CNS lesions seen in every case, with enhancement at lesion rims. Amplification index 1.19 ± 0.11 centrally and 1.47 ± 0.17 at the periphery; pituitary 1.18 ± 0.05; lateral ventricle plexuses 1.12 ± 0.09. Number of dogs, controls and histology are not in the abstract.
- CB437 reports brain histopathology and PCR at post mortem, but the abstract gives no findings.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Microvascular occlusion, thrombosis and congestion, inferred from histology (CB279). The explanation for poor treatment response was asserted, not tested.
- An antigen-driven systemic response rather than parasite load (CB189). No cytokine or coagulation data support it.
- Strain virulence (genotype 18S RNA-B) from one dog (CB437).
- Sequestration and hypoxia are stated in CB313 as literature, without data.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- The only outcome figure is the CH16 mortality above, in 11 dogs without a comparison group.
- No article in this category measured CRP, lactate, coagulation, cytokine or neural-injury markers in neurologically affected dogs.

**Convergence and conflict**

- Lesion pattern: two necropsy sources (CB189; CH16 via CB279) describe vascular and inflammatory change with thrombosis, oedema or haemorrhage. They cover 10 brains. Only CH16 confirmed species. CB535 shows focal lesions in vivo in *B. canis*, but cannot say what kind of lesion.
- Parasite load: CB189 found load a poor explanation of death. CB279 saw piroplasms in occluded vessels. The two differ in species, method and case selection, so this is not a clean contradiction.
- Anaemia: CB300 reports mild anaemia with severe signs, whereas CB437 had pancytopenia. No study compared cerebral with non-cerebral dogs.
- Outcome: CH16 shows about 82% death by day 28. The three single-case reports describe recovery (CB450, CB274) or death (CB437). Case reports favour survivors and have unclear case definitions, so they cannot test the CH16 figure.
- Internal conflicts in CH16: 24 versus 11 cerebral cases. CB313 and CB300 give no CSF sampling details or CSF data, and CB279 warns that CSF erythrocytes may be blood contamination.
- Species: *B. canis* and *B. gibsoni* both produce cerebral syndromes, but the evidence is single cases and one imaging abstract. Mechanisms are not shown to be shared.

**Unresolved questions**

- Is the CSF-parasite case definition valid? Test with paired CSF and blood qPCR load, CSF red cell counts, and the CSF sampling method, and explain why CH16 gives 24 and 11 cases.
- Does brain parasite burden (immunohistochemistry or qPCR) predict signs or death, or is it independent of endothelial lesions (CB189 versus CB279)?
- What do the MRI lesions in *B. canis* (CB535) represent? Pair imaging with histology and report the number of dogs.
- Is genotype 18S RNA-B associated with neurological signs? Genotype consecutive Polish dogs and record neurological status (CB437).
- Do cerebral dogs differ in Hb, platelets and parasitaemia from non-cerebral dogs of the same cohort, given the mild anaemia in CB300?
- Does treatment change survival in CSF-positive dogs? CH16 has no comparison arm.
- Do ataxia cases without CSF or imaging (CB274) have cerebral babesiosis at all once other causes are excluded?
