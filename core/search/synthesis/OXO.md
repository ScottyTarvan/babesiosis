### OXO — Oxidative stress, host-parasite interaction, omics and virulence

**Question this direction asks**
Does an oxidant-antioxidant imbalance accompany, or drive, erythrocyte and organ injury in canine babesiosis? Which parasite factors and genotypes separate mild from severe disease? Secondarily: do antioxidant adjuncts change recovery? Host omics belong to this label, but the evidence in it is now almost wholly parasite-side.

**Evidence base**
- 21 primary articles (2006-2026), plus 16 articles that carry OXO as a secondary NLP tag. Searches are in the [search log](search-log.md).
- By design: 9 redox or biomarker case-control studies or case series; 1 prognostic case-control study; 3 randomised treatment studies (2 antioxidant, 1 antiparasitic); 3 experimental infections (parasite genome, parasite peroxiredoxin, drug and detection trial); 1 parasite-genotype study with retrospective clinical comparison; 1 clinical survey; 3 narrative reviews.
- By evidence type: 10 associational, 5 descriptive, 2 mechanistic (experimental), 1 prognostic, 3 review.
- By species: *B. gibsoni* 8, *B. canis* 6 (one of them a survey of mixed *B. canis*-like cases), *B. vogeli* 2, *B. rossi* 1, *Babesia* sp. unspecified 1, multi-species reviews 3.
- Round 4 (2006-2016): 9 articles (CB335, CB353, CB379, CB453, CB477, CB481, CB499, CB515, CB529). Abstract-only: 16 of 21; full text was read for 5 (CB529, CB010, CB077, CB229, CB135).
- Independent sources: 18 primary studies, with no two primary articles in the same cohort. The cohorts CH08 (CB074), CH14 (CB229), CH21 (CB010) and CH25 (CB077) each have other members outside this category (CB202 and CB286; CB242; CB516; CB069). The 3 reviews add no data. CB142 and CB151 (both NAC in *B. gibsoni*) and CB051 and secondary CB154 (both Mizoram) have no assigned cohort; overlap is not stated.
- The category assignment is automatic (NLP). Four primary articles are low confidence (CB453, CB076, CB142, CB151). CB335, CB453 and CB529 look off-topic for OXO: they are detection, survey and antiparasitic treatment studies. CB074 and CB287 look closer to SEV and ERY (the agents' labels). None has been reclassified.

**What has been studied**
*Syndromes.* Anaemia is the commonest presentation in the redox studies, for example 15 PCR-confirmed *B. gibsoni* cases in Mizoram ([CB051](articles/CB051_2020_molecular-diagnosis-clinico-hemato-biochemical-alterations.md)) and eight *B. canis* cases with severe thrombocytopenia ([CB076](articles/CB076_2021_relationship-between-changes-hematological-parameters-levels.md)). Complicated disease and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) appear in [CB499](articles/CB499_2015_lipid-peroxidation-naturally-infected-babesia-canis.md) and, in secondary evidence, [CB006](articles/CB006_2017_relation-antioxidant-status-admission-disease-severity.md) (5 MODS dogs, 4 deaths). The French survey [CB453](articles/CB453_2013_clinical-signs-seasonal-occurrence-causative-agents.md) (836 reported cases) gives the clinical picture: lethargy and anorexia 98%, fever 80%, pallor 54%, altered urine 45%, splenomegaly 33%. Only 70 were tested and 25 (36%) were PCR-positive, so many cases may not have been babesiosis.

*Redox biomarkers (associational).*
- Lipid peroxidation was higher in infected dogs in every study that measured it. [CB353](articles/CB353_2008_erythrocytic-antioxidant-defense-lipid-peroxides-level.md) (*B. gibsoni*, 10 v 6): 6.01 v 1.89 nmol MDA/mg haemoglobin. CB499 (*B. canis canis*, 30 v 25): raised on day 1, lower but still above controls on day 7. CB051 and CB076 agree. Secondary: [CB394](articles/CB394_2010_malondialdehyde-levels-serum-infected-babesia-canis.md) (36.90 v 8.13 µmol/l, CH33), CB006, [CB083](articles/CB083_2021_molecular-characterization-assessment-hematobiochemical-oxidative-indices.md) and [CB154](articles/CB154_2026_comprehensive-assessment-babesia-gibsoni-infection-clinicopathological.md).
- Other oxidative markers: serum 8-hydroxy-2'-deoxyguanosine was higher in *B. vogeli* ([CB477](articles/CB477_2014_investigation-8-hydroxy-2-deoxyguanosine-total.md), P<0.05; group sizes not given). [CB515](articles/CB515_2015_nitric-oxide-protein-oxidation-total-antioxidant.md) found higher NOx, AOPP and FRAP overall, but only 5 dogs had *B. vogeli* alone. [CB229](articles/CB229_2023_investigation-thiol-disulphide-homeostasis-total-oxidant.md) (7 v 7, CH14): lower antioxidant capacity, higher oxidative stress index; thiol-disulphide and ischaemia-modified albumin unchanged; AUCs of 0.980 and 1.0 are probably overfitted.
- Trace elements: zinc and copper were lower in both [CB077](articles/CB077_2021_antioxidant-status-blood-zinc-copper-concentrations.md) (zinc 0.478 v 1.241, copper 0.722 v 1.392 µg/mL; 15 v 7; CH25) and CB353 (which also found lower iron, 89.87 v 126.44 µg/g). They may reflect acute-phase redistribution or anorexia.
- Paraoxonase 1: [CB481](articles/CB481_2014_relationship-between-paraoxonase-1-activity-high.md) (29 v 15) found low PON1 and HDL on admission, HDL recovering by day 1, PON1 rising and CRP falling by day 7. CB076 and secondary [CB057](articles/CB057_2020_evidence-acute-phase-reaction-asymptomatic-naturally.md) also report low PON1.
- After treatment, erythrocyte MDA stayed high at day 7 (CB499) and day 15 (CB076, n=8). Both used imidocarb, with no untreated comparison.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).* Reviews [CB114](articles/CB114_2023_host-oxidative-stress-piroplasmosis-review-domestic.md), [CB287](articles/CB287_2025_hematological-biochemical-determinants-anemic-syndrome-animals.md) and [CB319](articles/CB319_2026_host-oxidative-responses-piroplasmosis-current-perspectives.md) propose that haemoglobin oxidation and membrane peroxidation promote haemolysis and anaemia. None of the 18 primary studies tests this. CB319 cites experimental canine work on accelerated red cell clearance, but that work is not among our primary articles. CB287 lists LDH as an oxidative marker, though it mainly reflects haemolysis. No primary study measured haemolysis against oxidative markers over time. Each correlation equally fits oxidative change as a consequence of haemolysis, inflammation or anorexia.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).* In CB499, MDA correlated with outcome, was higher in complicated disease on day 7, and was higher in MODS. CB006 (secondary; 40 infected: 29 uncomplicated, 6 one complication, 5 MODS) found lower SOD, GPx and catalase and higher MDA with MODS and death. [CB074](articles/CB074_2021_prognostic-markers-their-discriminant-score-predicting.md) (*B. gibsoni*, 108 dogs, 30 controls, CH08): catalase, haemoglobin, platelets, urea, ALT, lactate and others correlated with survival; a discriminant score (cut-off 0.141) gave 79% sensitivity and 83% specificity, with no external validation. Secondary [CB202](articles/CB202_2020_serum-hepcidin-clinical-prognostic-marker-discriminate.md) (hepcidin, AUC about 0.984) is the same CH08 family and not independent. CB077 and CB229 did not analyse severity, so their prognostic claims are inferred.

*Parasite factors and virulence.*
- [CB010](articles/CB010_2017_genome-wide-analysis-gene-expression-protein.md) (CH21): genome of a virulent *B. canis* isolate with exported VESA, SA and Bc28 families, plus profilin and histones. One strain, one RNA-seq sample from a splenectomised dog, three pooled Beagles for proteomics, no avirulent comparator.
- [CB135](articles/CB135_2025_molecular-identification-thioredoxin-peroxidase-babesia-gibsoni.md): a *B. gibsoni* peroxiredoxin (BgTPx-2) protected plasmid DNA in a cell-free assay; antibody was detectable on day 4 in infected Beagles. Its role in the parasite is not tested.
- [CB379](articles/CB379_2009_preliminary-evaluation-brema1-gene-tool-associating.md) (Onderstepoort, 141 *B. rossi* samples): 12 BrEMA1 genotypes. The four commonest (19, 28, 29, 11; 36.2%, 20.6%, 20.6%, 12.7%) were also linked to the poorest prognosis. No statistics are given in the abstract.
- [CB335](articles/CB335_2007_babesia-gibsoni-detection-during-experimental-infections.md) (abstract only): parasite detected in tissues of chronically infected dogs, which the authors suggest may be sequestration. Secondary [CB369](articles/CB369_2009_detection-erythrocyte-binding-igm-igg-flow.md) (CH32): erythrocyte-bound IgG or IgM in 4 of 6 *B. c. vogeli* dogs but 0 of 24 *B. c. canis* dogs, with different anaemia types.

*Interventions.* NAC (70 mg/kg) plus triple antimicrobials in *B. gibsoni*: [CB142](articles/CB142_2025_effect-n-acetylcysteine-oxidative-stress-hematological.md) (12 randomised, 6 controls; only antioxidant capacity and MCHC differed significantly) and [CB151](articles/CB151_2026_n-acetylcysteine-adjunct-therapy-ameliorates-oxidative.md) (12 v 12; lower lipid peroxide and higher glutathione and antioxidant capacity reported; the abstract gives no effect sizes or primary-endpoint result). The antiparasitic studies, [CB529](articles/CB529_2016_therapeutic-effects-atovaquone-proguanil-combination-azithromycin.md) (8 dogs, 4 per arm) and CB335, lie outside the pathophysiology question.

**Convergence and conflict**
- Raised lipid peroxidation in infected dogs is consistent across *B. gibsoni* (CB353, CB051, CB154), *B. canis* (CB499, CB076, CB394, CB006) and *B. vogeli* (CB083). The earliest are CB353 (2008) and CB394 (2010), so the finding predates 2016. Low zinc and copper (CB353, CB077) and low PON1 (CB481, CB076, CB057) also recur.
- Enzyme direction conflicts. Erythrocyte SOD and catalase were higher in CB353 (*B. gibsoni*) and CB077 (*B. canis*) but lower in CB051 (*B. gibsoni*) and CB006. Total antioxidant capacity was lower in CB229 and CB051, only a non-significant trend in CB477, and higher (FRAP) in the mixed-infection CB515. Sampling stage, severity, sample type and assay differ, as CB319 notes.
- MDA and severity conflict: CB499 and CB006 link MDA to complicated disease or MODS, but CB394 (35 dogs, 7 complicated) found no difference by complication or anaemia grade. CB394 and CB499 are both Zagreb studies; overlap is not stated.
- CB379's genotype link cannot separate genotype from frequency and has not been replicated. CB010 has no avirulent comparator. Redox findings should not be moved between species; *B. rossi* has no redox data here, and antioxidant trials exist only for *B. gibsoni*.

**Unresolved questions**
- Does erythrocyte lipid peroxidation precede the haematocrit fall? Sample from admission, before treatment, with haemoglobin-normalised MDA, reticulocytes and haemolysis indices (CB499 started at day 1 of treatment).
- Is the SOD and catalase discordance (CB353, CB077 v CB051, CB006) an artefact of assay, normalisation or stage? Re-measure with one assay in one cohort, stratified by days of illness and severity.
- Does MDA grade severity within *B. canis*? Run a prospective cohort with MODS defined in advance, to settle CB499 and CB006 against CB394.
- Does the raised MDA after imidocarb (CB499 day 7, CB076 day 15) show ongoing red cell damage or red cell turnover? Follow beyond 15 days with reticulocyte adjustment and clinical sequelae.
- Does NAC change parasite clearance, transfusion need or survival in a blinded, adequately powered trial, with parasitaemia reported (CB142 and CB151 had 12 per arm)?
- Does BrEMA1 genotype predict outcome after adjustment for treatment delay and host factors in an independent *B. rossi* sample, and are the CB010 candidates (VESA, SA, Bc28) expressed in natural infection?
