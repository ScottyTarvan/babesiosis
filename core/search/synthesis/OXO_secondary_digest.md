- [CB357](articles/CB357_2008_babesia-canis-canis-babesia-canis-vogeli.md) 2008; B. canis canis, B. canis vogeli; clinicopathological and PCR-RFLP study of naturally infected dogs; n=45 PCR-positive of 164 blood samples tested; cohort=CH32; descriptive; primary=SEV: Dogs infected with B. c. canis mostly showed thrombocytopenia, non-regenerative anaemia, haemoglobinuria, raised C-reactive protein and hypoalbuminaemia, whereas the 11 B. c. vogeli cases showed no uniform clinicopathological pattern.
- [CB369](articles/CB369_2009_detection-erythrocyte-binding-igm-igg-flow.md) 2009; B. canis canis, B. canis vogeli; cross-sectional comparison of two Babesia groups by flow cytometry; n=30 infected (24 B. c. canis, 6 B. c. vogeli); cohort=CH32; associational; primary=INF: Erythrocyte-bound IgG and IgM were found by flow cytometry in most B. c. vogeli dogs (4 of 6) but in none of the 24 B. c. canis dogs, and the groups differed in anaemia type (mostly non-regenerative in B. c. canis, regenerative in B. c. vogeli).
- [CB394](articles/CB394_2010_malondialdehyde-levels-serum-infected-babesia-canis.md) 2010; B. canis; case-control study of serum MDA in natural infection; n=35 infected (7 complicated), 14 healthy controls; cohort=CH33; associational; primary=SEV: Serum MDA was higher in 35 dogs naturally infected with B. canis than in 14 healthy dogs (36.90 vs 8.13 μmol/l), but did not differ between complicated and uncomplicated cases or across anaemia grades.
- [CB452](articles/CB452_2013_altera-es-cl-nico-hematol-gicas.md) 2013; B. c. vogeli; cross-sectional molecular survey with clinical and haematological findings; n=311 dogs sampled; 37 PCR-positive; cohort=None; associational; primary=ERY: In 311 dogs sampled in Seropédica, 37 (11.90%) were PCR-positive for B. canis vogeli; the haematological findings reported were normocytic normochromic anaemia, haemoglobinaemia and thrombocytopenia (P<0.05), and clinical parameters showed no association with infection.
- [CB503](articles/CB503_2015_low-t3-syndrome-babesiosis-associated-increased.md) 2015; B. canis; case-control biomarker study with correlation analysis; n=13 infected dogs, 10 healthy controls; cohort=CH37; associational; primary=MET: Dogs with B. canis babesiosis had lower TT3, TT4, FT4 and TSH and higher IL-6, urea and creatinine than healthy dogs, with IL-6 negatively correlated with TT3 and TSH and TT3 negatively correlated with urea and creatinine.
- [CB006](articles/CB006_2017_relation-antioxidant-status-admission-disease-severity.md) 2017; B. canis canis; observational case-control with severity/outcome stratification, natural infection; n=40 infected dogs (29 uncomplicated, 6 one complication, 5 MODS; 4 deaths) and 30 healthy controls; cohort=None; prognostic (associational); primary=SEV: Lower SOD, GPx and catalase and higher MDA at admission were associated with MODS and fatal outcome in B. canis canis dogs, indicating oxidative imbalance as a severity and outcome marker.
- [CB057](articles/CB057_2020_evidence-acute-phase-reaction-asymptomatic-naturally.md) 2020; B. canis; cross-sectional comparison of asymptomatic exposed dogs vs naive controls; n=39 dogs: 9 PCR-positive, 20 PCR-negative/seroreactive, 10 naive controls; cohort=None; associational; primary=INF: Asymptomatic B. canis PCR-positive dogs had higher SAA, total protein and larger alpha-lipoprotein diameter than controls and seroreactive PCR-negative dogs, with lower PON-1 in all exposed dogs, indicating subclinical acute phase reaction.
- [CB202](articles/CB202_2020_serum-hepcidin-clinical-prognostic-marker-discriminate.md) 2020; B. gibsoni, B. vogeli, mixed; natural-infection case-control with survivor vs non-survivor comparison and ROC analysis; n=32 infected dogs (18 survivors, 14 non-survivors) and 32 healthy controls; cohort=CH08; prognostic; primary=INF: Admission serum hepcidin above 32.32 ng/mL separated non-survivors from survivors (AUC about 0.984, sensitivity 92.86%, specificity 100%) in a 32-dog PCR-confirmed cohort, in same-cohort analysis.
- [CB075](articles/CB075_2021_prevalence-genetic-biochemical-evaluation-immune-response.md) 2021; B. vogeli; cross-sectional natural-infection screening with case-control comparison; n=242 dogs screened; 62 Babesia-positive (28 co-infected with E. canis); haematology n=180 (127 healthy, 27 Babesia-only, 26 mixed); oxidative/cytokine subgroup sizes not stated (~4-5 per group); cohort=None; descriptive; primary=INF: B. vogeli-infected police dogs had anaemia, raised liver/renal indices and higher IFN-γ, TNF-α and SOD than healthy dogs, with co-infection with E. canis in many cases.
- [CB083](articles/CB083_2021_molecular-characterization-assessment-hematobiochemical-oxidative-indices.md) 2021; B. vogeli; cross-sectional natural-infection study with infected vs uninfected comparison (associational); n=239 dogs PCR-screened; 19 PCR-positive (7.95%), 9 cytology-positive (3.76%); biomarker group sizes ambiguous (methods mention fifteen dogs per group); cohort=None; associational; primary=ERY: Dogs naturally infected with B. canis vogeli had anaemia, raised ALT/AST/creatinine/urea, lower glucose, higher MDA, lower SOD and higher plasma cortisol than uninfected dogs.
- [CB207](articles/CB207_2021_correlation-oxidative-stress-hepatoprivial-syndrome-comorbidity.md) 2021; B. canis (with Dirofilaria co-infection); clinical associational study of co-infected dogs; n=not reported in abstract; cohort=None; associational; primary=MET: In Babesia-Dirofilaria co-infected dogs, oxidative stress correlated with hepatoprivial syndrome (anaemia, hypoglycaemia, raised enzymes, lower vitamin A), which authors attribute to Babesia-driven lipid peroxidation.
- [CB089](articles/CB089_2022_seasonal-differences-intensity-acute-phase-response.md) 2022; B. canis; natural-infection cohort with seasonal comparison (Feb-May vs other months); n=63 dogs with acute infection; cohort=CH07; associational; primary=INF: In 63 Serbian dogs with acute B. canis infection, Feb-May presentation had lower creatinine, albumin, iron and parasitaemia and more probable high acute phase indices, interpreted as more acute disease.
- [CB117](articles/CB117_2023_proteomics-identification-overexpressed-serum-proteins-babesiacanis.md) 2023; B. canis; exploratory case-control serum proteomics (2-DE + LC-MS/MS), natural infection; n=6 dogs (3 infected, 3 healthy); cohort=None; associational (exploratory); primary=SEV: Ten 2-DE spots, identified as serotransferrin, its X1 isoform and hemopexin, were more abundant in sera of three B. canis-infected dogs than three healthy dogs.
- [CB278](articles/CB278_2025_review-prognostic-biomarkers-babesiosis.md) 2025; B. gibsoni (emphasis); B. canis group discussed; narrative review; n=None; cohort=None; review; primary=SEV: Narrative review proposing that markers of leukocyte activation, endothelial injury and mediator release indicate severity and outcome in babesial multiple organ dysfunction; reviews triple antibiotic therapy for B. gibsoni.
- [CB285](articles/CB285_2025_hemolytic-anemia-caused-protozoan-parasite-babesia.md) 2025; B. canis; retrospective analysis of clinical cases, treatment-framed (descriptive); n=25 dogs; cohort=None; descriptive; primary=SEV: In 25 dogs with babesiosis in Dnipro, treatment within 24 h was accompanied by improved SOD, inorganic phosphorus, LDH and protein fractions, while urea, creatinine and electrolytes were unchanged.
- [CB154](articles/CB154_2026_comprehensive-assessment-babesia-gibsoni-infection-clinicopathological.md) 2026; B. gibsoni; cross-sectional clinicopathological study with oxidative stress indices and ultrasonography (descriptive/associational); n=138 samples screened; 43 antibody-positive, 70 smear-positive, 85 PCR-positive; control group size not reported in abstract; cohort=None; descriptive; primary=INF: In B. gibsoni-infected dogs from Mizoram (85/138 PCR-positive), anaemia, thrombocytopenia, raised CRP and multiorgan changes coincided with higher LPO and lower GSH and T-AOC, which correlated strongly with RBC count.
