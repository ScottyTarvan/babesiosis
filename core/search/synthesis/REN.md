### REN — Renal injury and renal function

**Question this direction asks**

How often, and where in the nephron, is the kidney injured in canine babesiosis? Is the raised urea so often seen a sign of renal injury? Can injury be detected before azotaemia, and does it change outcome? Do large and small *Babesia* species produce different renal phenotypes?

**Evidence base**

The category assignment is automatic (NLP) and has not been checked by a person; the search is described in the [search log](search-log.md). There are 40 primary articles: 15 from round 4 (2006–2016) and 25 from 2016–2026. Secondary-tagged articles (61) are used only where they add evidence.
- **Design or evidence type:** 18 associational (biomarker, omics, haemodynamic and urinary-index studies; one is a fox organ-weight survey), 1 prognostic, 12 descriptive series or surveys, 7 case reports, 2 narrative reviews. None is experimental or interventional.
- **Species:** *B. canis* 15, *B. rossi* 4, *B. gibsoni* 5 (four case reports and one five-dog series), *B. canis vogeli* 1, *B. canis* plus *B. gibsoni* (suspected) 1, *B. vulpes* plus *B. gibsoni* 1, a large unnamed *Babesia* 1, wild red foxes 1, unspecified 9, multi-species reviews 2.
- **Abstract-only:** 28 of 40, including 10 of the 15 round-4 papers. Twelve were appraised from full text.
- **Independent sources:** 37. Eleven articles share eight cohorts: CH01 ([CB001](articles/CB001_2017_utility-urinary-markers-assessment-renal-dysfunction.md), [CB046](articles/CB046_2019_urinary-proteome-kidney-injury-during-babesiosis.md)), CH06 ([CB071](articles/CB071_2021_combined-untargeted-targeted-metabolomics-approaches-reveal.md), [CB103](articles/CB103_2022_serum-urine-profiling-high-throughput-tmt.md), the same 22 dogs), CH10 ([CB124](articles/CB124_2024_diagnostic-efficacy-urinary-neutrophil-gelatinase-associated.md), [CB228](articles/CB228_2023_assessment-acute-kidney-injury-suffering-babesiosis.md)), and one primary article each in CH27, CH29, CH35, CH36 and CH37. **What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- **Where the raised urea comes from:** [CB325](articles/CB325_2006_elevated-serum-urea-creatinine-ratio-babesiosis.md) (2006, CH29; 25 severely anaemic dogs, 13 reference dogs) found higher urea and urea:creatinine ratio while creatinine and cystatin-C stayed in reference ranges. The authors judged the extra urea probably not renal. The source was not identified. Secondary-tag studies agree: [CB412](articles/CB412_2011_changes-serum-urea-creatinine-ratio-babesiosis.md) (same cohort) found normal GFR in all groups. In [CB478](articles/CB478_2014_renal-function-integrity-babesiosis.md) (20 dogs, no controls) all had raised urea nitrogen, 12 (60%) had raised creatinine, and all had USG above 1.030. In [CB330](articles/CB330_2007_biochemical-abnormalities-observed-serum-infected-large.md) (202 sera) urea nitrogen was raised in 62.4%, creatinine in a smaller share (within 18.3–32.7%).
- **Renal azotaemia and AKI:** in *B. rossi* ([CB025](articles/CB025_2018_renal-azotemia-associated-clinical-laboratory-findings.md), 152 dogs) 17% were azotaemic and 14% had renal azotaemia. A USG cut-off below 1.030 would have called only 6 of 26 azotaemic dogs renal. Renal azotaemia went with older age, collapse, hypoglycaemia, hyperphosphataemia, cerebral babesiosis, ARDS and lower survival to discharge. In CH10, AKI occurred in 15% of babesiosis dogs and in all leptospirosis dogs. In Austria ([CB061](articles/CB061_2020_complications-risk-factors-regarding-outcomes-babesiosis.md), 240 dogs, abstract) creatinine above 5 mg/dL and phosphate above 3 mmol/L were associated with death; mortality was 33.9% with renal involvement.
- **Tubular necrosis, indirect:** CB456 (abstract): lower urinary-to-serum creatinine ratio in infected dogs, and a higher renal failure index in azotaemic ones; the index correlated with delay to treatment (r = 0.89). CB425: higher fractional excretion of monovalent electrolytes in azotaemic dogs.
- **Tubular necrosis, tissue:** [CB341](articles/CB341_2007_histological-ultrastructural-studies-renal-lesions-babesia.md) (2007, abstract; 8 fatal *B. canis* cases) found proximal tubular necrosis, no significant glomerular change on histology, and capillary endothelial damage on electron microscopy. Seven of the eight dogs had received imidocarb. Small necropsy series and reports agree on tubular lesions ([CB249](articles/CB249_2024_pathological-diagnosis-babesiosis-labrador-retriever.md), [CB255](articles/CB255_2024_clinico-pathological-evaluation-babesia-gibsoni-associatedrenal.md), [CB307](articles/CB307_2026_enzyme-morphological-criteria-diagnosing-stages-hepatorenal.md)).
- **Proteinuric phenotype, *B. gibsoni*:** [CB404](articles/CB404_2011_resolution-proteinuric-nephropathy-associated-babesia-gibsoni.md) (2011, one dog) showed membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis with immune-complex deposition; proteinuria and PCR cleared after treatment that included immunosuppression. [CB018](articles/CB018_2018_azotemia-proteinuria-infected-babesia-gibsoni.md): 5 pit bull-type dogs, all hypoalbuminaemic and proteinuric, 3 azotaemic. Glomerulonephritis also appears in single necropsies ([CB097](articles/CB097_2022_pathomorphological-changes-german-boxer-infected-babesia.md), [CB188](articles/CB188_2020_pathomorphological-parasitological-molecular-diagnosis-babesia-gibsoni.md)).
- **Single clinical cases, none establishing causation:** acute renal failure ([CB520](articles/CB520_2016_clinical-management-acute-renal-failure-due.md), [CB072](articles/CB072_2021_therapeutic-management-babesia-gibsoni-induced-acute.md), [CB253](articles/CB253_2024_clinical-management-acute-kidney-injury-babesiosis.md), [CB258](articles/CB258_2024_renal-azotemia-associated-haematobiochemical-findings-labrador.md)); chronic renal failure ([CB179](articles/CB179_2019_babesiosis-associated-chronic-renal-failure-comprehensive.md)); renal secondary hyperparathyroidism ([CB303](articles/CB303_2026_rubber-jaw-syndrome-juvenile-labrador-retriever.md)).
- **Species frame:** [CB095](articles/CB095_2022_role-systemic-infections-kidney-diseases.md) (abstract) contrasts acute kidney injury with tubulointerstitial nephritis in large-species disease and rare immune-complex glomerulonephritis in small-species disease.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- **Hypotension, TNF-α and renal ischaemia:** CB463 (48 infected, 33 controls) found a mean arterial pressure below 80 mmHg in 7 dogs (14.6%). Azotaemic dogs had lower pressures, and all pressures correlated inversely with creatinine and urea. CB473 (CH37, 11 dogs) found TNF-α negatively correlated with systolic, diastolic and mean pressure (-0.7246, -0.6642, -0.7151); five dogs had undetectable TNF-α. CB485 (14 infected, 10 controls) found higher aldosterone, but only 4 dogs were above the reference interval. CB425 inferred mineralocorticoid excess from urinary indices. All are single-time-point correlations.
- **Immune-complex glomerulopathy:** CB404, CB018 (see above).
- **Epithelial-mesenchymal transition:** inferred from pathway enrichment in CB046.
- **Drug effect:** imidocarb confounds CB341 and CB258.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- **Urinary markers precede serum change:** in uncomplicated *B. rossi* ([CB435](articles/CB435_2012_assessment-renal-dysfunction-using-urinary-markers.md), 18 vs 8 dogs, abstract) urinary IgG, CRP and retinol-binding protein were higher, but serum urea and creatinine were not. [CB053](articles/CB053_2020_evaluation-acute-kidney-injury-complicated-or.md) (35 vs 10 dogs, abstract) found IgG, CRP, RBP and NGAL above control values (P < 0.001) with creatinine and SDMA unchanged. Tubular markers and UPC fell within 24 h (n = 11); all normalised by one month (n = 9). Complicated cases had higher glomerular markers, UPC and SDMA.
- **B. canis:** CB001 (10 vs 10): IgG, RBP and Tamm-Horsfall protein were higher in every infected dog. [CB024](articles/CB024_2018_glomerular-tubular-kidney-damage-markers-babesiosis.md) (42 vs 14): all markers except uromodulin differed; groups were defined by UPC, so correlations with UPC are partly circular.
- **Omics and early-AKI claims:** CB046 (one pooled sample per group) found 403 vs 176 urinary proteins. CB071 found 601 discriminating urinary features. CB103 proposed uNGAL and L-FABP as pre-azotaemic markers. CB124 found uNGAL and uKIM-1 raised in IRIS grade I AKI, with babesiosis pooled with leptospirosis.
- **AST/ALT:** CB093 (34 dogs; 9 renal, 13 pre-renal azotaemia) linked higher AST and AST/ALT to renal azotaemia. [CB424](articles/CB424_2012_increased-ast-alt-ratio-azotaemic-infected.md) (CH34, 182 dogs) found 2.68 in azotaemic vs 1.08 in non-azotaemic dogs.
- **Outcome:** CB025 and CB061 (above). Phosphate was also higher in non-survivors in [CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md) (15 dogs).

**Convergence and conflict**

- **Urea is not a renal marker here:** CB325, CB412, CB478 and CB330 point the same way. Urea rises more often than creatinine, and GFR markers can stay normal. The 2006–2011 work strengthens the earlier caution that azotaemia counts based on urea overstate renal disease. One conflict remains: [CB465](articles/CB465_2014_importance-haematological-changes-diagnosing-babesiosis.md) (300 dogs, abstract) links azotaemia to renal failure caused by haemolysis, which a descriptive design cannot show.
- **Cystatin-C disagrees:** it stayed in range in CB325 (a human assay was used), but [CB501](articles/CB501_2015_assessment-renal-function-using-cystatin-c.md) (secondary) found it raised in uncomplicated *B. c. vogeli* (5.28 vs 2.30 mg/L in controls) while creatinine did not differ. Species, assay and severity differ.
- **Early urinary change:** CB435 (2012) and CB053 (2020) agree in *B. rossi*: urinary injury markers rise while serum creatinine does not, and the change reverses. They come from the same research tradition; dog overlap is not stated. CH01 and CB024 agree for IgG and RBP in *B. canis*, but disagree on uromodulin.
- **Glomerular or tubular:** biomarker studies report mixed glomerular and tubular injury. CB341 and the necropsy series show mainly tubular lesions, and glomerulonephritis appears only in single necropsies and CB404. Urinary IgG and albumin could also reflect failed tubular reabsorption; this is untested.
- **Haemodynamic chain:** the 2012–2014 Polish papers turn the earlier TNF-α, hypotension and ischaemia idea (from the self-review [CB157](articles/CB157_2017_over-20-years-research-babesiosis-faculty.md)) into primary data. CB341 fits it. The evidence is small, cross-sectional and from one group. Direction is unknown, and the effects rest on minorities of dogs (7 of 48 hypotensive; 4 of 14 with high aldosterone).
- **AST/ALT:** CB093 and CB424 (different cohorts, probably one group) agree. In CB424 the ratio did not differ between anaemic and non-anaemic dogs (1.52 vs 1.76), which argues against haemolysis as the source, but muscle and liver are not excluded.
- **Frequency:** AKI or renal azotaemia in 14–17% (CB025, CB228); azotaemia in 50.9% in [CB291](articles/CB291_2025_haematological-changes-treatment-babesiosis-using-imidocarb.md), definition not stated.
- **Species:** *B. gibsoni* data (mostly case reports) are proteinuric or chronic; *B. rossi* and *B. canis* data are acute and reversible. *B. vogeli* has no renal-focused primary study (CB293 and CB392 give only routine biochemistry or none). Designs differ, so species cannot be ranked.

**Unresolved questions**

- What raises urea in anaemic dogs? Compare urea with measured GFR (clearance), gastrointestinal bleeding and protein catabolism in *B. canis* and *B. rossi*.
- Which cystatin-C assay and cut-off is valid in canine babesiosis, and does it resolve the CB325/CB501 conflict?
- Does falling blood pressure or rising TNF-α precede rising creatinine? Test with serial pressures in a prospective cohort larger than 50.
- Do urinary IgG and albumin reflect glomerular leak? Compare markers with necropsy or biopsy (immunofluorescence, electron microscopy) in non-survivors.
- Does *B. gibsoni* cause immune-complex glomerulopathy? Biopsy proteinuric dogs with co-infections excluded.
- Do survivors have residual CKD (raised SDMA or UPC) at 6–12 months?
- Does imidocarb add renal injury? Compare creatinine and urinary markers before and after imidocarb versus another drug.
