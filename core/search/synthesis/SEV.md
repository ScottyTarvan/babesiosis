### SEV — Multisystem clinical phenotype, severity and outcome

**Question this direction asks**
What clinical and clinicopathological phenotype does each canine *Babesia* species produce, how should uncomplicated, complicated and multiple organ dysfunction (MODS) disease be told apart, and which routine variables mark the dogs that die? Complications here are outcomes, not explained mechanisms.

**Evidence base**
- 59 primary articles (2006–2026). 26 are round 4 (2006–2016); 33 are 2017–2026. The window and screening are in the [search log](search-log.md).
- Design:
  - 36 primary studies: 2 experimental ([CB320](articles/CB320_2006_immunity-against-babesia-rossi-infection-vaccinated.md) vaccination and challenge, [CB021](articles/CB021_2018_first-description-pathogenicity-babesia-vogeli-experimentally.md)), 10 associational or prognostic, 24 descriptive.
  - 11 case reports or case series of 1–3 animals, including two wolf reports.
  - 12 narrative reviews, which add no data.
- Species, in the 47 articles that are not reviews:
  - *B. canis* 20 (one with *B. vulpes*), *B. gibsoni* 7, *B. rossi* 2, *B. vogeli* 2, *B. conradae* 1, mixed species 3, unspecified 12.
  - Many calls rest on smear morphology alone.
- Text read: 32 notes are abstract-only (17 of the 26 round-4 notes); 27 used full text.
- Independent sources: no two primary articles share a recorded cohort, so the ceiling is 47 sources with original data. It is a ceiling. The Zagreb *B. canis* papers ([CB358](articles/CB358_2008_white-blood-cell-count-neutrophil-lymphocyte.md), [CB399](articles/CB399_2010_systemic-inflammatory-response-syndrome-multiple-organ.md), [CB394](articles/CB394_2010_malondialdehyde-levels-serum-infected-babesia-canis.md), [CB006](articles/CB006_2017_relation-antioxidant-status-admission-disease-severity.md)) come from one clinic and overlap is not stated. Several primary articles belong to cohorts that also include secondary-digest papers: CH31, CH32, CH33, CH34, CH38, CH21, CH14, CH20.
- The category is assigned automatically (NLP). Four primary articles are high confidence, 48 low, and 7 had no NLP category and carry the agents' SEV label ([CB358](articles/CB358_2008_white-blood-cell-count-neutrophil-lymphocyte.md), CB430, CB444, CB448, CB457, CB043, CB108). Several look off-topic for SEV: CB320 (vaccine efficacy), CB041 (sequestration hypothesis), CB117 (serum proteomics), CB285 (treatment effect), CB537 (guidelines), CB518 (diagnostic reliability), CB218 (eye disease). CB352, CB394, CB424 and CB501 are marker studies that fit MET, OXO or REN better. None is reclassified here.
- The earlier version rested on 2016–2026 series, mostly of *B. gibsoni* and *B. canis*. The *B. rossi* evidence now in this category is the 2005–2008 Onderstepoort endocrine work (CH31). The Onderstepoort coagulation, cytokine and renal studies fall in other categories and are not read here.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- *B. canis*, Europe:
  - [CB106](articles/CB106_2023_autochthonous-babesia-canis-infections-49-germany.md), Germany, 49 dogs: all thrombocytopenic, 85% anaemic, 52% intravascular haemolysis, 37% SIRS; 7 died or were euthanised.
  - [CB357](articles/CB357_2008_babesia-canis-canis-babesia-canis-vogeli.md), Italy, 45 PCR-positive dogs (CH32; abstract only): *B. c. canis* gave thrombocytopenia, non-regenerative anaemia, haemoglobinuria (13/19) and hypoalbuminaemia (17/26). The 11 *B. c. vogeli* dogs had no uniform pattern.
  - [CB518](articles/CB518_2016_reliability-clinical-monitoring-diagnosis-babesiosis-nigeria.md), Nigeria: in 103 suspected dogs, temperature and packed cell volume did not separate smear-positive from smear-negative dogs.
- Grading:
  - [CB399](articles/CB399_2010_systemic-inflammatory-response-syndrome-multiple-organ.md), Croatia, 332 dogs (abstract only): 68% met SIRS criteria; 33 (10%) had MODS, kidney in 30, liver 20, muscle 19, lung 16, brain 3; 11 of 33 survived, falling with organ count (45% with two organs, 10% with three, none with four).
  - [CB043](articles/CB043_2019_clinical-occurrence-babesiosis-coastal-area-black.md), Romania (abstract only): recovery 81.6% (uncomplicated), 70.1% (one organ), 38.4% (MODS, 13 dogs).
  - [CB372](articles/CB372_2009_septic-shock-babesiosis.md) (CH33): 10 of 86 admissions had septic shock; all died within four days, against 2.6% mortality in the rest.
- Organs: kidney is the commonest MODS organ in CB399. Azotaemia was linked to a higher AST/ALT ratio (2.68 vs 1.08; [CB424](articles/CB424_2012_increased-ast-alt-ratio-azotaemic-infected.md), CH34). [CB501](articles/CB501_2015_assessment-renal-function-using-cystatin-c.md) found higher cystatin-C in 16 uncomplicated *B. vogeli* dogs while creatinine did not differ. [CB332](articles/CB332_2007_abdominal-compartment-syndrome-babesiosis.md) reports abdominal compartment syndrome in one dog.
- Brain: [CB189](articles/CB189_2020_encephalic-babesiosis-clinical-epidemiological-pathological-aspects.md) (7 necropsies): leukostasis, thrombosis and vasculitis, with a heavy parasite load in only one dog.
- Indian series, mostly *B. gibsoni*: seven small series ([CB171](articles/CB171_2018_clinical-haemato-biochemical-ultrasonographical-studies-naturally.md), CB176, CB234, CB237, CB294, CB301, CB310; 7–28 dogs each; CB310 found 23 PCR-positive among 1,628 examined) repeat anaemia, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia, raised ALT and low albumin. None tests outcome. [CB038](articles/CB038_2019_babesiosis-among-working-organised-kennels-india.md): 51 PCR-positive kennel dogs (47 *B. gibsoni*, 4 *B. vogeli*), frequent relapse.
- *B. conradae*: [CB026](articles/CB026_2018_babesia-conradae-infection-coyote-hunting-infected.md), 29 PCR-positive of 55 sighthounds, 16 co-infected; cytopenias with hypoalbuminaemia.
- Young and wild canids:
  - Pups: [CB391](articles/CB391_2010_note-incidence-babesia-canis-10-day.md) (10-day pup) and [CB195](articles/CB195_2020_role-babesiosis-pup-mortality.md) (3 of 77 pup deaths, aged 20–45 days; helminths and bacteria co-present).
  - Wolves: [CB471](articles/CB471_2014_fatal-acute-babesiosis-captive-grey-wolves.md) (4 of 12 captive wolves died) and [CB139](articles/CB139_2025_fatal-babesiosis-free-ranging-iberian-wolf.md) (one wild wolf, two co-infections, cachexia).
- Necropsy case reports ([CB097](articles/CB097_2022_pathomorphological-changes-german-boxer-infected-babesia.md), CB188, CB249, [CB290](articles/CB290_2025_electrolyte-imbalances-coagulopathy-multiorgan-dysfunction-concurrent.md)): lung oedema, tubular or glomerular injury, hepatic change, splenomegaly.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Sequestration and immunothrombosis: [CB041](articles/CB041_2019_mechanisms-involved-persistence-babesia-canis-infection.md) is a hypothesis review from one author's model. [CB220](articles/CB220_2022_features-hepatopathy-hematological-complications-acute-spontaneous.md) (abstract only) proposes a loop of DIC, shock and hepatopathy.
- Shock and circulatory failure: [CB522](articles/CB522_2016_septic-shock-similarities-differences-postmortem-findings.md) (5 dogs, CH38) and [CB080](articles/CB080_2021_disorders-blood-circulation-probable-cause-death.md) (6 dogs, CH27) describe lesion patterns; the shock interpretation is the authors'.
- Mediators: TNF-α correlated inversely with arterial pressure in 11 dogs ([CB473](articles/CB473_2014_increased-concentration-serum-tnf-alpha-correlations.md), CH37). PGE2 stayed high in MODS but not SIRS ([CB470](articles/CB470_2014_serum-concentrations-eicosanoids-lipids-naturally-infected.md)). Plasma proteins differed between SIRS and MODS in 5 dogs per group ([CB528](articles/CB528_2016_plasma-biomarkers-sirs-mods-associated-babesiosis.md)).
- Asplenia: two of three splenectomised dogs became critically ill in CB021; fatal cases in splenectomised dogs ([CB527](articles/CB527_2016_fatal-babesia-canis-canis-infection-splenectomized.md); [CB120](articles/CB120_2023_case-report-fatal-babesia-vulpes-infection.md), with *Mycoplasma*). CB357 lists splenectomised or immunocompromised dogs among its *B. vogeli* cases.
- Co-infection: CB290 (*B. gibsoni* with *Leptospira*) proposes synergy; the case cannot show it.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- Endocrine (CH31): higher cortisol with death in [CB352](articles/CB352_2008_adrenal-response-low-dose-acth-stimulation.md) (4 deaths among 68) and in [CB338](articles/CB338_2007_endocrine-predictors-mortality-babesiosis-caused-babesia.md) (7 of 95, with higher ACTH and lower thyroxine; TSH no different). No adrenal insufficiency; deltas and cortisol:ACTH did not separate outcomes.
- [CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md) (7 survivors, 8 non-survivors; CH21): raised lactate, triglyceride and phosphate; low haematocrit, leukocytes, protein and platelets. All non-survivors were hyperlactataemic, 6 of 7 survivors were not. Cut-offs were set on the same dogs. Parasitaemia did not differ.
- [CB061](articles/CB061_2020_complications-risk-factors-regarding-outcomes-babesiosis.md) (240 dogs): creatinine >5 mg/dL, phosphate >3 mmol/L, albumin <2.2 g/dL, temperature <38 °C; mortality 33.9% with renal involvement, 40.0% with pancreatitis.
- Oxidative: CB006 (40 infected, 30 controls; 5 MODS, 4 deaths): low SOD and GPx and high MDA with MODS and death.
- Haematology: neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio higher in 30 complicated than 30 uncomplicated dogs ([CB358](articles/CB358_2008_white-blood-cell-count-neutrophil-lymphocyte.md); no outcome analysis).
- Parasite genotype: four of 12 BrEMA1 genotypes were linked to poorer prognosis in 141 *B. rossi* samples ([CB379](articles/CB379_2009_preliminary-evaluation-brema1-gene-tool-associating.md); retrospective).

**Convergence and conflict**
- Organ count tracks outcome in two independent *B. canis* sources from different countries, CB399 (2010) and CB043 (2019), with a similar MODS survival of about a third. The 2010 paper makes the gradient older and better supported than the earlier version showed. It is shown only for *B. canis*.
- Phosphate is higher in non-survivors in two unrelated groups (CB516 Swiss, CB061 Viennese). Renal dysfunction dominates MODS in CB399 and CB372 (both Croatian). Lactate rests on one small series, CB516, so the earlier version's lactate convergence no longer stands.
- Cortisol rises with death in CH31, which is one cohort in two papers. Whether it adds to lactate or organ count is not tested.
- Parasitaemia: higher in complicated disease in CB358, not different by outcome in CB516. CB189 found low brain parasite load in six of seven fatal cerebral cases, and CB041 argues severe disease occurs with few circulating parasites. These sit uneasily with load as the driver.
- Oxidative markers conflict. MDA did not separate complicated from uncomplicated dogs in CB394 (7 complicated), yet was higher in MODS and fatal cases in CB006 (5 MODS). Possible overlap between the Zagreb groups is not excluded.
- Asplenia is the most consistent host factor: experiment (CB021), two fatal cases (CB527, CB120) and CB357. All are three animals or fewer; no spleen-intact comparison exists for the fatal cases.
- Mortality is not comparable: 6–7% in the *B. rossi* admission cohort (CB352, CB338), 9% in Portugal (4 of 45; [CB392](articles/CB392_2010_babesiosis-northern-portugal-molecular-characterization-vector.md)), 14% in CB106, 8 of 15 in CB516 (selected), 33.9% with renal involvement in CB061. Referral, case definition and treatment differ.
- Species: CB021 found mild disease in intact *B. vogeli* dogs, but CB357 includes young natural *B. vogeli* dogs with haemolytic anaemia. [CB119](articles/CB119_2023_diverse-pathogenicity-various-babesia-parasite-species.md) ranks species indirectly; CB448 still uses the older scheme that treats *B. rossi* as a *B. canis* subspecies. Indian *B. gibsoni* series are species-clean only when PCR-confirmed (CB038, CB301, CB310).
- Clinical indices alone are weak: CB518 found temperature and packed cell volume uninformative for diagnosis.

**Unresolved questions**
- Do admission lactate and phosphate (CB516, CB061) predict death in a new multi-centre cohort with cut-offs fixed beforehand and compared with organ count?
- Do cortisol, ACTH and thyroxine (CH31) add information after adjusting for lactate and organ count?
- Does the MODS survival gradient (CB399, CB043) hold in *B. gibsoni* and *B. rossi* under one SIRS and MODS definition?
- Are splenectomised dogs at higher risk of fatal natural infection than spleen-intact dogs of the same species (CB021, CB527, CB120)?
- Is brain capillary parasite burden at necropsy different in cerebral and non-cerebral fatal cases (CB189, CB195)?
- Are the SOD, GPx and MDA differences by severity (CB006, CB394) reproducible in an independent MODS group of 20 or more?
