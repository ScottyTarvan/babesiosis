### COA — Coagulation, platelets, immunothrombosis and DIC

**Question this direction asks**

Is haemostatic change in canine babesiosis a bystander (thrombocytopenia without bleeding), an adaptive acute-phase response, or a driver of organ failure through immunothrombosis, microthrombosis and DIC? Does the answer differ by *Babesia* species?

**Evidence base**

Forty primary articles from 2006 to 2026: 13 from round 4 (2006-2016) and 27 from 2017-2026. The category assignment is automatic (NLP), with no human review. See the [search log](search-log.md) for methods.

- **Design:** 15 associational studies (case-control, biomarker or cohort comparisons); 1 mixed descriptive/associational study ([CB199](articles/CB199_2020_disseminated-intravascular-coagulation-syndrome-complication-acute.md)); 1 prognostic cohort ([CB438](articles/CB438_2012_mortality-virulent-babesiosis-associated-consumptive-coagulopathy.md)); 1 experimental infection ([CB419](articles/CB419_2011_thrombocytopenia-platelet-activity-experimentally-infected-rangelia.md), *Rangelia vitalii*, 7 infected and 5 control dogs); 14 descriptive series or surveys; 7 case reports or very small series; 1 narrative review ([CB104](articles/CB104_2022_babesia-north-america-update.md)).
- **Species:** *B. rossi* 6 (five name South Africa); *B. canis* 7; *B. gibsoni* 7; *B. vogeli* 2; three named species together 3; *B. vulpes* 1; *R. vitalii* 1; large or unspecified *Babesia* 12; one multi-species review.
- **Text read:** 29 of 40 are abstract-only (11 of the 13 round 4 papers); 11 were read in full.
- **Independent sources:** 36. Cohort CH03 (Onderstepoort *B. rossi*, Oct 2011-Apr 2013) holds [CB145](articles/CB145_2026_thromboinflammation-virulent-babesiosis.md), [CB504](articles/CB504_2015_platelet-activation-platelet-leukocyte-interaction-naturally.md) and [CB513](articles/CB513_2015_platelet-indices-babesia-rossi-infection.md). CH24 (Mannuthy) holds [CB049](articles/CB049_2020_clinico-haematological-evaluation-molecular-identification-babesia.md) and [CB183](articles/CB183_2019_haemato-biochemical-changes-babesia-gibsoni-infection.md). [CB011](articles/CB011_2017_alteration-haemostatic-parameters-uncomplicated-babesiosis.md) (CH22), [CB439](articles/CB439_2012_hemostatic-abnormalities-uncomplicated-babesiosis-babesia-rossi.md) (CH36) and [CB148](articles/CB148_2026_clinicopathological-abnormalities-outcome-acute-babesia-canis.md) (CH11) share cohorts only with articles outside this category. [CB212](articles/CB212_2021_splenomegaly-development-disseminated-intravascular-coagulation-syndrome.md) and [CB213](articles/CB213_2021_pathogenetic-therapy-vascular-platelet-hemostasis-disorders.md) look like the same Ukrainian programme (CB213 notes a possible overlap with CB212) but have no cohort ID. **What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*

- **Thrombocytopenia** is near-universal: 99.5% of 248 dogs with large *Babesia* ([CB326](articles/CB326_2006_hematological-changes-during-course-babesiosis-caused.md), CH30), 100% of 34 ([CB100](articles/CB100_2022_clinical-hematologic-findings-babesia-canis-infection.md)), all 49 ([CB106](articles/CB106_2023_autochthonous-babesia-canis-infections-49-germany.md)), all 23 in CB148, and 84.2% of 101 in CB539. In CB326 and CB539 it was more frequent than anaemia (low red cell count 26.2%; anaemia 50.5%). Depth varies: day 0 mean 29 versus 259 ×10⁹/L in [CB374](articles/CB374_2009_alterations-some-blood-coagulation-parameters-naturally.md); 178 versus 288 in CB199; 26.67 in acute versus 116.8 in chronic cases in CB184 (n not reported).
- **Bleeding is not the rule.** Low platelets without bleeding signs are described in CB439 and CB513. [CB345](articles/CB345_2008_multisystemic-effects-babesiosis-management-critical-cases.md) lists haemorrhagic signs among those of 102 *B. gibsoni* dogs (frequency not given). In [CB254](articles/CB254_2024_incidence-various-types-bleeding-disorders-associated.md), a series selected for bleeding signs, 667 of 1,593 dogs with vector-borne disease had babesiosis; 129 of 541 *B. gibsoni* cases (23.8%) were grouped as pancytopenia with DIC (criteria not visible). Haemorrhage is reported mainly in cases ([CB252](articles/CB252_2024_evan-s-syndrome-hemolytic-anemia-thrombocytopenia.md), [CB272](articles/CB272_2025_urogenital-haemorrhage-secondary-babesia-gibsoni-induced.md)) and at necropsy ([CB497](articles/CB497_2015_cardiac-lesions-babesiosis.md): cardiac haemorrhage in 12 of 20 fatal *B. canis* dogs).
- **Consumptive coagulopathy and DIC.**
  - CB374 (30 infected, 29 controls): thrombin-antithrombin complexes (TAT) 49.7 and 87.7 µg/L on days 0 and 2 versus 7.2; antithrombin III (AT III) lower at every time; the authors conclude DIC occurs, with no DIC score.
  - CB438 (72 *B. rossi* dogs, 13 died): non-survivors had lower activity of all factors and of protein C, longer PT and aPTT and higher D-dimer. Antithrombin did not differ.
  - Ukrainian programme (CB199, CB217, CB212): higher soluble fibrin monomer complexes and fibrin degradation products, low fibrinogen, inconsistent PT/aPTT, read as consumption-stage DIC with grade II shock. No antithrombin or formal score in CB199.
  - Cases: overt DIC and organ infarcts at necropsy ([CB364](articles/CB364_2009_complicated-form-babesiosis-case-report.md)); DIC with pulmonary oedema ([CB079](articles/CB079_2021_multiple-complications-infected-babesia-gibsoni.md)).
- **Uncomplicated disease.** CB011 (25 dogs): raised TAT, low AT III. CB439 (19 *B. rossi*, 10 controls): most thromboelastography variables unchanged (only clotting time K longer), antithrombin lower, fibrinogen higher, read by the authors as a normocoagulable state.
- **Organ microthrombosis** (descriptive): fibrin microthrombi in several organs of CB145 non-survivors; clot-plugged splenic sinuses in CB212; lung fibrin in *B. rossi* ([CB111](articles/CB111_2023_lung-pathology-natural-babesia-rossi-infection.md), CH04); cerebral thrombosis in 7 fatal cases ([CB189](articles/CB189_2020_encephalic-babesiosis-clinical-epidemiological-pathological-aspects.md)); fibrin deposition with vasculitis-like change in the skin of one *B. gibsoni* dog ([CB454](articles/CB454_2013_generalized-alopecia-vasculitis-like-changes-babesiosis.md)).

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*

- **Thrombin generation and anticoagulant consumption:** TAT up and AT III down in CB374 and CB011. Protein C was lower in CB438 non-survivors and in [CB333](articles/CB333_2007_protein-c-activity-babesiosis.md) (n not reported), but in CB011 only activated protein C differed. Suggested causes (cytokines, hepatic synthesis, elastase) were not tested.
- **Endothelial activation and hypofibrinolysis:** in [CB446](articles/CB446_2013_markers-coagulation-activation-endothelial-stimulation-inflammation.md) (12 dogs), CRP, soluble ICAM-1 and fibrinogen were raised and von Willebrand factor activity lowered. In [CB004](articles/CB004_2017_blood-markers-fibrinolysis-endothelial-activation-babesiosis.md) (30 dogs), thrombomodulin, HMGB-1, VCAM-1 and suPAR exceeded controls, and plasminogen and PAI-1 were lower at admission than on day 6. CB145 found minimal fibrinolysis with raised α2-antiplasmin.
- **Fibrinogen compensation:** fibrin-only clot strength tracked fibrinogen (r = 0.735, CB034, 13 versus 5 dogs); agonist-induced platelet responses did not differ.
- **Platelet change:** raised mean platelet volume (MPV) with lower plateletcrit is read as activation ([CB393](articles/CB393_2010_erythrocyte-platelet-indices-babesiosis.md)). In CB419, platelet aggregation was lower and megakaryocytes higher, and the authors propose splenic pooling or immune-mediated loss. Immune-mediated thrombocytopenia is inferred in single cases ([CB047](articles/CB047_2019_molecular-diagnosis-babesia-canis-infection-concurrent.md), CB252, CB272) without platelet antibody tests.
- **Sequestration consolidated by coagulation** ([CB041](articles/CB041_2019_mechanisms-involved-persistence-babesia-canis-infection.md), review) and **hyperaggregation with hypovolaemic shock** (CB213, CB217). The dipyridamole-plus-dextran infusion reports give no allocation or group sizes, so they do not show that aggregation drives disease.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*

- *B. rossi:* factor activities, PT, aPTT and D-dimer separated non-survivors (CB438, CB145); CRP and serum amyloid A were raised in infected dogs. No platelet index differed by outcome in CB513 (12 of 96 died). In CB504, platelet-monocyte aggregates were above controls in survivors (58.9%) but not in the 7 non-survivors (39.2%), with no survivor-versus-non-survivor difference.
- *B. canis:* non-survivors had lower thrombocyte counts at admission ([CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md); 8 versus 7 dogs), but lactate and leukocyte count separated the groups better. Complicated and uncomplicated dogs were largely alike at admission in CB004.
- *B. gibsoni and others:* raised MPV in [CB304](articles/CB304_2026_studies-correlation-platelet-indices-molecular-diagnosis.md) and [CB166](articles/CB166_2017_are-platelet-indices-useful-clinico-pathological.md) is diagnostic. CB270 calls platelet variables prognostic but reports no outcome data.

**Convergence and conflict**

- **Agreement:** thrombocytopenia (84-100%) across species and sources, with raised MPV or macroplatelets (CB393, CB513, CB304, CB166, CB419).
- **Thrombin generation** appears in two studies (CB374, CB011). Their authors differ: CB374 concludes DIC; CB011 describes a procoagulant state short of DIC. Neither used a DIC score. The notes do not say whether they share dogs.
- **Platelet "activation" is not settled.** Indices point one way. CB504 found platelet P-selectin no different from controls, CB034 found agonist-induced responses unchanged, and CB419 found lower aggregation. CB145 reports platelet activation without the note naming an assay. CB145 and CB504 are the same cohort (CH03), so this is an inconsistency within one source.
- **Fibrinogen conflicts.** High in *B. rossi* (CB034, CB145, CB439), in experimental *B. canis* ([CB370](articles/CB370_2009_systemic-inflammatory-responses-experimentally-infected-babesia.md), n not reported) and in CB446. Low in the Ukrainian programme, which uses non-standard assays on untyped *Babesia*. No study compares these directly.
- **Clotting times differ.** APTT was shorter than in controls on days 2-3 in CB374, prolonged in *B. rossi* non-survivors (CB438, CB145) and in *B. gibsoni* ([CB134](articles/CB134_2025_clinical-clinicopathological-features-108-infected-babesia.md)), and unchanged in CB199 and CB419. Species, severity and timing are all possible explanations.
- **Platelets and outcome:** lower in *B. canis* non-survivors (CB516, small) but not in *B. rossi* (CB513).
- **Spleen:** splenectomised dogs are not protected from thrombocytopenia (CB021 *B. vogeli*, [CB382](articles/CB382_2010_babesiosis-caused-large-babesia-species-7.md) six splenectomised of seven dogs, [CB527](articles/CB527_2016_fatal-babesia-canis-canis-infection-splenectomized.md)), which argues against pooling as the main route. [CB120](articles/CB120_2023_case-report-fatal-babesia-vulpes-infection.md) had thrombocytosis, confounded by *Mycoplasma* and immunosuppression.
- **What the 2006-2016 papers change:**
  - They strengthen the procoagulant picture: TAT and AT III in *B. canis canis* come from 2009 (CB374), eight years before CB011.
  - They tie consumption to death: CB438 (2012) predates CH03 and shows the factor and protein C signature that CB145 later repeats. The *B. rossi* work is still one Pretoria group, but at least four sources, not one.
  - They show that high fibrinogen and preserved clot strength (CB439, 2012) coexist with consumption in fatal cases.
  - They include the only experimental infections in this category (CB419, CB370), though both are thin.
- **Species:** *B. rossi* has the deepest evidence; *B. gibsoni* data are descriptive and confounded by immune-mediated cytopenia; *B. vogeli* is sparse. Do not transfer the *B. rossi* phenotype to other species.

**Unresolved questions**

- Do PCR-typed *B. canis*, *B. gibsoni* and *B. rossi* cohorts, run on one panel (PT, aPTT, Clauss fibrinogen, TAT, AT III, D-dimer, thromboelastography), show the low-factor, long-PT/aPTT signature of CB438 and CB145, or the short APTT of CB374?
- Is platelet activation real? Measure platelet P-selectin, agonist-induced aggregation, MPV and immature platelet fraction in the same dogs, since CB513, CB504, CB034 and CB419 disagree.
- Is thrombocytopenia consumptive, immune-mediated or sequestrational? Test platelet-bound antibody, thrombopoietin and platelet lifespan, and compare splenectomised with intact dogs.
- Does an adapted DIC score at admission predict death independently of lactate and leukocyte count (CB516)? Use a CB438-type design with more non-survivors.
- Is the low fibrinogen from the Ukrainian programme real? Repeat with Clauss fibrinogen and species typing.
- Is protein C low in uncomplicated disease (CB333) or only in fatal disease (CB438; CB011 found only activated protein C changed)? Measure protein C and activated protein C in the same dogs by severity.
- Does microthrombosis cause organ failure? Use serial coagulation tests and tissue fibrin staining in an experimental model before any anticoagulant or antiplatelet trial.
