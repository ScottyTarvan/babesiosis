### ERY — Erythrocyte injury, haemolysis, anaemia and immune-mediated processes

**Question this direction asks**

How do *Babesia* infections reduce red-cell mass in dogs: direct parasite lysis, antibody-mediated destruction, macrophage and splenic clearance, or an inadequate marrow response? Which red-cell measures track severity and outcome, and do the answers differ by species?

**Evidence base**

96 primary articles, 2006-2026. The category assignment is automatic (NLP) and unchecked; see the [search log](search-log.md).
- Design: 50 descriptive series or surveys, 20 associational, 14 case reports (1-3 dogs), 8 narrative reviews, 2 prognostic cohorts ([CB067](articles/CB067_2020_analysis-risk-factors-associated-recurrence-babesiosis.md), [CB153](articles/CB153_2026_concurrent-babesia-gibsoni-infection-negative-prognostics.md)) and 2 experimental ([CB337](articles/CB337_2007_suppressive-effect-culture-supernatant-erythrocytes-serum.md), in vitro; [CB421](articles/CB421_2011_experimental-infection-babesia-microti.md), 2 dogs, no controls). The only other experimental dogs are the 5-dog arm of [CB115](articles/CB115_2023_regenerative-response-naturally-experimentally-infected-babesia.md).
- Species as named in the notes, often by smear only: *B. gibsoni* 27, *B. canis* 14, *B. vogeli* 6, *B. rossi* 4, two named species 7, other piroplasms 5, unnamed large *Babesia* 3, unspecified 22, plus the 8 reviews. All are dogs except the wolves of [CB005](articles/CB005_2017_prevalence-impact-babesia-canis-theileria-sp.md).
- 28 papers are round 4 (2006-2016). 52 of 96 are abstract-only, including 19 of the 28 round-4 papers.
- Independent sources: 92 (84 without the reviews). CH02 ([CB002](articles/CB002_2017_hematological-changes-affected-babesiosis.md), [CB015](articles/CB015_2017_clinicopathological-alterations-naturally-occurring-babesia-gibsoni.md)), CH05 ([CB178](articles/CB178_2019_electrophoretic-pattern-serum-proteins-babesiosis.md), [CB065](articles/CB065_2020_differences-serum-protein-electrophoretic-pattern-naturally.md)) and CH17 ([CB247](articles/CB247_2024_comparison-efficacy-different-therapeutic-regimen-treatment.md), [CB248](articles/CB248_2024_diagnosis-immune-mediated-haemolytic-anemia-affected.md), [CB257](articles/CB257_2024_haemato-biochemical-changes-affected-immune-mediated.md)) are one source each. Eleven other cohorts contribute one paper each.
- Looking off-topic for ERY, not reclassified: CB005, [CB069](articles/CB069_2020_elevated-serum-manganese-concentration-possible-predisposing.md) (manganese in cerebral babesiosis), [CB351](articles/CB351_2008_unusual-form-babesiosis.md) (neurological signs), [CB361](articles/CB361_2009_possible-emergence-drug-resistant-variants-babesia.md) (atovaquone resistance), [CB265](articles/CB265_2025_male-fertility-impairment-associated-babesia-gibsoni.md) (semen quality), [CB127](articles/CB127_2024_molecular-prevalence-epidemiology-phylogenetic-analysis-babesia.md) (prevalence in healthy dogs).

Most series are small, smear-diagnosed regional papers with healthy-only controls or none.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- **Anaemia is common, but thrombocytopenia is more consistent.** In 248 Warsaw dogs, RBC was low in 26.2% and Hct in 31.4%, against thrombocytopenia in 99.5% ([CB326](articles/CB326_2006_hematological-changes-during-course-babesiosis-caused.md), CH30, abstract only). In 113 smear-positive dogs, RBC was low in 62.8% and platelets in 100% ([CB432](articles/CB432_2012_clinic-laboratory-diagnosis-babesiosis-babesia-canis.md)). In 60 PCR-confirmed *B. gibsoni* dogs, 81.7% were anaemic, about half with PCV <20% ([CB086](articles/CB086_2021_clinical-characteristics-naturally-babesia-gibsoni-infected.md)).
- **Regeneration varies.** Anaemia was mildly to moderately regenerative in 86 uncomplicated *B. rossi* dogs ([CB418](articles/CB418_2011_serial-haematology-results-transfused-non-transfused.md)). A raised reticulocyte count occurred in only 5 of 113 dogs (CB432) but reticulocytosis in 13 of 21 ([CB164](articles/CB164_2017_anemia-description-babesia-spp-infected.md)). Analyser counts can mislead: 20 of 92 dogs with large-form babesiosis had abnormal reticulocyte profiles ([CB538](articles/CB538_2016_spurious-reticulocyte-profiles-large-form-babesiosis.md)), and one dog read 60 by analyser and 3.6 ×10^9/L manually ([CB540](articles/CB540_2016_spurious-reticulocyte-profiles-babesiosis.md)).
- **Secondary IMHA or Evans syndrome** is reported mainly in case reports ([CB495](articles/CB495_2015_diagnosis-treatment-immune-mediated-hemolytic-anemia.md), [CB250](articles/CB250_2024_anemia-hemol-tica-secund-ria-babesiose.md), [CB264](articles/CB264_2025_un-cas-clinique-classique-de-piroplasmose.md), [CB306](articles/CB306_2026_severe-erythrocyte-dysplasia-peripheral-blood-smears.md), [CB047](articles/CB047_2019_molecular-diagnosis-babesia-canis-infection-concurrent.md)). In CB264 it appeared on day 7 after imidocarb began.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- **Immune-mediated destruction:** Coombs-positive in 28/64 (43.8%) *B. gibsoni* dogs, saline agglutination in 32.8% and spherocytes in 28.1% ([CB248](articles/CB248_2024_diagnosis-immune-mediated-haemolytic-anemia-affected.md), CH17). *B. gibsoni* accounted for 75% of haemoparasite-associated IMHA in Mizoram ([CB235](articles/CB235_2023_first-report-immune-mediated-haemolytic-anaemiaassociated.md)). Neither separates autoantibody from antibody on parasitised cells.
- **Haemolysis and phagocytic clearance:** the urea:creatinine ratio was 41.35 in 25 babesiosis dogs against 13.6-15.36 in controls, IMHA and induced haemoglobinaemia, with normal GFR ([CB412](articles/CB412_2011_changes-serum-urea-creatinine-ratio-babesiosis.md), CH29, abstract only), so haemoglobin load alone did not reproduce it. Necropsy of 5 dogs showed extravascular haemolysis ([CB522](articles/CB522_2016_septic-shock-similarities-differences-postmortem-findings.md)). Fatal *B. rossi* marrow had more CD204+ macrophages ([CB129](articles/CB129_2024_cytological-histopathological-bone-marrow-findings-natural.md), CH04), and two experimental *B. gibsoni* dogs had haemosiderin-laden splenic macrophages ([CB397](articles/CB397_2010_babesia-gibsoni-detection-blood-smears-formalin.md)).
- **Inadequate erythropoiesis:** *B. rossi* absolute reticulocyte count exceeded controls but was below IMHA dogs (p=0.041) and fell about 48 h after parasitaemia in 5 experimental dogs (CB115, CH09). Fatal cases showed dyserythropoiesis and more marrow iron (CB129). *B. canis* dogs were non-regenerative on admission and reticulocytotic by day 14 ([CB251](articles/CB251_2024_reticulocyte-response-paired-blood-samples-babesia.md)). Serum EPO was 87.0 vs 16.4 mU/mL in controls, but ~9% of infected dogs had low values ([CB297](articles/CB297_2026_serum-erythropoietin-levels-babesia-affected.md), CH20). Serum from *B. gibsoni*-infected dogs slowed canine reticulocyte maturation in vitro (3 infected and 4 healthy donors; CB337).
- **Membrane and oxidative injury:** poikilocytosis in 90.5% of 21 dogs (CB164) but 25% of 248 (CB326). Osmotic fragility was not raised in babesiosis ([CB225](articles/CB225_2023_association-increased-osmotic-fragility-red-blood.md), CH28) and was reduced in 13 transfusion-requiring dogs ([CB315](articles/CB315_2026_clinicopathological-evaluation-molecular-detection-haemoparasites-severely.md)). SEM normocytes were 77.3% vs about 99% in controls ([CB070](articles/CB070_2021_ultramicroscopic-erythrocytes-profile-component-babesiosis-pathogenesis.md), CH26).
- **Cytokines and host factors:** cytokine-driven haemolysis is review-level only ([CB236](articles/CB236_2023_unraveling-pathogenesis-anemia-babesiosis-exploring-role.md), [CB239](articles/CB239_2023_anemia-babesiosis-diagnosis-therapeutic-management.md)); neither ties its mechanisms to canine primary studies. DEA 1 type did not change PCR positivity (63.0% vs 60.3%; [CB143](articles/CB143_2025_relationship-between-erythrocyte-antigen-dea-susceptibility.md)).

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- Of 80 anaemic Nigerian dogs, 18 (22.5%) died, and non-survivors had lower PCV, Hb and RBC on univariate testing ([CB238](articles/CB238_2023_prevalence-role-anemia-mortalities-babesiosis.md)). Admission haematocrit was also lower in 8 non-survivors than 7 survivors ([CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md), CH21). In 320 *B. rossi* dogs no variable was an independent predictor of death; low T4, bilirubin, urea and cortisol discriminated best ([CB040](articles/CB040_2019_clinical-pathological-description-320-cases-naturally.md)).
- In IMHA, *B. gibsoni* co-infection predicted death (HR 2.6, p=0.024; 42 of 55 dogs died within 60 days), as did marked spherocytosis (HR 2.1) ([CB153](articles/CB153_2026_concurrent-babesia-gibsoni-infection-negative-prognostics.md)).
- Recurrence was 11.8%, but 28.7% with secondary IMHA ([CB067](articles/CB067_2020_analysis-risk-factors-associated-recurrence-babesiosis.md)).
- An LHD% cut-off of 1.83% gave 80.43% sensitivity and 100% specificity, against anaemia status, not an iron reference standard ([CB286](articles/CB286_2025_low-haemoglobin-density-anaemic-indicator-babesiosis.md), CH08).

**Convergence and conflict**

- **Cytopenias.** Thrombocytopenia (84.2% in [CB539](articles/CB539_2016_perfil-hematol-gico-de-c-es.md) up to 100%) is more consistent than anaemia (26.2% in CB326 up to 82% in [CB099](articles/CB099_2022_occurrence-babesiosis-rhine-main-area-hesse.md)). Anaemia is common, not near-universal.
- **Marrow response.** The Onderstepoort *B. rossi* studies agree: red-cell responses weaker than the haemolysis predicts (CB418), ARC below IMHA with an early experimental fall (CB115) and dyserythropoiesis (CB129). These are separate cohorts from one group. *B. canis* (CB251) and non-regenerative *B. gibsoni* ([CB214](articles/CB214_2022_comparative-evaluation-darbepoetin-therapy-non-regenerative.md)) point the same way. But analyser artefact (CB538, CB540) may overstate reticulocytes, and the notes do not say which analysers it affects.
- **Iron conflicts.** Transferrin saturation was low in *B. canis* ([CB177](articles/CB177_2018_changes-erythrocytopoesis-indices-babesiosis.md)) but raised in non-regenerative *B. gibsoni* (CB214); fatal *B. rossi* marrow held more iron (CB129).
- **Parasite load.** In 342 *B. canis* dogs, parasitaemia and CRP correlated with haematological severity ([CB141](articles/CB141_2025_impact-levels-parasitemia-antibodies-acute-phase.md), CH12), and RBC, Hb and Hct were inversely related to parasitaemia in 18 cases ([CB459](articles/CB459_2013_correlation-between-hematological-parameters-babesia-spp.md)). In 7 *B. vogeli* dogs, load did not track cytopenias, although anaemia odds were raised (OR 11.8; [CB013](articles/CB013_2017_vector-borne-zoonotic-diseases-north-west.md)).
- **Spleen.** Severe disease followed splenectomy in experimental *B. vogeli* ([CB021](articles/CB021_2018_first-description-pathogenicity-babesia-vogeli-experimentally.md)) and in single cases of *B. canis* ([CB527](articles/CB527_2016_fatal-babesia-canis-canis-infection-splenectomized.md)), *B. vulpes* ([CB132](articles/CB132_2025_successful-treatment-severe-babesia-vulpes-infection.md)) and *B. gibsoni* ([CB377](articles/CB377_2009_clinicopathological-findings-molecular-detection-characterization-babesia.md)). Yet six splenectomised dogs with a large unnamed *Babesia* had only mild anaemia ([CB382](articles/CB382_2010_babesiosis-caused-large-babesia-species-7.md)), as did a *B. vogeli* isolate in 2 splenectomised dogs ([CB339](articles/CB339_2007_parasitological-molecular-characterization-venezuelan-isolate-babesia.md)).
- **Anaemia and organ injury.** Older studies found no difference between anaemic and non-anaemic dogs in urea and creatinine ([CB365](articles/CB365_2009_influence-anaemia-azotaemia-infected-babesia-canis.md), 127 dogs), AST/ALT ([CB424](articles/CB424_2012_increased-ast-alt-ratio-azotaemic-infected.md), 182 dogs, CH34) or MDA ([CB394](articles/CB394_2010_malondialdehyde-levels-serum-infected-babesia-canis.md), CH33). Anaemia grade is not a stand-in for organ injury.
- **Species.** Immune-mediated evidence comes almost entirely from *B. gibsoni* in Indian referral populations, with CH17 (CB247, CB248, CB257) one source, not three. *B. vogeli* is mild in intact experimental dogs (CB021, CB339) and small field series (CB048 and CB131 have 9 and 4 positive dogs).

**Unresolved questions**

- Does DAT positivity in *B. gibsoni* reflect autoantibody? Test eluate specificity and persistence after PCR clearance, in dogs not selected for IMHA (CB248, CB235, CB153).
- Is the early reticulocyte fall caused by a circulating factor (CB337, CB115)? Measure ARC (manual check), EPO, hepcidin and marrow cytology serially in experimental infection.
- Is erythropoiesis iron-restricted? Ferritin, hepcidin, CHr and marrow iron in the same dogs could reconcile CB177, CB214 and CB129.
- Does spleen status set severity? Challenge intact and splenectomised dogs with one isolate (CB021, CB339 and CB382 disagree).
- Does quantitative parasite load predict anaemia after adjusting for species and co-infection (CB141 against CB013)?
- Do spherocytosis or DAT add prognostic value beyond admission PCV in cohorts not selected for IMHA (CB040, CB153)?
