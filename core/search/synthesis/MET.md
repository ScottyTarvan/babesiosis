### MET — Hepatic, metabolic, endocrine and acid-base disturbances

**Question this direction asks**

Which hepatic, endocrine, electrolyte, glucose, lipid and acid-base derangements accompany canine babesiosis? Are they primary lesions, or downstream of haemolysis, hypoxia, hypotension and inflammation? Which carry prognostic information beyond renal and haematological markers?

**Evidence base**

Fifty primary articles, 2006–2026 (see the [search log](search-log.md)). The category assignment is automatic (NLP), with no human check. Nineteen articles are round 4 (2006–2016). Thirty-one of the 50 are abstract-only, including 12 of the 19 round-4 papers.

- Design: 21 descriptive series or small case-control studies; 15 associational studies with a comparison group; 2 outcome-linked series ([CB338](articles/CB338_2007_endocrine-predictors-mortality-babesiosis-caused-babesia.md), 95 dogs; [CB040](articles/CB040_2019_clinical-pathological-description-320-cases-naturally.md), 320 dogs); 4 experimental or randomised studies of 3–18 dogs ([CB105](articles/CB105_2022_serial-changes-concentrations-cortisol-thyroid-hormones.md) is the only one on host physiology); 1 assay comparison; 5 case reports; 2 narrative reviews.
- Species, as reported (often by smear only): B. canis 18, B. rossi 7, B. gibsoni 7, B. vogeli 3, unspecified Babesia 13, B. canis plus B. gibsoni 1, multi-species review 1.
- Independent sources: 46. [CB091](articles/CB091_2022_multi-platforms-strategies-metabolomics-approaches-investigation.md) and [CB137](articles/CB137_2025_metabolomics-insights-into-salivary-profile-babesia.md) share CH06; [CB280](articles/CB280_2025_main-biochemical-abnormalities-acute-form-uncomplicated.md) and [CB308](articles/CB308_2026_babesia-canis-b-gibsoni.md) share CH19; CB338, [CB340](articles/CB340_2007_plasma-insulin-concentrations-hypoglycaemic-babesia-canis.md) and [CB508](articles/CB508_2015_serum-pancreatic-specific-lipase-concentrations-naturally.md) share CH31. These 7 articles are 3 sources. Most cohort links are "probable", and the Warsaw cohorts CH30, CH34, CH35 and CH37 may overlap.
- Off-topic NLP assignments, not reclassified: [CB359](articles/CB359_2009_evaluation-efficacy-bruceine-natural-quassinoid-compound.md) (trial), [CB125](articles/CB125_2024_effects-gonadectomy-incidence-rate-babesiosis-risk.md) (gonadectomy), [CB536](articles/CB536_2016_prevalence-dirofilaria-repens-cats-healthy-concurrent.md) (Dirofilaria prevalence), [CB031](articles/CB031_2019_seroprevalence-ehrlichiosis-microscopic-screening-babesiosis-harare.md) (seroprevalence), [CB533](articles/CB533_2016_review-babesiosis-european-perspective.md) (practitioner review), [CB312](articles/CB312_2026_unique-case-babesiosis-hemoptysis-clinical-management.md) (haemoptysis case), and the renal-led [CB190](articles/CB190_2020_transient-acquired-fanconi-syndrome-unusual-rare.md), [CB231](articles/CB231_2023_pathogenetic-relationship-between-kidney-pathologies-microcirculatory.md) and [CB478](articles/CB478_2014_renal-function-integrity-babesiosis.md).

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- **Liver enzymes and bilirubin.** The commonest finding in about 15 descriptive series. Raised AST: 92.6% of 202 Warsaw dogs ([CB330](articles/CB330_2007_biochemical-abnormalities-observed-serum-infected-large.md), abstract only, CH30) and 83.6% of 55 Lviv dogs (CB280). Raised ALT: 64.9% and 25.5%. Raised ALP: 31.7% and 80.0%. CB280 does not discuss haemolysis as a source of AST. In 230 B. canis dogs, ALT, AST and ALP did not differ between anaemic and non-anaemic dogs ([CB409](articles/CB409_2011_liver-enzyme-activity-infected-babesia-canis.md), CH34, no uninfected controls). Cholestatic markers (GGT) are said to follow hepatocellular enzymes from day 2–3, without numbers ([CB366](articles/CB366_2009_informational-activity-indicating-liver-enzyme-during.md)). Fatal B. rossi livers show cholestasis, Kupffer cell hypertrophy and centrilobular necrosis, attributed to haemolysis and hypoxia, not primary hepatic failure ([CB147](articles/CB147_2026_liver-histopathology-naturally-acquired-babesia-rossi.md), 10 vs 4 controls, secondary-tagged). In 45 Ukrainian dogs, GGT was about four times normal at stage III ([CB307](articles/CB307_2026_enzyme-morphological-criteria-diagnosing-stages-hepatorenal.md), abstract only, no controls).
- **Pancreas.** In 87 B. rossi dogs, 24 (28%) had cPL above 400 µg/L on admission. cPL was higher with SIRS (median 158 vs 75 µg/L, P = 0.018) and not linked to death (6 deaths); pancreatitis was not confirmed by imaging (CB508, CH31). Only 2 of 20 B. gibsoni dogs passed the same cut-off ([CB045](articles/CB045_2019_retrospective-study-intercurrent-pancreatitis-babesia-gibsoni.md)).
- **Glucose and insulin.** Of 94 B. rossi dogs, 16 were hypoglycaemic, 62 normoglycaemic and 16 hyperglycaemic. Median insulin was 10.7, 10.7 and 21.7 pmol/L (no significant difference), mostly below the assay limit; two dogs had inappropriately high insulin (CB340, abstract only). Hypoglycaemic dogs had higher cortisol before and after ACTH and lower thyroxine ([CB342](articles/CB342_2007_response-pituitary-adrenal-pituitary-thyroidal-axes.md), 12 per group, survivors only). Glucose was low in 18.3% (CB330) and 9.1% (CB280), and high in 30.9% (CB280).
- **Endocrine axes.** B. rossi non-survivors (7 of 95) had higher cortisol and ACTH and lower total and free T4; TSH did not differ (CB338, CH31). In B. canis, 23 dogs had low TT4 and FT4 correlated with urea and creatinine ([CB441](articles/CB441_2012_euthyroid-sick-syndrome-babesiosis-caused-babesia.md), CH35, no controls). In 13 dogs vs 10 controls, T3, T4, fT4 and TSH were lower and IL-6 higher, with IL-6 inversely related to T3 and TSH ([CB503](articles/CB503_2015_low-t3-syndrome-babesiosis-associated-increased.md), CH37, abstract only). Aldosterone was higher and blood pressure lower in 14 B. canis dogs, but only 4 had values above the reference interval ([CB485](articles/CB485_2014_hyperaldosteronism-association-hypotension-azotaemia-babesiosis.md), secondary-tagged).
- **Electrolytes and acid-base.** In 70 B. canis dogs, low sodium, low potassium and high chloride were common ([CB431](articles/CB431_2012_strong-monovalent-electrolyte-imbalances-serum-infected.md), abstract only). In 40 early-stage dogs, most were alkalaemic and nearly all hypochloraemic ([CB423](articles/CB423_2012_electrolyte-level-blood-ph-infected-various.md)). Half of 14 dogs had mixed disorders, typically metabolic acidosis (lactate, chloride) with alkalosis (hypochloraemia, hypoalbuminaemia) ([CB068](articles/CB068_2020_acid-base-status-babesiosis-caused-babesia.md)). Twelve anaemic B. rossi dogs showed hypoalbuminaemic alkalosis with dilutional acidosis, and central venous oxygen tension stayed low for 72 h after either transfusion ([CB368](articles/CB368_2009_prospective-randomized-comparison-oxyglobin-hb-200.md)). Three of 21 non-azotaemic dogs met SIADH criteria ([CB044](articles/CB044_2019_hyponatraemia-syndrome-inappropriate-antidiuretic-hormone-secretion.md)).
- **Lipids and metabolome.** In 29 dogs vs 10 controls, SAA was higher (168.3 vs 3.2 µg/mL), cholesterol lower (4.79 vs 6.15 mmol/L) and ApoA-1 higher ([CB039](articles/CB039_2019_association-acute-babesia-canis-infection-serum.md)). Serum and saliva metabolomics (CH06) point to glutathione, amino-acid and purine pathways (CB091, CB137).

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Hypoglycaemia in B. rossi is not usually insulin-driven (CB340). The authors suggest glucose use, glycogen stores and liver function; none was measured.
- Non-thyroidal-illness physiology: cortisol rose and T4 fell with normal TSH in experimental B. rossi infection (CB105, 5 dogs), earlier at high dose (cortisol peak 96 vs 108 h; T4 nadir 108 vs 144 h). IL-6 suppression of the thyroid axis rests on a correlation (CB503) and a self-review ([CB157](articles/CB157_2017_over-20-years-research-babesiosis-faculty.md)).
- Hypotension-triggered vasopressin (CB044; vasopressin not measured) and aldosterone-driven hypokalaemia (CB485, CB157).

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).*
- B. rossi, 320 dogs (CB040, abstract only): death was associated with hypoglycaemia, hyperlactataemia, azotaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, hypercortisolaemia and low thyroxine. No independent predictor emerged; ROC cut-offs were not validated.
- B. canis: phosphate above 3 mmol/L and albumin below 2.2 g/dL with death in 240 dogs ([CB061](articles/CB061_2020_complications-risk-factors-regarding-outcomes-babesiosis.md), secondary-tagged); lactate separated 8 non-survivors from 7 survivors ([CB516](articles/CB516_2016_prognostic-markers-acute-babesia-canis-infections.md), CH21, secondary-tagged).

**Convergence and conflict**

- **Cortisol and T4 (best agreement).** Higher cortisol and lower T4 go with death or severity in four B. rossi sources: the 2005 cohort (CB338, CH31), CB342, the 320-dog series (CB040) and the experiment (CB105). The 2007 Onderstepoort work is therefore strengthened, but all four come from one hospital. In B. canis, low T4 appears in two small Warsaw series (CB441, CB503) with no outcome link. TSH was normal in B. rossi (CB338, CB105) and low in CB503. [CB204](articles/CB204_2021_euthyroid-sick-syndrome-changes-thyroid-hormones.md) cannot be attributed to Babesia, because E. canis was in every dog.
- **Glucose.** The old contrast of B. rossi hypoglycaemia against Lviv hyperglycaemia does not hold: 16 of 94 B. rossi dogs were also hyperglycaemic (CB340). High insulin does not explain hypoglycaemia. CB340 had no healthy controls, and secondary CB486 reports higher insulin in B. canis than in healthy dogs (abstract). These are unreconciled.
- **Liver.** AST rises more often than ALT in both large series (CB330, CB280). Histology in B. rossi (CB147) and suspected B. canis (CB307) agrees on cholestasis, but both sampled severe or fatal cases. CB409 argues against mild anaemia alone as the cause.
- **Chloride.** CB431 found high chloride common, CB423 low chloride in nearly all, and CB068 both. Timing or case mix may matter. Only CB068 and CB368 measured blood gases.
- **Pancreas.** B. rossi (28% above cut-off, CB508) looks higher than B. gibsoni (2 of 20, CB045), but this compares separate studies.

**Unresolved questions**

- Are cortisol and T4 predictive independently of lactate, urea and bilirubin? CB040 found no independent predictor. Test with free T4, T3 and ACTH in prospective B. rossi and B. canis cohorts.
- What causes hypoglycaemia when insulin is low? Measure lactate, bile acids and cytokines against serial glucose in hypoglycaemic and normoglycaemic dogs.
- How much hyperbilirubinaemia is cholestatic rather than haemolytic? Use fractionated bilirubin, bile acids and GGT over days, with histology.
- Is hyponatraemia vasopressin-mediated? Measure copeptin and osmolality against MAP, and aldosterone against potassium, in the same dogs.
- Does chloride direction depend on time since onset? Take serial blood gases with strong-ion analysis on days 0–3 in one cohort.
- What is the true pancreatitis frequency per species, with cPL plus ultrasound under one protocol?
