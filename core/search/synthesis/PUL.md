### PUL — Pulmonary injury and gas exchange

**Question this direction asks**

Which lung lesions and gas-exchange failures occur in canine babesiosis, how often, and in which species? Does the clinical label "Babesia-associated ARDS" match a recognisable tissue lesion? Is the injury caused by large-vessel obstruction, microvascular and inflammatory injury, the host response, or treatment such as transfusion and fluids?

**Evidence base**

Seven articles have PUL as their NLP primary category, published from 2008 to 2023. Five are round 4 (2006-2016): [CB356](articles/CB356_2008_scintigraphic-evaluation-pulmonary-perfusion-pattern-hospitalised.md), [CB365](articles/CB365_2009_influence-anaemia-azotaemia-infected-babesia-canis.md), [CB398](articles/CB398_2010_evolution-clinical-haematological-biochemical-findings-young.md), [CB447](articles/CB447_2013_cerebral-babesiosis-acute-respiratory-distress-syndrome.md) and [CB474](articles/CB474_2014_clinico-biochemical-responses-experimental-infection-babesia.md). Two are later: [CB102](articles/CB102_2022_successful-treatment-suspect-babesia-induced-ards.md) and [CB111](articles/CB111_2023_lung-pathology-natural-babesia-rossi-infection.md). By design:

- 2 single-dog case reports (CB447, CB102)
- 1 necropsy series with immunohistochemistry (CB111)
- 1 prospective case-control scintigraphy study, 14 infected and 9 healthy dogs (CB356)
- 1 experimental infection, 5 infected and 5 control dogs (CB474)
- 2 associational or descriptive clinical studies with no lung outcome (CB365, 127 dogs; CB398, two groups of 17 dogs)

By species: B. rossi 2 (CB356, CB111; both suspected); B. canis or B. canis canis 3 (CB365, CB447, CB474; none confirmed); B. vogeli 2 (CB398, CB102; both confirmed). Four are abstract-only (CB365, CB398, CB447, CB111). The seven are seven independent sources. CB111 belongs to cohort CH04 (Onderstepoort necropsy tissue bank), but the other CH04 members (CB054, CB129, CB147) are not PUL primaries.

The category assignment is automatic (NLP) and was not checked by a person. Three primaries look off-topic for PUL: CB365 (anaemia and azotaemia), CB398 (haematology in young dogs with vector-borne infections) and CB474 (general clinical biochemistry; respiratory distress is one sign among many). They are kept as assigned. Only four primaries (CB356, CB447, CB102, CB111) study lung disease directly.

Twenty-seven further articles carry PUL as a secondary tag. About eleven report a lung finding (for example [CB032](articles/CB032_2019_babesiosis-male-boerboel-hematobiochemical-anatomic-pathological.md), [CB188](articles/CB188_2020_pathomorphological-parasitological-molecular-diagnosis-babesia-gibsoni.md) and [CB249](articles/CB249_2024_pathological-diagnosis-babesiosis-labrador-retriever.md)), mostly single necropsy reports. The digest lines for the others (for example CB093, CB246, CB394, CB424, CB473) report no lung result. Articles used in the earlier synthesis but outside the NLP PUL set (CB312, CB025, CB068) are no longer covered. See the [search log](search-log.md) for how articles were found.

**What has been studied**

*Syndromes.*
- **ARDS, B. rossi (tissue).** CB111 examined lungs from fatal natural infections. Alveolar oedema accompanied every death. Histology showed acute interstitial pneumonia with haemorrhage and mononuclear infiltrates, and fibrin in alveoli in slightly over half of cases. MAC387 and CD204 (monocyte-macrophage) and CD3 (T cell) labelling was higher than in controls. The authors say the pattern only partly matches the exudative phase of diffuse alveolar damage. Sample size is not in the abstract. Most deaths occurred within a day of presentation.
- **ARDS, B. canis (tissue).** CB447 (France, 2013) describes one Scottish Terrier with a diffuse interstitial and alveolar radiographic pattern that died six hours after presentation. Necropsy showed cerebral babesiosis and ARDS, with red cell clumps in lung, liver and brain. The authors call it the first histology-confirmed Babesia-associated ARDS in a dog. The lesion was therefore recorded in B. canis ten years before CB111, but from one dog and an abstract.
- **ARDS, B. vogeli (clinical).** CB102 describes a puppy that became hypoxaemic about 14 hours after admission for haemolytic anaemia (P:F 140). It survived on lung-protective ventilation with neuromuscular blockade and was well at three months. Confounders: about 42 mL/kg red cell transfusion (TRALI not excluded), ehrlichiosis co-infection, and an effusion with bacteria on cytology.
- **Large-vessel embolism not found.** CB356 (Onderstepoort, 2008) looked for wedge-shaped perfusion defects in 14 hospitalised dogs. None had them, and the perfusion pattern did not differ from 9 healthy Beagles (P = 1.00). Oxygenation indices were similar in both groups (full text). Small or partly occluding emboli and microvascular disease were not excluded. Scans were read visually, the sample was small and the groups differed in breed.
- **Respiratory distress as a sign.** In the CB474 experimental infection, respiratory distress occurred in 20% of infected dogs (one of five). Lungs were not examined. [CB332](articles/CB332_2007_abdominal-compartment-syndrome-babesiosis.md) describes respiratory distress with abdominal compartment syndrome in one dog. [CB389](articles/CB389_2010_clinical-management-babesiosis.md) (review) lists ARDS among complications linked to a poorer outcome.
- **Pulmonary oedema elsewhere.** Single necropsies or small series report oedema, haemorrhage or interstitial pneumonia in B. gibsoni (CB188, CB249), B. canis ([CB097](articles/CB097_2022_pathomorphological-changes-german-boxer-infected-babesia.md)), unspecified Babesia (CB032) and 3 of 77 puppy deaths ([CB195](articles/CB195_2020_role-babesiosis-pup-mortality.md)). [CB079](articles/CB079_2021_multiple-complications-infected-babesia-gibsoni.md) reports non-cardiogenic oedema with DIC in a B. gibsoni survivor. [CB260](articles/CB260_2024_studies-clinical-syndromes-linked-babesia-gibsoni.md) assigned 9 of 24 PCR-positive dogs to a syndrome, with ARDS among the four definitions (1 dog in the earlier extraction). [CB080](articles/CB080_2021_disorders-blood-circulation-probable-cause-death.md) (6 fatal B. canis dogs, cohort CH27) reports multi-organ congestion and oedema and proposes circulatory failure.

*Proposed mechanisms (largely untested).*
- Macrophage and T-cell inflammation with intra-alveolar fibrin, suggesting coagulation-linked injury (CB111).
- Microvascular red cell aggregation (CB447). This fits the negative large-vessel scan in CB356, but neither study tested it.
- Circulatory failure and congestion (CB080).
- Increased vascular permeability (CB079).
- Transfusion-related injury (raised as a confounder in CB102).

CB365 found no difference in urea or creatinine between anaemic and non-anaemic dogs (abstract only). It has no lung content.

*Severity and outcome markers (associations only).* No PUL primary tests a marker against pulmonary outcome. CB398 reports thrombocytopenia and lymphocytosis in B. vogeli-infected young dogs, but co-infections prevent attribution to the species (abstract only). Pulmonary complications appear as outcome labels in reviews such as CB389 and [CB278](articles/CB278_2025_review-prognostic-biomarkers-babesiosis.md).

**Convergence and conflict**

- **Lesion.** Alveolar oedema with interstitial inflammation recurs in B. rossi (CB111, CH04), B. canis (CB447, CB097, CB080), B. gibsoni (CB188, CB249) and the Boerboel (CB032). Outside CB111 these are single dogs or small series, mostly smear-identified.
- **Species.** ARDS or an ARDS-like lesion is now reported in B. rossi, B. canis and B. vogeli. Each comes from one study or one dog, so incidence and virulence cannot be compared. [CB119](articles/CB119_2023_diverse-pathogenicity-various-babesia-parasite-species.md) ranks B. vogeli as the least pathogenic of four main species, yet the clinical ARDS survivor (CB102) had B. vogeli, with confounders.
- **Mechanism.** CB356 found no large-vessel perfusion defects, so macroscopic pulmonary thromboembolism is not supported as a common cause of breathlessness in hospitalised dogs. The fibrin and cell-aggregation findings (CB111, CB447) point to microvascular or inflammatory injury. CB080 favours circulatory failure, which fluid therapy could also produce. These readings are not reconciled.
- **Gas exchange.** CB356 found oxygenation indices similar to controls in hospitalised dogs, whereas CB102 recorded P:F 140. Hypoxaemia is therefore not general, and lung failure looks like a subset of complicated cases. The note does not say whether the CB356 dogs were dyspnoeic.

**Unresolved questions**

- What is the incidence of ARDS (P:F below 300 with bilateral infiltrates) in the first 48 hours in prospective B. rossi versus B. canis cohorts, and does it predict death independently of renal and cerebral complications?
- Do dyspnoeic dogs with babesiosis have microvascular obstruction that scintigraphy misses? CT or contrast imaging, D-dimer and platelet counts could be paired with perfusion scans.
- Does intra-alveolar fibrin in the CH04 dogs track systemic DIC markers or the monocyte-macrophage count?
- Is the CB111 lesion present in survivors or early in disease, or only terminal? A serial experimental design like CB474, with lung imaging and histology, could test this.
- Does the CB447 red cell aggregation pattern also appear in B. rossi lungs?
- Does transfusion volume or fluid balance in the first 24 hours predict hypoxaemia after adjusting for parasitaemia and haematocrit (CB102, CB080)?

**Data available**

No dataset with pulmonary data is verified. The PUL primaries have none (status 4 for CB356, CB447, CB474 and CB102; status 5 for CB365, CB398 and CB111). The datasets linked to this category are all status 1 (public download verified) and none holds lung data:
