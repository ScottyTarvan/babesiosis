## COA

- CB096 (2022; B. canis; single case report with treatment follow-up; n=1 dog; descriptive): A 2-month-old pup with smear-diagnosed B. canis had severe anaemia, Coombs-positive haemolysis, thrombocytopenia, azotaemia and raised ALT, and recovered after diminazine with supportive care.
- CB098 (2022; B. rossi; experimental infection with two inoculum doses, treated at predefined endpoints; n=6 beagles (1 donor for inoculum, 3 high-dose, 2 low-dose); mechanistic (experimental)): In experimental B. rossi infection of Beagles, inflammation began before clinical disease, inoculum dose changed its timing, and treatment transiently amplified cytokine and CRP responses.
- CB101 (2022; B. rossi; experimental infection, prospective longitudinal (data article); n=6 Beagles (1 splenectomised donor, 5 infected: 3 high-dose, 2 low-dose); mechanistic (experimental)): Data article releasing daily clinical, CBC, CRP and 13-cytokine data from five Beagles experimentally infected with B. rossi at two inoculum doses and treated at endpoints.
- CB105 (2022; B. rossi; experimental infection, two inoculum doses, serial sampling; n=5 dogs (2 low dose, 3 high dose); mechanistic (experimental)): In experimental B. rossi infection of five Beagles, cortisol rose and T4 fell in step with severity and inoculum dose, while TSH stayed within reference limits.
- CB109 (2023; B. canis (with D. repens co-infection); natural-infection cohort (healthy, single-infection and co-infection groups) with haematology/biochemistry and blood mRNA expression; n=140 dogs: 39 healthy, 82 B. canis, 12 D. repens, 7 co-infected; gene expression subset n=54 (3 healthy, 32 babesiosis, 12 D. repens, 7 co-infected); associational): B. canis dogs had anaemia, thrombocytopenia and renal/hepatic abnormalities; severe babesiosis showed highest IFN-γ and SOCS3 expression, and co-infected dogs had milder ALP, urea and creatinine changes.
- CB110 (2023; B. vogeli (with A. platys, E. canis co-infections); cross-sectional descriptive haematology by infection status; n=122 dogs: 58 uninfected, 64 infected with at least one of A. platys, E. canis, B. vogeli; per-group counts only in Figure 1 (not in text); descriptive): In subclinical Thai shelter dogs, infected dogs had lower RBC, Hb, HCT and platelets than uninfected, lowest with triple A. platys/B. vogeli/E. canis infection; B. vogeli alone did not clearly differ in Table 1.
- CB112 (2023; B. rossi; retrospective single-time-point case-control omics study (archived serum, TMT label-based LC-MS/MS); n=52 dogs: 32 infected (18 uncomplicated, 14 complicated) and 20 healthy; associational): TMT serum proteomics in 32 B. rossi-infected and 20 healthy dogs found 78 proteins differing across groups, implicating haemostasis, innate immunity, lipid metabolism and inflammation, with candidate severity markers.
- CB115 (2023; B. rossi; retrospective observational study of haematology analyser records plus experimental infection; n=103 naturally infected; 10 IMHA; 14 healthy controls; 5 experimentally infected; associational): ARC was raised but lower in B. rossi dogs than IMHA dogs despite similar HCT, and fell about 48 h after parasitaemia in experimental dogs, indicating an inadequate regenerative response.
- CB123 (2024; B. canis; natural-infection cohort with age/sex/breed subgroup comparisons; n=61 dogs; descriptive / associational): In 61 dogs with natural B. canis infection, young dogs had lower seropositivity, higher parasitaemia and more severe anaemia and leukopenia than adults, with minor sex and breed effects on neutrophils.
- CB139 (2025; B. canis; single-animal necropsy/histopathology case report plus retrospective molecular survey of banked spleens; n=1 fatal case; survey of 91 wolves (B. canis 5/79, H. canis 78/81, L. infantum 21/88); descriptive): A wild wolf's death was attributed to systemic B. canis haemolytic disease with H. canis and L. infantum co-infection; B. canis prevalence in 91 Cantabrian wolves was low (6.3%).
- CB145 (2026; B. rossi; prospective observational natural-infection cohort with healthy controls and necropsy of non-survivors; n=97 infected dogs (85 survivors, 12 non-survivors) and 15 healthy controls; associational): In 97 B. rossi-infected dogs vs 15 controls, severe thrombocytopenia, platelet activation, factor consumption, fibrinolytic shutdown, fibrin microthrombi and high CRP/SAA indicate thromboinflammation; 12% died.
- CB146 (2026; mixed/unspecified (European Babesia, mainly B. canis); retrospective necropsy/histopathology series; n=not reported in abstract; descriptive): Hearts of dogs dying of European babesiosis showed hemorrhage and pericardial effusion grossly and monocytic leukostasis, hemorrhage, edema, necrosis, congestion and myocarditis histologically.
- CB153 (2026; B. gibsoni; prospective cohort (primary IMHA vs IMHA with B. gibsoni), 60-day follow-up; n=55 dogs (26 primary IMHA; 29 IMHA with B. gibsoni); prognostic): In 55 dogs with IMHA followed 60 days, male sex, marked spherocytosis and concurrent PCR-confirmed B. gibsoni (HR 2.6, p=0.024) independently predicted death.

## co-infection (secondary)
- CB126 (2024; B. rossi; cross-sectional natural-infection cohort with co-infection qPCR and haematology/biochemistry association analysis; n=150 dogs; descriptive): Among 150 dogs with babesiosis (149 B. rossi), only 3 (2.0%) had E. canis co-infection and no haematological variable was associated with co-infection, suggesting such changes are intrinsic to babesiosis.
