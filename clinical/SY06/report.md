## Definition

**Changed.** The working definition ("icterus beyond what haemolysis explains") cannot be applied: no source gives a test that separates haemolytic from hepatic bilirubin in a dog with babesiosis. The page now:
- treats icterus as a sign with both causes;
- defines hepatopathy by the only babesiosis criteria found: ALT >80 U/L, ALP >380 U/L or GLDH >16 U/L, or bile acids >30 µmol/L with one abnormal enzyme (Welzl 2001, B. rossi).

Histology of fatal B. rossi (Horrell 2026) shows secondary injury: hypoxic centrilobular necrosis, cholestasis and Kupffer cell activation. It does not show primary liver failure.

## Not found

- No test of any treatment aimed at the liver in B. rossi or B. canis.
- No source on vitamin K, lactulose for hepatic encephalopathy, or nutrition in babesiosis. These are listed as evidence gaps.
- No fractionated bilirubin or serial bile acid study.
- No liver histology from surviving dogs.
- No evidence of liver injury from an antibabesial drug at the label dose. Only the 10-fold imidocarb overdose case was found.

## Least sure

- The NAC doses rest on abstracts only. The frequency in Mundassery 2025 is not stated, and the page says so.
- The ademetionine series does not name the Babesia species. Heptral-vet is a Ukrainian product.
- The UDCA, silymarin, vitamin E and oral SAMe doses come from a chronic hepatitis consensus. They are labelled as such.
- Leisewitz 2019 contradicts itself on sensitivity and specificity for bilirubin >14.7 µmol/L. The page gives the Table 3 values and notes the conflict.
- "Liver damage in 30 of 56" in Welzl 2001 is counted from the paper's figures, not stated in its text.
