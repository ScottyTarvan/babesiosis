## Definition

Confirmed, with changes. The page covers glucose, lactate, acid-base, electrolytes and the adrenal and thyroid axes. The literature does not treat these as one syndrome. They are markers of severe disease that cluster in collapsed, severely anaemic B. rossi dogs. The cortisol and thyroxine changes are a stress response and a non-thyroidal-illness pattern, not adrenal or thyroid disease (ACTH stimulation showed no adrenal insufficiency). Hypoglycaemia is the only derangement with a direct, effective treatment.

## Answers to the page questions

1. Hypoglycaemia (<3.3 mmol/L): 9% of all and 19.8% of admitted B. rossi dogs (Keller); 19.2% of 320 (CB040). Collapse is the main risk factor; young age, severe anaemia, icterus and vomiting also count. OR for death 3.47 (CB040).
2. Treatment: dextrose bolus then infusion. Recurrence after treatment is not reported in any babesiosis source.
3. Lactate: admission >5 mmol/L, or failure to halve in 8 to 16 h, or persistently >4.4 mmol/L, predicts death (Nel 2004, via Schoeman 2009 in mmol/L). CB040 cut-off 4.1 mmol/L.
4. Acid-base: mixed metabolic acidosis (lactate, hyperchloraemia, unmeasured anions) with respiratory alkalosis and hypoalbuminaemic alkalosis. No babesiosis evidence for bicarbonate; the human guideline suggests against it for lactic acidosis.
5. Electrolytes: hypokalaemia and hyponatraemia are common; SIADH in 3 of 21 non-azotaemic B. canis dogs. No babesiosis treatment data.
6. Cortisol high and T4 low with severity and death; no adrenal insufficiency. No evidence for steroids or thyroid hormone.

## For the coordinator

- Dextrose dose for SY07. SY07 gives 0.5 g/kg from one non-babesiosis case report. Two better sources state the dose as a volume of 50% dextrose: (a) a B. rossi dog given 0.5 mL/kg of 50% dextrose IV, which responded within 90 s (Keller MMedVet dissertation, 2004, UPSpace https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/23003, full text); (b) a canine review giving 0.5 to 1 mL/kg of 50% dextrose, diluted at least 1:2, over 5 to 10 min, then a 2.5% to 5% dextrose CRI (Idowu & Heading 2018, Can Vet J 59:642-649, PMID 29910479, full text in PMC). By arithmetic, 0.5 to 1 mL/kg of 50% is 0.25 to 0.5 g/kg, so the SY07 value is the top of the review's range. Neither source states a g/kg dose, so this page does not convert.
- Two references have no DOI or PMID and use a URL: R9 (Keller dissertation, UPSpace) and R23 (DiBartola 2010, dvm360 proceedings). Both were confirmed on the publisher page.
- Whether a veterinarian should review the potassium guidance once the AAHA table can be read.
